Ringgit Among Asia's Top Performing Currencies in 2026

The Edge Malaysia · 23 June 2026 · Business

The Malaysian ringgit has cemented its place among Asia's best-performing currencies in 2026, appreciating approximately 2.64% against the US dollar as of end-April and trading at around RM3.95 per USD — levels not seen in several years. The currency's gains place it in elite company alongside the Chinese yuan and the Singapore dollar, both of which have also logged notable advances against a broadly weaker greenback. For a currency that spent much of 2023 and early 2024 languishing near historic lows, the turnaround has been remarkable.

The ringgit's ascent builds on an even more dramatic recovery in 2025, when the currency surged more than 10% against the US dollar over the course of the year. That rally was driven by a combination of factors: a shift in global investor sentiment away from US assets, a softening of Federal Reserve rate expectations, and growing recognition of Malaysia's improving economic fundamentals. The momentum has carried into 2026, with the currency finding additional support from strong trade surpluses and a robust current account position that continues to provide a structural buffer against external volatility.

Bank Negara Malaysia's monetary policy has played a central role in underpinning currency stability. The central bank has maintained an overnight policy rate that balances growth support with inflation control, while also actively managing liquidity conditions to prevent excessive volatility in the foreign exchange market. Policymakers have signalled a preference for gradual, fundamentals-driven currency appreciation rather than sharp swings that could disrupt export competitiveness or create speculative pressures.

Improving investor sentiment has been another key pillar of the ringgit's strength. Foreign portfolio inflows into Malaysian equities and bonds have risen appreciably, drawn by the country's attractive yield differentials, a more stable political outlook, and the IMF's upgraded growth forecast. Malaysia's status as a major beneficiary of the global data centre and semiconductor investment wave has also elevated its profile among institutional investors seeking exposure to Southeast Asia's digital economy expansion.

Currency strategists caution that the path ahead is not without risks. Any resurgence in US dollar strength, a deterioration in global trade conditions, or a sharp drop in commodity prices — particularly crude palm oil and liquefied natural gas, which remain important export earners — could introduce headwinds for the ringgit. Nevertheless, the broad consensus among analysts is that Malaysia's improved macro fundamentals and diversified investor base provide a more resilient platform for the currency than at any point in the past decade, making a sustained correction back toward the RM4.50-plus levels of recent years unlikely in the near term.
