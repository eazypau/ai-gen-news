Ajinomoto Malaysia Set for RM603 Million Privatisation by Japanese Parent

New Straits Times · 23 June 2026 · Business

Japan's Ajinomoto Co. Inc. has launched a takeover offer for its Malaysian listed subsidiary, Ajinomoto (Malaysia) Berhad, in a privatisation exercise valued at approximately RM603.4 million. The Japanese parent is offering RM20 per share for the shares it does not already own, representing a premium of 31.6 percent over the last traded price of RM15.20 before the deal was announced and trading in the stock was suspended. The offer signals Ajinomoto Co.'s intention to take full control of its Malaysian operations and delist the company from the main market of Bursa Malaysia.

Ajinomoto Co. currently holds a 50.38 percent stake in its Malaysian unit, meaning the offer is directed at the remaining approximately 49.62 percent of shares held by independent shareholders. As part of the transaction structure, bonus shares are to be issued to existing shareholders to facilitate the mechanics of the deal, a process that will be explained in detail in the formal offer document expected to be released in the coming weeks. The deal requires approval from at least 75 percent of independent shareholders, a threshold that reflects the statutory requirements for privatisation exercises under Malaysian corporate law.

Ajinomoto (Malaysia) Berhad is one of the longest-listed companies on Bursa Malaysia and has been a household name in the country for decades, best known for its monosodium glutamate (MSG) seasoning products sold under the Ajinomoto brand. The company also produces a range of other food seasoning and flavour enhancement products distributed across Malaysia and exported to regional markets. Its manufacturing facility in Shah Alam has been a significant employer in the area, and the company has maintained a reputation for consistent dividend payouts, making it a favoured stock among retail investors seeking stable income.

The offer price of RM20 per share was received positively by the market, with analysts noting that the 31.6 percent premium is well above the typical range seen in privatisation exercises on Bursa Malaysia. Independent shareholders who had held the stock for a number of years at lower prices are expected to find the offer attractive, though some longer-term investors may weigh the steady dividend history and potential long-term value of the business against the lump-sum payout. Trading in Ajinomoto Malaysia shares was suspended following the announcement and is set to resume on Tuesday, when the stock is widely expected to trade up sharply towards the offer price.

If the privatisation succeeds and the required 75 percent shareholder approval is obtained, Ajinomoto Malaysia would be delisted from Bursa Malaysia, ending its decades-long history as a publicly listed company. The delisting would give Ajinomoto Co. full operational and strategic flexibility over its Malaysian subsidiary without the disclosure obligations and minority shareholder considerations that come with a public listing. Analysts noted that the move is consistent with a broader trend of Japanese parent companies privatising their Southeast Asian listed subsidiaries as they seek tighter integration of regional operations and more efficient capital allocation across their global business portfolios.
