Toh Ee Wei Refuses to Shut Door on World Championships Despite ACL Injury

Malay Mail · 23 June 2026 · Sports

Malaysia's national mixed doubles number one Toh Ee Wei has vowed to target a return for the BWF World Championships in New Delhi and the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, despite suffering a partial anterior cruciate ligament tear in her left knee during the quarter-final round of the Singapore Open. The injury, which also forced her withdrawal from the Indonesia Open alongside partner Chen Tang Jie, represents a significant setback at a critical juncture in the badminton calendar. Yet Ee Wei has refused to adopt a defeatist outlook, declaring that she intends to do everything in her power to be ready for the year-end showcase events that carry the greatest prestige on the international circuit.

The Badminton Association of Malaysia's medical team has been assessing the full extent of the injury and developing an appropriate rehabilitation and treatment protocol. A partial ACL tear, while less immediately debilitating than a complete rupture, nevertheless demands careful management — aggressive rehabilitation risks re-injury or aggravating the damage into a full tear, while excessive caution could leave Ee Wei underprepared when she eventually returns to competition. The medical team is understood to be exploring conservative non-surgical management options given the relatively short timeline between now and the World Championships, though the precise course of treatment will depend on ongoing assessments of the injury's severity and Ee Wei's response to initial therapy.

Head coach Nova Widianto, the former Indonesian mixed doubles specialist who took charge of Malaysia's mixed doubles programme, faces a disrupted planning cycle as a result of the setback. Ee Wei and Tang Jie had been progressing well as a partnership heading into the second half of the 2026 season, with the pair having posted some competitive performances at premier-level tournaments. Their enforced withdrawal from the Indonesia Open — traditionally one of the most important events on the BWF Super 1000 calendar — means a missed opportunity to accumulate crucial world ranking points and match sharpness ahead of the season's final stretch.

The World Championships in New Delhi represent the pinnacle of non-Olympic badminton, and a podium finish for Ee Wei and Tang Jie would represent a landmark achievement for the Malaysian mixed doubles programme. Malaysia has historically been stronger in the singles and men's doubles disciplines, and the emergence of Ee Wei and Tang Jie as a credible world-class mixed doubles pairing has been one of the sport's more encouraging domestic storylines in recent years. The pair's ability to return at full fitness for New Delhi will be watched closely by fans and selectors alike.

Toh Ee Wei's determination to fight back from injury reflects a wider culture of resilience that has long characterised Malaysian badminton. The country has produced multiple world champions across different generations, and the sport holds a special place in Malaysia's national sporting identity. The BAM has invested heavily in developing the next generation of elite players, and Ee Wei's resolve in the face of adversity exemplifies the mindset that the association has sought to cultivate. Her recovery timeline will become clearer in the coming weeks as medical assessments progress and her rehabilitation gets underway in earnest.
