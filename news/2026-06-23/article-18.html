FIFA World Cup 2026: Malaysians Can Watch All 104 Matches Free on RTM

Malay Mail · 23 June 2026 · Sports

Football fans across Malaysia have been celebrating the government's decision to make all 104 matches of the FIFA World Cup 2026 freely available to the public, with live coverage broadcast via RTM and accessible through the Unifi TV platform using a MyDigital ID login. The arrangement, which came into force from 11 June — the day of the tournament's opening matches — has been widely praised as a public service triumph, allowing millions of Malaysians who lack pay-television subscriptions to follow the most-watched sporting event on the planet without any additional cost. The free-to-air arrangement was the result of negotiations between the government and broadcasting rights holders, with Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil playing a central role in securing the access deal.

The 2026 edition of the World Cup is historic in its own right, marking the first time the tournament has been staged with 48 participating nations rather than the traditional 32. Spread across co-host nations Canada, Mexico, and the United States, the expanded format delivers a record-breaking 104 matches, nearly a third more than previous editions. The sheer volume of football on offer has been a particular boon for Malaysian fans, who have eagerly embraced the chance to watch a wider range of teams, styles, and storylines across the five-week tournament. The expanded group stage means more matches, more upsets, and more moments for global football audiences to savour.

One practical challenge for Malaysian viewers is the timing of matches. Given the significant time difference between Malaysia and the North American host cities, the majority of games kick off between midnight and noon in Malaysian time. This has meant late-night and early-morning viewing sessions have become a common feature of domestic life during the tournament, with many fans adjusting their sleep schedules or gathering in groups at restaurants and mamak stalls to catch live broadcasts together. The communal aspect of World Cup viewing has long been a feature of Malaysian culture, and the free streaming arrangement has ensured that this tradition continues regardless of household income or subscription status.

Minister Fahmi noted that the free broadcast arrangement has been enthusiastically received by the public and has delivered an additional practical benefit: restaurants, mamak establishments, and food courts no longer need to pay commercial broadcasting fees to screen the matches, reducing their operating costs at a time when many food and beverage operators continue to manage tight margins. The minister highlighted the initiative as an example of government digital policy delivering tangible economic and social value to everyday Malaysians. Access through the MyDigital ID system also serves the secondary purpose of driving registration and adoption of the government's national digital identity platform.

Viewers accessing the tournament through RTM's streaming application have reported a generally positive experience, though some users noted initial congestion on the platform during high-interest matches involving prominent footballing nations. RTM has indicated it deployed additional server capacity to manage peak streaming loads, and technical improvements have been applied progressively as the tournament has progressed. With the knockout rounds now underway, viewership figures are expected to climb further as the field narrows and the stakes intensify — ensuring that Malaysia's free World Cup coverage experiment will be put to its most rigorous test in the coming weeks.
