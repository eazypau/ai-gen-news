Government Allocates RM2 Billion to Build Malaysia's Sovereign AI Cloud

Bernama · 23 June 2026 · Technology

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has announced a RM2 billion allocation for the development of a sovereign AI cloud infrastructure, a landmark investment that positions Malaysia as one of the first countries in Southeast Asia to build a nationally controlled cloud computing platform dedicated to artificial intelligence workloads. The announcement forms part of a significantly larger RM5.9 billion commitment by the government toward AI research, development, and commercialisation over the coming years, reflecting the Madani administration's determination to establish AI as a cornerstone of Malaysia's long-term economic transformation strategy.

The sovereign AI cloud is designed to address a structural vulnerability that has long concerned Malaysian policymakers: the country's heavy dependence on foreign hyperscalers — primarily American cloud platforms such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud — for the storage and processing of sensitive government and national data. By building sovereign infrastructure, Malaysia aims to retain greater control over critical datasets, reduce exposure to foreign jurisdictional risks, and ensure that AI systems deployed across public services operate on platforms subject to Malaysian law and oversight. Officials framed the initiative as a matter of both digital security and national economic resilience.

The Ministry of Digital, which received an allocation of RM1.36 billion under Budget 2026, is the lead agency responsible for overseeing the sovereign cloud programme. The ministry is expected to work in partnership with domestic technology companies, state-linked enterprises, and select international technology partners to design, build, and operate the infrastructure. Policymakers have been careful to describe the sovereign AI cloud not as a move toward digital isolationism but as a complementary layer to the existing commercial hyperscaler ecosystem — one that will serve government agencies, public research institutions, and strategic industries while the broader private sector continues to leverage global cloud platforms.

The announcement accelerates Malaysia's ambition to become a regional AI hub and a leading player in Southeast Asia's rapidly expanding digital economy. Officials have pointed to the country's existing advantages — its position as a dominant data centre host, a growing pool of AI talent, and strong bilateral technology partnerships with leading nations — as foundations upon which a credible sovereign AI capability can be constructed. The government has also outlined plans to use the sovereign AI cloud as a platform for developing homegrown large language models and AI applications tailored to Malaysian languages, including Bahasa Malaysia, Tamil, and Mandarin Chinese.

The RM2 billion sovereign AI cloud initiative is expected to create thousands of high-skilled jobs and catalyse downstream economic activity in areas such as AI application development, data engineering, and cybersecurity. Critics have questioned whether the timeline and budget are sufficient for a project of this technical complexity, and called for robust governance frameworks to ensure the infrastructure is built to international standards and avoids becoming a costly underutilised asset. The government has indicated it will release detailed technical specifications and procurement timelines in the coming months as the initiative moves from announcement to implementation.
