Parliament Opens with Acrimony Over Hamzah's Return as Opposition Leader

The Star · 23 June 2026 · Politics

The second meeting of the Fifth Session of the 15th Parliament opened on June 22 with a public display of friction within the Perikatan Nasional opposition bloc, as Datuk Seri Hamzah Zainudin of Bersatu resumed his position as Leader of the Opposition. Hamzah's return to the opposition frontbench came after a brief period in which Ahmad Samsuri Mokhtar of PAS had occupied the role, and the transition back sparked an immediate and heated exchange on the floor of Dewan Rakyat that set a combative tone for the parliamentary sitting, which is scheduled to run until July 16.

PAS members were vocal in their dissatisfaction with the arrangement, with several MPs accusing Bersatu of acting in bad faith and behaving in a manner that some characterised as "bullying" within the coalition. The row reflects the deepening fracture between PAS and Bersatu that has been building since PAS formally broke off political cooperation with Bersatu on June 8, even as both parties nominally remain members of the Perikatan Nasional coalition. The public nature of the dispute marks one of the most visible ruptures within PN since the coalition's formation.

Adding to the theatre of the opening day, former Prime Minister Tan Sri Muhyiddin Yassin's designated seat was moved away from the opposition frontbench, a shift that several observers read as a symbolic marginalisation of Bersatu's most senior figure within the PN parliamentary group. Muhyiddin, who faces ongoing corruption charges, was present in the chamber and the repositioning of his seat drew comment and scrutiny from both government and opposition MPs, as well as from the gallery of journalists covering the parliamentary opening.

The current dispute over the Opposition Leader post traces its origins to the ongoing power struggle between PAS and Bersatu for dominance within PN. PAS, which won the largest number of seats among PN component parties in GE15, has long chafed at Bersatu's outsized influence in coalition affairs despite holding fewer parliamentary seats. The role of Opposition Leader carries both symbolic weight and practical parliamentary privileges, making it a prize that neither party is willing to cede without a fight.

The government side of the aisle largely observed the internal PN dispute with a mixture of amusement and strategic interest. Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim is expected to use the parliamentary session to advance several landmark pieces of legislation, including the Cybercrime Bill 2026 and a constitutional amendment to separate the roles of the Attorney General and the Public Prosecutor. With the opposition distracted by its internal divisions, government whips are cautiously optimistic about securing the numbers needed to pass the more contentious bills on the legislative agenda.
