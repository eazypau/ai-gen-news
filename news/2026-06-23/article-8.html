Constitutional Amendment Bill to Separate Attorney General and Public Prosecutor Roles

The Star · 23 June 2026 · Politics

The Constitution Amendment Bill (No.2) 2026, which proposes the formal separation of the roles of the Attorney General and the Director of Public Prosecutions, has been retabled in parliament and is expected to be a focal point of debate during the current Dewan Rakyat session running until July 16. The bill represents one of the most significant constitutional reform proposals in years, addressing a structural feature of Malaysia's legal system that critics have long argued is incompatible with the principles of judicial independence and the rule of law.

Under the current constitutional arrangement, the Attorney General serves simultaneously as the country's chief legal adviser to the government and as the Public Prosecutor with sole discretion over whether to initiate, continue, or discontinue criminal proceedings. This dual function has been the subject of sustained criticism from legal scholars, bar council representatives, and civil society organisations, who argue that it creates an inherent conflict of interest and makes the prosecutorial process susceptible to political considerations. High-profile cases over the decades — involving both the prosecution and the halting of prosecutions against politically connected individuals — have repeatedly been cited as evidence of the system's vulnerability.

The proposed amendment would create a separate and independent office of the Public Prosecutor, whose holder would be appointed through a transparent process and would exercise prosecutorial discretion free from direction by the executive. The Attorney General would retain the role of legal adviser to the government but would be explicitly excluded from directing or interfering in prosecution decisions. Proponents argue this model, which is used in many Commonwealth jurisdictions including the United Kingdom, Australia, and Singapore, would strengthen public confidence in the integrity of the criminal justice system.

The bill is expected to face robust debate in parliament from both sides of the aisle, though for different reasons. Some opposition members have expressed support for the principle of separation while raising concerns about the specific appointment mechanism for the new Public Prosecutor, arguing that it must be sufficiently insulated from executive influence to be meaningful. Government backbenchers, meanwhile, are broadly supportive but some have queried the transitional provisions and the timeline for implementing the structural changes within the Attorney General's Chambers.

Legal reform advocates have welcomed the bill's tabling as a long-overdue step, noting that successive governments had promised similar reforms but failed to follow through. The Malaysian Bar Council issued a statement expressing cautious optimism, while calling on parliament to ensure that any amendments to the bill during the committee stage do not dilute the core principle of prosecutorial independence. With the required two-thirds parliamentary majority potentially within reach given the unity government's numbers and likely cross-party support for the reform, observers believe there is a genuine chance of the bill passing before the current session concludes.
