Bersama to Contest 15 Seats in Upcoming Johor State Election

The Star · 23 June 2026 · Politics

Parti Bersama Malaysia (Bersama), a political party established just one month ago, announced on Friday evening in Johor Bahru that it will contest 15 seats in the upcoming Johor state election scheduled for July 11. The announcement was made by PKR President and Economy Minister Rafizi Ramli, who explained that Bersama's participation in the Johor election is intended primarily as a platform to gauge the party's level of support among voters before it scales up its activities ahead of the 16th general election. The Johor state assembly was dissolved on June 1 by Menteri Besar Datuk Onn Hafiz Ghazi, setting the stage for the July 11 polling day.

Rafizi said the selection of the 15 seats was not arbitrary but was guided by voter surveys conducted across Johor and by the strength of membership response in particular areas. The party identified constituencies where its candidates are likely to be competitive or where their participation could meaningfully influence the outcome, rather than fielding candidates purely for symbolic purposes. The candidate announcements made at the Friday evening press conference drew significant media attention given the novelty of the party and the compressed timeline under which it is organising its election machinery.

Bersama's entry into the Johor election adds a new dimension to what was already shaping up to be a fiercely contested state poll. Johor is traditionally considered a Barisan Nasional stronghold, given UMNO's historical dominance in the state and its large base of working-class and rural Malay voters. However, recent elections have shown that even in Johor, voter preferences have shifted, with Pakatan Harapan making significant inroads in urban and semi-urban seats during GE15. The addition of Bersama as a contender could complicate vote-splitting calculations for both the ruling PH-BN coalition and the PN opposition.

Political observers have noted that the timing of Bersama's establishment — just weeks before a state election — is consistent with Rafizi's stated strategy of using electoral contests as stress tests for the party's organisation and messaging. The party has positioned itself as a reformist vehicle that transcends ethnic and coalition lines, appealing to voters who feel underserved by both the unity government and the opposition. Whether this positioning will translate into votes in Johor, where communal and class-based loyalties remain strong, remains to be seen.

The Johor Menteri Besar, Onn Hafiz Ghazi, who called for the early dissolution of the state assembly, had framed the election as an opportunity to seek a fresh mandate for the BN-led state government. With the election just weeks away, all competing parties are in the final stages of deploying ground operations, mobilising volunteers, and rolling out manifesto commitments targeted at Johor voters. Bersama's participation, while unlikely to win a majority of its contested seats on a first outing, signals the party's ambitions to build a foothold in peninsular Malaysia's southern heartland ahead of the next federal election.
