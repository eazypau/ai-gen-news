Selangor Government Faces Pushback on Children's Playground Licensing Change

Malay Mail · 23 June 2026 · Lifestyle

The Malaysian Association of Theme Parks and Family Attractions, known as Matfa, has urged the Selangor state government to reconsider a proposed regulatory change that would reclassify children's indoor playlands under entertainment licensing — a reclassification that operators warn would increase their annual licensing fees by nearly 13 times compared to current levels. The proposal, which emerged from a review of licensing categories by Selangor authorities, has provoked alarm among business owners who operate children's play centres, indoor playgrounds, and family entertainment venues across the state, with many arguing that the fee increase would threaten the commercial viability of their businesses.

Under the proposed reclassification, children's playlands — which have until now been licensed under a different regulatory category reflecting their nature as child-focused family venues rather than conventional entertainment establishments — would be subjected to the higher fee schedule applicable to entertainment outlets. Operators have described the financial impact as potentially catastrophic, particularly for smaller, independently run play centres that operate on thin margins and are already managing elevated costs following years of post-pandemic recovery. A near-13-fold increase in licensing fees would represent a sudden and substantial addition to fixed operating costs, and many smaller operators say they would have little choice but to close or significantly scale back operations if the change proceeds as proposed.

Matfa has been vocal in pointing out that children's indoor playlands serve a distinctly different social function from conventional entertainment venues. These facilities provide safe, supervised play environments for young children, particularly in urban and suburban settings where outdoor play spaces may be limited or pose safety concerns due to traffic and other hazards. Families from a broad range of income backgrounds rely on these venues for children's birthday celebrations, weekend outings, and holiday activities. The association argues that access to affordable family recreation spaces is a legitimate policy interest that should be weighed against any revenue considerations motivating the reclassification.

Industry representatives have called on the Selangor government to engage in substantive consultation with operators and the broader industry before implementing any changes to the licensing framework. Matfa's position is that the current proposal was developed without adequate input from those who would be most directly affected, and that a proper consultation process — involving operators of various sizes, consumer groups, and relevant government agencies — could yield a more balanced regulatory outcome that addresses any legitimate policy objectives while preserving the viability of an industry that serves hundreds of thousands of Selangor families each year.

The Selangor government has not formally responded to Matfa's appeal at the time of publication, though officials are understood to be reviewing the industry's concerns. The episode highlights a broader tension in Malaysian state-level business regulation between the imperative to generate licensing revenue and the need to maintain a business environment that supports the small and medium enterprises forming the backbone of the local economy. Consumer advocacy groups have joined the chorus of voices urging a rethink, noting that any closures of children's play centres resulting from unaffordable licensing costs would ultimately fall hardest on families who depend on these venues for affordable, accessible recreation.
