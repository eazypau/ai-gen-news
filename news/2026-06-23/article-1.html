LRT3 Line to Begin Operations on June 29 After Years of Delay

New Straits Times · 23 June 2026 · Infrastructure

Transport Minister Anthony Loke confirmed on Monday that the Light Rail Transit 3 (LRT3) line will officially begin passenger operations on June 29, marking a long-awaited milestone for commuters in the Klang Valley. The announcement ends years of construction delays, cost overruns, and public frustration surrounding what was once billed as one of the most transformative public transport projects in Malaysia's history. The line will connect Shah Alam, Klang, and several surrounding townships, providing a direct rail link that residents in these areas have lacked for decades.

Stretching 37.8 kilometres and serving 25 stations, the LRT3 is designed to handle hundreds of thousands of daily commuters. The line runs from Bandar Utama in Petaling Jaya to Johan Setia in Klang, passing through major population and commercial centres including Subang Jaya, Shah Alam, Bukit Raja, and Klang town centre. The project was originally targeted for completion in 2020 but suffered repeated setbacks due to construction challenges, procurement issues, and the economic disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic.

The total cost of the LRT3 project has ballooned significantly from its original estimate of RM9 billion. After a government-ordered review in 2018, the scope of the project was scaled back and the budget revised in an effort to control costs. Despite the reductions, the project remained one of the largest infrastructure investments in Malaysia's transportation history. Prasarana Malaysia Berhad, the state-owned rail operator, oversaw the completion of the line alongside contractor MRCB George Kent Sdn Bhd.

The LRT3 will seamlessly integrate with the existing Kelana Jaya Line and Ampang Line at interchange stations, allowing commuters to transfer between services and access the broader Klang Valley rail network. Commuters from Klang will for the first time be able to travel by train all the way to Kuala Lumpur city centre without switching to road transport. Park-and-ride facilities, feeder bus services, and dedicated cyclist access have also been incorporated at key stations to encourage multimodal travel.

Minister Loke urged the public to give the new line a try during its opening week, noting that reduced congestion on the Federal Highway and New Klang Valley Expressway would be among the immediate benefits. The government has pledged ongoing monitoring of service reliability and passenger volumes during the initial weeks of operation, with adjustments to train frequency expected as ridership patterns become clearer. The LRT3's opening is seen as a key step in the government's broader agenda to shift commuters away from private vehicles and reduce urban carbon emissions across the Klang Valley.
