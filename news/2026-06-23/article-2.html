Malacca Chief Minister: State Polls Likely in September

Malaysiakini · 23 June 2026 · Politics

Malacca Chief Minister Datuk Seri Ab Rauf Yusoh has indicated that the state election is most likely to be held in September, as political parties on both sides of the aisle step up their preparations for a contest that is widely seen as a bellwether for the next general election. Rauf disclosed on May 17 that there was a possibility the election could be called within 120 days, and with that window drawing closer, speculation has intensified over the exact timing of the dissolution of the state assembly.

The Malacca state election is being closely watched by national political analysts who view it as a key barometer for the relative strengths of the ruling Pakatan Harapan-Barisan Nasional (PH-BN) unity government and the Perikatan Nasional (PN) opposition coalition ahead of GE16. Malacca has historically been a contested state, having seen a dramatic change in government in 2021 when a motion of no confidence toppled the then-Pakatan Harapan administration, triggering a snap election that returned BN to power.

Relations between BN and PAS within the PN coalition are under particular scrutiny in Malacca, where seat allocation negotiations have reportedly strained cooperation between the two parties. Analysts note that BN's performance in the state will partly depend on whether the PH-BN unity government can demonstrate credible local governance and economic delivery to fence-sitters who have grown weary of political instability. The state's economy, anchored by tourism, manufacturing, and the Malacca port, has shown modest recovery following the pandemic years.

Voter sentiment surveys conducted in recent months suggest that the Malacca electorate remains divided along similar lines to the 2023 state elections held in other peninsular states. Support for PH remains strong in urban and Chinese-majority constituencies, while PN retains a firm base in Malay-majority rural and semi-urban areas. The key battleground seats are expected to be mixed-constituency areas where the swing of just a few thousand votes could determine the outcome of the election.

Political parties are already conducting ground surveys and finalising candidate shortlists. Both PH and BN are expected to contest under the unity government banner, while PN will field candidates under a combined PAS-UMNO or independent arrangement depending on the finalised seat negotiations. The Election Commission has yet to comment on the readiness of electoral rolls and logistics, but preparations are understood to be at an advanced stage in anticipation of an imminent dissolution notice from the state palace.
