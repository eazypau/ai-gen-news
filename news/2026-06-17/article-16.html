Malaysia's Digital Economy Reaches USD 31 Billion — AI and 5G Lead Transformation

Technology Source: OpenMinds Resources / BusinessToday Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia's digital economy has expanded to USD 31 billion, cementing the country's position as one of Southeast Asia's most advanced digital markets. The milestone reflects years of sustained government investment in digital infrastructure, the accelerated adoption of artificial intelligence across industries, and the expanding reach of 5G connectivity — which now covers major urban centres and key economic corridors nationwide.

The government's Malaysia Digital Economy Blueprint (MyDIGITAL) has been a central driver of the transformation, setting ambitious targets for digital adoption across government services, education, healthcare, manufacturing, and agriculture. Initiatives under the blueprint include the digitisation of government e-services, nationwide broadband expansion, AI upskilling programmes for the workforce, and the creation of digital free trade zones to attract technology investment.

Kuala Lumpur's startup ecosystem has emerged as a significant beneficiary of the digital economy surge, drawing international venture capital attention to Malaysian founders in fintech, health-tech, edtech, retail tech, and mobility. Analysts note that Malaysia's advantages — English proficiency, multicultural market exposure, regulatory stability, and competitive operating costs — make it an increasingly attractive base for tech companies seeking a Southeast Asian hub.

The 5G rollout, led by Digital Nasional Berhad (DNB) and complemented by telco operators, is expected to unlock new use cases in smart manufacturing, connected agriculture, autonomous logistics, and remote healthcare delivery. The government has projected that 5G adoption could contribute an additional USD 5 billion to the economy by 2028 through efficiency gains and new revenue streams across sectors.

Data security remains a key concern as Malaysia's digital footprint expands. A recent survey of Malaysian executives found that 73.3% consider securing data storage and backups their top cybersecurity priority, reflecting heightened awareness of ransomware and data breach risks. The government is finalising amendments to Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) to align with international standards and introduce stricter breach notification requirements.
