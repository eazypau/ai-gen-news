Students Transferring Schools Must Now Submit Mental Health and Discipline Reports

Education Source: Says.my / The Sun Malaysia Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia's Ministry of Education has introduced a significant new school safety reform: students transferring between schools are now required to submit mental health and discipline reports as part of the transfer process. The policy, which took effect in 2026, is part of a sweeping school safety initiative designed to ensure continuity of care and prevent high-risk students from falling through the cracks during school transitions.

The reform is structured around five key pillars: mental health support, reproductive and social education, teacher welfare and professional development, student voice and participation, and the implementation of a Student Protection Policy that clearly outlines procedures for reporting and responding to instances of bullying, abuse, and neglect. Together, these pillars represent the most comprehensive overhaul of Malaysia's school safety framework in over a decade.

The mental health report requirement has received broad support from child psychologists and counsellors, who note that students with unaddressed mental health challenges often experience disrupted educational journeys — and that school transitions can be particularly triggering periods without adequate support structures in place. The policy ensures receiving schools are properly briefed and equipped to provide appropriate support from day one.

Critics, including some parent groups, have raised concerns about the privacy implications of mental health records being passed between schools, and have called for robust data protection safeguards to prevent stigmatisation. The Ministry of Education responded by clarifying that reports are handled confidentially, accessible only to authorised school counsellors and principals, and are governed by the Personal Data Protection Act.

Malaysia's decision to make secondary education compulsory, combined with the launch of the National Education Plan 2026–2035, signals a broader commitment to not just keeping students in school, but ensuring their holistic wellbeing throughout their educational journey. The Ministry stated that it plans to evaluate the effectiveness of the mental health report requirement after one full academic year, with adjustments based on feedback from schools, parents, and students.
