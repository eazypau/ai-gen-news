Perak Hit by Mid-June Flooding — NADMA Activates Relief Operations

Environment Source: AHA Centre / NADMA Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia's National Disaster Management Agency (NADMA) reported flooding in several districts of Perak during the week of June 8–14, 2026, with relief operations swiftly activated to assist affected communities. Rescue teams from NADMA, the Fire and Rescue Department, and civil defence forces were deployed, with temporary relief centres opened to house displaced residents unable to remain in their flood-affected homes.

Perak's flooding followed sustained heavy rainfall across the northern peninsula, exacerbated by insufficient drainage capacity in low-lying areas. Local authorities reported that farmland, access roads, and several residential areas were inundated, with residents in affected villages receiving assistance including food supplies, clean water, and basic shelter provisions at relief centres.

Meteorological authorities warned that weather patterns in June 2026 could become more erratic as the region begins a transition toward El Niño conditions forecast for the June–July period. The El Niño phenomenon typically reduces rainfall on the peninsula's west coast while increasing risks of flash floods in certain zones due to concentrated intense rainfall events.

Malaysia's Department of Irrigation and Drainage (DID) noted that flood losses nationally eased by 32% in 2025 compared to prior years, partly attributable to improved early warning systems, expanded retention pond networks, and enhanced inter-agency coordination. However, the 2026 flooding events have underscored that infrastructure investments have not yet fully addressed the vulnerability of older residential and agricultural areas to flooding.

Civil society groups and environmental advocates have reiterated calls for stronger protections for forest reserves and wetlands, arguing that deforestation and illegal land clearing on hillslopes and riverbanks continue to amplify flood risks. The government's ongoing review of the National Water Policy and National Land Policy is expected to address these concerns, with stricter enforcement of environmental protection zones being discussed as a priority measure.
