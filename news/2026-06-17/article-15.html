Federal Court Partially Overturns 2014 Fatwa Against Sisters in Islam

Society Source: Malay Mail / The Star Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia's Federal Court has issued a landmark ruling partially overturning a 2014 fatwa issued by the Selangor Islamic Religious Council that declared Sisters in Islam (SIS) — a prominent women's rights advocacy organisation — as "deviant" and propagating teachings contrary to Islamic principles. The decision, celebrated by civil society groups and human rights advocates, restores some legal protections for SIS and narrows the scope of the original fatwa's application.

Sisters in Islam, founded in 1988, has been a vocal advocate for Muslim women's rights in Malaysia, calling for more equitable interpretations of Islamic family law and opposing discriminatory practices. The 2014 fatwa had cast a legal shadow over the organisation's activities, affecting its ability to operate freely, attract donors, and publish advocacy materials without risking enforcement action by religious authorities.

The Federal Court's ruling drew a distinction between the organisation's right to advocate for legal and policy reform — which the court found to be constitutionally protected speech — and the specific religious characterisation of its members as "deviants," which the court found overstepped the appropriate scope of a religious authority's fatwa in relation to civil society activities.

SIS Executive Director Rozana Isa called the ruling a "significant, if partial, vindication" of the organisation's decade-long legal fight. "We are grateful that the court has recognised that civil society's role in advocating for reform cannot be silenced by a fatwa," she said in a statement. The organisation intends to study the full judgment before deciding whether to pursue further appeals on the portions of the fatwa that were not overturned.

The ruling has prompted a broader debate in Malaysia about the boundaries of religious authority and civil liberties in a pluralistic society. Human rights groups, bar council representatives, and academic legal scholars have welcomed the decision as a step toward clearer constitutional demarcations between civil and religious jurisdictions in Malaysia — a debate that has intensified in recent years amid growing advocacy for legal reforms.
