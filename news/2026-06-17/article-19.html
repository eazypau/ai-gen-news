Malaysia Real Estate Market Valued at USD 42.43 Billion in 2026, Set to Grow to USD 55.82 Billion by 2031

Property Source: Mordor Intelligence / IQI Global Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia's real estate market is valued at USD 42.43 billion in 2026 and is on a strong growth trajectory, projected to reach USD 55.82 billion by 2031 at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 5.64%. The market is being propelled by a combination of domestic end-user demand, foreign investor interest, government infrastructure spending, and the transformational impact of projects like the Johor Bahru–Singapore RTS Link.

Kuala Lumpur remains the market's engine, offering the strongest rental yields and sustained capital appreciation, particularly in transit-oriented developments (TODs) clustered around MRT and LRT station nodes. Lifestyle-integrated developments featuring retail, workspace, and residential components within walkable distances of public transport hubs are commanding premium valuations among urban professional buyers.

The Johor Bahru property market is experiencing a particularly dynamic period, buoyed by the approaching RTS Link completion and the establishment of the Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone. Foreign buyers — particularly from Singapore and China — have returned in significant numbers, attracted by comparatively affordable land costs and the prospect of cross-border economic integration elevating the value of strategically located assets.

Sustainable and green building trends are gaining traction in Malaysia, with developers increasingly seeking GBI (Green Building Index) certification and incorporating smart home technologies, solar energy systems, and rainwater harvesting into new residential and commercial projects. Buyers in the premium segment are increasingly factoring environmental credentials into purchase decisions, reflecting growing awareness of climate risk and long-term asset value sustainability.

Co-living spaces and serviced apartments are also gaining popularity, particularly among young professionals and expatriates in urban centres, reflecting evolving lifestyle preferences and the growth of the gig economy. Property analysts expect residential sector growth to be front-loaded in the first half of the projection period, driven by strong job market conditions, government first-home buyer incentives, and the pull of new infrastructure corridors in Johor, Penang, and Kuala Lumpur's outer ring.
