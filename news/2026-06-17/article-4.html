Malaysia Evaluates Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Naval Strike Missile Licence

Defence Source: Free Malaysia Today Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia's Ministry of Defence is actively evaluating alternative suppliers for anti-ship missiles after Norway's government revoked the export licence for the Naval Strike Missile (NSM) system, which had been earmarked for the Royal Malaysian Navy (RMN). The revocation has disrupted planned procurement timelines and left naval planners reassessing the fleet's surface warfare capabilities.

Norway's decision was reportedly linked to broader concerns about the re-export of advanced weaponry in the current geopolitical climate, a factor that has affected several defence procurement agreements across Southeast Asia. Malaysian defence officials stressed that the revocation was not related to any bilateral dispute with Norway, but acknowledged it created a significant gap in planned naval modernisation programmes.

The RMN is understood to be reviewing options from European manufacturers including MBDA's Exocet and Kongsberg's successor products, as well as American suppliers and regional alternatives from South Korea and India. Defence analysts suggest that political considerations will play a major role in the final selection, as Malaysia seeks to maintain balanced relationships with multiple global powers.

The incident has reignited debate within Malaysia's defence establishment about the risks of dependence on single-source suppliers for critical weapons systems. Defence Minister Datuk Seri Mohamed Khaled Nordin said that the government would "ensure continuity of the RMN's operational readiness" and that negotiations with multiple potential suppliers were already underway.

Malaysia's defence budget for 2026 allocates a significant portion for naval modernisation, including new patrol vessels, submarine upgrades, and maritime surveillance systems. The missile procurement issue is seen as one piece of a broader multi-year effort to upgrade Malaysia's maritime defence posture amid a more complex regional security environment in the South China Sea.
