Dengue Surge: Malaysia Records 30,311 Cases — Selangor Accounts for 47% of National Burden

Health Source: The Edge Malaysia / Ministry of Health Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia recorded 30,311 dengue fever cases in the first half of 2026 — a 9.7% increase compared to the same period in 2025 — with 23 fatalities representing a concerning 35% year-on-year rise in dengue-related deaths. Public health authorities have escalated their response, declaring multiple localities as high-risk zones and ramping up vector control operations nationwide.

Selangor state remains the epicentre of the outbreak, accounting for approximately 47% of all national dengue cases as of early June 2026. The state has recorded 14,502 cases, with thirteen deaths — a staggering 6.5-fold increase in fatalities compared to the same period last year. Selangor is also home to 286 of Malaysia's 442 active dengue hotspots, or roughly 65% of the national total, with the most heavily affected areas concentrated in densely populated urban and peri-urban districts.

The Ministry of Health (MOH) attributed the surge to a combination of factors: above-normal rainfall in the first half of 2026 creating abundant Aedes mosquito breeding conditions, rapid urbanisation outpacing drainage infrastructure upgrades, and reduced community vigilance toward container inspection and fogging cooperation compared to recent years.

Selangor state health authorities have stepped up fogging operations, increased the frequency of community cleanup campaigns, and deployed additional surveillance teams to identify and destroy Aedes mosquito breeding sites. The state is also working with local councils to ensure standing water in construction sites, longhouses, and residential areas is promptly addressed.

Public health experts have renewed calls for Malaysia to accelerate its dengue vaccination programme, pointing to evidence that licensed dengue vaccines could significantly reduce hospitalisations and fatalities. The MOH is currently reviewing its vaccination strategy, with a phased rollout being discussed for high-risk endemic communities in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur. Residents are urged to practice the "10-minute Aedes inspection" daily and to seek immediate medical attention if dengue symptoms — high fever, severe headache, muscle pain, and skin rash — are observed.
