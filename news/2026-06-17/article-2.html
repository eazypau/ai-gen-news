Domestic Tourism Expenditure Surges 13.6% to RM121.3 Billion in 2025

Economy Source: Bernama / DOSM Date: 17 June 2026

Malaysia's domestic tourism sector continued its impressive post-pandemic recovery in 2025, with total domestic visitor expenditure rising 13.6 percent to RM121.3 billion from RM106.7 billion in 2024, according to the Domestic Tourism Survey 2025 released by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) on June 16.

The figure represents one of the strongest annual growth rates recorded for domestic tourism in Malaysia's recent history, driven by increasing consumer confidence, more frequent short-haul holidays, and the government's sustained efforts to promote "Cuti-Cuti Malaysia" campaigns that encourage Malaysians to explore local destinations.

The number of domestic tourist trips also rose significantly, with Malaysians making more frequent visits to beaches, highlands, heritage towns, and eco-tourism sites across the country. Penang, Langkawi, Cameron Highlands, and Sabah remained the top domestic destinations, while emerging attractions in Kelantan and Pahang saw notable visitor increases.

The tourism and hospitality industry welcomed the data as a strong indicator of sector health. Hotel occupancy rates, food and beverage spending, and transport revenues all reflected the uptick in domestic travel. The Malaysia Tourism Promotion Board (Tourism Malaysia) noted that the growth provides a solid foundation for the nation's broader target of reaching RM150 billion in total tourism receipts by 2027.

Tourism Minister Datuk Seri Tiong King Sing attributed the success to a combination of attractive domestic packages, improved connectivity through expanded highway networks and regional airports, and the rising middle-class appetite for experiential travel. He added that the government would continue investing in tourism infrastructure and digital promotion to sustain this momentum into 2026.
