Two Army Soldiers Killed in Fatal Grenade Blast During Training — Police Probe Seven

Defence Source: Bernama / Malay Mail Date: 17 June 2026

Police have recorded statements from seven individuals in connection with a fatal grenade explosion that claimed the lives of two Malaysian Army personnel during a live-fire training exercise. The incident, which occurred at a military training facility, has sent shockwaves through the armed forces and prompted calls for an urgent review of safety protocols governing the use of live ordnance during training.

The Royal Malaysia Police's (PDRM) Criminal Investigation Division is leading the investigation, working in coordination with the Malaysian Armed Forces' own internal board of inquiry. Authorities have not publicly named the individuals whose statements were recorded, but confirmed that they include personnel present at the training ground at the time of the incident, as well as supervising officers responsible for the exercise.

The Malaysian Army expressed its deep condolences to the families of the two deceased soldiers and pledged a "thorough and transparent" investigation into the circumstances surrounding the tragedy. The Chief of Army announced that all similar live grenade training exercises at facilities nationwide have been temporarily suspended pending a comprehensive safety review.

The Ministry of Defence said it takes the safety of serving military personnel with the utmost seriousness, calling the incident "deeply regrettable." Defence Minister Datuk Seri Mohamed Khaled Nordin was briefed on the incident and directed the armed forces to fast-track the safety review and implement corrective measures without delay.

Fatal training accidents involving live munitions, while rare in Malaysia, have occurred periodically and typically prompt wide-ranging safety reforms in their aftermath. Defence analysts noted that this incident is likely to accelerate discussions within the armed forces about upgrading training simulation technologies as a supplement to — or partial replacement for — live-fire exercises, reducing personnel risk while maintaining combat readiness.
