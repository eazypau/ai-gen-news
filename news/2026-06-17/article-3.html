EU-Malaysia Trade Ties Deepening, Says EU Envoy

Business Source: Malay Mail / NST Date: 17 June 2026

The European Union's Ambassador to Malaysia has reaffirmed that trade will continue to spur deeper bilateral ties between the EU and Malaysia, as the long-delayed EU-Malaysia Free Trade Agreement (FTA) negotiations gather renewed momentum. The envoy described the relationship as entering a "new phase of strategic partnership" anchored by shared interests in green technology, digital innovation, and supply chain resilience.

Malaysia and the EU resumed FTA talks in recent months after a multi-year pause, with both sides expressing optimism about completing a framework agreement by 2027. Key areas of focus include tariff reductions on Malaysian palm oil and electronics exports, EU market access for Malaysian professional services, and mutual recognition of standards in sustainability-linked manufacturing.

The EU remains one of Malaysia's largest trading partners, with bilateral trade estimated to exceed EUR 42 billion in 2025. European investors have a significant presence in Malaysia's semiconductor, pharmaceutical, and renewable energy sectors, and the envoy indicated that European companies are increasingly looking at Malaysia as a regional base for ASEAN market expansion.

Malaysia's Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) has signalled strong government support for accelerating FTA negotiations, seeing the agreement as a key pillar of the nation's strategy to diversify export markets amid global trade tensions. Officials noted that the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) also adds urgency to aligning Malaysia's industrial policies with European sustainability standards.

Civil society groups have called for the FTA negotiations to include robust provisions on labour rights, environmental standards, and data privacy, aligning with the EU's own regulatory frameworks. The Malaysian government has indicated willingness to engage on these issues, viewing them as opportunities to upgrade domestic standards rather than barriers to trade.
