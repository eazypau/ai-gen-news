Malaysian Ringgit Strengthens to 4.07 Per USD, Up 4.7% Year-to-Date in 2026

Finance Source: Bank Negara Malaysia / Xe.com Date: 17 June 2026

The Malaysian Ringgit extended its recovery against the US Dollar this week, trading at approximately RM4.07 per USD as of June 16, 2026 — its strongest level in nearly two years. The currency has appreciated by 4.70% year-to-date in 2026, driven by a combination of improved domestic economic fundamentals, narrowing US-Malaysia interest rate differentials, and sustained foreign direct investment inflows.

Economists attribute the Ringgit's resilience to Malaysia's robust trade surplus performance, particularly in the electronics and electrical goods sector, as well as recovering palm oil export revenues. Bank Negara Malaysia's prudent monetary policy stance, maintaining the Overnight Policy Rate at 3.00% while the US Federal Reserve began its rate-cutting cycle in late 2025, has also supported the currency's relative strength.

Currency strategists noted that the MYR/USD rate found resistance near the 4.05 level — a psychologically important threshold — but the broader trend remains supportive of further gradual appreciation. Several investment banks have revised their year-end Ringgit forecasts upward, with some now projecting a level as strong as RM3.90–3.95 per USD if global risk appetite remains elevated.

The stronger Ringgit carries mixed implications for the Malaysian economy. While it benefits importers, reduces imported inflation, and lowers the cost of servicing US Dollar-denominated debt, it also presents headwinds for exporters — particularly smaller manufacturers competing in price-sensitive global markets. The plantation sector, whose revenues are largely denominated in US Dollars, has flagged currency appreciation as a near-term earnings headwind.

Bank Negara Malaysia's Governor said the central bank continues to monitor the foreign exchange market and will intervene if necessary to prevent excessive volatility. The Ringgit's appreciation trajectory is seen by the government as a vote of confidence in Malaysia's economic management, and officials have expressed a preference for an orderly, fundamentals-driven currency adjustment rather than a sharp rally that could disrupt export competitiveness.
