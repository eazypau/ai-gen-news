Visit Malaysia 2026 Tourism Set to Rebound in H2 After Middle East Disruptions

Tourism Source: New Straits Times 1 July 2026

A new industry report forecasts a strong rebound in Malaysia's tourism sector during the second half of 2026, as the easing of travel disruptions stemming from the Middle East conflict unlocks pent-up demand from Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries. The report, cited by the New Straits Times, projects that full-year 2026 arrivals will surpass pre-pandemic 2019 levels for the first time since the COVID-19 crisis.

The Visit Malaysia 2026 (VM2026) campaign has a stated target of 47 million international arrivals and RM329 billion in tourism receipts for the full year. While the first half was impacted by Middle East travel hesitancy and disruptions to flight routes, tourism economists expect the second half to see a significant acceleration as airline connectivity normalises and GCC travellers — who typically visit Malaysia in large numbers during July and August — return in force.

BMI Research has forecast Malaysia will receive 27.97 million international tourist arrivals in 2026, representing a 5.1% increase from 2025 and 7.2% above the 2019 pre-pandemic benchmark. The organisation noted that Malaysia's strong hospitality infrastructure, competitive value proposition, and political stability give it a structural advantage over some regional competitors in attracting international visitors.

The VM2026 extension of Malaysia's visa-free policy for key markets — including China, India, and selected Middle Eastern nations — is expected to be a significant demand driver in H2 2026. Tourism Malaysia has also rolled out targeted promotional campaigns in Germany, the UK, and Australia to diversify Malaysia's source markets beyond Asia and reduce vulnerability to any single region's travel fluctuations.

Hotel industry representatives have reported a significant uptick in advance bookings for Q3 and Q4 2026, particularly in Kuala Lumpur, Langkawi, Penang, and Kota Kinabalu. The return of major MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, and exhibitions) events to Malaysia in the second half of 2026 — following a slow first half — is also expected to drive hotel occupancy rates above 70%, a level that tourism authorities consider indicative of a healthy market.
