RON95 Petrol Subsidy Maintained at RM1.99 Per Litre Under BUDI95

Economy Source: Bernama / MOF 1 July 2026

The Malaysian government has confirmed that the subsidised retail price of RON95 petrol will remain at RM1.99 per litre through its targeted BUDI95 programme, despite a significant rise in global oil prices that has pushed Malaysia's monthly fuel subsidy bill to approximately RM4 billion — nearly six times the RM700 million monthly bill recorded when the programme was first introduced.

The Ministry of Finance stated that the government remains committed to shielding ordinary Malaysians from the full impact of rising global energy prices, while continuing to ensure that subsidies are directed to those who genuinely need them. The BUDI95 mechanism uses MyKad verification at the pump to deliver the subsidised price exclusively to eligible Malaysian citizens.

Starting April 1, 2026, the monthly individual subsidy quota under BUDI95 was revised downward from 300 litres to 200 litres per person. This allocation is shared with the BUDI Diesel programme. The government noted that approximately 90% of BUDI95 users consume less than 200 litres per month, meaning the quota reduction has a minimal practical impact on the vast majority of recipients.

E-hailing drivers and gig economy workers retain their higher allocation of 800 litres per month, recognising the significantly higher fuel consumption associated with their livelihoods. The government has also indicated it is exploring additional petroleum sourcing options, including supply arrangements with Brazil and Canada via Petronas, to manage the fiscal burden of the subsidy programme.

Non-Malaysian citizens and high-income households who do not qualify for BUDI95 pay the market rate for RON95, which fluctuates weekly based on global oil benchmarks. The differentiated pricing system has generally been welcomed by economists as a more equitable model than the previous blanket subsidy, though administrative challenges around enforcement at petrol stations remain a work in progress.
