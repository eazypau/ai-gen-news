Syed Saddiq Federal Court Ruling Postponed to July 13 After Judge Falls Ill

Politics Source: Malay Mail 1 July 2026

The Federal Court's much-anticipated decision on the prosecution's appeal against Muar MP Syed Saddiq Syed Abdul Rahman's acquittal has been postponed to July 13, 2026, after one of the judges on the five-member panel fell ill. Court of Appeal President Datuk Seri Abu Bakar Jais announced that the court could not deliver its ruling in the absence of Federal Court judge Datuk Che Mohd Ruzima Ghazali, who is on medical leave.

Syed Saddiq, who arrived at the Federal Court in Putrajaya in anticipation of a verdict on June 30, said he will skip Johor election campaigning during this period as a result of the postponement. The Muar MP had been expected to play an active role in the July 11 Johor state election campaign as a prominent figure in Muda.

The prosecution is challenging the Court of Appeal's decision of June 25, 2025, which unanimously acquitted and discharged Syed Saddiq of four criminal charges involving funds belonging to Parti Pribumi Bersatu Malaysia's youth wing, Armada. The charges related to criminal breach of trust, dishonest misappropriation, and money laundering of Armada funds.

In November 2023, the High Court had found Syed Saddiq guilty and sentenced him to seven years' imprisonment, two strokes of the cane, and a RM10 million fine. The Court of Appeal overturned that conviction in a landmark decision that drew widespread public interest given Syed Saddiq's profile as a young politician and founder of Muda.

Legal observers note that July 13 — just two days after the Johor state election — is a significant date. Syed Saddiq's lawyer expressed confidence that the Federal Court's decision would ultimately favour their client. The outcome of the appeal will be closely watched as it carries major implications for the political careers of young opposition figures in Malaysia.
