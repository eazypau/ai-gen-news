Malaysia Tops Southeast Asia Tourism in Q1 2026 with 10.65 Million Arrivals

Tourism Source: Travel And Tour World 1 July 2026

Malaysia emerged as Southeast Asia's most visited tourism destination in the first quarter of 2026, welcoming 10.65 million international tourists between January and March — a 5.4% year-on-year increase and a record for Q1 performance. The milestone was announced by Tourism Malaysia as part of the ongoing Visit Malaysia 2026 (VM2026) campaign, which has set an ambitious target of attracting 47 million international visitors over the full year.

ASEAN countries remained the largest source of visitors, accounting for approximately 70% of total arrivals, led by Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brunei. China was the standout growth market, with arrivals rising 21% year-on-year in the first five months of 2026 to 1.87 million visitors, supported by Malaysia's extended visa-free policy for Chinese nationals and improved air connectivity between Chinese cities and major Malaysian airports.

India, South Korea, and Japan also registered strong growth in visitor numbers, with the Islamic tourism segment from Middle Eastern countries providing a significant contribution — though that market was partially impacted by geopolitical tensions in West Asia during the first half of 2026. Tourism authorities expect the Middle Eastern market to recover in the second half of the year as the situation stabilises.

The Visit Malaysia 2026 campaign, backed by over RM700 million in government marketing and infrastructure spending, has been credited with raising Malaysia's international profile as a diverse, affordable, and culturally rich destination. Key promotional strategies include digital marketing campaigns in Chinese and Indian social media platforms, partnerships with international airlines, and the introduction of new themed tourism products such as halal culinary trails, eco-adventure packages, and heritage city tours.

Tourism Malaysia Director-General Datuk Manoharan Periasamy expressed confidence that Malaysia is on track to achieve its 47-million arrival target, noting that air connectivity improvements — including new direct routes from China, India, and Australia — are playing a critical role in making Malaysia more accessible. The domestic tourism sector also remains robust, with Malaysians contributing significantly to hotel occupancy rates, theme parks, and resort destinations nationwide.
