BUDI Diesel Launches Nationwide from July 1 at RM2.10 Per Litre

Economy Source: Human Resources Online 1 July 2026

The Malaysian government's new BUDI MADANI Diesel (BUDI Diesel) subsidy scheme officially took effect nationwide starting July 1, 2026, offering eligible Malaysian citizens subsidised B10/B15 diesel at RM2.10 per litre via MyKad verification at petrol stations. The scheme standardises the diesel subsidy mechanism across Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah, and Sarawak, replacing the previous fragmented cash-transfer approach.

To access the subsidised diesel price, eligible individuals must be Malaysian citizens holding a valid MyKad, a valid driving licence, and own a privately-registered diesel vehicle with a current road tax. Each eligible recipient is entitled to a basic monthly quota of 200 litres, which is shared with the RON95 (BUDI95) quota. Those requiring more than 200 litres per month may apply for an additional 100-litre quota through the budimadani.gov.my website or at a nearby LHDN office.

For those who were previously enrolled in the BUDI Diesel Individual programme receiving RM400 monthly cash assistance, the transition to the new mechanism is automatic — no new application is required. The RM400 cash payout will be discontinued and replaced by direct pump discounts via MyKad verification. The scheme has also been extended to company-owned pickup trucks and jeeps used for small traders and daily work purposes, following feedback from the business community.

There was an early-access transition period for Peninsular Malaysia users between June 27 and July 1, 2026, during which the price was set at RM2.15 per litre before the official RM2.10 rate took effect. The Malaysian Automobile Association (MAA) welcomed the new BUDI Diesel scheme while expressing hope that diesel MPV owners would also be allowed to apply for an additional quota given their higher consumption needs.

The government estimates that the targeted subsidy approach under BUDI Diesel will be more fiscally sustainable than the previous blanket subsidy, while ensuring that the benefits reach deserving Malaysians rather than high-income households and foreigners. With global diesel prices remaining elevated, the scheme is expected to provide meaningful relief to the approximately 5 million diesel vehicle owners in Malaysia, particularly small traders, farmers, and logistics operators.
