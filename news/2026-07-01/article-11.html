Government Allocates RM2 Billion for Sovereign AI Cloud and Infrastructure

Technology Source: KPMG Malaysia 1 July 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim announced the allocation of RM2 billion for the construction and operation of a sovereign AI cloud as a centrepiece of Malaysia's digital economy strategy. The investment is part of a broader RM5.9 billion commitment to research, development, commercialisation, and innovation (RDCI) initiatives focused on artificial intelligence and advanced technology sectors.

The sovereign AI cloud is designed to give Malaysia independent national AI computing capacity, reducing reliance on overseas cloud providers for sensitive government data and critical AI workloads. It will serve as the backbone for public sector AI applications including smart governance, predictive healthcare, and intelligent transport systems, while also being made available to local technology companies and research institutions at preferential rates.

Malaysia's investment in sovereign AI infrastructure follows a growing global trend of nations seeking to secure their own AI capabilities rather than relying entirely on foreign cloud providers. Countries including France, Germany, and Singapore have made similar moves to develop nationally controlled AI cloud resources, recognising the strategic importance of AI sovereignty in an era of digital geopolitics.

KPMG Malaysia's analysis of the country's data centre boom highlights the unique opportunity Malaysia has to establish itself as an AI hub. With abundant land, a pro-business regulatory environment, competitive electricity tariffs (currently under review to reflect AI infrastructure's higher power demands), and a growing pool of technology talent, Malaysia is well positioned to attract further AI investment beyond the current RM2 billion sovereign commitment.

The government has also pledged to use the sovereign AI cloud to train and deploy large language models in Bahasa Malaysia and other local languages, addressing a significant gap in regional AI capabilities. This initiative is seen as both a cultural and economic priority, with locally trained AI models expected to find applications in government services, education, and the Malay-language media industry.
