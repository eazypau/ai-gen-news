Malaysia Consulate Warns Citizens in Southern Thailand Amid Heavy Rain Alert

International Source: Malay Mail 1 July 2026

The Malaysian Consulate General in Songkhla, southern Thailand, has issued an official advisory urging Malaysian citizens residing in or travelling to the region to exercise heightened caution following a forecast of heavy to very heavy rainfall across several southern Thai provinces from June 28 to July 3, 2026. The consulate warned that the weather conditions could trigger flash floods, widespread flooding, landslides, and rough seas along the Andaman Sea coastline.

The advisory specifically highlights risks in provinces along Malaysia's northern border including Songkhla, Satun, Pattani, Yala, and Narathiwat — areas that are home to a significant number of Malaysians living and working in southern Thailand. Tourists travelling to southern Thai islands and coastal resorts via the Andaman coast have also been cautioned against sea activities during the warning period.

Malaysian officials advised citizens in the affected areas to monitor local Thai weather authority announcements, avoid low-lying flood-prone areas, and refrain from crossing rivers or streams in spate conditions. Those planning overland travel between Malaysia and southern Thailand — particularly through the Sadao, Bukit Kayu Hitam, and Padang Besar border crossings — were urged to check road conditions and weather updates before setting out.

The Malaysian Consulate in Songkhla reiterated that it maintains a 24-hour emergency hotline for Malaysian citizens facing distress situations in southern Thailand. The consulate reminded Malaysians travelling abroad to register their trips through the Foreign Ministry's eConsular portal to facilitate assistance in the event of an emergency.

The advisory comes as MetMalaysia also noted that southern Peninsular Malaysia may experience spillover effects from the heavy rain system affecting southern Thailand, with the possibility of localised flooding in Kedah, Perlis, and northern Perak. Malaysian authorities have placed relevant disaster management agencies on standby alert as a precautionary measure during the weather event period.
