IMF Raises Malaysia's GDP Growth Forecast to 4.7% for 2026

Economy Source: The Edge Malaysia 1 July 2026

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has revised upward its real GDP growth forecast for Malaysia to 4.7% for 2026, representing an upgrade of 0.4 percentage points from its earlier projection. The revision reflects Malaysia's stronger-than-expected first-quarter performance, continued resilience of domestic demand, and robust foreign investment inflows driven by the global AI and semiconductor boom.

In its latest World Economic Outlook update, the IMF highlighted Malaysia as one of the standout performers in Southeast Asia, crediting the government's fiscal discipline, targeted subsidy reforms, and pro-investment policy environment. The fund also cited Malaysia's strategic positioning within the global semiconductor supply chain as a key growth driver for 2026 and beyond.

The IMF's 4.7% forecast aligns broadly with the Malaysian government's own projections of 4.5% to 5.5% growth for the full year. Other multilateral institutions including the World Bank and Asian Development Bank have also maintained positive outlooks for the Malaysian economy, with forecasts ranging from 4.5% to 5%.

The IMF did flag some downside risks to Malaysia's growth outlook, including prolonged geopolitical tensions in West Asia that could disrupt energy supply chains, a sharper-than-expected global slowdown stemming from US monetary policy tightening, and climate-related disruptions that could affect agricultural output and tourism. The fund urged Malaysia to continue structural reforms to boost productivity and reduce reliance on commodity exports.

For 2027, the IMF projects Malaysia's growth to moderate slightly to 4.3%, reflecting a normalisation of the AI investment cycle and a gradual slowdown in global electronics demand. Nevertheless, the fund views Malaysia's medium-term outlook as favourable, supported by ongoing efforts to develop the digital economy, upskill the workforce, and attract high-quality foreign investment.
