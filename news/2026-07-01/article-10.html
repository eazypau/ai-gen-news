Malaysia Prioritises High-Value AI Data Centres with Moratorium on Non-AI Projects

Technology Source: The Star 1 July 2026

Malaysia has solidified its strategy of concentrating data centre development on high-value artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure, with PM Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim announcing a moratorium on proposals for non-AI-related data centres in February 2026. The policy reflects the government's determination to ensure that large-scale data centre investments create meaningful economic value, skilled jobs, and technology transfer — not merely consume electricity and water with limited local benefits.

The shift in policy was informed by criticism that earlier waves of data centre construction, while generating significant investment figures, resulted in few high-quality jobs for Malaysians and placed strain on the national power grid and water resources. Under the new framework, only data centres with credible AI workloads and demonstrated commitments to hiring and training Malaysian talent will receive approval.

Major global technology companies have responded positively to Malaysia's AI data centre focus. NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google, Oracle, Equinix, NTT Data, and GDS Holdings are among the firms that have committed to AI-oriented projects in Malaysia, particularly in Johor and the Klang Valley. Malaysia attracted over $10 billion in data centre investments in 2023, which tripled to over $30 billion in 2024, making it the world's top destination for two consecutive years according to Knight Frank research.

Malaysia's first NVIDIA-powered sovereign AI data centre, located in Kulai, Johor, was completed in October 2025. The facility, operated by YTL Power, features NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 GPUs for AI training at scale and is powered by 500MW of solar energy, making it one of the largest renewable-powered AI facilities in Southeast Asia with a total capacity of 600MW.

BMI Research noted that Malaysia's focus on AI data centres positions the country as the premier AI infrastructure hub in Southeast Asia, competing with Singapore for regional technology investment leadership. The government estimates that the high-value AI data centre strategy will generate significantly more skilled employment per investment dollar than traditional hyperscale data centres, helping Malaysia move up the global value chain in the digital economy.
