Unifi TV Offers Fans Full Coverage of All 104 FIFA World Cup 2026 Matches

Entertainment Free Malaysia Today (FMT) 8 June 2026

Telecommunications giant TM (Telekom Malaysia) has announced that its Unifi TV platform will broadcast every single one of the 104 matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, cementing its position as one of the go-to destinations for football fans in the country. The full season pass is priced at an accessible RM60, giving subscribers unlimited access to all group stage games, knockout rounds and the final in July.

For existing Unifi TV subscribers, an exclusive discounted package is available at RM50 — a RM10 saving on the standard season pass price. Fans who prefer more flexibility can also opt for a daily pass priced at RM20, which will be available from the tournament's opening day on June 12, 2026. This per-day option is aimed at viewers who want to catch specific high-profile matches without committing to the full tournament.

The announcement marks a significant milestone for Unifi TV, which has aggressively expanded its live sports content portfolio over the past two years. The platform has invested in enhanced streaming infrastructure to handle the anticipated surge in concurrent viewers, particularly during peak hours such as weekend matches and the knockout stages of the tournament.

TM has also confirmed that the Unifi TV app — available on smartphones, smart TVs and web browsers — will carry the full World Cup coverage, meaning subscribers can watch matches on the go or from any room in their home without the need for a traditional set-top box. The move aligns with broader industry trends toward device-agnostic streaming.

Alongside RTM's free-to-air coverage through the MyGOV app, Unifi TV's entry into the World Cup broadcasting space represents a significant democratization of access to the tournament for Malaysian viewers. Consumer groups have welcomed the competitive pricing, noting that the season pass at RM60 is substantially more affordable than historical pay-TV sports bundles, which often required long-term contracts and high monthly fees.
