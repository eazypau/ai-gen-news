Malaysia's Social Media Ban for Under-16s Now in Full Effect

Technology Fortune / Malay Mail 8 June 2026

Malaysia's landmark social media restriction for children under the age of 16 entered into force on June 1, 2026, making the country one of the first in Southeast Asia to implement such sweeping online safety legislation. The law bars minors from creating accounts on major platforms — including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube — that have at least eight million users in Malaysia.

The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) confirmed that platforms have a six-month window to roll out age-verification systems for existing users. Individuals identified as being under 16 will be given a one-month grace period to download or transfer their data — including photos, videos and messages — before their accounts are restricted or actioned by the platform.

Platforms are permitted to use a range of age-verification methods, including government-issued ID uploads, facial age estimation technology, and cross-referencing with other linked accounts. Companies that fail to comply face fines of RM1 million, with additional daily penalties imposed for continued non-compliance beyond the prescribed deadline.

The legislation has drawn both praise and concern. Proponents argue that children need stronger protections from online harms such as cyberbullying, sexual exploitation, and algorithmic manipulation. The government points to mounting evidence that social media use is linked to mental health challenges among adolescents.

Critics, however, have raised concerns about data privacy and the expansion of state surveillance. One academic warned that requiring users to upload government IDs for age verification could create new vulnerabilities and risks. Parental liability is not triggered by the legislation — the law targets the platforms rather than users or their families — though parents are encouraged to actively monitor their children's digital activity.
