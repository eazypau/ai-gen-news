Cosmetics Overtake Agriculture as Malaysia's Number One Consumer Goods Export

Business The Edge Malaysia 8 June 2026

In a notable shift in Malaysia's trade profile, cosmetics have emerged as the top export item among the country's five major consumer goods categories, surpassing the long-dominant agricultural and fishery products segment. The development reflects the rapid growth of Malaysia's beauty and personal care manufacturing sector, which has attracted significant investment from both domestic brands and international companies setting up regional production facilities in the country.

Trade data tracked by the Malaysia External Trade Development Corporation (MATRADE) shows that exports of cosmetics, toiletries and personal care products have been on a steep upward trajectory over the past three years, driven by strong demand from markets across the Middle East, East Asia and South Asia. Halal-certified beauty products, in particular, have found receptive audiences in Muslim-majority countries where Malaysian certification carries significant consumer trust.

The cosmetics industry's ascent has been supported by the government's push to develop Malaysia as a leading global hub for halal product manufacturing. Several multinational beauty conglomerates have established or expanded their Malaysia-based manufacturing operations in recent years, attracted by the country's well-established supply chains, competitive operating costs and strategic geographic position for export to Asia-Pacific and Middle Eastern markets.

Agricultural and fishery exports — long the backbone of Malaysia's consumer goods trade — remain significant but have faced headwinds from climate variability, labour shortages and increasing competition from regional producers. Industry observers note that the structural shift toward higher-value manufactured goods such as cosmetics is broadly aligned with Malaysia's economic development goals.

MATRADE has announced initiatives to further support the growth of Malaysian cosmetics and personal care exports, including enhanced participation in international trade fairs, dedicated buyer-matching programmes and expanded market access negotiations in new territories. Officials say the sector's performance demonstrates that Malaysia's consumer goods industry is maturing and capable of competing at the premium end of global markets.
