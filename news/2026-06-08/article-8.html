Malaysia's Employment Pass Salary Thresholds Double in Major Foreign Worker Policy Overhaul

Business The Silicon Review / BERNAMA 8 June 2026

Effective June 1, 2026, Malaysia has significantly revised the minimum salary thresholds for Employment Pass (EP) holders, in what is being described as the most substantial overhaul of the country's foreign skilled worker policy in recent memory. The Category I Employment Pass minimum monthly salary has doubled from RM10,000 to RM20,000, while other EP categories have also seen substantial increases aimed at attracting only the most highly skilled international talent.

The policy change reflects the government's broader ambition to shift Malaysia's economic profile toward higher-value industries and reduce dependence on lower-wage foreign labour. By raising the bar for Employment Pass eligibility, authorities hope to ensure that foreign workers entering the country are filling genuine skills gaps at the senior professional and managerial level, rather than competing with local graduates in the general workforce.

Employers across sectors including technology, finance, manufacturing and healthcare have been notified of the revised thresholds and must ensure that any new Employment Pass applications submitted from June onward meet the updated salary requirements. Existing EP holders whose passes were issued before the effective date are understood to be subject to transitional arrangements, with renewals expected to align with the new thresholds.

Business groups have offered mixed reactions to the changes. Industry associations representing technology companies welcomed the move as a positive signal for attracting senior international talent, while some smaller businesses and start-ups cautioned that the doubled salary threshold could constrain their ability to hire specialised foreign professionals at earlier career stages. The Malaysian Employers Federation has called for clarity on the transitional rules and grace periods for affected companies.

The Employment Pass revision is part of a suite of labour market reforms that the government has introduced over the past 18 months, alongside changes to the Resident Pass, Professional Visit Pass and the newly created Tech Entrepreneur Pass. Together, these measures reflect Malaysia's effort to position itself as a competitive hub for global talent in a regional landscape where Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia are also actively courting skilled international professionals.
