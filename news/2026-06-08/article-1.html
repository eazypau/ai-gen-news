PM Anwar Rejects Fuel Price Hike, Government to Continue Bearing Subsidy

Economy The Sun Malaysia 8 June 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has firmly stated that he does not agree with any move to increase fuel prices while Malaysia continues to navigate a challenging global supply environment. Speaking on Sunday, Anwar emphasized that the government remains committed to shielding ordinary Malaysians from the financial burden of rising global energy costs.

RON95 petrol remains pegged at RM1.99 per litre — one of the lowest pump prices in the world — as the government continues to absorb the difference through a large-scale subsidy programme. Monthly fuel subsidy expenditures, which at one point surged to as high as RM7 billion, have since moderated to approximately RM4 billion per month, providing some fiscal breathing room.

Anwar's remarks come amid renewed international pressure on crude oil prices and speculation that Malaysia may be forced to revise its long-standing subsidy model. The Prime Minister dismissed suggestions of an imminent price revision, reiterating that the government's primary responsibility is to protect the purchasing power of the rakyat, particularly lower-income households that depend on affordable fuel for their daily livelihoods.

The fuel subsidy debate is closely watched by economists and market analysts alike. While maintaining the subsidy cushions consumers in the short term, critics argue that targeted subsidy reform — directing aid only to those who genuinely need it — would be more fiscally sustainable and reduce the risk of wealthy consumers benefiting disproportionately from blanket price controls.

The government has previously signalled that a revamp of the fuel subsidy mechanism is on the policy agenda, though Anwar indicated that any changes would be implemented carefully and only when economic conditions are more stable. The administration has said it will continue to monitor global energy markets and adjust policy as needed while keeping public welfare as its foremost priority.
