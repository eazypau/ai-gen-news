FIFA World Cup 2026: Your Complete Guide to Watching All 104 Matches in Malaysia

Sports Malay Mail 8 June 2026

The 2026 FIFA World Cup kicks off on June 12 and Malaysian football fans are in for a significantly different viewing experience this time around. For the first time in nearly two decades, Astro has failed to secure the primary broadcasting rights for the tournament, leaving the nation's long-dominant pay-TV operator on the sidelines of the world's biggest sporting event.

Instead, RTM (Radio Television Malaysia) and Unifi TV will be the primary broadcasters carrying all 104 matches. Fans can access RTM's live World Cup broadcasts through the MyGOV Malaysia app's built-in streaming feature, which provides content from TV1, TV2, and Okey TV — making free access available to anyone with a smartphone and an internet connection.

Unifi TV has launched a dedicated World Cup season pass priced at RM60, which grants access to all matches throughout the tournament. Existing Unifi TV subscribers can enjoy a discounted rate of RM50. For casual viewers who only want to watch selected matches, a daily pass priced at RM20 will be available from June 12 onwards.

The shift away from Astro reflects broader changes in Malaysia's media landscape. Younger audiences have increasingly moved toward over-the-top (OTT) streaming platforms such as Netflix and Disney+ Hotstar for their entertainment, and the loss of World Cup rights is seen as a significant blow to Astro's subscriber value proposition. Industry observers note the development signals a structural change in how Malaysians consume premium live content.

With the United States, Canada and Mexico co-hosting this expanded 48-team tournament, Malaysian fans will be watching games across multiple time zones, with some early-round matches scheduled in the early morning hours. RTM's free-to-air coverage ensures that no Malaysian fan needs to pay to watch any match, a point the government has highlighted as a win for public access to major sporting events.
