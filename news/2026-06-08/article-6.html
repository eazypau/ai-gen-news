Malaysia Evaluates Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Naval Strike Missile Export Licence

Defence Free Malaysia Today 8 June 2026

Malaysia's defence establishment is reassessing its naval armament procurement strategy after Norway revoked the export licence for the Naval Strike Missile (NSM) system, a precision anti-ship weapon that had been earmarked for the Royal Malaysian Navy (RMN). The revocation has prompted the Ministry of Defence to expedite the search for viable alternative suppliers who can meet Malaysia's maritime strike capability requirements.

The Naval Strike Missile, developed by Norwegian defence firm Kongsberg, is considered one of the most capable sea-skimming anti-ship missiles currently in production. Its advanced stealth characteristics, terrain-following flight profile, and long-range precision guidance systems made it a favoured choice for the RMN's surface combatants. Norway's decision to revoke the licence reflects growing international scrutiny over arms exports and the geopolitical considerations that increasingly shape such decisions.

Defence analysts note that Malaysia has a range of alternatives to consider, including the MBDA Exocet series from France, the Boeing Harpoon from the United States, and various missile systems offered by India and other regional defence exporters. The selection process is expected to weigh not only technical performance but also offset arrangements, technology transfer opportunities, and diplomatic considerations.

The episode underscores the vulnerabilities that arise when nations depend heavily on single-source suppliers for critical defence equipment. Malaysia's defence planners have in recent years sought to diversify their procurement portfolio and build stronger bilateral defence-industrial relationships to reduce such risks. The RMN's modernisation programme — which includes new patrol vessels, submarines and surface combatants — makes securing reliable weapons systems a matter of strategic urgency.

Government sources indicated that an evaluation committee has been convened to assess alternative systems and that a decision is expected before the end of the current procurement cycle. Malaysia's defence budget for 2026 includes provisions for naval capability enhancement, and officials have emphasised that the revocation of the Norwegian licence, while a setback, will not derail the broader modernisation agenda.
