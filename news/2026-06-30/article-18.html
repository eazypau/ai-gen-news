BUDI Diesel Subsidy Extended to Company-Owned Pick-Up Trucks and Jeeps

The government announced a significant expansion of the BUDI Diesel subsidy scheme, confirming that company-owned private vehicles with pick-up truck (pikap) and jeep (jip) body types will also be eligible for subsidised diesel at RM2.10 per litre from July 1, 2026. The original scheme was designed primarily for individually-owned private vehicles, but the expanded eligibility addresses the concerns of small and medium-sized businesses that rely on diesel-powered utility vehicles.

Under the expanded scheme, eligible companies can register their pick-up trucks and jeeps through the Budi Madani portal and will be granted the standard 200-litre monthly quota, with the option to apply for an additional 100 litres given the higher fuel consumption typical of larger utility vehicles. Companies must ensure the vehicles have current road tax and are used for commercial activities as specified in the registration.

The Malaysian Automotive Association (MAA) welcomed the inclusion of company-owned utility vehicles, noting that pick-up trucks are among the most popular vehicle categories in Malaysia and are widely used by contractors, farmers, and logistics operators. MAA president Datuk Aishah Ahmad expressed hope that the government would also consider extending additional quotas to diesel-engine MPV owners, who face similar fuel cost pressures.

The inclusion of company-owned vehicles is expected to benefit tens of thousands of small businesses, particularly in the construction, agriculture, and plantation sectors where diesel-powered utility vehicles are indispensable. Industry groups had lobbied for the inclusion since the scheme was first announced, arguing that excluding commercial vehicles would create an uneven playing field and undermine the scheme's economic equity objectives.

The Finance Ministry clarified that the subsidy transfers are non-transferable across vehicle types — meaning a company's pick-up truck quota cannot be used to fill another vehicle in the same fleet that is not registered under the scheme. Enforcement will be carried out through the MyKad verification system at petrol stations, with random audits planned to detect abuse.
