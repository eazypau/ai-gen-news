Government Allocates RM100 Million for AI-Focused TVET Upskilling Programme

The Skills Development Fund Corporation (PTPK) has earmarked RM100 million under a high-impact TVET (Technical and Vocational Education and Training) financing programme targeting seven high-growth sectors: artificial intelligence, green technology, advanced manufacturing, digital finance, cybersecurity, healthcare technology, and logistics automation. The programme is positioned as the government's most direct response to the threat of AI-driven job displacement and forms part of the broader BUDI Madani workforce development agenda.

Eligible participants can access fully subsidised training at approved TVET institutions, community colleges, and industry training centres nationwide. Courses range from short two-to-four-week digital skills bootcamps to six-month professional certification programmes aligned with globally recognised frameworks such as CompTIA, AWS, and Microsoft Azure. Displaced workers who have received Socso retrenchment benefits are given priority access.

Human Resources Minister Datuk Seri R. Ramanan said the RM100 million allocation represents a down payment on what will need to be a sustained multi-year investment. "The disruption is real and accelerating. Our job is to ensure that every Malaysian worker has access to the tools they need to adapt," he told reporters at the programme launch.

The TVET initiative is complemented by the government's MyMahir digital platform, which provides workers with personalised career intelligence including gap analysis between their current skills and in-demand roles, recommended training pathways, and real-time job matching with partnered employers. As of June 2026, over 1.2 million Malaysians are registered on the MyMahir platform.

Critics note that RM100 million, while significant, may fall short of the scale required to retrain the 697,000 workers estimated to be at high risk from AI disruption. Policy researchers have recommended that the government explore public-private funding models and employer training levy systems similar to those used in Singapore and Germany, where employers contribute a portion of their payroll to a national training fund that workers can draw on throughout their careers.
