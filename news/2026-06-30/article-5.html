Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.4% in Q1 2026, Defying Global Uncertainty

Malaysia's gross domestic product expanded by 5.4% in the first quarter of 2026, outpacing both the government's own advance estimate of 5.3% and Bloomberg's median forecast. The result marks the second consecutive quarter of growth above analyst expectations, cementing Malaysia's status as one of Southeast Asia's most resilient economies.

Domestic demand grew by 5.2%, supported by steady household spending amid a buoyant labour market and higher disposable incomes following progressive minimum wage increases and targeted cash transfers. Business investment also expanded as new data centre projects and semiconductor facility expansions broke ground in Penang, Johor, and Selangor.

On the supply side, the manufacturing sector performed strongly, driven by surging global demand for electrical and electronics (E&E) components tied to AI infrastructure and cloud computing data centres. The services sector, Malaysia's largest, also posted solid growth led by financial services, retail trade, and tourism.

The Malaysian Ringgit emerged as one of Asia's top-performing currencies during the quarter, while net foreign direct investment inflows hit RM22.8 billion — a record figure that analysts attributed to Malaysia's competitive positioning in the global semiconductor supply chain and investor confidence in the Madani Government's reform agenda.

Bank Negara Malaysia projected full-year 2026 GDP growth within the 4% to 5% range, accounting for potential headwinds from slower global trade and lingering uncertainty from geopolitical tensions in West Asia. The central bank signalled it would maintain its accommodative monetary policy stance to support domestic growth momentum.
