Jiuzhou Cup National Lion Dance Championship Dazzles Crowd in Port Dickson

The 2nd Jiuzhou Cup National High-Pile Lion Dance Championship and Flag Promotion Invitational Race was held on June 28, 2026, at the Port Dickson beachfront, drawing lion dance troupes from across Malaysia in a spectacular display of athleticism, artistry, and cultural heritage. The competition, one of the most prestigious of its kind on the national circuit, attracted large crowds of spectators who lined the seafront to witness the acrobatic performances high above ground on tall stilted poles.

High-pile lion dance — known as "Gao Zhuang" — is one of the most demanding forms of traditional Chinese martial arts performance, requiring competitors to perform intricate choreography and acrobatic manoeuvres on poles standing up to six metres tall. The discipline demands years of training in both lion dance technique and acrobatics, and Malaysia's high-pile lion dance troupes are internationally regarded for their technical excellence.

The championship featured both open and age-graded categories, with youth and junior divisions showcasing the depth of Malaysia's lion dance development pipeline. Several troupes from Selangor, Johor, and Penang delivered standout performances, with judges awarding marks for technique, synchronicity, creativity, and adherence to traditional forms. Port Dickson's beachfront setting added a scenic backdrop that drew additional spectators from the wider tourism crowd visiting the coastal town.

Organiser Jiuzhou Cultural Association chairman Tan Ah Kow said the event aims to promote Chinese traditional culture among younger Malaysians and to provide competitive opportunities for troupes that have few platforms outside major metropolitan areas. "Port Dickson has been a wonderful host. We hope to make this championship an annual fixture on the national calendar," he said.

Lion dance and dragon dance remain vibrant cultural traditions in Malaysia, performed not only during Chinese New Year celebrations but increasingly at national and community events throughout the year. Several Malaysian lion dance troupes have represented the country at international competitions in Hong Kong, Taiwan, and China, consistently earning medals and recognition for Malaysia's standing in this living cultural art form.
