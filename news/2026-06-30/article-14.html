Ringgit Remains One of Asia's Best-Performing Currencies in 2026

The Malaysian Ringgit has sustained its rally to become one of the best-performing currencies in Asia in 2026, supported by robust economic fundamentals, strong foreign direct investment inflows, and improving investor sentiment toward emerging markets. After a prolonged period of weakness that saw the Ringgit depreciate sharply in 2023 and 2024, the currency's recovery has been one of the more remarkable reversals in the regional currency landscape.

Net foreign direct investment inflows reached RM22.8 billion in Q1 2026 alone — a figure that reflects confidence in Malaysia's economic trajectory, particularly in the semiconductor, data centre, and renewable energy sectors. Bloomberg noted in a mid-year analysis that Malaysia's combination of political stability under the Madani Government, structural economic reforms, and strategic positioning in global supply chains has made it an increasingly attractive destination for long-term capital.

The FTSE Bursa Malaysia KLCI Index has also recovered materially, climbing back to levels last seen in 2018 and reflecting improved corporate earnings expectations, particularly in the banking, technology hardware, and plantation sectors. Fund managers have cited the Ringgit's improved trajectory as a key factor in their decision to increase Malaysian equity allocations.

Bank Negara Malaysia has maintained a supportive monetary policy stance while managing currency volatility through its foreign exchange reserves. The central bank's reserves stood at a comfortable level at end-Q1 2026, providing ample buffer against any sudden capital outflows. Governor Datuk Seri Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour reiterated that BNM would continue to ensure orderly market conditions.

Analysts caution that the Ringgit's strength could face headwinds in H2 2026 if global risk appetite wanes amid US Federal Reserve policy uncertainty and the continued geopolitical premium in global energy markets. Nevertheless, the structural drivers — Malaysia's current account surplus, improving terms of trade, and high FDI pipeline — are expected to provide a durable floor for the currency through year-end.
