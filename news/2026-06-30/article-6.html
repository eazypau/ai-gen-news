697,000 Malaysian Jobs at Risk from AI Without Urgent Upskilling

Human Resources Minister Datuk Seri R. Ramanan issued a stark warning in Parliament this week, stating that up to 697,000 jobs in Malaysia face significant disruption from artificial intelligence, digitalisation, and the green economy within the next three to five years if workers do not urgently upgrade their skills. The figure is drawn from a comprehensive impact study commissioned by TalentCorp and the Skills Development Fund Corporation.

The minister identified roles in administrative support, data processing, basic accounting, and certain manufacturing assembly functions as most vulnerable to automation. However, he emphasised that the intent of government policy is not to prevent AI adoption, but to ensure the Malaysian workforce is equipped to work alongside AI systems and transition into higher-value roles.

The government's response includes an expanded MyMahir platform that provides workers with personalised labour market intelligence, career pathway recommendations, and skills assessments aligned with emerging occupations. The SkillsLab programme has also introduced AI-focused training modules, and more than 26,500 individuals received certification through the Accredited Programme Training System between January and May 2026.

Skills Development Fund Corporation (PTPK) has allocated RM100 million under a high-impact TVET financing programme targeting seven high-growth sectors including AI, clean energy, advanced manufacturing, digital finance, cybersecurity, healthcare technology, and logistics automation. Participants can access fully subsidised training courses at approved institutions nationwide.

Observers have noted that Malaysia's challenge is not unique — a World Economic Forum report in 2025 projected that 40% of all global jobs would be impacted by AI over the next decade. What distinguishes Malaysia's situation is the speed of technology adoption in its export-oriented economy, making timely upskilling not just desirable but essential for economic competitiveness.
