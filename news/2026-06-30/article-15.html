Malaysia's Climate Change Bill Set for Mid-2026 Parliament Tabling

Malaysia's Ministry of Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability is moving closer to tabling the country's first dedicated climate change bill in Parliament, with the minister targeting a mid-2026 window. The legislation, which has been in development for several years, is expected to establish a legal framework for Malaysia's net-zero commitments, set binding emissions reduction targets for major industries, and create accountability mechanisms for government climate action.

Malaysia is also finalising its inaugural National Adaptation Plan (NAP), with a 2026 target for completion. The NAP is designed to guide the country's response to the physical risks of climate change, including more frequent and intense flooding, coastal erosion, urban heat islands, and agricultural disruption. The plan incorporates lessons from recent flood disasters that have caused significant economic and human losses.

The urgency of climate legislation is underscored by Malaysia's worsening flood statistics. Annual flood-related losses reached RM933.4 million in 2024, up from RM755.4 million in 2023, as the frequency and severity of extreme weather events has increased. The World Bank has warned Malaysia that inaction on climate adaptation would impose rising economic costs that could undermine the gains from the country's recent growth trajectory.

Environmental civil society groups have cautiously welcomed the pending legislation but have called for the bill to include robust climate finance provisions, independent oversight mechanisms, and meaningful penalties for corporate emitters who fail to meet their targets. They have also called for the inclusion of a just-transition framework to protect workers in carbon-intensive industries such as coal, oil palm, and cement manufacturing.

Malaysia has committed to achieving net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050 and has set interim targets under its Updated Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) submitted to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. The climate change bill is seen as a critical legislative step to embed these commitments in domestic law and create the enforceable standards needed to meet them.
