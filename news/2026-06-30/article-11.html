Thomas Cup 2026: Malaysia's Quarterfinal Exit Exposes Gap With China

Malaysia's campaign at the 2026 BWF Thomas & Uber Cup Finals ended in disappointment as the national men's team was comprehensively beaten 3-0 by China in the quarterfinals held in Horsens, Denmark. Despite a spirited run through the group stage — including a narrow 3-2 defeat to Japan that still allowed them to progress as Group B runners-up — Malaysia could not replicate that fighting spirit against a dominant Chinese side.

Lee Zii Jia, Malaysia's world-class singles anchor, was unable to save the tie single-handedly. While he delivered a quality performance in his match, the Malaysians were outclassed in both men's doubles rubbers, highlighting the structural weakness in that discipline that has haunted the team for several years. Former national coach Pekan Ramli said the defeat exposed a gap with China that could take years to close without decisive investment in depth.

In the Uber Cup, Malaysia's women's team also bowed out 3-0 at the quarterfinal stage against China. Young Malaysian shuttlers showed flashes of potential in the group phase — particularly the doubles pairing of Pearly Tan and M. Thinaah — but were ultimately outclassed by the depth and consistency of the Chinese squad.

Badminton Association of Malaysia (BAM) president Tan Sri Norza Zakaria acknowledged the results were below expectations and committed to a review of the national development programme. He pointed to Malaysia's recent investments in new training facilities in Bukit Kiara and an expanded junior development pipeline as reasons for cautious optimism about the medium-term future.

China won both the Thomas Cup and Uber Cup for the fourth consecutive edition, reinforcing their dominance of world badminton. Indonesia placed second in the Thomas Cup, while Japan took silver in the Uber Cup. For Malaysia, the benchmark now is to qualify for both semifinals at the 2028 edition — a goal that will require notable improvement in men's doubles and greater consistency from the women's singles department.
