Storms and Floods Hit Perak, Kuala Lumpur, and Selangor in Late June

Malaysia's National Disaster Management Agency (NADMA) reported storms and flash flooding across Perak, Kuala Lumpur, and Selangor during the week of June 22–28, 2026. Several low-lying residential areas in Klang Valley suburbs experienced inundation following intense localised rainfall, prompting temporary evacuations of hundreds of residents to relief centres.

The AHA Centre's weekly disaster update for June 22–28 recorded the flood events as part of a broader pattern of extreme weather affecting the Klang Valley during the pre-Southwest Monsoon transition period. NADMA activated district-level disaster response teams and coordinated with local councils to deploy water pumps and clear storm drains blocked by accumulated debris.

In Kuala Lumpur, flash floods in areas including Taman Sri Muda, Cheras, and Kepong disrupted traffic and caused damage to vehicles and ground-floor properties. The KL City Hall announced that emergency drain-clearing operations had been conducted in 47 hotspot areas ahead of the wet season, though residents and NGOs noted that these efforts have yet to fully address the underlying capacity issues.

Perak saw more prolonged flooding in lower Perak districts, with Teluk Intan and Bagan Datuk recording elevated river levels. The Perak State Disaster Management Committee opened temporary relief centres at local schools, accommodating approximately 200 displaced residents at the peak of the event. All centres were stood down by June 26 as water levels receded.

The recurring nature of Klang Valley floods has intensified calls for greater investment in urban drainage master plans and nature-based solutions such as retention ponds and urban wetlands. The Department of Irrigation and Drainage has allocated RM2.3 billion for flood mitigation projects in Peninsular Malaysia under the 12th Malaysia Plan, though implementation has been uneven across states.
