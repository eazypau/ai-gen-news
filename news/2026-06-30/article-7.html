42,807 Workers Lost Jobs in First Half of 2026 as Socso Data Reveals Labour Strain

Malaysia's Social Security Organisation (Socso) recorded 42,807 worker retrenchments between January 1 and June 12, 2026, with more than 40% of the job losses attributed to business closures and workforce downsizing. The figure has raised concerns among labour unions and economists who warn that the pace of layoffs, while not yet alarming, signals structural change in the job market.

The sectors most affected include retail, business process outsourcing (BPO), and light manufacturing — industries particularly exposed to automation and shifting consumer behaviour. Several multinational BPO firms operating in Kuala Lumpur and Penang have downsized their headcounts following the deployment of AI-powered customer service platforms.

Despite the headline retrenchment figure, the broader labour market picture remains relatively healthy. The government's MYFutureJobs portal listed 605,168 job vacancies against only 188,062 active job seekers as of mid-June — a ratio that points to a structural skills mismatch rather than an absolute shortage of employment opportunities.

HR Minister Datuk Seri R. Ramanan noted that the mismatch reflects the growing premium on digital, technical, and soft skills that many displaced workers currently lack. The ministry is working with state governments to accelerate retraining programmes in affected industrial areas, particularly in Selangor, Penang, and Johor, where the majority of layoffs have been concentrated.

Labour unions have called for a strengthened severance framework and mandatory outplacement services for workers displaced by automation. The Human Resources Ministry has indicated it is reviewing the Employment Act to consider such provisions, though no timeline for legislative changes has been confirmed.
