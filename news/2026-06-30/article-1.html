LRT3 Shah Alam Line Now Fully Operational, Free Rides Until July 31

After years of delays, the LRT3 Shah Alam Line officially began full passenger operations on June 29, 2026, ushering in a new era of public transport connectivity for the Klang Valley's western corridor. Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim launched the line on June 28 with a high-profile ceremony, describing the opening as a milestone for Malaysia's urban mobility.

The line spans 37.8 kilometres, connecting Bandar Utama in Petaling Jaya to Johan Setia in Klang through 20 stations. Key stops include Glenmarie 2, Stadium Shah Alam, Universiti Teknologi MARA (UiTM) Shah Alam, Bandar Baru Klang, Pasar Klang, and Bandar Bukit Tinggi, running through one of the most congested road corridors in the Klang Valley.

To encourage early ridership, the government announced that all LRT3 services — including train rides, feeder buses, and park-and-ride facilities — will be offered free of charge for one month until July 31, 2026. Commuters have praised the move as long overdue relief for the hundreds of thousands of people who live and work along the Shah Alam–Klang corridor.

The project, which was originally contracted in 2016, faced multiple significant delays due to design revisions, procurement challenges, and technical issues with signalling systems. The previous target of a December 2025 opening was pushed back following unresolved technical evaluations. The final cost and revised scope placed it as one of Malaysia's most complex transit infrastructure projects.

Transport Minister Anthony Loke expressed confidence that LRT3 would relieve traffic congestion along Federal Highway and the Shah Alam Expressway. Plans are in place to integrate the Shah Alam Line with existing Kelana Jaya Line and KTM Komuter services at interchange stations, creating a seamless multimodal travel experience for western Klang Valley residents.
