Malaysia IC Design Park on Track for 400-Engineer Target by End-2026

The Malaysia Semiconductor IC Design Park has engaged 302 engineers to date and is on course to achieve its 400-engineer target before year-end, according to Selangor Information Technology and Digital Economy Corporation (Sidec), which manages the facility. The park represents one of the most tangible early deliverables of Malaysia's National Semiconductor Strategy (NSS), which aims to move the country up the semiconductor value chain into higher-margin IC design activities.

Approximately 80% of the engineers hired are fresh graduates, reflecting the park's dual role as both a commercial entity and a talent incubator. The facility provides structured mentorship programmes, access to industry-standard EDA (Electronic Design Automation) software tools, and exposure to real-world tape-out projects — experiences that are typically unavailable in academic settings and take years to acquire in conventional employment.

Sidec has established collaboration agreements with five Malaysian public universities to co-develop curriculum and second final-year engineering students to the park for industrial attachment programmes. A six-month accelerated "IC Design Foundation" course, jointly developed with a Taiwanese IC design house, has been piloted successfully with the first cohort of 30 participants completing the programme in May 2026.

The park's commercial output has also grown, with several of its resident companies completing their first successful tape-outs — the final stage before a chip design is sent to a foundry for manufacturing. This milestone demonstrates that the park is not merely a training ground but a functioning commercial ecosystem capable of producing intellectual property in integrated circuit design.

The success of the IC Design Park is seen as a model that could be replicated in Penang, Johor, and Sarawak — states with existing semiconductor manufacturing clusters that could benefit from locally-grown design capability. The NSS roadmap envisions 10 such design parks across Malaysia by 2030, creating a distributed network of IC design talent that would significantly strengthen Malaysia's position in the global semiconductor value chain.
