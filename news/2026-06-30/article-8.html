Malaysia's Semiconductor Talent Gap: 50,000 Engineers Needed, Only 5,000 Produced Annually

SEMICON Southeast Asia 2026, held in May at the Malaysia International Trade and Exhibition Centre (MITEC) in Kuala Lumpur under the theme "Transform Tomorrow", cast a spotlight on one of the most pressing challenges facing Malaysia's burgeoning semiconductor industry: a severe and growing engineering talent gap. The country needs approximately 50,000 skilled engineers to meet current industry demand, yet Malaysian universities produce only around 5,000 engineering graduates per year — a tenfold deficit that threatens to constrain the sector's growth ambitions.

Malaysia has attracted billions of ringgit in semiconductor investment over the past three years, driven by global supply chain diversification away from Taiwan and China. Intel, Infineon, Texas Instruments, and multiple Taiwan-based chip packaging firms have either expanded or announced new facilities in Penang, which accounts for over 70% of Malaysia's semiconductor output. This investment surge has dramatically outpaced the domestic talent pipeline.

The National Semiconductor Strategy (NSS), launched by the government in 2023, targets the training of 60,000 highly skilled semiconductor engineers by 2030. Initiatives under the NSS include industry-linked degree programmes, accelerated postgraduate training, and partnerships with Taiwan's ITRI and South Korea's KAIST. However, critics argue that the timeline is too slow relative to the pace of investment arriving.

The Malaysia Semiconductor IC Design Park has made tangible progress, hiring 302 engineers to date with a target of 400 by year-end. About 80% of those hired are fresh graduates, reflecting both the opportunity and risk in relying on newly-trained talent for technically demanding IC design work. The park is now collaborating with five local universities to co-develop curriculum aligned with industry requirements.

Industry leaders at SEMICON SEA 2026 called on the government to expand immigration pathways for experienced foreign semiconductor engineers as a bridging measure. Malaysia currently grants a five-year Employment Pass for skilled foreign workers, but industry groups have requested a streamlined approval process specifically for semiconductor talent to fast-track critical hires.
