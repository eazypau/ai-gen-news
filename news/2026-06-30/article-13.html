Malaysia Year of Medical Tourism 2026 Targets RM12 Billion Revenue by 2030

Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC) is leading the Malaysia Year of Medical Tourism 2026 (MYMT 2026) — the nation's first-ever dedicated year to celebrate and advance healthcare travel — under the theme "Healing Meets Hospitality". The initiative aims to strengthen Malaysia's global brand as a premier healthcare destination while stimulating economic activity across the hospital, hospitality, and transport sectors.

As part of MYMT 2026, MHTC has partnered with over 35 accredited hospitals nationwide, tourism authorities, and major airlines to launch an integrated marketing push across key source markets including Indonesia, China, India, Bangladesh, and the Middle East. A flagship Medical Tourism Hospital Award was unveiled, recognising institutions that meet the highest standards of patient experience, clinical excellence, and medical tourism-specific services.

The government is positioning Malaysia's healthcare sector as a pillar of its economic diversification strategy. Combined with the country's reputation for affordable private healthcare, its pool of internationally-trained specialists, and its multilingual medical workforce, Malaysia competes directly with Thailand and Singapore for the regional healthcare travel dollar — while offering a significantly lower cost-to-quality ratio.

As of 2025, international patient arrivals exceeded one million, with cumulative revenue surpassing RM3 billion for the year. The pace of growth — driven by post-pandemic pent-up demand and Malaysia's aggressive regional marketing — has prompted the government to revise its target from RM8 billion to RM12 billion in medical tourism revenue by 2030.

A new digital portal, launched alongside MYMT 2026, allows international patients to browse accredited hospitals, compare treatment costs, make appointments, and arrange integrated medical-stay packages including accommodation and transportation. The platform supports eight languages and accepts payments in six major currencies, lowering barriers for first-time visitors.
