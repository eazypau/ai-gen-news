PM Anwar Warns Against Growing Racial and Religious Rhetoric in Politics

Politics Bernama 5 July 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has delivered a forceful warning against the escalating use of racial and religious rhetoric in Malaysian politics, describing it as a threat to the nation's social fabric and long-term stability. Speaking at a national unity forum in Kuala Lumpur, Anwar said Malaysia's future depends on rejecting extremism and embracing mutual respect among its diverse communities.

"We cannot allow our politics to be defined by who is more Malay, more Chinese, more Indian or more Muslim," Anwar told attendees. "The measure of good governance is whether we can uplift all Malaysians, regardless of background." He stressed that the MADANI framework — the government's guiding philosophy of compassion, respect and good governance — must serve as a bulwark against divisive politics.

The Prime Minister also directed sharp criticism at certain political factions that frequently invoke Malay supremacy in their campaigns while simultaneously allowing Malay reserve land to be transferred into the hands of non-Malays or politically connected parties. He called such behaviour hypocritical and said it undermines the very communities these politicians claim to champion.

Anwar's remarks come in the lead-up to the Johor state election on July 11, where PAS and Bersatu have ramped up identity-based messaging. Observers noted that the speech was also an implicit rebuke of elements within his own coalition who have occasionally drifted toward communal positioning to shore up Malay voter support.

Civil society groups welcomed the Prime Minister's statement, with Suhakam chair Datuk Foo said the remarks set an important tone ahead of Johor polling day. However, opposition leaders argued that Anwar's own coalition partners have not consistently practiced the unity politics he preaches.
