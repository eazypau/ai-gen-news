80% of Malaysian CEOs Make AI Sovereignty a Core Strategic Priority — IBM Study

Technology IBM Malaysia / GovInsider Asia 5 July 2026

A landmark study by IBM released in 2026 has revealed that 80% of chief executives surveyed in Malaysia now regard AI sovereignty — the capacity of a country to control its own artificial intelligence infrastructure, data and governance frameworks — as essential to their corporate and national strategy. The finding marks a significant shift in executive mindset from just two years ago, when AI was still viewed primarily as an operational efficiency tool rather than a geopolitical and strategic imperative.

AI sovereignty encompasses a range of concerns including data localisation, the ability to train and run AI models on domestic infrastructure, protection against foreign surveillance of sensitive data, and the development of local AI talent and intellectual property. As AI becomes more deeply embedded in critical sectors including healthcare, financial services, defence and public administration, executives are increasingly wary of over-dependence on foreign hyperscalers.

The IBM study found that Malaysian companies are increasingly investing in on-premises and hybrid AI infrastructure, with government guidance through initiatives such as the Malaysia Digital Economy Blueprint and the National AI Roadmap playing an important role in shaping private-sector investment decisions. The Digital Investment Office (DIO) under MIDA has attracted over RM100 billion in data centre and AI-related investment commitments since 2023.

Malaysia has positioned itself as a regional AI hub, leveraging its political stability, multilingual population, competitive energy costs and improving connectivity infrastructure. The government's target is for Malaysia to become an AI-ready nation by 2030, with at least 20,000 certified AI practitioners in the workforce and a thriving domestic AI product and services industry.

However, experts caution that sovereignty goals must be balanced against pragmatic realities. Full stack AI independence remains beyond the reach of most mid-sized economies, and effective AI sovereignty more realistically means building meaningful domestic capability and governance levers rather than complete independence from global technology ecosystems.
