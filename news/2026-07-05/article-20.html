Syed Saddiq Appeal Postponed to July 13 After Federal Court Judge Falls Ill

Legal Malay Mail 5 July 2026

The Federal Court has postponed its long-awaited ruling in the prosecution's appeal against the acquittal of Muar Member of Parliament Syed Saddiq Syed Abdul Rahman to July 13, after one of the judges on the five-member panel fell ill and was unable to attend the scheduled delivery of judgment. The panel confirmed that both parties have been notified of the new date, subject to their availability and the presiding judge's recovery.

The case stems from four criminal charges against Syed Saddiq involving criminal breach of trust, dishonest misappropriation and money laundering, all related to funds belonging to the youth wing of Parti Pribumi Bersatu Malaysia (Bersatu), known as Armada. The charges covered alleged misappropriation of party funds totalling hundreds of thousands of ringgit during the period when Syed Saddiq was Armada's chief.

The Court of Appeal previously acquitted Syed Saddiq of all four charges, leading to the prosecution's current appeal before the Federal Court, which is Malaysia's apex court and the final arbiter on questions of law. A successful prosecution appeal could result in a retrial or a conviction being substituted, which would carry significant political implications given that Syed Saddiq is a sitting MP and the founder of the Muda party.

Syed Saddiq arrived at the Federal Court in Putrajaya on June 30 alongside a crowd of supporters, before the postponement was announced from the bench. His legal team has consistently maintained that the acquittal by the Court of Appeal was correct in law and that the prosecution has not identified sufficient grounds to overturn it at the Federal Court level.

Political observers note that the outcome of the case will be scrutinised across party lines. Syed Saddiq, once a cabinet minister under the Mahathir administration, parted ways with Bersatu and has since built Muda as an independent youth-focused political movement. A conviction would bar him from contesting elections, while an acquittal would strengthen his political standing ahead of the next general election cycle.
