Dewan Rakyat Passes Cybercrimes Bill 2026 to Strengthen Digital Law Enforcement

Technology New Straits Times 5 July 2026

Malaysia's Dewan Rakyat has passed the Cybercrimes Bill 2026, a landmark piece of legislation that significantly strengthens the country's legal framework for tackling online offences. The bill, which now heads to the Dewan Negara for senate approval, introduces a comprehensive range of cybercrime offences and updated penalties designed to keep pace with the rapidly evolving digital threat landscape.

The legislation covers a wide spectrum of cybercrimes including unauthorised access to computer systems, data theft, online fraud, cyberharassment, the spread of malicious code, and crimes facilitated by artificial intelligence tools such as deepfakes. Penalties under the new law are substantially stiffer than those under the existing Computer Crimes Act 1997, with maximum fines and imprisonment terms increased to reflect the severity of modern cybercrime.

Law enforcement agencies including the Royal Malaysia Police's Commercial Crime Investigation Department (CCID) and CyberSecurity Malaysia have welcomed the legislation, noting that current laws have at times been insufficient to deal with sophisticated cross-border cybercrime syndicates operating out of countries including Myanmar, Cambodia and China. The new bill includes provisions for enhanced international cooperation and data sharing with foreign law enforcement.

Privacy advocates have raised concerns about some of the bill's broader surveillance provisions, calling for robust parliamentary oversight and clear safeguards to prevent misuse by authorities. The Digital Freedom Alliance urged the government to ensure that legitimate online speech and whistleblower activity are not swept up in enforcement actions under the new law.

Communications Minister said the bill strikes a necessary balance between protecting Malaysians from the rising tide of online crime — which cost the country an estimated RM3.2 billion in losses in 2025 — and preserving fundamental digital rights. The ministry will establish a technical advisory committee to review implementation and propose amendments as technology evolves.
