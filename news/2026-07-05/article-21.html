Lim Guan Eng and Wife's Bid to Quash Charges Fails — Trial Proceeds at Penang High Court

Legal Bernama 5 July 2026

Former Finance Minister and DAP Secretary-General Lim Guan Eng, his wife Betty Chew Gek Cheng, and businesswoman Phang Li Koon have failed in their legal bid to have corruption and misconduct charges against them struck out at the Penang High Court. Presiding judge Justice Noorin ruled that the charges in the present case are distinct in law and fact from those in the earlier bungalow case, which had also involved Lim, and that the prosecution has the right to proceed to trial.

The ruling clears the way for the trial to proceed on a set of charges that relate to alleged criminal misconduct connected to a land reclamation project in Penang during the period when Lim served as Chief Minister of the state. Phang Li Koon, the businesswoman named in the charges, had argued that the case was an abuse of process and a duplication of earlier proceedings that had already been resolved.

Justice Noorin rejected all arguments advanced by the defence counsel, noting that each charge must be assessed on its own legal elements and factual matrix, and that the existence of earlier related proceedings does not automatically bar new charges arising from different alleged acts or omissions. Case management has been set at the Penang High Court to determine a trial schedule, with the prosecution and defence teams expected to agree on the number of witnesses and the anticipated length of proceedings.

Lim Guan Eng maintained his innocence outside the courthouse, saying he welcomed the opportunity to clear his name in a full trial and that he has full confidence in the Malaysian judiciary to render a fair verdict based on the evidence. His legal team said it will continue to mount a vigorous defence and reserved the right to appeal the ruling on the quashing application if legally advised to do so.

The case has attracted sustained public interest given Lim's prominence as one of Malaysia's most senior opposition politicians and a former federal Finance Minister. His supporters have characterised the charges as politically motivated, while the prosecution maintains that the case is a straightforward matter of applying the law without fear or favour to persons in positions of public trust regardless of their political affiliation.
