Malaysia Faces Toughest Commonwealth Games Challenge Yet as Badminton, Squash Absent from Glasgow 2026

Sports Bernama 5 July 2026

With the 2026 Commonwealth Games set to open at the OVO Hydro in Glasgow, Scotland on July 23, Malaysia's contingent faces what analysts are calling the most difficult edition of the Games in the modern era. The absence of badminton, squash, diving, rhythmic gymnastics and table tennis from the Glasgow 2026 programme strips away the sports that have historically been Malaysia's primary sources of gold medals at the Commonwealth Games.

At Birmingham 2022, Malaysia collected seven gold, eight silver and eight bronze medals — a creditable haul. But four of those seven gold medals came from sports not featured in Glasgow. Badminton alone contributed two golds, while squash and diving added one each. The Malaysian contingent will now be tested across 10 remaining sports: athletics, swimming, track cycling, lawn bowls, boxing, judo and weightlifting, plus six para-sports in an integrated format.

The Olympic Council of Malaysia (OCM) accepted Glasgow's scaled-down Games format after a prolonged period of negotiation and uncertainty about the event's viability following financial difficulties in the original host Scotland. Despite the reduced programme, officials have expressed confidence that Malaysia's investment in developing athletes across a broader range of disciplines will begin to bear fruit in Glasgow.

Athletics and swimming will be the key battlegrounds for Malaysia's medal aspirations, with national track and field federation MAFAN targeting podium performances from middle-distance runners and sprinters. Para-sports, which are being integrated into the main Games for the first time in this format, offer another avenue for medals with Malaysia's Paralympic programme having strengthened considerably since Tokyo 2020.

Team Malaysia is expected to send approximately 150 athletes and officials to Glasgow. Sports Minister expressed measured optimism, saying that while the medal count will likely be lower than Birmingham, Glasgow represents an important opportunity to test the depth of Malaysia's athletic development pipeline and identify the next generation of champions ahead of the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics.
