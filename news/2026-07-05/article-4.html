Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.4% in Q1 2026, Unemployment Hits 10-Year Low

Business Ministry of Finance Malaysia 5 July 2026

Malaysia's economy expanded by 5.4% in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the same period a year earlier, outpacing market expectations and demonstrating resilience despite significant global headwinds. The Ministry of Finance attributed the stronger-than-expected performance to robust domestic consumption and continued investment inflows into the technology and manufacturing sectors.

The unemployment rate fell to 2.9% — its lowest in a decade — as strong job creation in services, construction and digital industries absorbed new entrants into the labour market. Inflation remained well-contained at 1.6% during the quarter, easing pressure on household budgets and supporting consumer spending.

Growth did moderate compared to the 6.2% recorded in Q4 2025, driven by weaker performance in the mining and quarrying sector, which contracted by 2.1%. Agriculture, manufacturing and construction also reported softer but still positive growth. The services sector, which remains the backbone of the economy, expanded by 5.6% and continued to be the primary engine of overall GDP growth.

Bank Negara Malaysia noted that the external environment remains challenging, with global trade tensions and West Asia geopolitical risks creating uncertainty for Malaysia's export-oriented industries. The central bank maintained its growth projection for the full year of 2026 at between 4% and 5%, describing this range as achievable given Malaysia's strong fundamentals.

The Edge Malaysia described the numbers as indicating "tougher times" may lie ahead as global conditions tighten, but noted that Malaysia's diversified economic base and government stimulus programmes should provide adequate buffers through the remainder of the year.
