Microsoft and Lightstorm to Build 3,600km I-2SEA Undersea Cable Linking India, Malaysia and Singapore

Technology New Straits Times 5 July 2026

A consortium led by Microsoft and Singapore-based Lightstorm has announced plans to construct the I-2SEA undersea cable system, a 3,600-kilometre high-capacity fibre-optic link that will connect India with Malaysia and Singapore. The project represents one of the most significant undersea infrastructure investments in the region and is expected to dramatically increase data transmission capacity between South Asia and Southeast Asia when it goes live in late 2029.

The I-2SEA cable is being built specifically to meet the surging demand for AI workloads, cloud computing and data centre traffic that is expected to continue growing rapidly through the end of the decade. Microsoft's Azure cloud platform, which operates data centres in Malaysia and Singapore, will be a primary user of the cable's capacity. Lightstorm brings deep expertise in subsea cable engineering and project management for the Asia-Pacific region.

Malaysia's inclusion as a landing point on the cable is a significant endorsement of the country's growing role as a regional digital hub. The government has invested heavily in data centre infrastructure and connectivity over the past three years, attracting hyperscale data centre campuses from Microsoft, Google, AWS and several Asian operators to key tech corridors in Selangor, Johor and Sabah.

The cable will terminate at cable landing stations in Chennai or Mumbai on the Indian side and at facilities in Penang or Johor in Malaysia, with an onward connection to Singapore's Tuas or Changi cable stations. The exact landing configurations are subject to regulatory approvals in all three countries, which are expected to be finalised by end of 2026.

Industry analysts say the cable is particularly timely given rapidly growing internet traffic between India — now the world's most populous country and one of its fastest-growing digital economies — and the data centre hubs of Malaysia and Singapore that serve as the backbone of Southeast Asian cloud infrastructure. Several competing cable projects are also underway in the region, reflecting the scale of anticipated demand growth.
