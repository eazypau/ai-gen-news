Malaysia Launches SemiconStart to Propel Semiconductor Startup Ecosystem

Technology New Straits Times 5 July 2026

SemiconStart Malaysia has officially launched operations, marking a significant milestone in the government's strategic push to build a world-class semiconductor startup ecosystem. The initiative, which was first announced in Budget 2026, provides a comprehensive support framework for early-stage companies operating in semiconductor design, advanced packaging, embedded systems and related deep-tech domains.

Under the SemiconStart programme, qualifying startups gain access to mentorship from senior industry practitioners, connections to global funding and investment networks, prototyping tools including access to cleanroom facilities, and commercialisation support to help them navigate the complex process of bringing chip-related products to market. The programme is operated in partnership with Malaysia Semiconductor Industry Association (MSIA) and international partners including IMEC from Belgium.

Malaysia has long been a significant player in the global semiconductor supply chain, particularly in back-end packaging, testing and assembly. The government's ambition is to climb the value chain and develop indigenous capability in chip design and advanced manufacturing — areas historically dominated by the United States, Taiwan, South Korea and Japan.

The launch comes at an opportune moment as global semiconductor companies continue to diversify their supply chains away from single geographies. Several major players have announced or expanded investments in Malaysia in recent years, including Intel, Infineon, Texas Instruments and Micron. SemiconStart aims to ensure that domestic startups can plug into this expanding ecosystem rather than remain passive recipients of foreign investment.

The Science, Technology and Innovation Ministry said it expects to support at least 50 semiconductor startups in the programme's first cohort, with an ambition to scale to 200 by 2028. Successful graduates of the programme are expected to contribute to Malaysia's goal of becoming a top-10 global semiconductor ecosystem by 2030.
