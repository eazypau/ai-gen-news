PM Anwar Launches National Education Blueprint 2026–2035, Targets Grade C Minimum in Core Subjects

Education New Straits Times 5 July 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim officially launched Malaysia's National Education Blueprint (Rancangan Pendidikan Nasional, RPN) 2026–2035 at the Putrajaya International Convention Centre, outlining a decade-long framework of sweeping reforms designed to transform the country's education system from primary level through upper secondary. The blueprint sets ambitious academic, holistic and character development targets for all Malaysian students.

Among the most notable provisions of the new blueprint is the mandatory requirement for all primary and secondary school pupils to achieve at least a grade C in Bahasa Melayu, English, Mathematics and History. Students who do not meet this minimum threshold in Bahasa Melayu and English will be required to attend remedial support classes during the following academic year. The Ministry of Education said the standards are achievable with the right support structures in place and reflect the minimum competencies needed for effective participation in the workforce and civil life.

The blueprint also sets a target for 90% of students from upper primary through Form Five to meet passing standards in physical fitness and health assessments, reflecting a broader push to address sedentary lifestyles and rising rates of childhood obesity and non-communicable diseases among Malaysian youth. Schools will be required to increase the frequency of physical education and extracurricular activity time.

Digital literacy has been integrated throughout the blueprint as a cross-cutting competency rather than a standalone subject. All schools will be equipped with high-speed internet by 2027 under a separately announced infrastructure programme, enabling teachers to deploy blended learning approaches and students to access a national digital content library. Coding and computational thinking elements will be embedded into Mathematics and Science curricula from Year 4 onward.

The British Council welcomed the blueprint's ambition, noting that Malaysia's expanded role as a regional higher education hub requires a strong foundation of English proficiency at secondary level. Teacher unions called for adequate training, resources and salary support to accompany the higher standards being demanded of educators, warning that without investment in the teaching profession the blueprint's goals risk remaining aspirational rather than achievable.
