Anwar Dismisses Speculation that Bersatu Is Seeking to Join Pakatan Harapan

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim on Thursday firmly rejected speculation that Parti Pribumi Bersatu Malaysia (Bersatu) is in discussions to join Pakatan Harapan (PH), saying there have been no indications whatsoever that such talks are taking place. The clarification came in response to rumours circulating in political circles that Bersatu, currently aligned with the opposition Perikatan Nasional (PN), might be seeking a way back into the governing fold ahead of GE16.

"There is no such thing. No one has approached us. The unity government is stable and focused on governing for the people," Anwar told reporters at Putrajaya. He dismissed the reports as political noise designed to destabilise the coalition ahead of the anticipated general election.

Bersatu, which was a founding member of PH before its dramatic exit that triggered the Sheraton Move political crisis in 2020, has been a central component of the opposition PN coalition led by former PM Muhyiddin Yassin. Its potential return to the PH fold would reshape the Malaysian political landscape significantly, though analysts say such a move would face massive resistance from within both PH component parties and the broader electorate.

The speculation may have been fuelled in part by reports that the Islamist party PAS has ended its formal cooperation with Bersatu within PN, potentially leaving Bersatu politically isolated and searching for new alliances. However, senior Bersatu figures have publicly denied any interest in reconciliation with PH, describing the reports as "baseless fabrications."

Political analysts said the episode, while ultimately resolving as a non-event, highlighted the febrile atmosphere surrounding Malaysia's political scene as parties position themselves for an election that many believe is imminent. The speed with which rumours about coalition shifts can gain traction underscores how volatile and fluid the pre-GE16 environment has become.
