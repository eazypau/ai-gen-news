Shah Firdaus Outpaces Azizulhasni to Claim Men's Elite Keirin Gold at National Track Cycling Championships

Muhammad Shah Firdaus Sahrom delivered a stunning victory at the 2026 National Track Cycling Championship, outpacing the celebrated Mohd Azizulhasni Awang — one of Malaysia's most decorated Olympic cyclists — to claim the men's elite keirin gold medal. The result sent shockwaves through the Malaysian track cycling community and marked a significant moment in the generational transition of the country's sprint cycling programme.

Azizulhasni, popularly known as "The Pocket Rocketman," is a two-time Olympic medallist and the most successful Malaysian track cyclist in history. His presence in any keirin final guarantees intensity, and the fact that Shah Firdaus was able to outmanoeuvre and outsprint him in the final laps of the race is a testament to the younger rider's growing tactical sophistication and explosive power. Shah Firdaus has been training intensively under the national cycling programme and has shown steady improvement at international track events over the past two years.

Shah Firdaus' coach attributed the victory to months of refined sprint technique and race-reading ability, noting that keirin is as much a tactical chess match as a pure speed contest — reading opponents' moves, positioning for the final sprint, and avoiding being boxed in are as important as raw wattage. "Shah Firdaus showed maturity beyond his years tonight. He read the race perfectly and found the gap when it mattered," the coach said.

The result has implications for the national team's composition heading into international competitions in the second half of 2026, including the Asian Track Cycling Championships and build-up events toward the next Olympics cycle. Cycling Malaysia officials said both Shah Firdaus and Azizulhasni will be included in the national training programme and that internal competition for positions is healthy for the programme's long-term development.

For Malaysian sports fans, the emergence of Shah Firdaus as a genuine challenger to Azizulhasni's domestic dominance is an exciting development that adds depth to one of the country's few internationally competitive Olympic cycling programmes. Both riders are expected to represent Malaysia at upcoming international events, with selectors now having the welcome challenge of choosing between two world-class sprinters for future assignments.
