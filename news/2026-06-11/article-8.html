RON95 Petrol Stays at RM1.99 Under BUDI95 as Decision on T20 Subsidy Cut Looms

The subsidised price of RON95 petrol remains fixed at RM1.99 per litre for eligible Malaysians under the government's BUDI95 targeted subsidy programme, even as the Ministry of Finance and Cabinet continue deliberations on removing the subsidy for higher-income households. The current weekly petrol prices (June 4–10, 2026) show RON97 at RM4.25 per litre and diesel at RM3.35 per litre, while subsidised RON95 at RM1.99 remains the most popular fuel grade by volume.

Under the BUDI95 framework, eligible Malaysians with a valid driving licence can purchase up to 200 litres of subsidised RON95 per month — reduced from the earlier 300-litre cap following disruptions linked to global market conditions. Motorists who exceed the monthly quota, or those who are ineligible (including foreign-registered vehicles and the to-be-determined high-income households), must purchase fuel at the unsubsidised "float" price of RM4.02 per litre.

Cabinet is understood to have reached broad agreement that the RON95 subsidy should be removed for the highest earners, but the specific income bracket that will trigger disqualification — whether T5 (top 5%), T10, T15, or T20 — remains unresolved. Government sources say the political sensitivity of the decision has been heightened by the proximity to GE16, with any announcement likely to be timed carefully to minimise electoral blowback while still demonstrating fiscal responsibility.

The government has also been studying the administrative mechanisms required to enforce the income-based disqualification. Current options include cross-referencing vehicle registration data with Inland Revenue Board (LHDN) income records at petrol stations via the MyKad or a dedicated app, though privacy and implementation concerns have slowed finalisation of the technical solution.

Consumer advocacy groups have urged the government to provide a clear timeline and adequate notice before any changes take effect, noting that many middle-income households budget around the current RM1.99 price and would face significant cost-of-living adjustments if the subsidy is suddenly removed. The government has said it will study a transition mechanism and has not ruled out offsetting measures such as targeted cash transfers to cushion the impact for affected groups.
