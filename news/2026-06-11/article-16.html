Indonesia Remains Malaysia's Top Medical Tourism Source Market with RM2.2bn Revenue in 2025

Indonesia cemented its position as Malaysia's largest healthcare travel source market in 2025, with more than 970,000 Indonesians travelling to Malaysia for medical treatment and collectively generating RM2.2 billion in medical tourism revenue — a 23% increase compared to 2024 figures. The data, highlighted at Malaysia Fair 2026 in Jakarta, underscores the extraordinary economic significance of the Malaysia-Indonesia healthcare travel corridor and Malaysia's positioning as the region's most accessible quality medical destination for Indonesian patients.

Malaysian healthcare providers attribute their strong appeal among Indonesian patients to several structural advantages: a geographic proximity that makes day-trip or short-stay medical visits practical from much of Sumatra, Java, and Kalimantan; a shared Malay language base that eliminates communication barriers; competitive pricing that frequently offers savings of 30-50% compared to equivalent treatments in Singapore; and an increasingly sophisticated network of internationally accredited hospitals and specialist centres across Penang, Kuala Lumpur, Johor Bahru, and other medical hubs.

The range of treatments sought by Indonesian patients is broad, covering everything from routine health screenings and dental procedures to complex cardiac surgery, oncology treatment, IVF fertility procedures, and ophthalmology interventions. Institut Jantung Negara (National Heart Institute) and Alpha IVF are among the facilities that have seen particularly strong Indonesian patient volumes, reflecting both the clinical expertise available in Malaysia and the relative scarcity of certain specialist services in many Indonesian cities.

Malaysia's Ministry of Health and the Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC) are working to further streamline the process for Indonesian medical travellers, including expanding partnerships with Indonesian insurance providers to allow direct billing, developing digital pre-consultation platforms that allow patients to access Malaysian doctors remotely before committing to travel, and establishing dedicated welcome lounges at major airports catering specifically to medical travellers.

Malaysia is the second-largest medical tourism hub in ASEAN after Thailand, and is on a trajectory to challenge Thailand's lead given the growth momentum. The sector contributed an estimated RM5.5 billion to Malaysia's economy in 2025 across all source markets, and the government has set a target of RM7 billion in medical tourism revenue by 2028. Sustaining growth will require continued investment in hospital capacity, medical specialist training, and the digital infrastructure that increasingly supports patient decision-making and care coordination across borders.
