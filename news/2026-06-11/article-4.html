GE16 Political Landscape Transformed by New Parties, Elite Fractures and Shifting Alliances

Malaysia's electoral landscape heading into GE16 has been profoundly reshaped by a wave of new party formations, high-profile defections, and unpredictable alliance realignments that are turning a once-predictable contest into a genuinely open fight for power. Analysts say the 2026 election, whenever it is called, will be unlike any previous Malaysian general election in terms of the complexity of political options available to voters.

The most dramatic recent development has been the departure of former Economy Minister Rafizi Ramli from PKR. In May 2026, Rafizi — once seen as a rising star in PH and a key figure in the 2022 general election campaign — announced he was taking over BERSAMA, a new party, along with a group of followers who simultaneously resigned their MP positions. The move introduced a significant wild card into opposition politics and may splinter votes in key urban constituencies.

Beyond the Rafizi episode, the emergence of several smaller reform-oriented parties is adding further fragmentation to the multi-cornered races expected in GE16. Political scientists note that with Bersatu potentially weakened by PAS's distancing and facing questions about its leader's legal battles, the PN bloc may struggle to maintain the unified front it presented in GE15.

For the ruling unity coalition, the landscape changes create both opportunities and risks. Fragmentation of the opposition vote could benefit coalition candidates in swing seats, but the proliferation of independent-minded candidates and new parties also risks siphoning support in areas where PH-BN alliances expected smooth sailing. Campaign strategists from multiple parties told The Sun Malaysia they are rebuilding their ground models from scratch to account for the new variables.

With Malaysia constitutionally required to hold GE16 by February 2028, and signals mounting that an early dissolution could come as soon as later this year, parties are racing to finalise their strategies. The next few weeks of candidate announcements and alliance declarations are expected to clarify the picture — though in Malaysia's current political climate, every new day seems capable of producing another surprise.
