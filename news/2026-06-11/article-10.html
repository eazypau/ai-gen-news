Budget 2026: Malaysia's Record RM419bn Spending Plan Targets 4–4.5% GDP Growth with Fiscal Reform Focus

Malaysia's Budget 2026, tabled at RM419.2 billion — approximately USD$99 billion — is the largest in the country's history and marks the fourth consecutive Madani Budget under Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. The budget projects GDP growth of 4% to 4.5% for 2026, driven by resilient domestic demand, private investment, stable employment, and ongoing export strength in key sectors.

The overarching themes of Budget 2026 are fiscal resilience, economic foundation strengthening, and improving the dignity and livelihoods of ordinary Malaysians. Total subsidy and social assistance spending has been cut by 14.1% to RM49 billion, down from RM57.1 billion in 2025, as the government accelerates its rationalisation agenda. The reduction reflects both lower projected commodity prices and the narrowing of subsidy eligibility through targeted programmes like BUDI95 and BUDI MADANI.

Development expenditure has been maintained at high levels to fund critical infrastructure, with major allocations for public transportation, rural development, digitalisation of public services, and the national education and healthcare systems. The budget introduces targeted cash transfers and wage enhancement measures to offset the impact of subsidy rationalisation on lower-income households, including expanded STR (Sumbangan Tunai Rahmah) disbursements.

On the revenue side, the government is pressing ahead with a phased expansion of the Sales and Services Tax (SST) and strengthening tax compliance through a revamped Inland Revenue Board enforcement framework. The introduction of a Capital Gains Tax on listed shares — deferred from the 2025 budget amid market concerns — is back on the table for 2026 implementation, though at a modest rate designed to minimise capital market disruption.

Budget analysts broadly welcomed the fiscal direction, noting that Malaysia's deficit is on track to narrow to approximately 3.8% of GDP in 2026, down from over 5% in recent years. Moody's and Fitch have maintained their stable outlook on Malaysia's sovereign credit rating, citing the government's demonstrated commitment to fiscal consolidation while preserving space for growth-enhancing public investment. However, both agencies flagged the rising subsidy bill as a key downside risk should global oil prices remain elevated through the year.
