Constitutional Bill to Impose 10-Year PM Term Limit Fails to Win Two-Thirds Parliamentary Majority

A landmark constitutional amendment bill that would have capped the tenure of Malaysia's prime minister at ten years failed to secure the two-thirds parliamentary supermajority required for passage, dealing a significant blow to Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's reform agenda. The bill was one of several major legislative initiatives announced early in 2026 as part of a push to demonstrate the unity government's commitment to institutional reform.

The defeat comes despite Anwar having personally championed the measure in January 2026, framing it as a key step toward re-energising supporters and distinguishing the Madani government from the prolonged tenures and perceived authoritarianism of past administrations. The proposal had been widely welcomed by civil society groups and opposition reformists, even as some coalition partners expressed reservations about its political implications.

Parliamentary vote counts indicate that while the bill commanded a simple majority, several BN legislators from the ruling coalition either abstained or voted against the amendment, falling short of the 148-seat threshold required to alter the constitution. Coalition whips declined to comment on individual voting records, but political observers said the outcome exposes fracture lines within the unity government on fundamental questions of governance reform.

The bill was one component of a broader legislative package that also included proposals for an Ombudsman Act, a Freedom of Information Act, and the separation of the Attorney-General's prosecutorial role from advisory functions. The fate of these companion bills remains uncertain following the term limit defeat, with government sources saying they are reviewing the sequencing and strategy for remaining reform legislation.

ISEAS analysts noted that the episode illustrates the tension inherent in Malaysia's unity government model — a coalition built on the shared goal of defeating the opposition but comprising parties with very different visions of how far institutional reform should go. As GE16 approaches, the pressure on the government to show tangible reform achievements will only intensify, making the failure of the term limit bill a politically costly setback for Anwar's narrative of transformative leadership.
