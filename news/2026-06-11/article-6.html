Ringgit Strengthens to 4.07 Against US Dollar as Bank Negara Cites Strong Fundamentals

The Malaysian ringgit has appreciated 3.3% against the US dollar on a year-to-date basis, with the USD/MYR exchange rate reaching 4.07 as of June 10, 2026. Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) attributed the currency's strong performance to a combination of robust domestic economic fundamentals, continued non-resident capital inflows into local financial markets, and positive momentum from the government's economic reform programme.

The ringgit has also strengthened by 2.9% on a nominal effective exchange rate (NEER) basis, meaning it has gained ground against a broad basket of trading-partner currencies — not just the dollar. This broader appreciation indicates that the ringgit's gains reflect genuine improvements in Malaysia's economic standing rather than merely a reaction to US dollar weakness.

Bank Negara Governor Shaik Abdul Rasheed Abdul Ghaffour, speaking at the central bank's mid-year briefing, said the ringgit is being supported by Malaysia's strong economic outlook, with GDP growth on track to hit the government's 4% to 4.5% target for 2026. He highlighted robust private investment, stable employment conditions, and healthy export performance — particularly in the semiconductor and electrical and electronics sectors — as key pillars underpinning demand for the currency.

Non-resident flows into Malaysian government securities and equities have also played a significant role. Foreign investors have been attracted by Malaysia's relatively high real yields compared to regional peers and by confidence in the country's fiscal consolidation trajectory under Budget 2026. The ringgit's strength has been welcomed by importers but has prompted some exporters to hedge their foreign currency exposures more actively.

Analysts at investment banks said the near-term outlook for the ringgit remains broadly positive, though they cautioned that the currency could face headwinds if global risk sentiment deteriorates or if the US Federal Reserve signals a slower pace of rate cuts. BNM is expected to maintain its overnight policy rate at 3% through the rest of 2026, providing a stable interest rate differential that supports the currency's attractiveness to foreign investors.
