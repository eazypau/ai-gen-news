Malaysia's Healthcare Digitalisation Drive Accelerates with Budget 2026 Commitments and New Policy Framework

Malaysia's healthcare system is undergoing a sweeping digital transformation, driven by increased Budget 2026 allocations and a new comprehensive policy framework covering electronic health records, telemedicine, and the integration of AI-assisted diagnostics into public hospital workflows. The push marks the most ambitious government effort to modernise healthcare delivery since the establishment of the Ministry of Health's previous five-year plans, and is intended to address longstanding gaps in healthcare access and efficiency across the public system.

At the centre of the digitalisation initiative is the nationwide rollout of Malaysia's unified electronic health record (EHR) system, MySejahtera Health, which is being expanded from its pandemic-era roots into a comprehensive platform integrating patient records from government hospitals, community clinics, and — progressively — private healthcare providers. By unifying patient data across care settings, the system aims to eliminate redundant diagnostic tests, reduce medication errors, and enable more coordinated care for patients with complex or chronic conditions.

Telemedicine has grown substantially since its regulatory formalisation under the Medical Act amendments of 2024, with registered telemedicine platforms now serving over 2 million consultations per month across Malaysia. Budget 2026 has allocated additional funding to expand telemedicine coverage to underserved rural areas, particularly in Sabah and Sarawak where geographic distances make physical access to specialist care prohibitively difficult for large segments of the population.

AI-assisted diagnostic tools are being piloted at several major public hospitals, with applications ranging from AI-powered radiological image reading — helping radiologists identify early-stage cancers in CT and MRI scans — to predictive risk-scoring systems that flag high-risk patients for early intervention before acute health events occur. The Ministry of Health has committed to an evidence-based evaluation framework to assess the clinical impact of these pilots before wider deployment, recognising the need to validate AI tools rigorously in Malaysian clinical contexts rather than simply adopting tools developed for different patient demographics.

Health economists and public health researchers have praised the direction of Malaysia's digitalisation agenda but cautioned that technology alone cannot solve the underlying challenges of healthcare affordability and equity. They argue that continued investment in the human healthcare workforce — particularly specialist doctors and nurses in underserved states — is as critical as digital infrastructure, and that the government must guard against a scenario where sophisticated technology is deployed in well-resourced urban hospitals while rural and estate communities continue to face basic access deficits. The Ministry has acknowledged these concerns and said its digitalisation strategy explicitly prioritises equity of access as a core design principle.
