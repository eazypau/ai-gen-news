El Niño Conditions Forecast for June-July, Raising Extreme Rainfall and Heat Risks Across Malaysia

The ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre (ASMC) has issued an updated regional forecast predicting a transition to El Niño conditions during June-July 2026, carrying significant implications for Malaysia's weather patterns and disaster preparedness posture. The forecast indicates a slight increase in the likelihood of extreme rainfall across northern Peninsular Malaysia, while extreme heat is also expected to intensify, particularly in central and southern Peninsular regions.

El Niño events, characterised by warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean surface, tend to produce drier-than-normal conditions in western Malaysia during their peak phase — but can also generate intense, localised convective rainfall events that overwhelm urban drainage infrastructure, leading to severe flash floods. The pattern is consistent with Malaysia's weather history during previous El Niño cycles, when Kuala Lumpur and the Klang Valley have experienced both extended dry spells and sudden deluges within short time windows.

Authorities are on heightened alert following a major flash flood event that struck the Klang Valley on May 6, 2026, when a torrential downpour inundated key roads including Jalan Kuchai Lama, Jalan Klang Lama, and parts of Shah Alam, stranding vehicles and causing widespread traffic gridlock that lasted several hours. The incident prompted renewed calls for accelerated investment in Kuala Lumpur's stormwater management infrastructure, with the city's ageing drainage system repeatedly struggling to cope with extreme rainfall events.

The Department of Irrigation and Drainage (DID) and MetMalaysia are working with state governments to pre-position disaster response resources and ensure that early warning systems are operational ahead of the forecast high-risk period. The National Disaster Management Agency (NADMA) has urged Malaysians in flood-prone areas to monitor MetMalaysia advisories closely, clear home drainage systems, and prepare emergency kits in the event of rapid-onset flooding.

Malaysia's annual flood-related losses fell to RM636.9 million in 2025, down from higher levels in previous years, following targeted investments in flood mitigation projects in the most vulnerable states — Terengganu, Kelantan, and Johor. However, the potential combination of El Niño-driven convective rainfall on top of already saturated soils from the current wet season could produce conditions that test the resilience of even recently upgraded infrastructure. Climate scientists noted that the increasing frequency and intensity of extreme rainfall events in Malaysia is consistent with global climate change trends, and argued that adaptation investment needs to be treated as a permanent priority rather than a reactive response to individual disaster events.
