Glasgow 2026: Malaysia Faces Tougher Commonwealth Games as Four Gold-Medal Sports Dropped

Malaysia's Commonwealth Games team heads to Glasgow (July 23 – August 2) with a revised medal target and a new game plan, after four of the sports that delivered gold at Birmingham 2022 — badminton, squash, diving, and table tennis — have been removed from the programme.

The 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow, Scotland, will present Malaysia with its most challenging campaign in a decade, according to Sports Ministry officials. The slimmed-down 10-sport programme for Glasgow 2026 has dropped several disciplines in which Malaysia has traditionally excelled, including badminton, squash, diving, rhythmic gymnastics, and table tennis — collectively responsible for four of Malaysia's seven gold medals at the Birmingham 2022 Games.

The Glasgow programme features athletics, swimming, track cycling, lawn bowls, boxing, judo, and weightlifting, along with six para sports integrated into the competition schedule. Malaysia's realistic medal prospects now rest most heavily on weightlifting, where nine qualified athletes (six men, three women) provide a reasonable foundation for podium finishes, and on lawn bowls and the various para disciplines.

The national team's preparation has been structured around the ASIAKOM project, a specialised programme launched to prepare Malaysian athletes for both the 2026 Asian Games in Nagoya and the Glasgow Commonwealth Games. Covering 39 athletes across nine sports, the project provides tailored training camp scheduling, sports science support, and elevated competition exposure.

Chef de Mission Datuk Megat Zulkarnain, speaking ahead of the team's departure, said the target is to return with a respectable medal haul, while acknowledging that the absence of traditional Malaysian medal sports changes the calculus considerably. "We are not going to Glasgow to make up the numbers. But we are realistic — this is a different programme, and we must adapt," he said.

Para Malaysia athletes, who will compete in an integrated format alongside able-bodied competitors for the first time at a Commonwealth Games, have generated some of the strongest anticipation within the national team. Para athletics and para powerlifting athletes have posted personal bests in preparation and are viewed as genuine medal contenders. The Glasgow Games open on July 23 and close on August 2.
