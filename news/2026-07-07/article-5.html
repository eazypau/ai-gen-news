BN's Odds of a Landslide Win in Johor Rising, Analysts Say

Political analysts tracking the Johor state election say Barisan Nasional's chances of a landslide victory on July 11 are strengthening, as the opposition struggles to present a united front against the ruling coalition.

With four days to go before Johor votes on July 11, the mood in BN's campaign rooms is reportedly confident — and for good reason. Political analyst Ong Kian Ming, who has closely tracked Johor's electoral history, told Free Malaysia Today that BN's odds of securing a landslide are rising, pointing to the absence of a formal seat-sharing agreement between Pakatan Harapan and Perikatan Nasional as a critical factor that will split the opposition vote in many constituencies.

"In 2022, BN won 40 of 56 seats in a two-horse race. This time, with three-cornered and multi-cornered fights across most seats, the vote-splitting dynamic is even more favourable for BN," Ong said. He added that in constituencies with a significant Malay-majority electorate, PN's presence as a third force is likely to draw votes away from PH, effectively doubling BN's advantage.

However, PH leaders have pushed back against the narrative of certain defeat, pointing to urban and semi-urban constituencies in Johor Bahru, Kulai, and Segamat where the coalition has maintained a competitive presence. PH campaign officials say their ground operation has been among the best-resourced in recent memory, with significant youth voter engagement particularly in areas with large Chinese and Indian Malaysian communities.

The Johor election is being watched not only as a test of BN's strength in its traditional fortress but also as a signal of the political weather ahead of the more nationally significant Negeri Sembilan polls on August 1. A convincing BN win in Johor could embolden the coalition heading into August, while any unexpected upsets would be seized upon by the opposition as evidence of shifting public sentiment.

Voting begins at 8am on July 11 and closes at 5pm, with results expected by late evening. Voter turnout — forecast by most analysts between 55 and 65 percent — will be a key variable that could determine the scale of any BN victory.
