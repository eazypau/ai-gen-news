Malaysia Climbs to 2nd in Southeast Asia Startup Ecosystem Rankings for 2026

The Global Startup Ecosystem Index 2026 has ranked Malaysia 41st in the world and 2nd in Southeast Asia, behind Singapore but ahead of Indonesia — reflecting the country's growing maturity as a hub for tech entrepreneurship and venture activity.

Malaysia's startup ecosystem has earned a significant endorsement from the Global Startup Ecosystem Index 2026, which placed the country at 41st worldwide and second in Southeast Asia behind the perennial regional leader, Singapore. The ranking was published as part of the July 2026 edition of the Southeast Asia startup tracker, and represents a meaningful step forward for a market that has historically been overshadowed by its neighbours in the region.

The report highlights a notable broadening of Malaysia's startup landscape beyond simple consumer app development. The latest cohort of high-growth companies spans fintech, mobility solutions, AI-driven tooling, education technology (EdTech), health technology, e-commerce enablement, and deep technology — the latter encompassing semiconductor-adjacent software and advanced manufacturing process optimisation.

Touch 'n Go Digital, operator of Malaysia's dominant e-wallet platform, and Digital Nasional Berhad (DNB), the entity managing the country's single-wholesale-network 5G infrastructure, were both cited as exemplars of the ecosystem's institutional depth. Both companies have become critical digital infrastructure players rather than pure commercial startups, reflecting the maturation of Malaysia's technology sector.

Government-backed initiatives have also played a meaningful role. The SemiconStart Malaysia programme, allocated funding under Budget 2026, provides mentorship, prototyping access, and global funding connections for semiconductor-focused startups. This programme, combined with the broader digital economy investments under the MADANI framework, has helped attract venture capital and corporate investment into the ecosystem.

While Malaysia's ranking represents progress, the report notes challenges ahead. Talent retention remains a concern, with a significant number of Malaysian tech graduates continuing to be drawn to higher salaries in Singapore. The government has responded with targeted schemes to incentivise skilled Malaysians working abroad to return home, but observers say the results have been mixed thus far.
