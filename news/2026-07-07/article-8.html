World Cup 2026: Malaysian Fans Brace for Late Nights as 62% of Key Matches Kick Off After Midnight

With the FIFA World Cup 2026 hosted across the United States, Canada, and Mexico, Malaysian football enthusiasts face an extraordinary time-zone challenge — the majority of the tournament's most-anticipated group-stage fixtures begin between midnight and 5am Malaysia time.

Malaysia's football community has embraced the World Cup 2026 with characteristic passion, but the scheduling realities are testing even the most devoted fans. Because the tournament is hosted in North American cities, most matches kick off between midnight and noon Malaysia time (MYT), with roughly 62 percent of high-profile group-stage matches featuring major European and South American nations falling in the midnight-to-5am window.

England, France, and Germany — three of the world's most closely followed football nations in Malaysia — are each scheduled to play all three of their group-stage matches within that challenging time frame. "I've already told my boss I'll be working from home on match mornings," said one Kuala Lumpur-based England fan, echoing a sentiment shared by thousands of Malaysian supporters.

Mamak restaurants across the country are reporting bumper business as fans descend on the 24-hour establishments to watch matches on large-screen televisions. Some popular outlets in Kuala Lumpur, Penang, and Johor Bahru have put up special World Cup booking systems to manage the crowds. Annual leave applications at many Malaysian companies have spiked, with HR managers noting a surge in requests for the days following key fixtures.

For Malaysian broadcasters, the time difference has created logistical challenges as well. Malaysians are watching via RTM and Unifi TV after Astro failed to secure primary broadcasting rights to the tournament for the first time in two decades. The government's MyGOV Malaysia app also streams live matches via RTM's feed, requiring users to register with MyDigital ID for access.

The World Cup has generated a unique moment of national bonding, with Malaysians supporting adopted teams from the Socceroos to Argentina, Brazil, and Japan — the latter having a particularly enthusiastic following among younger Malaysian fans. Despite never qualifying for the World Cup themselves, Malaysians have long celebrated the tournament as a shared cultural event, and 2026 is proving no different.
