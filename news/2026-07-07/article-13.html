Johor and Penang Emerge as Malaysia's Twin Engines for Data Centre and Semiconductor Growth

Malaysia's two most investment-active states — Johor in the south and Penang in the north — have each carved out complementary roles in the country's tech economy, with Johor dominating data centre investment and Penang leading in semiconductor manufacturing.

A new investment landscape analysis published this week identifies Johor and Penang as the twin pillars of Malaysia's technology economy, drawing in billions of ringgit annually from global hyperscalers, semiconductor equipment makers, and electronics manufacturers. Together, the two states account for the vast majority of Malaysia's foreign direct investment in the technology sector.

Johor's rise as a data centre hub is directly linked to its proximity to Singapore, which faces acute land constraints that make large-scale data centre development increasingly difficult and expensive. Hyperscalers including Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, and local telco-affiliated cloud providers have established or are building significant facilities in Johor's digital industrial parks, effectively treating the state as a natural extension of Singapore's data centre ecosystem. Analysts estimate that Johor now hosts more planned data centre capacity than any other Southeast Asian location outside Singapore itself.

Penang, dubbed by some as Malaysia's Silicon Valley, continues to attract semiconductor and high-value electronics manufacturers supported by its mature supply chain, skilled engineering talent pool, and decades of accumulated institutional knowledge in the industry. The China+1 trend — where multinational corporations seek manufacturing bases outside mainland China — has brought fresh waves of investment from the world's largest semiconductor equipment and component companies.

The Malaysia Data Center Market was valued at USD 6.14 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 11.4 billion by 2031, representing a compound annual growth rate of approximately 10.8 percent. This growth is underpinned by demand for AI compute infrastructure, enterprise cloud migration, and Malaysia's positioning as a neutral digital hub in a geopolitically sensitive region.

YTL Power International and Nvidia Corporation earlier this year signed an agreement to develop USD 2.36 billion of AI infrastructure in Malaysia, underlining the scale of ambition that major global players are bringing to the country. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim has described these investments as transformative for Malaysia's digital economy, while also urging that local companies and talent benefit from the ecosystem rather than simply serving as a passive host for foreign capital.
