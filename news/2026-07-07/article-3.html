PM Anwar: Malaysia Needs Sovereign Cloud to Safeguard Critical Data from Big Tech

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim has called for the urgent establishment of a Malaysian sovereign cloud, warning that over-reliance on US-based tech giants poses real risks to national data security in an era of heightened geopolitical tensions.

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim used a digital economy forum this week to reiterate his government's commitment to developing a sovereign AI cloud infrastructure — a dedicated, government-controlled computing environment that keeps Malaysian national security data and citizens' personal information within the country's legal jurisdiction. The RM2 billion sovereign cloud initiative, announced under Budget 2026, is being implemented by the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC).

Anwar pointed specifically to the US Cloud Act as a key concern. "The Cloud Act allows US authorities to compel American technology companies to hand over data stored on their servers, regardless of where in the world that data physically resides," the Prime Minister explained. "This is not a hypothetical threat — it is a legal reality that every government in Southeast Asia must now grapple with."

Despite the push for data sovereignty, Anwar was careful to frame the sovereign cloud not as an anti-Big Tech move but rather as a complementary layer of national infrastructure. He stressed that Malaysia remains fully open to investments from major hyperscalers including Microsoft, Google, Amazon Web Services, and Alibaba Cloud — all of which have existing or planned data centre operations in the country.

The MCMC is also establishing an AI Transformation Centre in partnership with Multimedia University (MMU) and a Centre of Excellence in Ethics for Emerging Technologies. This component of the initiative is described as a culturally sensitive research and development hub that will ensure AI systems deployed in Malaysia reflect local values, laws, and societal norms.

Malaysia's push for a sovereign cloud comes as South-east Asia becomes an increasingly important battleground for global digital infrastructure, with data centre investments in the region running into tens of billions of US dollars annually. The initiative signals that Putrajaya intends for Malaysia to be not just a host for foreign cloud infrastructure, but an active participant in building and controlling its own digital future.
