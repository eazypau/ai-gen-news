Malaysia's Fuel Subsidy Bill on Track to Hit RM40 Billion — Double Budget Estimate

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim has warned that Malaysia's total fuel subsidy expenditure in 2026 could reach nearly RM40 billion, vastly exceeding the RM15 billion originally allocated under Budget 2026.

Speaking at a policy forum in Kuala Lumpur, Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim said the government is on course to spend close to RM40 billion on fuel subsidies this year — a figure that is more than double what was projected when Budget 2026 was tabled. The unexpected surge is being driven by elevated global energy prices and higher-than-anticipated domestic fuel consumption following the implementation of BUDI95, the targeted subsidy mechanism for RON95 petrol.

Under BUDI95, Malaysian citizens pay a heavily subsidised RM1.99 per litre for RON95, verified by MyKad at the pump and capped at 200 litres per month. Non-citizens and high-income earners (T20 group) are charged the market rate of RM3.37 per litre. While the programme has successfully shielded lower and middle-income households from rising energy costs, the fiscal burden on the government has grown considerably.

The July 1 implementation of Budi Diesel — a parallel programme capping subsidised diesel at RM2.10 per litre for eligible private vehicle owners using MyKad verification — has added further strain to the subsidy bill. Around 700,000 private diesel vehicle owners now share a monthly fuel quota of up to 200 litres between diesel and RON95 under the linked programme.

Finance Ministry officials indicated that discussions are underway to review the subsidy architecture, with potential adjustments to the T20 threshold and monthly quota limits being considered for the second half of the year. However, Anwar stressed that any changes would be gradual and carefully calibrated to avoid putting undue pressure on households already dealing with cost-of-living challenges.

Malaysia's fuel subsidy commitment reflects a broader regional trend in Southeast Asia, where governments have struggled to balance fiscal prudence with the social imperative of keeping energy affordable for ordinary citizens. For now, Putrajaya has signalled it will maintain the current subsidy structure through at least the end of 2026.
