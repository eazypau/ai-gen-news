Malaysia Launches SemiconStart Initiative to Boost Semiconductor Startup Ecosystem

The SemiconStart Malaysia initiative, earmarked under Budget 2026, is now actively supporting semiconductor startups with mentorship, prototyping resources, and connections to global funding networks — as Malaysia moves to capitalise on surging chip demand driven by AI and data centres.

Malaysia's ambition to become a major player in the global semiconductor ecosystem took a concrete step forward with the active rollout of SemiconStart Malaysia, an initiative that was identified as a strategic priority in Budget 2026 and has now begun accepting and supporting its first cohort of semiconductor-focused startup companies. The programme provides a comprehensive package of support: industry mentorship from established chip design and manufacturing firms, access to prototyping tools and facilities, pathways to commercialisation, and introductions to venture capital and international investor networks.

Malaysia has long been a significant participant in the global semiconductor supply chain, particularly in back-end processes such as chip assembly, testing, and packaging. However, SemiconStart aims to move the country upstream — nurturing local companies capable of chip design, embedded software development, and semiconductor-adjacent deep tech that can capture higher margins in the global value chain.

The timing is strategic. Malaysia's semiconductor sector is benefiting from the global China+1 supply chain diversification trend, as multinational companies seek to reduce their dependence on China for critical electronic components. This has brought significant foreign direct investment into Penang, Kuala Lumpur, and Johor, and SemiconStart is designed to ensure Malaysian companies — not just foreign investors — benefit from the opportunity.

Industry analysts from Hong Leong Investment Bank (HLIB) noted in a May 2026 report that rising demand for optical and power semiconductors tied to AI-driven data centre expansion represents a structural growth driver for the sector. The report estimated that Malaysia's semiconductor exports would reach record levels in 2026, providing a favourable backdrop for new domestic entrants supported by SemiconStart.

The initiative is overseen by the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA) in collaboration with the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI). Officials said the first SemiconStart cohort of 15 companies began their structured support programme in June, with applications for the second cohort opening in September 2026.
