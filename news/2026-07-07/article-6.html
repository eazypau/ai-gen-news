BUDI95 and New Diesel Subsidy Programme Now Fully Active Nationwide

Malaysia's twin targeted fuel subsidy programmes — BUDI95 for RON95 petrol and Budi Diesel for private diesel vehicles — are now fully operational across the country, offering subsidised fuel to eligible Malaysians via MyKad verification at the pump.

As of the first week of July 2026, both the BUDI95 and Budi Diesel programmes are running smoothly at petrol stations nationwide, according to the Ministry of Finance. Under BUDI95 (officially the BUDI MADANI programme), Malaysian citizens pay RM1.99 per litre for RON95, while non-citizens and those in the T20 (top 20%) income bracket face the market-linked unsubsidised price, currently set at RM3.37 per litre — a differential of RM1.38 per litre.

The Budi Diesel programme, which came into effect on July 1, caps subsidised diesel at RM2.10 per litre for private vehicle owners who register their vehicles with MyKad. Private diesel prices without subsidy stand at RM3.97 per litre in Peninsular Malaysia. Around 700,000 private diesel vehicle owners are eligible for the programme, with a shared monthly quota of 200 litres covering both diesel and RON95 purchases.

Both programmes represent a significant shift from Malaysia's previous universal fuel subsidy system, which made no distinction between income groups. The targeted approach is designed to redirect savings from removing subsidies for high-income earners towards broader social programmes under the Madani government's cost-of-living agenda.

For the week of July 1–7, RON97 remains at RM4.00 per litre, reflecting a 10-sen drop from the previous week amid easing global crude oil prices. Diesel for commercial and industrial users is pegged at RM3.97, down 10 sen from the prior week.

The Finance Ministry has noted that the transition has been largely smooth, with MyKad readers at most major petrol stations functioning correctly. However, minor technical glitches at a small number of rural stations were reported in the first days of July, with PETRONAS and Shell Malaysia teams dispatched to resolve the issues. Affected customers were advised to use alternative nearby stations until systems were fully operational.
