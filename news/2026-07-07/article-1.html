Johor Early Voting Begins as State Election Campaign Reaches Final Stretch

Early voters across Johor's 56 state constituencies cast their ballots today, four days ahead of the July 11 state election — the first major electoral test of mid-2026.

Today marks early voting day for the 2026 Johor State Election, with postal and advance voters — primarily members of the military, police, and election workers — heading to polling stations across the southern state. The Election Commission confirmed 172 candidates are contesting all 56 seats in the Johor State Legislative Assembly, setting the stage for what analysts describe as a decisive but potentially one-sided contest.

Barisan Nasional, led by incumbent Menteri Besar Onn Hafiz Ghazi, enters the election as the firm favourite after its commanding two-thirds supermajority win in the 2022 state polls. BN secured 40 out of 56 seats three years ago, and analysts say conditions are once again favourable, with the opposition fragmented between Pakatan Harapan and Perikatan Nasional running largely independent campaigns rather than a unified front.

The Johor-Singapore causeway and its chronic congestion have emerged as a key campaign issue, with voters in border areas calling for concrete commitments from candidates on easing the notorious bottlenecks. The daily commute for thousands of Malaysian workers who cross into Singapore for employment has been a long-standing frustration, and all three major coalitions have pledged to address the infrastructure challenge.

As of the updated electoral roll on May 31, 2026, there are 2,727,926 registered voters in Johor. A Pakatan Harapan candidate who requested anonymity predicted a voter turnout of around 60 percent — a figure that, if accurate, would represent solid civic participation but potentially benefit BN given its broader grassroots mobilisation machinery across the state's rural and semi-urban constituencies.

Election Day is July 11, with results expected by late evening. Political observers will be watching closely to see if BN can consolidate its dominance ahead of the more strategically significant Negeri Sembilan polls scheduled for August 1.
