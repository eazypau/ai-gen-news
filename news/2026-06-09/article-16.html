Southeast Asia Eyes USD21 Billion Muslim Sports Tourism Market by 2030

Sports Travel and Tour World 9 June 2026

Malaysia, Indonesia, and Singapore are positioning themselves as the leading trio of destinations for a rapidly expanding segment of global travel: Muslim sports tourism. According to a new analysis by the Global Islamic Economy Gateway, spending linked to Muslim travellers attending sporting events could climb to USD21 billion worldwide by 2030, provided that destinations and event organisers improve the quality and visibility of faith-friendly services.

Malaysia is particularly well-placed to capture a substantial share of this market, given its existing infrastructure for Muslim-friendly travel, including certified halal food options at sports venues, prayer rooms and facilities in stadiums and arenas, and a regulatory environment that is familiar to Muslim visitors from across the ASEAN region, South Asia, and the Middle East. The country's growing calendar of international sporting events — including Formula E races, international badminton tournaments, MotoGP at Sepang, and football friendly matches — provides ample opportunities to attract Muslim sports fans.

Industry experts say the key to unlocking this market is visibility and reliability. Muslim sports tourists want to know in advance that their needs will be met — from halal food certification at concession stands to prayer schedule information in event programs. Many venues in Southeast Asia already provide these amenities but fail to market them effectively to Muslim-majority source markets in the Gulf, South Asia, and North Africa.

Tourism Malaysia has launched targeted campaigns in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Indonesia highlighting the country's credentials as a Muslim-friendly sports destination. New partnerships with tour operators specialising in Islamic travel packages are being developed to offer bundled sports event tickets, accommodation, and halal dining itineraries for group travellers.

The potential economic multiplier from Muslim sports tourism goes beyond ticket sales, with visitors spending on accommodation, halal restaurants, shopping, and cultural experiences. Analysts say cities that invest in comprehensive halal hospitality ecosystems around major sports venues stand to gain a disproportionate share of this growing global travel segment.
