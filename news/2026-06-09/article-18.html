Malaysia Begins Enforcing Social Media Ban for Children Under 16

Lifestyle NBC News / Bernama 9 June 2026

From 1 June 2026, Malaysia began enforcing regulations that bar children younger than 16 from creating or maintaining social media accounts, joining a growing international movement to strengthen online safety protections for minors. The enforcement places legal obligations on social media platforms operating in Malaysia to implement robust age-verification mechanisms and to proactively identify and remove accounts belonging to underage users.

The Communications and Digital Ministry has given platforms a transition period to implement compliant age-verification systems, but has warned that companies found to be in breach of the new rules face significant financial penalties under the amended Communications and Multimedia Act. Platforms that consistently fail to comply could face more severe sanctions, including restrictions on their operation in the Malaysian market.

The policy mirrors similar measures adopted in Australia, which enacted a pioneering under-16 social media ban in late 2024, and the United Kingdom, which has tightened its Children's Code under the Online Safety Act. Malaysia's approach has been welcomed by child safety advocates and parents' groups, who have long argued that social media platforms are exposing young children to harmful content, cyberbullying, predatory behaviour, and addictive engagement mechanisms.

Critics of the ban include digital rights groups that argue age verification requirements often fail in practice, can compromise the privacy of all users through invasive data collection, and may push underage users to less regulated platforms where they are even less protected. They have called for a complementary strategy that includes digital literacy education and platform transparency requirements rather than relying solely on access restrictions.

The government has signalled that the social media ban is part of a broader digital safety agenda that will also include regulations on harmful online content, algorithmic transparency requirements for major platforms, and strengthened parental controls. Officials say the enforcement approach will be phased, beginning with warnings and compliance notices before escalating to financial penalties for persistent non-compliance.
