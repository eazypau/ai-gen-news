Malaysia's Semiconductor Talent Gap Threatens to Undermine Record Chip Investments

Technology TechWire Asia 9 June 2026

A significant mismatch between the supply of qualified semiconductor engineers and the voracious demand of Malaysia's booming chip industry is emerging as the single biggest structural risk to the country's technology ambitions. Industry observers at SEMICON SEA 2026 warned of a tenfold gap between the number of engineers graduating with relevant qualifications each year and the number the industry needs to sustain its growth trajectory.

Malaysia secured RM28.5 billion in electronics and electrical (E&E) sector approvals last year, with companies like Infineon planning major wafer fabrication expansions at Kulim Hi-Tech Park and Intel continuing to operate one of its largest assembly and test facilities globally in Penang. While these investments are a testament to Malaysia's established reputation in semiconductor back-end operations, moving up the value chain to front-end wafer fabrication and IC design requires a fundamentally different — and much scarcer — skill set.

The challenge has been further complicated by new Employment Pass salary thresholds effective from 1 June 2026, which have doubled the minimum monthly salary for Category I work visa holders from RM10,000 to RM20,000. While the government intends the new thresholds to attract elite global talent, industry groups warn the sharp increase makes hiring foreign engineers to bridge the talent gap prohibitively expensive for many companies.

Universities and polytechnics are being urged to expand their electrical engineering and semiconductor-related programmes, and the government has initiated a matching grant scheme to encourage industry-academia partnerships for curriculum development. However, educators note that building a pipeline of qualified semiconductor engineers takes at least four to six years from policy intervention to graduation, meaning the gap will persist through the near term regardless of what action is taken today.

Malaysia's Semiconductor Industry Association (MSIA) has called for an emergency talent strategy, including government-sponsored scholarships for semiconductor engineering degrees, fast-track recognition of overseas qualifications, and a specific talent mobility framework that distinguishes high-skills tech workers from general labour migration for visa purposes.
