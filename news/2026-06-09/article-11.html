Digital Minister: Malaysia Must Build Digital Sovereignty, Not Just Host Global Tech Giants

Technology BusinessToday Malaysia 9 June 2026

Malaysia's Digital Minister issued a pointed challenge to the nation's tech industry, warning that successfully attracting data centre investments from hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, and Amazon is necessary but not sufficient for genuine digital development. True digital sovereignty, the minister argued, requires Malaysia to build its own capabilities in AI model development, cloud platform engineering, and digital infrastructure ownership — not merely provide land, power, and fibre to global corporations.

The government has allocated RM1.36 billion to the Ministry of Digital in Budget 2026, including RM1.11 billion for development projects aimed at accelerating the creation of homegrown digital capabilities. A key priority is the establishment of a sovereign AI cloud — backed by a RM2 billion allocation — that would enable Malaysian government agencies, universities, and companies to develop and deploy AI models without dependence on foreign platforms.

The minister acknowledged that Malaysia has made impressive progress as an investment destination, with over RM30.95 billion in digital investments recorded under the Malaysia Digital initiative in recent years. But she stressed that owning and controlling digital infrastructure — including data, algorithms, and computing resources — is critical for national security, economic resilience, and long-term technological competitiveness.

Critics within the tech community have welcomed the ambition but cautioned that building indigenous digital capabilities requires patient, long-term investment in education, research, and startup ecosystems. They point to examples like South Korea and Taiwan, where decades of deliberate state investment in technical education and industry support were prerequisites for the emergence of genuinely world-class technology companies.

The ministry's digital sovereignty agenda also includes strengthening data localisation requirements for critical sectors, expanding the domestic talent pool through targeted scholarships and industry attachments, and creating procurement preferences for locally developed software and platforms in public sector contracts.
