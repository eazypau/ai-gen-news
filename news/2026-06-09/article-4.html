Kedah Stands Firm on Banning Number-Betting Outlet Licences Despite Court Loss

Politics New Straits Times 9 June 2026

Kedah Menteri Besar Datuk Seri Muhammad Sanusi Md Nor has reaffirmed that the PAS-led state government will not reverse its policy of refusing to issue or renew licences for number-betting outlets, despite losing a legal challenge at the Court of Appeal. The ruling was seen as a setback for the state government's moral economy agenda, but Sanusi made clear it would not alter the administration's stance.

The Kedah government's decision to stop issuing betting licences drew legal action from affected operators, who argued the state's refusal to renew their federal-issued licences was unlawful. The Court of Appeal agreed, finding procedural grounds to uphold the operators' challenge, but Sanusi argued the state retains discretionary powers over local licensing matters under the relevant legislation.

Critics, including federal government representatives and civil liberties groups, have pointed to the ruling as evidence that state governments cannot unilaterally impose religious or moral prohibitions on lawfully licensed businesses operating under federal frameworks. The tension highlights the ongoing friction between PAS-governed states and the federal government over jurisdiction and the boundaries of state executive power.

Sanusi, a vocal advocate for an Islamic economic model in Kedah, has repeatedly clashed with the federal government over similar issues, including the management of water resources and entertainment licences. His administration frames the betting ban as a matter of upholding Kedah's identity and values, a position that resonates strongly with the state's conservative voter base.

Legal experts say the saga is likely to continue through the court system, potentially reaching the Federal Court, as the broader constitutional question of state discretion over federal licensees remains unresolved.
