Government to Finalise Fuel Subsidy Review Targeting High-Income Groups

Economy Free Malaysia Today / The Star 9 June 2026

Putrajaya is closing in on a decision to remove RON95 petrol subsidies for Malaysia's wealthiest households, with a comprehensive study now underway to determine the appropriate income cut-off point — whether it falls at the top 5%, 10%, 15%, or 20% of earners (T5 to T20). The government has agreed in principle that high-income households should no longer benefit from the blanket fuel subsidy, but ministers stress they do not want the measure to inadvertently burden upper-middle income groups.

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim confirmed in May 2026 that a fuel subsidy revamp is coming, framing it as a necessary step toward fiscal sustainability and social equity. Currently, RON95 petrol is priced at RM1.99 per litre for all Malaysians with a valid driving licence, regardless of income level, under the BUDI95 programme. The cost to the federal government of maintaining this blanket subsidy runs into the billions of ringgit annually.

Economist Ahmed Razman Abdul Latiff of Putra Business School estimated that removing subsidies for the wealthiest Malaysians could save the government up to RM1.5 billion per month. These savings could then be redirected as targeted cash assistance to lower and middle-income groups, helping them manage the persistent cost-of-living pressures that remain a key concern for the majority of Malaysians.

A Merdeka Center survey found that 64% of Malaysians remain dissatisfied with the government's efforts to address rising living costs, making subsidy rationalisation a politically sensitive undertaking. The government is also wary of the timing, given the upcoming state elections in Johor and Negeri Sembilan, where a poorly received announcement could damage the ruling coalition's electoral prospects.

Meanwhile, in East Malaysia, subsidised diesel continues to be sold at RM2.15 per litre, compared to RM5.17 in Peninsular Malaysia, a regional disparity that forms part of the broader ongoing debate about equitable resource distribution across the federation.
