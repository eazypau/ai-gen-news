Ringgit Hits Multi-Year High as Malaysia's Economy Rides AI Investment Wave

Economy NST / Bloomberg 9 June 2026

The Malaysian ringgit has strengthened to approximately RM3.97 against the US dollar, its highest level since June 2018, as confidence in Malaysia's economic fundamentals continues to build. Analysts attribute the currency's rally to a combination of strong trade performance, booming foreign direct investment in data centres and semiconductors, and a general rotation of capital into Southeast Asian markets.

Malaysia's economy expanded by 4.9% in 2025, following 5.1% growth the previous year, with unemployment falling to a decade-low of 2.9%. HSBC ASEAN economist Yun Liu has projected further expansion of 4.6% in 2026, driven largely by the country's growing role in the global AI supply chain. Trade surpassed a record RM3 trillion last year, reflecting strong demand for Malaysia's electronics, chemicals, and palm oil exports.

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim welcomed the ringgit's strengthening, noting it reflects rising international confidence in Malaysia as an investment destination. He cautioned, however, that a stronger currency requires careful monitoring of its effects on export competitiveness, particularly for manufacturers and agricultural exporters who rely on competitive exchange rates to keep their products affordable in global markets.

The data centre and cloud computing boom has been a key catalyst, with tech giants including Microsoft, Google, and Amazon Web Services committing billions of dollars to Malaysian infrastructure. The first half of 2025 alone saw RM13.29 billion in approved AI-related investments, cementing Malaysia's status as the preferred destination for data centre construction in Southeast Asia.

Market watchers say the ringgit could push below RM4.00 on a sustained basis if global risk appetite remains positive and Malaysia continues to attract high-quality FDI. However, geopolitical risks — including the ongoing Middle East conflict and trade tensions between the US and China — remain as potential headwinds that could reverse the currency's gains.
