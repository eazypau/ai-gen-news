Malaysia Evaluates Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Naval Strike Missile Licence

Defence New Straits Times 9 June 2026

Malaysia's Royal Malaysian Navy (RMN) and Ministry of Defence are actively evaluating alternative anti-ship missile suppliers after Norway revoked the export licence for the Naval Strike Missile (NSM) system, citing concerns over its potential end-use in the context of the ongoing Middle East conflict. The NSM, developed by Norwegian defence company Kongsberg, had been under consideration as part of Malaysia's naval modernisation programme.

Norway's decision is part of a broader tightening of Western arms export controls following international pressure to prevent sophisticated weapons technology from potentially reaching conflict zones or being transferred to third parties. While Malaysia has not been directly implicated in any arms diversion concerns, the blanket licence revocation reflects the risk-averse posture adopted by several European defence exporters in the current geopolitical climate.

Defence ministry officials have stated that Malaysia's naval procurement plans will not be derailed by the licence revocation, and that the country has been engaging with multiple alternative suppliers including those from France, Sweden, South Korea, and India. Each alternative comes with its own set of technical specifications, pricing structures, offset requirements, and technology transfer arrangements that must be evaluated against Malaysia's specific operational requirements.

The incident highlights the vulnerabilities that can arise when defence procurement decisions rely on single-source suppliers from countries whose export policies may shift unpredictably in response to geopolitical events. Malaysia's defence planners are said to be reviewing the broader naval procurement programme with an eye toward diversifying supplier relationships and building in greater resilience against future export control disruptions.

The RMN is in the midst of a multi-year force modernisation programme that includes new patrol vessels, submarine capability enhancements, and the upgrading of its electronic warfare systems. Securing a reliable anti-ship missile capability is considered a high priority for Malaysia's maritime defence posture, given the country's extensive coastline, strategic shipping lanes, and overlapping territorial claims in the South China Sea.
