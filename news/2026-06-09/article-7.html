RON95 Subsidy Capped at 200 Litres Monthly Under BUDI95 Programme

Economy MalaysiaNow / DSF.my 9 June 2026

Malaysia's subsidised RON95 petrol under the BUDI95 scheme remains at RM1.99 per litre but is now capped at 200 litres per month per licensed driver, a temporary measure introduced to manage demand during ongoing global supply disruptions linked to the Middle East conflict. The cap took effect on 1 April 2026 and the government has pledged to review it once supply conditions normalise.

Prior to the cap, all Malaysians with a valid driving licence were entitled to unlimited subsidised RON95 fuel at the RM1.99 per litre rate. The government introduced the cap after concerns arose that commercial operators and speculators were purchasing large volumes of subsidised fuel for resale or industrial use, distorting the retail market and placing undue strain on the subsidy bill.

Drivers who exceed the 200-litre monthly threshold will pay the unsubsidised market rate for additional fuel. The government says the 200-litre cap is designed to cover the reasonable monthly fuel needs of a private vehicle owner, while ensuring the subsidy does not disproportionately benefit high-mileage commercial users who can afford market rates.

Industry groups representing taxi drivers, e-hailing operators, and small logistics companies have raised concerns that the 200-litre cap is insufficient for drivers who rely on their vehicles for their livelihoods and may drive upward of 3,000 to 4,000 kilometres per month. The government has indicated it is studying sector-specific exemption schemes for commercial vehicle operators most affected by the cap.

Consumer associations have generally welcomed the cap as a step toward more targeted subsidy delivery, arguing that the current system disproportionately benefits higher-income Malaysians who drive larger vehicles and consume more fuel. Further reforms are expected in the second half of 2026 as the broader subsidy rationalisation framework takes shape.
