New Employment Pass Salary Thresholds Take Effect June 2026, Doubling Category I Minimum

Business TechWire Asia / Mean CEO 9 June 2026

Malaysia's Ministry of Home Affairs implemented significantly revised Employment Pass (EP) salary thresholds effective 1 June 2026, in the most sweeping overhaul of the foreign talent work permit framework in over a decade. The changes include a doubling of the Category I minimum monthly salary from RM10,000 to RM20,000, while Category III has been moved from the RM3,000–RM4,999 band to RM5,000–RM9,999.

The government's stated rationale is to ensure that employment passes are granted only to genuinely high-skilled foreign workers who add value to Malaysia's economy and do not compete with locals for mid-tier positions. By raising the salary floor, Putrajaya aims to shift the country's foreign talent intake toward the upper end of the skills and knowledge spectrum — executives, specialised engineers, researchers, and senior professionals.

The changes have generated mixed reactions from the business community. Multinational corporations and large technology companies have broadly welcomed the new thresholds as a signal that Malaysia is serious about becoming a high-value investment destination. However, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and mid-size tech firms argue that the sharp increase in Category I requirements makes it extremely difficult to bring in the mid-level foreign specialists they need at a price point that makes business sense.

The semiconductor industry is among the sectors most concerned, given the ongoing talent shortage in chip engineering. Companies had been relying on foreign engineers to fill critical gaps while domestic talent pipelines are developed, and the new salary requirements could slow that strategy significantly. Industry lobby groups are in dialogue with the Ministry of Home Affairs to explore whether sector-specific carve-outs or a structured transition period can be established.

Human resource consultancies have reported a surge in enquiries from companies seeking to understand how the new thresholds affect their existing foreign worker populations and future hiring plans. Employees already on Employment Passes are understood to have their pass validity unaffected until renewal, at which point the new salary requirements will apply.
