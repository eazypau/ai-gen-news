Negeri Sembilan Calls Snap Election After Royal and Coalition Crisis

Politics SCMP / The Online Citizen 9 June 2026

Negeri Sembilan's state legislative assembly was dissolved on 5 June 2026, triggering a snap election that must be held by 4 August 2026. The dissolution followed a political crisis that had been brewing since April when 14 Umno assemblymen withdrew their support for Pakatan Harapan Chief Minister Aminuddin Harun, claiming he had mishandled a dispute involving the state's unique royal institution.

The crisis is rooted in Negeri Sembilan's singular constitutional arrangement, where the state is governed by a Yang di-Pertuan Besar elected by four territorial chiefs known as the Undangs, rather than through direct hereditary succession. In April 2026, the four Undangs allegedly sought to remove the incumbent ruler Tuanku Muhriz, seeking to replace him with Tunku Nadzaruddin Tuanku Ja'afar, the son of a previous ruler — a move that sent shockwaves through the state's political establishment.

Chief Minister Aminuddin Harun revealed that Pakatan Harapan (PH) intends to contest all 36 seats in the state assembly and has already finalised seat allocations among coalition partners. The contest is expected to be fiercely fought, with both BN-aligned forces and Perikatan Nasional eyeing opportunities to exploit the coalition fractures that precipitated the crisis.

Bloomberg and the South China Morning Post have both described the twin state elections — in Johor and Negeri Sembilan — as the most significant test of Anwar Ibrahim's federal government since taking power. A clean sweep for PH-aligned forces would cement Anwar's authority, while setbacks could reinvigorate calls within his own coalition for a reshuffle of cabinet and strategy.

International observers note that Negeri Sembilan's election comes at a sensitive moment for Malaysia's constitutional monarchy, with the controversy over the Undangs' attempted removal of a sitting ruler raising questions about the limits of traditional authority in a modern democratic state.
