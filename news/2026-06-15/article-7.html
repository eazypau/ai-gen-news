Malaysia's Under-16 Social Media Ban: Two Weeks After Launch, Compliance Reviewed

Technology The Silicon Review / Malay Mail 15 June 2026

Malaysia's landmark social media ban for users under 16 marks its two-week anniversary today, having taken effect on June 1, 2026 under the Online Safety Act 2025. The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) is now conducting its first formal compliance review of platforms operating in the country.

Under the law, social media platforms with at least 8 million Malaysian users — including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube — must implement robust age-verification systems and block users under 16 from creating new accounts. Existing accounts of minors must be removed within 30 days of identification, and platforms that fail to comply face fines of up to RM1 million.

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil has described the law as among the strictest of its kind globally, noting that Malaysia's version contains no parental consent exemption — a provision absent in similar laws in Australia and the United Kingdom. The law targets platforms, not families: parents are not penalised if their children manage to circumvent the system.

Early feedback from parents has been mixed. Some welcome the protection for children from cyberbullying, sexual exploitation, and inappropriate content. Others have raised concerns about practical enforcement, particularly around age-verification methods that may compromise user data privacy. Civil society groups and digital rights organisations continue to monitor the implementation closely.

MCMC has not yet released detailed compliance data but is expected to publish a two-week status report later this week. Platforms that have been proactive in implementing age-gate features say the initial rollout has gone smoothly, though edge cases involving children with falsified age data remain a technical challenge.
