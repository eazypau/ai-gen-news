Indonesian Patients Driving Malaysia's Medical Tourism Boom in 2026

Health New Straits Times 15 June 2026

Malaysia continues to be the preferred destination for Indonesian patients seeking specialised medical treatment, a trend prominently highlighted at the Malaysia Fair 2026 held in South Jakarta from June 5. The fair, which showcased Malaysia's healthcare, tourism and education offerings, drew large crowds of Indonesians interested in accessing Malaysian medical expertise.

Malaysia Healthcare brand ambassador Datuk Seri Siti Nurhaliza delivered the opening address, emphasising the psychological aspect of medical travel — including the comfort of receiving treatment in a culturally familiar, Malay-speaking environment — as a key reason for the strong preference among Indonesian patients.

Malaysian private hospitals, particularly those in Penang, Kuala Lumpur, and Johor Bahru, have reported high occupancy rates among Indonesian patients throughout the first half of 2026. Oncology, cardiac care, orthopaedics, and fertility treatments are among the most sought-after specialisations. The geographic proximity and language compatibility with Indonesia give Malaysia a natural advantage over competitors like Singapore and Thailand in this segment.

Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC) has set an ambitious target for 2026 medical tourism, aiming to attract over 1.5 million international patients and generate RM3 billion in healthcare revenue. Indonesia is expected to account for the largest share of this patient pool, followed by Bangladesh, the United Kingdom, and the Middle East.

To capitalise on the opportunity, Malaysian hospitals are investing in expanded international patient services, including dedicated Indonesian-language coordinators, prayer facilities, and halal-certified dietary options. Airlines have also increased direct flights between major Indonesian cities and key Malaysian healthcare hubs to ease patient travel.
