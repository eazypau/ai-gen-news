Malaysia Expanding Digital Hospital Information System to 16 More Facilities

Health Healthcare IT News 15 June 2026

Malaysia's Ministry of Health is pressing ahead with a major digital health transformation in 2026, expanding its Total Hospital Information System (THIS) to 16 additional government hospitals across the country. Health Minister Dr Dzulkefly Ahmad has described 2026 as the critical implementation year for the nation's sweeping health system digitisation agenda.

The expansion of THIS is one of three simultaneous digital programmes underway. In parallel, a cloud-based Clinic Management System (CMS) is being rolled out to approximately 2,489 primary care settings, and a Dental Information System is being deployed to 157 dental clinics nationwide. Together, these initiatives will create a more integrated and data-driven national health infrastructure.

The digital expansion aims to reduce paperwork burdens on frontline healthcare workers, improve patient record accuracy, enable real-time clinical decision support, and allow the Health Ministry to monitor service delivery trends at a national level. Interoperability between hospital and primary care systems is also a stated objective, allowing seamless referral and record transfer across facilities.

Malaysia's health digitisation agenda aligns with the 13th Malaysia Plan (RMK13) and the Madani government's broader push for a digital economy. International health IT benchmarks suggest that countries with integrated health information systems experience improved patient outcomes, reduced administrative costs, and better pandemic preparedness.

The Ministry has acknowledged that implementation challenges exist, including training of healthcare staff, ensuring cybersecurity of patient data, and managing the transition from legacy paper-based systems. A dedicated Change Management Unit has been established to oversee rollout and address teething issues. The full nationwide deployment is targeted for completion by end-2027.
