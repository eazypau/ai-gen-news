Lim Guan Eng Corruption Trial to Proceed After High Court Rejects Bid to Quash Charges

Bernama 3 July 2026 Crime

The Penang High Court has dismissed the application by former Penang Chief Minister Lim Guan Eng, his wife Betty Chew Gek Cheng, and businesswoman Phang Li Koon to quash corruption and money laundering charges linked to a foreign workers' hostel project valued at RM11.6 million. The ruling clears the way for the long-delayed trial to proceed, with case management scheduled for July 3 at the Penang High Court.

The charges against Lim Guan Eng allege that he sought and obtained a gratification from Phang Li Koon, who was awarded a contract to manage a foreign workers' hostel project under the Penang State Development Corporation. His wife and Phang face related charges of abetting the offence and money laundering respectively. All three have consistently maintained their innocence and denied all wrongdoing.

The defence had argued that the charges were defective and that the prosecution had failed to establish a prima facie case sufficient to warrant a full trial. The High Court judge rejected those arguments, finding that the prosecution had presented adequate evidence to justify proceeding to a full hearing where witnesses will be called and cross-examined.

Lim Guan Eng's legal team indicated they would study the written grounds of the court's decision before deciding on any further interlocutory challenges. The case has attracted intense public and political attention given Lim's prominent role in Malaysian politics — he served as Penang Chief Minister for over a decade and as Finance Minister in the Pakatan Harapan government from 2018 to 2020.

The trial's resumption is expected to dominate political commentary in the coming weeks, with government and opposition figures offering sharply divergent interpretations of the court proceedings. Anti-corruption advocates have welcomed the decision to proceed, arguing that high-profile cases must be allowed to run their full course in open court to demonstrate that Malaysia's rule of law applies equally to all, regardless of political status.
