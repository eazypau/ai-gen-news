Malaysia's Islamist PAS Party Gathers Strength as Government Eyes Snap Poll

Bloomberg 3 July 2026 Politics

Malaysia's Islamist opposition party PAS is capitalising on a nationwide surge in religious conservatism, emerging as a formidable force that is reshaping the country's political landscape. With state elections in Johor on July 11 and Negeri Sembilan on August 1, the party's growing support base is putting pressure on Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's unity government.

Bloomberg reported that the possibility of a PAS-led federal government is increasingly entering the calculations of foreign investors, who perceive the party as more economically nationalist. Analysts warn that a PAS government could alter Malaysia's investment-friendly posture, particularly in sectors seen as conflicting with Islamic values.

The ruling coalition, which pairs UMNO's Barisan Nasional with Anwar's Pakatan Harapan, has struggled to present a unified image after governing together since the November 2022 general election. The arrangement has occasionally produced public disagreements on policy, giving PAS and its Perikatan Nasional alliance an opening to portray the government as fractured.

PAS president Tan Sri Abdul Hadi Awang has been vocal about his party's ambition to contest federal power, framing its platform as the authentic voice of Malay Muslim Malaysia. The party has made significant inroads among rural and semi-urban constituencies, where economic anxieties and cost-of-living pressures have made voters receptive to its messaging.

Political scientists note that while PAS is unlikely to form a federal government without coalition support, its growing grassroots strength gives it substantial leverage in any future coalition negotiations. The July and August state election results will be closely watched as a barometer of the country's political direction ahead of the next general election, which must be held by 2027.
