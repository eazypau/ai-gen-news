Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.4% in Q1 2026, Outpacing Expectations

Ministry of Finance Malaysia 3 July 2026 Economy

Malaysia's gross domestic product expanded by 5.4% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026, slightly above preliminary estimates of 5.3% and exceeding most market forecasts despite a turbulent global environment. The result marks the second consecutive quarter of above-expectation growth, reinforcing confidence in the resilience of the Malaysian economy.

Domestic demand was the primary growth engine, expanding 5.2% as household spending remained steady amid a buoyant labour market and rising disposable incomes. The services sector — the backbone of the economy — grew 5.6%, moderating slightly from 6.2% in the previous quarter but remaining the single largest contributor to overall expansion.

The Finance Ministry highlighted that the economy entered 2026 with sufficient momentum to withstand the initial shock from the ongoing Iran war and its knock-on effects on global energy markets. However, officials acknowledged that the more painful secondary effects — higher energy costs, supply chain disruptions, and weakening global demand — are only beginning to materialise and will need careful monitoring.

The manufacturing sector posted solid gains, supported by sustained demand for electronics and semiconductor products from key trading partners. Malaysia's export-oriented industries benefited from front-loading by buyers anticipating further trade policy disruptions, providing a temporary lift that may moderate in subsequent quarters.

Bank Negara Malaysia noted that continued job creation pushed the unemployment rate to a decade-low 2.9%, while inflation held at a moderate 1.6%, providing a supportive environment for consumer spending. Economists at major investment banks forecast full-year 2026 GDP growth of between 4.5% and 5.2%, with the balance of risks skewed to the downside given ongoing global uncertainty.
