RM1.23 Billion Flood Mitigation Projects Launched to Protect Kuala Langat Basin

Media Selangor 3 July 2026 Infrastructure

Three major flood mitigation projects with a combined estimated cost of RM1.23 billion are under way across the Langat river basin, aimed at significantly reducing the flood risk in downstream areas including Kuala Langat — a district that has repeatedly suffered severe flooding in recent years. The projects are being implemented under the federal government's expanded flood mitigation programme following the catastrophic December 2021 floods.

Two of the three projects are already under active construction, with physical progress reported at 17.66% and 1.31% respectively as of May 2026. The more advanced project is targeted for completion in May 2027, while the second construction project has a completion target of April 2031, reflecting its larger scope and technical complexity. The third project — the Sungai Langat Flood Mitigation Plan Phase 2 Package 1 — is still undergoing detailed design preparation and is expected to be put out to tender in 2027.

The Department of Irrigation and Drainage (DID), which is overseeing the projects, said the works involve a combination of river channel widening, the construction of new retention ponds, the upgrading of flood barriers, and the installation of an advanced flood early warning and monitoring system. The integrated approach is designed to address both the immediate symptoms of flooding and the underlying hydraulic limitations of the Langat river system.

Selangor Menteri Besar Datuk Seri Amirudin Shari said the state government had been lobbying the federal government for increased flood mitigation funding for the Langat basin for several years, and welcomed the commitment of resources to the three projects. He urged contractors to maintain construction timelines and quality standards, noting that delays or defects would directly affect the safety of thousands of residents in flood-prone areas.

Community groups in Kuala Langat said they hoped the projects would bring lasting relief after years of recurring floods that have damaged homes, displaced families, and disrupted livelihoods. Environmental groups called on the authorities to ensure that river dredging and widening activities are conducted in a manner that preserves the ecological integrity of the Langat river system, which supports biodiversity including freshwater fish species and migratory birds.
