Nearly 60% of Perkeso-Screened Workers Found Overweight or Obese

Free Malaysia Today 3 July 2026 Health

A comprehensive occupational health screening programme conducted by Perkeso (the Social Security Organisation) between 2023 and 2025 has revealed that nearly 60% of participating workers are overweight or obese, raising serious concerns about workforce health, productivity, and the long-term sustainability of Malaysia's social insurance system. The data, released at a workplace wellness forum in Kuala Lumpur, covers hundreds of thousands of screened workers across multiple industries.

Health officials noted that the obesity rate among the working population mirrors broader national trends, with Malaysia consistently ranking among the most obese nations in Southeast Asia. The consequences extend well beyond individual health — overweight workers are more likely to develop chronic diseases such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular conditions that lead to higher absenteeism rates, increased medical insurance claims, and longer periods of medical leave.

Perkeso chief executive officer Datuk Seri Dr Mohammed Azman Aziz Mohammed urged employers to adopt structured workplace wellness programmes as a core component of their human resource strategies, emphasising that healthier employees translate directly to improved productivity, reduced turnover, and lower insurance premiums. "The data is stark. We cannot afford to ignore the health of our workforce," he said.

Industry groups responded cautiously, with some employer representatives noting that implementing comprehensive wellness programmes requires significant investment that smaller businesses may struggle to justify without financial incentives. The Ministry of Human Resources has indicated it is exploring tax relief mechanisms to encourage companies — particularly SMEs — to adopt certified workplace health programmes.

Nutritionists and public health experts at the forum called for a multi-pronged approach combining education, subsidised healthy canteen options in workplaces, physical activity programmes, and regular health screenings. Several large Malaysian corporations that have already implemented such programmes reported measurable improvements in employee health metrics within two years, suggesting the return on investment is achievable within a medium-term timeframe.
