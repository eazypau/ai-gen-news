FIFA World Cup 2026 Knockout Rounds Captivate Malaysian Football Fans

NST Sports 3 July 2026 Sports

The 2026 FIFA World Cup has entered its dramatic knockout phase, with Malaysian football fans staying up well into the early hours to watch matches broadcast live in Malaysia Time. Sports bars and mamak stalls across the country have been packed to capacity as the tournament reaches its decisive stages, with games running from late evening through to dawn for Malaysian viewers.

The expanded 48-team tournament format — making its debut at this World Cup — has delivered on its promise of more matches and more drama, with several shock upsets in the round of 32 that sent favourites home earlier than expected. Fans in Malaysia have been particularly engaged with the performances of Asian nations, with several squads from the region advancing further than they have at any previous World Cup.

New rules introduced for this edition of the tournament have generated significant debate among local football commentators. A restriction on long throw-ins aimed at reducing aerial set-piece dominance has proven controversial, as has the expansion of VAR to award or disallow corner kicks based on deliberate handball in the build-up. Opinion among Malaysian supporters is sharply divided on whether the rule changes improve the spectacle.

RTM, Astro Arena and streaming platform Sooka have all reported record viewership numbers for the knockout stage broadcasts, with combined audiences in Malaysia exceeding 8 million per prime knockout match. The surge in viewership has also boosted advertising revenues for Malaysian broadcasters, who secured early rights to the expanded tournament at premium prices.

The Football Association of Malaysia (FAM) has been using the World Cup as a motivational platform for the national squad, with head coach Kim Pan-gon screening knockout matches for players as part of tactical education sessions. Malaysia has set an ambitious target of qualifying for the 2030 World Cup, and the FAM believes exposure to the world's highest level of football will accelerate the development of the current national team generation.
