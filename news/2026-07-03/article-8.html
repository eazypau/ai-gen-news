Bursa Malaysia Technology Index Surges 34% in First Half of 2026 on Chip Boom

The Edge Malaysia 3 July 2026 Technology

The Bursa Malaysia Technology Index surged 34% in the first half of 2026, making it one of the best-performing sector indices on the exchange this year, as a broadening semiconductor upcycle lifted the entire chip supply chain beyond its initial AI-driven catalyst. Analysts noted that the rally has migrated from pure-play AI component suppliers to a wider basket of test-and-measurement, packaging, and substrate companies.

The Edge Malaysia reported that investors who initially focused on artificial intelligence chip demand — which drove the first wave of the rally in late 2025 — are now rotating into adjacent semiconductor plays that serve traditional end markets such as automotive, industrial automation, and consumer electronics. This diversification has made the current upcycle more durable than previous cycles that were concentrated in a single demand theme.

Among the standout performers in H1 2026 were Malaysian Automated Test Equipment (ATE) companies and advanced packaging specialists, which reported order book growth of 30% to 60% year-on-year as global chip fabs increased outsourcing of back-end operations. Penang-based players with long-standing relationships with global chipmakers were particularly well-positioned to capture this demand.

Analysts at several Malaysian investment banks maintained their positive outlook on the technology sector, with most setting price targets that implied further double-digit upside from current levels. The key risk, they noted, is a potential correction in global chip inventory if demand from end consumers softens faster than expected as higher energy prices dampen spending in Western markets.

The strong equity performance in the tech sector has also attracted increased foreign institutional interest in Bursa Malaysia, with net foreign inflows into the technology counters reaching a record high in June 2026. Fund managers say Malaysia offers a unique combination of established semiconductor manufacturing pedigree, a large pool of engineering talent, and relative geopolitical stability that makes it an attractive destination for chip supply chain investment.
