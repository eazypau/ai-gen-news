Malaysia Gears Up for Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games on July 23

Bernama 3 July 2026 Sports

Malaysia's national athletes are in final preparation mode ahead of the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games, with the opening ceremony at the OVO Hydro scheduled for July 23. The contingent is expected to compete across a range of disciplines including athletics, swimming, squash, cycling, shooting, and gymnastics, with officials targeting a medal haul that surpasses the country's performance at the previous Commonwealth Games in Birmingham 2022.

Bernama reported that this edition of the Commonwealth Games marks a notable first: several sports that have been the traditional backbone of Malaysia's success — including badminton and lawn bowls — are not part of the Glasgow programme, requiring athletes and selectors to broaden the medal strategy across alternative sports. Squash and shooting are seen as the most likely sources of gold medals for the Malaysian team.

Chef de mission for the Malaysian contingent, Datuk Seri Norza Zakaria, expressed confidence that the team would rise to the challenge of adapting to the revised sports programme. He noted that many athletes have trained internationally over the past year specifically to prepare for events they will contest in Glasgow, and the technical preparation has been thorough.

The Games also present an opportunity for a new generation of Malaysian athletes to establish themselves on the international stage. Youth stars in athletics and swimming who impressed at the 2025 SEA Games in Manila will be making their Commonwealth Games debut, with coaching staff eager to see how they perform against the wider Commonwealth field.

Malaysia has participated in every edition of the Commonwealth Games since 1950 and has accumulated over 400 medals across the decades. The Kuala Lumpur-based National Sports Council has provided additional preparation grants to athletes identified as podium contenders, and a live broadcast partnership with RTM and Astro ensures Malaysians can follow the action from Glasgow in full.
