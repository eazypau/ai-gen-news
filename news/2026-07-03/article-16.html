Diabetes Now a National Productivity Crisis, Affecting 15.6% of Malaysian Adults

The Sun Malaysia 3 July 2026 Health

Healthcare experts at the Diabetes Malaysia Conference 2026 in Kuala Lumpur have declared the country's diabetes epidemic a national productivity crisis, warning that the economic cost of unmanaged diabetes — through absenteeism, reduced work performance, and long-term disability — is placing a growing burden on businesses and the healthcare system alike. National Health and Morbidity Survey (NHMS) data reveals that 15.6% of Malaysian adults are currently living with diabetes.

Equally alarming is the finding that a further 5.9% of adults have elevated blood glucose levels without being aware of their condition — a so-called "hidden" population at high risk of progressing to full-blown diabetes if left undiagnosed and untreated. Experts estimate that every diagnosed diabetic in Malaysia is matched by at least one undiagnosed case, meaning the true scale of the problem is significantly larger than official figures suggest.

Endocrinologist Dr Zanariah Hussein called for urgent national action, including systematic screening programmes in workplaces, schools, and community health centres. She noted that Malaysia's diabetes prevalence rate is among the highest in Southeast Asia and is on track to worsen further as urbanisation, dietary changes, and sedentary lifestyles continue to shape population health trends.

The conference highlighted that poorly managed diabetes carries a range of costly complications including kidney failure, blindness, lower-limb amputations, and cardiovascular disease — all of which drive up healthcare utilisation and reduce workforce participation. Malaysia's public hospital system already dedicates a significant portion of its budget to managing diabetes-related complications, with dialysis costs for diabetic kidney disease patients alone running into hundreds of millions of ringgit annually.

Health Minister Datuk Seri Dr Dzulkefly Ahmad pledged that the Ministry of Health would accelerate the rollout of community-level diabetes prevention programmes under the National Strategic Plan for Non-Communicable Diseases 2025–2030. The plan includes a nationwide blood glucose screening campaign targeting adults over 30, a tax on sugary beverages, and enhanced nutritional labelling requirements for processed food products sold in Malaysian supermarkets.
