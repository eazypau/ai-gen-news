Ten Startups Chosen for Malaysia's Inaugural SemiconStart Semiconductor Programme

New Straits Times 3 July 2026 Technology

Ten semiconductor startups have been selected for the inaugural cohort of SemiconStart Malaysia, the country's first dedicated semiconductor venture incubation programme. The initiative, announced in the 2026 Budget, is backed by an initial government allocation of RM10 million, with an additional RM10 million earmarked for the next phase as companies progress through the programme.

The selected startups span a range of specialisations including chip design, embedded systems, advanced packaging solutions, and semiconductor manufacturing equipment components. Participating companies will receive mentorship from industry veterans, access to global funding and investment networks, prototyping facilities, and commercialisation support to help bring their innovations to market.

The programme is part of Malaysia's broader National Semiconductor Strategy, which aims to move the country up the chip value chain from predominantly back-end assembly and test operations toward higher-value design, intellectual property development, and specialised manufacturing. Malaysia currently hosts manufacturing operations for over half of the world's top ten semiconductor companies.

Minister of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) Datuk Seri Tengku Zafrul Tengku Abdul Aziz said SemiconStart represents "a pivotal moment" in Malaysia's semiconductor journey. He added that global chip firms including Lam Research and Applied Materials are progressively expanding their Malaysian footprint, with Lam Research's second Kulim facility expected to come online by end-2026.

The programme runs for 12 months and will culminate in a Demo Day where startups pitch to a panel of investors, corporate venture capital arms, and government-linked funds. Successful graduates may qualify for follow-on grants under Cradle Fund and matching investments from Malaysia Venture Capital Management (MAVCAP). The government views SemiconStart as a key pillar in its ambition to build a homegrown semiconductor ecosystem capable of competing regionally by 2030.
