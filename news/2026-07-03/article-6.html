Malaysia Unemployment Hits Decade-Low 2.9% as Job Creation Accelerates

Bank Negara Malaysia 3 July 2026 Economy

Malaysia's unemployment rate fell to 2.9% in the first quarter of 2026, marking the lowest reading in over a decade and reflecting sustained momentum in the country's labour market. Bank Negara Malaysia's quarterly economic review highlighted the decline as evidence that broad-based job creation is underway across multiple sectors of the economy.

The services sector, which accounts for the majority of employment, continued to lead hiring activity, with significant job gains recorded in financial services, information technology, hospitality, and healthcare. The manufacturing sector, buoyed by strong semiconductor and electronics output, also added workers at a healthy pace, particularly in Penang and the Klang Valley.

Wage growth picked up modestly compared with a year ago, reflecting tighter labour market conditions and the phased implementation of the revised minimum wage structure. Inflation remained moderate at 1.6% over the same period, meaning real purchasing power of workers improved slightly — a positive development for domestic consumption.

Youth unemployment, however, remains a persistent challenge despite the overall improvement in employment conditions. The rate for workers aged 15 to 24 remains at 11.3%, more than three times the national average, underscoring structural mismatches between the skills produced by the education system and those demanded by employers in high-growth sectors.

Economists caution that the decade-low unemployment rate may mask some labour market slack, particularly among part-time and gig workers who are classified as employed but may be working fewer hours than they would prefer. The government's ongoing labour market reforms and TVET expansion are seen as critical to sustaining employment quality alongside employment quantity in the coming years.
