Perlis Forestry Department Discovers 83 Untouched Caves Along Nakawan Range

Source: The Star 7 June 2026 Environment

A comprehensive 21-day survey conducted by the Perlis State Forestry Department has yielded an extraordinary environmental discovery: 83 previously uncharted caves nestled within the Nakawan Range in northern Malaysia. The expedition, involving a team of forestry officers, geologists and biodiversity researchers, mapped and catalogued each cave, revealing a remarkable hidden karst landscape.

Of the 83 caves discovered, six have been identified by surveyors as having significant potential for sustainable ecotourism development. These caves feature impressive geological formations including stalactites, stalagmites and underground water features, making them attractive destinations for nature-based tourism once appropriate access and conservation measures are established.

The Nakawan Range, which straddles the Malaysia-Thailand border in the Perlis hinterland, is a limestone massif that has remained largely unexplored due to its remote terrain. The survey team noted that the caves showed no signs of prior human entry, with several hosting rare cave-dwelling fauna including blind fish, cave crickets and species of micro-bats not previously recorded in Perlis.

Perlis Forestry Director Mohd Zaki Abdul Aziz described the discovery as one of the most significant environmental findings in the state in recent decades. He emphasised that any future ecotourism development would be conducted within a strict conservation framework to preserve the caves' ecological integrity and geological heritage.

Researchers plan to submit detailed reports to the Department of National Heritage and the Ministry of Natural Resources, Environment and Climate Change for potential gazettal of the cave complex as a protected natural heritage site. The discovery adds to growing evidence of Perlis's rich but underappreciated natural biodiversity.
