Malaysia Evaluates Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Export Licence

Source: New Straits Times 7 June 2026 Defense

The Royal Malaysian Navy (RMN) is actively reviewing alternative missile suppliers following Norway's decision to revoke the export licence for the Naval Strike Missile (NSM) system that Malaysia had sought to procure. The NSM, developed by Norwegian defence company Kongsberg, is a precision anti-ship and land-attack missile system that the RMN had been considering as part of a broader modernisation of its naval strike capabilities.

Norway's decision to withdraw the export licence is understood to be linked to evolving Norwegian foreign policy guidelines on arms exports, which have been subject to stricter review criteria in the current geopolitical environment. Malaysian defence officials expressed disappointment at the development, stressing that Malaysia's procurement was purely defensive in nature and fully compliant with international arms transfer norms.

The Ministry of Defence has directed the Defence Procurement Division to conduct a comprehensive assessment of alternative air and surface-launched anti-ship missile systems from other approved suppliers. Options under consideration reportedly include systems from France, the United States, Turkey and India, all of which have developed competitive naval strike missile platforms with track records in allied naval services.

The NSM procurement was part of the RMN's broader Armada Transformation Plan 2035, which aims to modernise the navy's surface combatant fleet and enhance Malaysia's maritime defence capabilities, particularly in the South China Sea region where territorial disputes continue to simmer. Defence analysts note that the episode underscores the risks of reliance on single-country weapons systems and the importance of diversifying procurement relationships.

A decision on an alternative missile supplier is expected within the next six to twelve months, with the selected system needing to be compatible with Malaysia's existing naval platform integration requirements. Parliament's Defence and Home Affairs Select Committee has been briefed on the development and has urged the ministry to expedite a solution to avoid gaps in the navy's planned capability timeline.
