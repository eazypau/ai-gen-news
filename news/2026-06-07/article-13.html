International Scam Syndicates Relocating Operations to Malaysia After Regional Crackdowns

Source: Free Malaysia Today 7 June 2026 Security

Malaysian law enforcement authorities have raised an alarm over intelligence indicating that international fraud syndicates are increasingly relocating parts of their operations to Malaysia in the wake of intensified crackdowns in neighbouring countries, including Cambodia, Myanmar and the Philippines. The syndicates, which run sophisticated phone and online scam operations, are believed to be exploiting gaps in cross-border enforcement cooperation.

Commercial Crime Investigation Department (CCID) director Datuk Seri Ramli Mohamed Yoosuf confirmed that authorities have detected a shift in syndicate activity patterns, with new scam call centres and online fraud operations being established in several Malaysian states. He warned that Malaysia risks becoming a regional staging ground for cybercrime if law enforcement and legislative measures are not swiftly intensified.

The scam syndicates typically recruit foreign workers under false pretences, forcing them to work in compounds where they conduct phone fraud, cryptocurrency investment scams and romance scams targeting victims across Asia, Europe and North America. Authorities in Cambodia and Myanmar have conducted major raids on such facilities in recent months, disrupting established operations and prompting syndicates to seek alternative host countries.

The Malaysian government has responded by strengthening intelligence-sharing arrangements with regional partners through the ASEAN Senior Officials Meeting on Transnational Crime (SOMTC) framework. The Royal Malaysia Police is working with the Immigration Department to identify suspicious employment patterns and flag work permit applications that may be linked to fraudulent operations.

Members of the public are urged to be vigilant and report suspicious activity to the National Scam Response Centre (NSRC) at 997. Authorities have also intensified awareness campaigns targeting vulnerable demographics, including retirees and young adults, who remain the most frequent targets of phone-based investment fraud and impersonation scams.
