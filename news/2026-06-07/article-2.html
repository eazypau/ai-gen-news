PM Anwar Rejects Fuel Price Hikes Amid Global Supply Crisis

Source: New Straits Times 7 June 2026 Politics

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has categorically ruled out any increases to fuel prices in Malaysia, even as global energy markets experience significant turbulence. Speaking at a press conference on Sunday, he affirmed that the government will continue absorbing subsidy costs to shield ordinary Malaysians from the impact of rising international crude oil prices.

The announcement comes against a backdrop of mounting global supply pressures, with crude oil prices climbing steadily due to geopolitical tensions and production adjustments by major oil-producing nations. Several neighbouring countries have already moved to reduce fuel subsidies, leading to sharp price increases for consumers.

Anwar stressed that the Malaysian government's priority remains the welfare of the rakyat, particularly lower-income households who would be disproportionately affected by any fuel price hike. He noted that the Madani government has been carefully studying subsidy rationalisation measures but would only proceed when robust targeted assistance programmes are fully in place.

The Finance Ministry is understood to be working on an updated subsidy framework that would redirect savings from blanket subsidies toward cash transfers for eligible recipients. This approach mirrors models adopted in Indonesia and Thailand but requires substantial administrative groundwork before implementation.

Opposition lawmakers have urged the government to set a concrete timeline for subsidy reform, warning that open-ended commitments could place unsustainable pressure on the federal budget. Economists, however, broadly support the government's cautious approach, given the sensitivity of fuel prices on consumer sentiment and inflation expectations.
