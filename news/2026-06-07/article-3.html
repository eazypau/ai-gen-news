27 Tahfiz Students Stable After Suspected Food Poisoning in Rawang

Source: Malay Mail 7 June 2026 Health

Twenty-seven individuals, comprising tahfiz students and elderly residents at a religious centre in Rawang, Selangor, are in stable condition following a suspected food poisoning incident that occurred on Saturday evening. The affected individuals were brought to Hospital Rawang for treatment, where medical staff assessed them for symptoms including nausea, vomiting and abdominal cramps.

Rawang district police confirmed they received a distress call from the religious centre shortly after 8pm on Saturday. Officers from the Rawang police station arrived at the scene alongside paramedics from the State Health Department. All 27 patients were reported conscious and responsive upon admission, with none requiring intensive care.

Preliminary investigations suggest the incident may be linked to a meal consumed at the centre during the evening. Health inspectors have collected food samples from the kitchen for laboratory analysis, with results expected within 48 to 72 hours. The centre's kitchen operations have been temporarily suspended pending the investigation outcome.

Police are conducting investigations under Section 51 of the Food Act 1983, which covers offences related to the preparation and sale of unsafe food. The centre's management has cooperated fully with authorities and expressed deep concern for those affected.

Food poisoning incidents at residential religious institutions have been an ongoing concern in Malaysia, prompting the Health Ministry to periodically issue guidelines on food safety standards for dormitory-based facilities. Officials are urging all similar institutions to conduct regular hygiene audits of their food preparation areas.
