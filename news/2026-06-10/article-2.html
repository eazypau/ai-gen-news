Malaysia Seeks Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Naval Strike Missile Licence

Politics New Straits Times 10 June 2026

Malaysia is actively evaluating alternative missile suppliers after Norway revoked the export licence for the Naval Strike Missile (NSM) system previously contracted for delivery to the Royal Malaysian Navy. The decision by Oslo has disrupted Malaysia's near-term naval modernisation plans and prompted an urgent review of its defence procurement strategy.

Defence officials confirmed that the government is in contact with several friendly nations to identify equivalent or superior anti-ship missile systems. Countries under consideration reportedly include France, South Korea, and Turkey, all of which manufacture advanced naval strike weapons compatible with Malaysia's existing frigate platforms.

Norway's move is believed to be linked to broader Western geopolitical considerations regarding arms exports and regional security dynamics in Southeast Asia. Malaysian officials have expressed disappointment over the decision, noting that the contract had been properly negotiated and that Malaysia is a non-belligerent nation with legitimate defence needs.

The Royal Malaysian Navy has emphasised that the absence of the NSM does not immediately impair operational readiness, as existing weapons systems remain functional. However, planners note that the delayed modernisation could affect the navy's long-term deterrence posture in contested maritime zones, particularly in the South China Sea.

Parliament members from both the government and opposition benches have called for transparency in the alternative procurement process. Defence analysts say the episode underscores the importance of Malaysia diversifying its arms supply chains to avoid over-reliance on any single supplier or geopolitical bloc.
