Former MACC Chief Azam Baki Files Defamation Suit Over Podcast Claims

Politics The Star 10 June 2026

Former Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission (MACC) chief commissioner Tan Sri Azam Baki has initiated defamation proceedings against businessman Albert Tei Jiann Cheing over statements published via podcast, YouTube Shorts, and related social media platforms. The lawsuit, filed at the Kuala Lumpur High Court, claims that the statements were false, malicious, and have caused serious damage to Azam's reputation.

According to the court filing, the alleged defamatory content was published between early and mid-2026, reaching hundreds of thousands of viewers online. Azam's legal team argues that the statements insinuated misconduct and abuse of power during his tenure as the head of the anti-graft body, which he vehemently denies.

Albert Tei, a well-known figure in business circles, is yet to publicly respond to the lawsuit. His legal representatives have reportedly been served the writ of summons and are expected to file a defence within the court-stipulated timeframe. Legal observers anticipate the case will draw significant public attention given both parties' public profiles.

Azam Baki served as MACC chief commissioner until his retirement and oversaw several high-profile corruption investigations during his tenure. His time at the helm was not without controversy, including questions raised in Parliament about share ownership matters—questions he repeatedly denied being improper.

The case is a reminder of the increasingly litigious environment surrounding commentary on public officials in Malaysia, particularly as social media platforms amplify narratives beyond traditional media boundaries. Legal experts say the outcome could set an important precedent on the boundaries of commentary permissible on digital platforms regarding public officials.
