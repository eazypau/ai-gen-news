Japan Tops Malaysia's Foreign Investors with RM21.5 Billion in Q1 2026

Business MIDA 10 June 2026

Japan has emerged as Malaysia's single largest source of approved foreign investments in the first quarter of 2026, injecting RM21.5 billion into the Malaysian economy—a staggering increase from just RM1.6 billion in Q1 2025. The surge represents a 13-fold year-on-year rise and firmly establishes Japan as a pivotal partner in Malaysia's industrial transformation journey.

Over 93.6% of Japanese approved investments in the period were channelled into digital transformation activities, according to MIDA data. This includes investments in semiconductor fabrication, data centres, cloud computing infrastructure, and advanced electronics manufacturing—all aligned with Malaysia's ambitions under the New Industrial Master Plan 2030.

The significant jump in Japanese investment is partly attributed to Japan's own industrial policy push to diversify supply chains away from China following geopolitical tensions. Malaysia, with its stable governance, competitive infrastructure, and skilled workforce, has become a natural destination for Japanese companies seeking reliable manufacturing and technology hubs in Southeast Asia.

Several major Japanese corporations have either announced or broken ground on new facilities in Malaysia this year, including semiconductor equipment makers and automotive electronics suppliers. The Penang and Selangor corridors continue to be the preferred destinations for Japanese high-tech investments.

Japan's ambassador to Malaysia expressed confidence that bilateral investment flows would continue to grow, citing the deep historical and economic ties between both nations. Trade and Industry officials from both governments are scheduled to meet next month to further facilitate investment facilitation mechanisms and explore additional cooperation in green technology and digital infrastructure.
