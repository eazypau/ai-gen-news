World Bank Raises Malaysia's 2026 Growth Forecast to 4.4%

Business Free Malaysia Today 10 June 2026

The World Bank has revised upward its economic growth forecast for Malaysia in 2026 to 4.4%, up from its earlier projection of 4.1%, acknowledging the country's resilience in the face of mounting geopolitical headwinds. The upgrade reflects stronger-than-expected domestic consumption, robust trade performance, and continued confidence in Malaysia's reform trajectory.

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) has similarly maintained its own projection of between 4% and 5% growth for 2026, pointing to healthy labour market conditions, sustained private investment, and government spending as key growth drivers. The central bank noted that inflation remains within manageable levels, preserving household purchasing power.

The World Bank's assessment highlighted Malaysia's proactive approach to diversifying its trade partnerships and reducing exposure to any single economic bloc as a key strength. This diversification has helped cushion the economy from shocks arising from US-China trade tensions and regional supply chain disruptions.

Finance Minister Datuk Seri Amir Hamzah Azizan welcomed the revised forecast, saying it validates the government's fiscal consolidation efforts and structural reform agenda. He added that the focus remains on sustaining quality growth that benefits all segments of Malaysian society, particularly the B40 and M40 income groups.

Economists cautioned, however, that downside risks remain, including potential escalation of the Middle East conflict, commodity price volatility, and slower growth among Malaysia's key trading partners. Sustaining the growth momentum will require continued policy dexterity and decisive implementation of the 13th Malaysia Plan's strategic targets.
