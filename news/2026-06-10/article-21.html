Malaysia's Marine Protected Areas at Risk from Oil and Gas Expansion, Warns Think Tank

Environment Eco-Business 10 June 2026

Environmental think-tank Rimbawatch has released a landmark report warning that the majority of Malaysia's designated Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) are under serious threat from the ongoing and planned expansion of offshore oil and gas operations. The report, the first of its kind to use satellite imagery and geospatial analysis to document the full extent of the overlap, paints a deeply troubling picture for Malaysia's marine biodiversity.

According to Rimbawatch's analysis, offshore oil and gas exploration and production blocks now overlap with approximately 40% of Malaysia's mapped shallow coral reef ecosystems. Shallow coral reefs are among the most biodiverse marine habitats on the planet and are critical nurseries for commercially important fish species, yet they face compounding stresses from both climate change and direct human activity.

Several of Malaysia's gazetted MPAs—intended to provide legal protection for marine habitats—were found to fall within or immediately adjacent to active oil and gas concession areas. The report calls into question whether the current legal framework governing MPAs provides adequate protection when it conflicts with the economic interests of the hydrocarbon sector.

Rimbawatch has called on the Malaysian government to conduct a comprehensive review of all oil and gas concession blocks to identify and exclude those that pose unacceptable risks to marine ecosystems. The organisation also urges Petronas and its production-sharing contractors to adopt no-go zones around sensitive marine habitats as a voluntary commitment pending formal regulatory action.

The report arrives as Malaysia prepares to implement its carbon tax and expands its carbon market framework, creating an apparent contradiction between the country's green economy ambitions and its continued dependence on hydrocarbon revenues. Environmental advocates are calling for an integrated ocean policy that balances economic development with the imperative of marine conservation, especially as Malaysians increasingly look to tourism, fisheries, and blue economy activities as sustainable livelihood alternatives.
