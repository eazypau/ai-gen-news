Malaysia Launches Carbon Market Platform as Carbon Tax Implementation Begins

Environment The Borneo Post 10 June 2026

Malaysia has taken a decisive step toward building a green economy with the operational launch of its Digital Platform for Carbon Knowledge (DPKK), approved by Cabinet on April 1, 2026. The platform forms the backbone of Malaysia's emerging national carbon market, designed to facilitate transparent trading of carbon credits and provide a credible national carbon registry.

The DPKK is structured around four strategic pillars: facilitating compliance carbon market operations, developing and maintaining the national carbon registry, providing carbon data analytics and reporting tools for both public and private sector users, and educating market participants on carbon accounting methodologies. The platform is expected to attract both domestic and international carbon credit buyers, positioning Malaysia as a regional hub for voluntary and compliance carbon markets.

The carbon tax itself entered its implementation phase in 2026, initially targeting the iron, steel, and energy sectors—collectively among the largest industrial emitters in Malaysia. The phased approach is intended to give industries time to adapt their processes and invest in emission reduction technologies before the tax is broadened to cover additional sectors in subsequent years.

Business groups in the targeted sectors have responded with a mix of concern and pragmatic acceptance. Several major steel manufacturers have announced investments in hydrogen-based direct reduction ironmaking as a longer-term decarbonisation pathway, while energy companies are accelerating their renewable energy transition plans. Industry representatives have sought government assurance that revenue from the carbon tax will be ring-fenced for green investment incentives and just-transition support programmes for workers.

Environmental economists and climate policy experts have broadly welcomed Malaysia's carbon pricing framework as a significant step forward, though some caution that the initial carbon price level will need to be reviewed and raised progressively to deliver meaningful emission reductions. Malaysia's successful implementation of the carbon tax and DPKK will be closely watched by ASEAN neighbours who are considering similar mechanisms, as the region grapples with its collective obligation to meet the Paris Agreement goals.
