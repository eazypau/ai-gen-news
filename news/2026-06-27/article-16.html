KLIMS 2026 Wraps Up With Over 200,000 Visitors, Showcasing Future Mobility

Sin Chew Daily / Bernama | 27 June 2026

The Kuala Lumpur International Mobility Show 2026 (KLIMS 2026) has concluded with a total of over 200,000 visitors over its run, making it one of the best-attended editions of Malaysia's premier automotive exhibition. The show, held at the Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre (KLCC), featured exhibitors from across the global automotive ecosystem presenting electric vehicles, autonomous driving technology, connected mobility solutions, and next-generation transportation concepts under the "Beyond Mobility" theme.

Electric vehicles dominated the show floor, with over a dozen new EV models either officially launched or showcased in preview form during the event. Domestic automaker Proton displayed its latest electrified lineup alongside models from Chinese EV manufacturers BYD and Chery, European brands including BMW and Mercedes-Benz, and Japanese manufacturers Honda and Toyota, all of whom have announced significant EV commitments for the Malaysian market over the coming years.

KLIMS 2026 also served as a platform for policy discussions, with government ministers using the event to reaffirm Malaysia's commitment to achieving a 15% EV share of new vehicle sales by 2030 under the National Automotive Policy (NAP). Incentives for EV ownership including import duty exemptions, road tax reductions, and charging infrastructure subsidies were highlighted as key pillars of the government's strategy to accelerate the EV transition.

Beyond passenger vehicles, the show featured a significant focus on commercial mobility including electric buses, logistics drones, and micro-mobility solutions aligned with Malaysia's growing interest in sustainable urban transportation. Several Kuala Lumpur and Penang public transport authorities used the event to announce pilot programmes for electric buses and demand-responsive transit services.

Organisers described the show's success as confirmation that Malaysian consumers and businesses are ready to engage with the mobility transition, with strong interest particularly among younger visitors for EVs, ride-sharing-integrated vehicles, and subscription-based automotive services. Industry analysts said KLIMS 2026 had reinforced Malaysia's ambition to develop a domestic EV supply chain ecosystem anchored by battery assembly, electric powertrain components, and software-defined vehicle platforms.
