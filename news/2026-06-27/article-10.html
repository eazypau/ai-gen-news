All 20 Malaysian Public Universities Now Running Google Gemini for Education

Google Blog / Malay Mail | 27 June 2026

Google has confirmed that all 20 Malaysian public universities have officially enabled Gemini for Education across their academic and administrative platforms, making Malaysia one of the most comprehensively covered nations in the world for AI-powered education tools at the university level. The rollout reaches approximately 600,000 students and 75,000 faculty members, fundamentally changing how learning, research, and institutional management are conducted at Malaysia's public higher education institutions.

As part of the deployment, the Ministry of Higher Education has also equipped 40,000 faculty members across these institutions with Google AI Pro for Education, giving lecturers and researchers access to advanced AI assistance for content creation, data analysis, lesson planning, and academic writing. Additionally, 128,000 students now have access to NotebookLM Enterprise, Google's AI-powered research and note-taking tool that can synthesise and interrogate large volumes of documents, making it particularly valuable for postgraduate researchers and dissertation writers.

The initiative is part of Malaysia's broader national AI strategy, which seeks to embed artificial intelligence literacy and tools across the education system from secondary school through to postgraduate level. Officials said the deployment would accelerate Malaysia's capacity to produce AI-literate graduates capable of contributing to the country's rapidly expanding digital economy, particularly in sectors such as semiconductor design, data engineering, and AI-driven enterprise software.

University administrators highlighted several early use cases emerging from the pilot phases, including AI-assisted curriculum design, automated administrative workflows, multilingual study assistance for students learning in Bahasa Malaysia and English simultaneously, and enhanced accessibility tools for students with learning differences. Several universities reported measurable improvements in student engagement and completion rates in courses where AI tools were piloted.

Critics of the rapid AI deployment cautioned that universities need to also invest in digital ethics education and academic integrity frameworks to ensure students develop genuine skills rather than becoming over-reliant on AI-generated content. The Ministry acknowledged these concerns and confirmed that a national framework for responsible AI use in higher education was being finalised alongside the technology rollout.
