FIFA World Cup 2026: Six Group-Stage Matches on June 27 for Malaysian Viewers

SAYS / New Straits Times | 27 June 2026

Malaysian football fans have a packed Saturday of World Cup action to look forward to on June 27, 2026, with six group-stage matches scheduled across the day in Malaysian Standard Time (MYT). The 2026 FIFA World Cup is being co-hosted by Canada, Mexico, and the United States — the first tournament to feature 48 national teams competing across 104 matches, making it the largest World Cup in history.

The early morning schedule kicks off at 3am MYT with two simultaneous matches: Norway vs France and Senegal vs Iraq, both group-stage contests with significant knockout qualification implications. The highly anticipated midday double-header at 8am MYT features Cabo Verde vs Saudi Arabia and the blockbuster clash between Uruguay and Spain, two traditional powers of world football competing for a top-two group finish.

The day concludes at 11am MYT with Egypt vs IR Iran and New Zealand vs Belgium, both fixtures that could see multiple knockout scenarios played out simultaneously. Football fans across Malaysia have been staying up late and waking early throughout the group stage to follow the action live, with several Malaysian sports bars and fan zones reporting record-breaking crowds during marquee fixtures.

Malaysia did not qualify for the 2026 World Cup, but local interest has been enormous, with the national team's improved performances in the Asian qualifiers generating a new wave of enthusiasm for the sport. ASTRO and RTM have secured broadcast rights for the tournament, ensuring comprehensive coverage across free-to-air and subscription channels for Malaysian viewers at home.

Sports analysts noted that the expanded 48-team format has provided more Asian representation in the tournament, with seven Asian Football Confederation (AFC) sides qualifying. Malaysian fans have been particularly supportive of AFC representatives Japan, South Korea, Australia, and Saudi Arabia, adopting them as home-continent teams to cheer in the absence of the national Harimau Malaya squad.
