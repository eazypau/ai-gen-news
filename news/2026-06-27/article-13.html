Jiuzhou Cup National High Pole Lion Dance Championship Set for Port Dickson

Sin Chew Daily | 27 June 2026

The 2026 Second "Jiuzhou Cup" National High Pole Lion Dance Championship and Flag Promotion Invitational Competition will be held in Port Dickson, Negeri Sembilan, on June 28, bringing together 16 teams from across Malaysia for a spectacular display of one of the country's most beloved traditional performing arts. The event is sanctioned by the national lion dance governing body and draws both seasoned competitors and emerging talent from the Chinese Malaysian community.

High pole lion dance — known as Gao Jhuang (高桩) — is a technically demanding form of the art in which performers balance on wooden poles of varying heights while executing precise choreography, acrobatic manoeuvres, and symbolic interactions between lion pairs. Teams are judged on technical execution, artistic expression, synchronisation, and the difficulty of their routines, with the top finishers earning national ranking points and invitations to international competitions.

The 16 competing teams represent clubs from Kuala Lumpur, Selangor, Penang, Johor, Perak, Negeri Sembilan, and other states, showcasing the breadth of the lion dance tradition across Malaysia's Chinese community. Several defending champions and previous national-level title holders are expected to be among the competitors, promising a high standard of competition throughout the event.

Organisers said the choice of Port Dickson as the host city was deliberate, as the coastal location provides a festive backdrop and the town's existing hospitality infrastructure can accommodate the influx of competitors, officials, and spectators. Local businesses and tourism operators are anticipating a boost from the event, which is expected to draw thousands of spectators over the competition day.

The event is also part of broader efforts to preserve and promote high pole lion dance as a living cultural heritage and to encourage the next generation of Malaysian Chinese youth to take up the tradition. Workshops and exhibition performances for school groups are being organised alongside the main championship as part of a cultural outreach programme.
