Kedah Immigration Detains Suspect Linked to Migrant Smuggling Network

The Sun Malaysia | 27 June 2026

Kedah Immigration Department authorities have detained a 36-year-old Malaysian man suspected of involvement in a migrant smuggling syndicate during Ops Serkap, an ongoing enforcement operation conducted at the Bukit Kayu Hitam Immigration Complex near the Malaysia-Thailand border. The suspect was apprehended by officers from the Immigration Intelligence and Special Operations Division (BSSK) while he was present at the complex reportedly to assist with travel arrangements for other individuals.

Authorities said the suspect is believed to be a facilitator within a larger smuggling network that uses the northern border crossing to move undocumented migrants — primarily from Myanmar, Bangladesh, and other South and Southeast Asian countries — into Malaysia. The network is suspected of charging migrants significant fees for transit assistance and documentation forgery services, making it a lucrative criminal enterprise.

The suspect has been handed over to the Enforcement Unit at Bukit Kayu Hitam ICQS (Immigration, Customs, Quarantine and Security) for further investigation under the Anti-Trafficking in Persons and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Act 2007 (ATIPSOM). Under ATIPSOM, conviction for smuggling of migrants carries a maximum penalty of 15 years' imprisonment and a fine for individuals found guilty of organising or facilitating illegal migration.

Ops Serkap is part of a broader national crackdown on human trafficking and migrant smuggling networks, which have been identified as significant security and humanitarian concerns along Malaysia's northern border. The operation involves coordinated enforcement by the Immigration Department, Royal Malaysia Police, the Malaysian Maritime Enforcement Agency (MMEA), and other relevant agencies.

Immigration officials said investigations were ongoing to identify other members of the network and trace the full extent of the syndicate's operations. Members of the public with information about suspected human trafficking or migrant smuggling activities are urged to contact the Immigration Department's hotline or the National Anti-Human Trafficking and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Council (MAPO) tip-off line.
