International Dive Against Debris Campaign Kicks Off at Pulau Redang

Media OutReach Newswire | 27 June 2026

Phase 4 of the International Volunteer Project: Dive Against Debris officially commenced on June 27 at Pulau Redang, Terengganu, bringing together local and international divers, environmental advocates, and community members for a three-day campaign running until June 29. The initiative combines underwater debris removal with beach clean-up activities and environmental education workshops aimed at inspiring sustainable ocean practices among participants and the wider public.

Pulau Redang, one of Malaysia's most pristine and biodiverse marine protected areas, was selected as the host location for Phase 4 due to concerns about the increasing volume of anthropogenic waste — including single-use plastics, fishing gear, and beverage containers — washing onto its reefs and beaches, threatening coral ecosystems and marine wildlife that draw both domestic and international tourists each year.

Volunteer divers will conduct systematic underwater surveys to collect and catalogue debris by type, weight, and suspected origin, contributing data to a global ocean waste database maintained by the Project AWARE Foundation. The findings from Pulau Redang's reefs will inform both local marine park management decisions and global policy advocacy on ocean plastic pollution at international forums including the United Nations Environment Assembly.

The environmental education component of the programme includes interactive sessions for students from nearby Terengganu schools, teaching them about the lifecycle of ocean plastic, the importance of coral reef ecosystems, and individual actions that can reduce marine pollution. Participants will also learn about citizen science methodologies and how they can continue contributing to reef monitoring in their local communities beyond the organised event.

Organisers emphasised that the Dive Against Debris campaign is part of a longer-term commitment to protecting Malaysia's marine environment, noting that annual events at different sites around the country have collectively removed thousands of kilograms of waste from Malaysian waters. They called on the government to strengthen marine protected area regulations and enforcement, particularly around single-use plastic disposal by fishing and tourism operators in and around gazetted marine parks.
