Malaysia Launches Over 20 Cultural and Reading Festival Events on June 27

Sin Chew Daily | 27 June 2026

More than 20 cultural and reading festival events launched across Malaysia on June 27, celebrating the country's rich literary traditions and multicultural heritage in a nationwide initiative coordinated by state cultural agencies, community organisations, and publishing houses. The events span book fairs, author talks, storytelling sessions, heritage exhibitions, and community arts performances, designed to foster a love of reading and cultural appreciation among Malaysians of all ages and backgrounds.

The simultaneous launch across multiple cities and towns — including Kuala Lumpur, George Town, Ipoh, Johor Bahru, Kota Kinabalu, and Kuching — reflects the broad scope of the initiative, which aims to bring cultural programming beyond the major urban centres and into smaller towns and rural communities that are often underserved by the mainstream arts and literary calendar. Organisers highlighted the importance of providing culturally resonant content in Bahasa Malaysia, English, Mandarin, Tamil, and indigenous languages to reach Malaysia's diverse communities.

Among the headline events is a reading marathon at Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka's Kuala Lumpur complex, featuring notable Malaysian authors reading excerpts from their latest works and engaging in discussions about the state of Malaysian literature, the challenges facing local publishers in the digital age, and efforts to translate Malaysian stories for international audiences. A dedicated children's reading corner with interactive activities has been set up to encourage young Malaysians to develop a reading habit early.

The events also include a travelling cultural showcase featuring traditional performing arts, visual art exhibitions, and culinary demonstrations representing Malaysia's Malay, Chinese, Indian, and indigenous Orang Asli and Borneo communities. Organisers said the programme was designed to strengthen intercultural understanding and remind Malaysians of the shared values and complementary traditions that underpin the country's social fabric.

Cultural ministry officials said the June 27 launch was timed to coincide with the school holiday period to maximise participation by families and young people. They announced that the festival calendar would run through July, with additional events scheduled weekly, and encouraged Malaysians to check local community notice boards and social media channels for events in their areas. The initiative is partly funded through the National Book Council (DBP) and private sector cultural sponsorship.
