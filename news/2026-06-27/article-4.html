PM Anwar Directs Local Authorities to Slash Project Approval Delays

Bernama | 27 June 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has directed local councils and planning authorities across Malaysia to streamline their project approval processes, with a specific focus on reducing bureaucratic delays and cost overruns affecting housing and factory developments. The directive was issued following a Cabinet-level review of complaints from developers and investors about lengthy approval timelines.

The Prime Minister indicated that some development approvals currently take more than a year from submission to issuance, a situation he described as unacceptable in the context of Malaysia's economic development goals. He called for a standardised digital-first approval workflow to be adopted by local authorities within the next six months, reducing face-to-face submission requirements and enabling parallel processing of permit applications.

Industry groups representing property developers and manufacturers welcomed the announcement, noting that faster approvals would help lower project financing costs and allow them to deliver housing units and industrial facilities more quickly. The Real Estate and Housing Developers' Association (REHDA) estimated that approval delays add an average of 8–15% to project costs, which ultimately filters through to buyers and tenants.

The Ministry of Local Government Development has been given the mandate to coordinate the reform effort across all 151 local authorities in Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah, and Sarawak. Agencies will be assessed against new key performance indicators (KPIs) tied to approval turnaround times, with underperforming councils subject to targeted intervention and capacity-building support.

Analysts say the move is part of the government's broader push to reduce the cost of doing business in Malaysia and improve the country's rankings in international business environment indices. The reform is expected to be one of the headline deliverables under the Madani Economy agenda in the second half of 2026.
