Malaysia Launches RM185 Million Semiconductor R&D Initiative

BusinessToday | 27 June 2026

The Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation (MOSTI) has launched a RM185 million Research, Development, Innovation, Commercialisation and Economy (RDICE) programme to accelerate Malaysia's rise in the global semiconductor value chain. The initiative is being executed in partnership with the Academy of Sciences Malaysia through the Malaysia Science Endowment (MSE) and is squarely focused on advanced semiconductor packaging technology.

A centrepiece of the programme is the development of Malaysia's first proof-of-concept High Bandwidth Memory 4 (HBM4) test chip, a cutting-edge memory architecture used in high-performance computing, artificial intelligence accelerators, and next-generation graphics processing. Achieving a domestic HBM4 capability would position Malaysia several rungs higher in the global chip supply chain and attract new tiers of foreign and domestic investment.

MOSTI's stated goal is to help Malaysia capture a 7% share of the global advanced packaging market within the next five years. Malaysia already accounts for a significant share of conventional semiconductor back-end assembly and test work, but the government has long sought to move up the value chain toward more complex, higher-margin processes such as system-in-package (SiP) and 2.5D and 3D chip stacking.

The RDICE programme will fund university research collaborations, grant support for local chip design and materials companies, and the establishment of a national advanced packaging pilot line accessible to both academia and industry. The Ministry confirmed that partnerships with major multinational semiconductor firms already operating in Malaysia are being actively explored to co-develop talent and technology.

Industry watchers described the investment as timely, given the global semiconductor supply chain restructuring triggered by geopolitical tensions and the rapid expansion of AI-driven chip demand. Malaysia's established ecosystem of over 50 semiconductor multinationals, combined with this renewed R&D push, is expected to make the country an increasingly attractive location for advanced chip manufacturing investment over the coming decade.
