Ringgit Recovers to 3.98 Per USD as Global Risk Sentiment Improves

Trading Economics / The Edge Malaysia | 27 June 2026

The Malaysian ringgit strengthened by more than 1% to approximately 3.98 against the US dollar in recent trading, recovering from two-month lows that had seen the currency slide toward the 4.10 level. The rebound was triggered by improved global risk appetite after US President Donald Trump announced a two-week postponement of a potential military strike on Iran, reducing geopolitical risk and weakening the safe-haven appeal of the US dollar.

The ringgit had come under significant pressure earlier in the month, falling roughly 4% as US rate-hike expectations strengthened the greenback and weighed broadly on emerging-market currencies. Analysts noted that the ringgit's weakness was not unique to Malaysia but reflected a wider pattern of dollar strength affecting ASEAN peers including the Thai baht, Indonesian rupiah, and Philippine peso.

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) signalled it would step up measures to stabilise the currency, including targeted efforts to attract fresh foreign capital inflows and encourage major state-linked enterprises and large Malaysian corporates to repatriate their overseas earnings back into ringgit. The central bank has previously used administrative guidance and moral suasion as tools to support the currency without directly intervening in the open foreign exchange market.

Looking ahead, currency strategists said the ringgit's trajectory in the near term would be closely linked to US Federal Reserve signals on interest rate policy and the outcome of the upcoming Johor and Negeri Sembilan state elections, which could affect domestic investor confidence. Malaysia's strong economic fundamentals — underpinned by resilient exports, robust tourism recovery, and foreign direct investment in data centres — provide a supportive backdrop for the ringgit over the medium term.

Economists at several Malaysian investment banks maintained their year-end ringgit forecasts in the 3.85–3.95 range, contingent on the US dollar entering a gradual weakening cycle as the Federal Reserve moves toward an easing bias. They recommended that Malaysian importers take advantage of the current relief rally to hedge near-term foreign currency obligations.
