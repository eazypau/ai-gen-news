Malaysia Braces for El Niño Transition as Flash Flood Warnings Intensify

Eco-Business / MetMalaysia | Environment | 22 June 2026

Climate scientists and meteorological authorities have warned that Malaysia is entering a period of heightened weather volatility, with a transition to El Niño conditions predicted for the June–July 2026 window. The shift in climate pattern is expected to bring drier conditions to certain parts of the country while increasing the risk of intense, short-duration rainfall events in others — a combination that raises flash flood dangers in urban areas.

MetMalaysia has issued advisories urging local authorities in flood-prone areas of Klang Valley, Johor Bahru, and coastal Sarawak to review their drainage infrastructure and emergency response readiness. The agency noted that the South-West Monsoon season, which typically runs from May through September, has already produced several severe weather incidents in 2026.

The World Bank, in a recent report, warned that Malaysia is grappling with rising economic costs from flooding, with flood-related losses reaching RM636 million in 2025 even as the figure represented an improvement from previous years. The bank cautioned that without sustained investment in climate resilience, these costs could climb significantly as extreme weather events intensify globally.

Malaysia's Natural Disaster Management and Relief Committee has convened an emergency preparedness review, focusing on urban stormwater management systems in high-risk cities. Engineers and urban planners have pointed out that many existing drainage networks were designed for rainfall intensity levels that are now being regularly exceeded due to climate change.

Environmental NGOs have called on the government to accelerate its commitments under the Paris Agreement and invest more aggressively in nature-based solutions such as mangrove restoration, urban green corridors, and upstream reforestation, which can help absorb and slow rainfall runoff, reducing the severity of downstream flooding.
