UN Committee Commends Malaysia for Progress in Maternal and Infant Health Care

Bernama / OHCHR | Health | 22 June 2026

The United Nations Committee on the Rights of the Child has formally commended Malaysia for its significant achievements in improving maternal health care and reducing infant mortality rates, citing sustained government investment and the expansion of community-based health services as key contributing factors. The recognition was conveyed during the Committee's review of Malaysia's national report on the implementation of the Convention on the Rights of the Child.

Malaysia's maternal mortality ratio has declined notably over the past decade, driven by improved antenatal care coverage, better-equipped district hospitals, and increased access to skilled birth attendants particularly in rural and indigenous communities. The country's infant mortality rate has similarly improved, reflecting advances in neonatal care and nutrition programmes.

Health Minister Datuk Seri Dr Dzulkefly Ahmad welcomed the Committee's recognition, saying it validates the government's commitment to universal health coverage and the Madani government's agenda of leaving no one behind. He highlighted ongoing investments in upgrading rural health clinics, expanding specialist obstetric care, and strengthening the community health worker programme.

The Committee also raised several areas for continued attention, including ensuring equitable health access for Orang Asli communities, reducing teenage pregnancy rates, and addressing the nutritional needs of children in low-income households. These recommendations will form part of Malaysia's next cycle of action on child rights.

Non-governmental health organisations operating in Malaysia welcomed the international recognition while urging the government to address systemic gaps in healthcare delivery, particularly for undocumented migrant workers and their children, who face significant barriers to accessing maternal and child health services. The Ministry of Health has indicated it is reviewing policy options to address this challenge within its existing framework.
