Malaysia Begins Enforcing Social Media Ban for Children Under 16

MalaysiaNow | Technology | 22 June 2026

Malaysia has commenced active enforcement of regulations prohibiting children under the age of 16 from creating social media accounts, requiring platforms operating in the country to implement robust age verification mechanisms. The move places Malaysia among a growing cohort of governments worldwide taking firm action to protect minors' digital wellbeing.

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said platforms that fail to comply with the age restriction requirements face regulatory action, including potential fines and restrictions on their operations in Malaysia. He noted that the government had held consultations with major social media platforms over the past year and that most have already begun implementing age check improvements.

Digital rights advocates have given a cautious welcome to the policy, while raising concerns about privacy implications of age verification methods that require minors to submit identification documents. They urged the government to ensure that data collected for age verification purposes is stored securely and not used for other commercial or governmental purposes.

Child welfare organisations have praised the initiative, citing growing evidence of the harm that unregulated social media exposure can cause to adolescent mental health, self-image, and academic performance. They called on schools and parents to work alongside the government's enforcement efforts with complementary digital literacy programmes.

The enforcement comes amid a broader government push to regulate the digital space in Malaysia, including measures against online scams, cyberbullying, and the spread of harmful content. The government has indicated it will review the effectiveness of the under-16 ban in 12 months and consider whether the age threshold should be adjusted based on outcomes.
