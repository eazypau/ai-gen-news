Malaysia and Japan Forge New Cooperation in AI, Quantum Tech and Semiconductors

Malay Mail | Technology | 22 June 2026

Malaysia and Japan have formalised a new high-technology cooperation framework covering quantum computing, artificial intelligence, digital industries, and semiconductor manufacturing following a bilateral summit between Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim and Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi. The agreement is seen as a pivotal step in elevating the two nations' economic partnership to a technology-focused tier.

IBM has selected Malaysia as a strategic base to expand its quantum technology initiatives in Asia, a development that PM Anwar described as a strong vote of confidence in Malaysia's growing status as a regional technology hub. The tech giant is expected to establish quantum computing research facilities and workforce training programmes within Malaysia over the next two years.

The quantum technology collaboration with Japan will focus on joint research, talent development, and commercialisation pathways. Japanese firms including Fujitsu and Hitachi have expressed strong interest in establishing collaborative research centres in Malaysia, particularly in the Cyberjaya and Iskandar Malaysia technology zones.

On semiconductor cooperation, both countries agreed to explore joint investments in advanced chip packaging technology, an area where Malaysia is increasingly competitive. Japanese semiconductor equipment manufacturers have also pledged to expand their support infrastructure in Malaysia, which will facilitate capacity expansion by chip assembly and testing firms.

PM Anwar emphasised that Malaysia's participation in the global AI and quantum technology revolution is essential for achieving the country's vision of a high-income, knowledge-driven economy. He announced plans to establish a National Quantum Computing Roadmap to be unveiled before the end of 2026, coordinated across government, academia, and industry.
