Global AI Supercycle Drives Record Surge in Malaysia's Electronics Exports

The Edge Malaysia | Business | 22 June 2026

The global artificial intelligence investment supercycle is serving as a powerful tailwind for Malaysia's electrical and electronics (E&E) sector, which continues to record unprecedented export figures in 2026. Demand for chips, server components, and AI-enabling hardware has accelerated sharply as technology companies worldwide race to build out AI infrastructure.

Malaysia's strategic position in the global semiconductor supply chain has allowed it to capture a significant share of this AI-driven demand. Major chip manufacturers including Intel, Infineon, and several Asian semiconductor firms have expanded their Malaysian operations, boosting output and employment in the Penang, Kulim, and Klang Valley technology corridors.

Malaysia Semiconductor Industry Association (MSIA) President Datuk Seri Wong Siew Hai said the AI supercycle represents a generational opportunity for Malaysia to move further up the value chain, from assembly and testing toward chip design and advanced packaging. He called on the government to invest aggressively in talent development and research and development infrastructure.

The surge in E&E exports has also attracted renewed interest from global data centre operators. Several hyperscale companies, including Microsoft, Google, and regional players, have announced significant expansions of their Malaysian data centre footprints, further cementing the country's reputation as a preferred digital infrastructure hub in Southeast Asia.

Economists project that the AI-driven demand cycle could sustain elevated E&E export performance through at least 2027, provided Malaysia continues to manage its power infrastructure, skilled labour supply, and regulatory environment effectively. The government has signalled it will unveil additional incentives for high-value technology investments later this year.
