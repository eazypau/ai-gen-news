Malaysians Working Abroad to Receive Social Security Under New Traveller Scheme

New Straits Times | Business | 22 June 2026

Human Resources Minister Steven Sim Chee Keong has announced the launch of the Traveller Scheme, a new social security initiative that will extend Malaysia's SOCSO (Social Security Organisation) protections to Malaysians working in foreign countries. The scheme is targeted particularly at the significant number of Malaysian workers employed in Singapore, which hosts one of the largest communities of Malaysian migrant workers.

Under the Traveller Scheme, eligible Malaysian workers based overseas will be able to enrol in SOCSO's Employment Injury Scheme, gaining coverage for work-related accidents and occupational diseases even while working abroad. This represents a major policy expansion for SOCSO, which has traditionally focused on workers employed within Malaysian borders.

The Minister said the initiative reflects the government's recognition that a large number of skilled and semi-skilled Malaysian workers contribute significantly to the national economy through remittances and savings, and deserve the same social protections available to those working at home. He added that negotiations with Singapore's Ministry of Manpower have been constructive.

Malaysia's diaspora community in Singapore numbers in the hundreds of thousands, and many workers have long expressed concern about the gaps in social protection they face when living and working abroad. Labour rights advocates have welcomed the announcement but called for swift implementation and clear eligibility criteria.

The scheme is expected to be rolled out in phases beginning in the third quarter of 2026. The Human Resources Ministry said it will conduct outreach sessions in Singapore and other countries with significant Malaysian worker populations to educate beneficiaries on how to register and access their entitlements.
