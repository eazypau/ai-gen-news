World Bank Raises Malaysia's 2026 Economic Growth Forecast to 4.4%

The Edge Malaysia | Business | 22 June 2026

The World Bank has revised upward its 2026 economic growth forecast for Malaysia to 4.4%, from a previous estimate of 4.1%, reflecting the country's resilient domestic demand and robust export performance. The upgraded projection comes despite ongoing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and global monetary policy uncertainty.

The multilateral lender highlighted that Malaysia's economy grew by 5.2% in 2025, outperforming many regional peers, driven by a combination of strong private consumption, public investment in infrastructure, and buoyant electronics exports. The World Bank said this momentum is expected to carry into 2026, albeit at a slightly moderated pace.

The report noted that Malaysia's economic diversification strategy, including its push into digital services, data centres, and green energy, is beginning to yield tangible results. Foreign direct investment inflows have remained strong, with Malaysia continuing to attract significant capital from global technology companies looking to establish regional operations.

Risks to the outlook include a potential slowdown in China, a key trading partner, and the impact of any escalation in the Middle East on global oil prices. The World Bank also flagged domestic fiscal pressures related to subsidy rationalisation, noting that Malaysia's fuel subsidy reforms need to be managed carefully to avoid dampening consumer sentiment.

Finance Minister Tengku Zafrul expressed confidence that Malaysia's economic fundamentals remain sound and that the government's Madani Economy framework positions the country well for sustained growth. He announced that a mid-year economic review would be published next month to provide updated guidance to investors and policymakers.
