Malaysia Enforces Social Media Ban for Children Under 16

Malaysia on June 1, 2026, began enforcing sweeping new online safety rules barring millions of children younger than 16 from holding social media accounts, joining a growing global movement to tighten digital protections for minors. The enforcement, which follows months of legislative debate, requires all social media platforms with at least 8 million active users in Malaysia — including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X (formerly Twitter), and YouTube — to implement robust age-verification systems and block under-16 users from creating new accounts.

Age verification must be conducted through government-issued identification documents such as MyKad for Malaysian citizens or passports for foreign nationals. Platforms that fail to comply face penalties of up to RM10 million per violation. During a six-month transition period, existing users will be required to verify their ages, and those identified as under 16 will be given one month to download or transfer their data — including photos, videos, and messages — before restrictions are applied to their accounts.

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil said the rules are designed to protect children from online harms including cyberbullying, predatory behaviour, and the mental health impacts of excessive social media use. He cited research linking heavy social media consumption among teenagers to rising rates of anxiety, depression, and sleep disorders, arguing that government intervention is necessary to safeguard the nation's youth.

However, the policy has not been without controversy. Critics, including digital rights groups and child welfare organisations, have raised concerns about the data privacy implications of large-scale age verification systems, warning that requiring children and parents to submit identity documents to private tech companies could expose sensitive personal data to misuse or breaches. Legal experts at Jurist have also cautioned that the blanket ban could backfire by pushing teenagers away from regulated, safety-moderated platforms into unregulated corners of the internet where protections are even weaker.

Malaysia becomes one of the strictest jurisdictions in Southeast Asia on this issue, following the lead of Australia which implemented a similar ban in late 2024. The government has pledged to monitor implementation closely and work with international platform operators to ensure compliance, with enforcement expected to intensify after the six-month grace period concludes in late November 2026.
