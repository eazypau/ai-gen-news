BUDI95: RON95 Petrol Stays at RM1.99 — But Monthly Quota May Be Cut to 150L

The Malaysian government has confirmed that the subsidised RON95 petrol price will remain at RM1.99 per litre under the BUDI95 (Budi Madani RON95) targeted subsidy programme, providing relief to the majority of Malaysian drivers who depend on the fuel for daily transportation. However, senior government officials have signalled that the monthly subsidised quota per eligible user — currently set at 200 litres — may be reduced to 150 litres as part of ongoing efforts to improve subsidy targeting, reduce leakage, and manage fiscal pressures.

The BUDI95 programme, which fully replaced the blanket RON95 subsidy on September 30, 2025, uses MyKad and JPJ driving licence data to verify eligible recipients at petrol pump kiosks. Malaysian citizens aged 16 and above (17 for car drivers) can access the subsidised RM1.99/litre rate for their monthly allocation, with any additional fuel purchased at the unsubsidised market rate. Deputy Finance Minister confirmed that consumption data shows approximately 60% of Malaysians use less than 150 litres of RON95 per month, suggesting that a quota reduction to 150 litres would affect only a minority of users.

The World Bank, which has reviewed Malaysia's fuel subsidy reform programme, described BUDI95 as "a step in the right direction" for fiscal sustainability, particularly given the pressures arising from the West Asia crisis which has elevated global oil prices. The Washington-based institution highlighted that Malaysia's approach of maintaining subsidised prices for the majority of the population while ensuring the wealthy pay market rates represents a more equitable distribution of public resources than the previous blanket subsidy system, which disproportionately benefited high-income vehicle owners.

Since the programme launched, the government has been working to identify alternative petroleum sources from countries including Brazil and Canada to reduce Malaysia's dependence on Middle Eastern oil supplies, which have been subject to price volatility. Deputy Prime Minister Datuk Seri Fadillah Yusof confirmed that supply diversification negotiations are ongoing and that the government is confident of maintaining supply continuity at the subsidised RM1.99 price point throughout 2026.

Consumer groups have welcomed the price stability but expressed concern about any further reduction in the monthly quota, particularly for residents of suburban and rural areas who depend heavily on personal vehicles for commuting. The government has pledged to consult stakeholder groups before implementing any quota changes and to ensure that lower-income households in less-served public transport areas receive appropriate consideration in the final policy design.
