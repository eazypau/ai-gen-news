Thomas Cup 2026: Malaysia's Exit Exposes Gap with China — Pekan Ramli

Malaysia's campaign at the 2026 BWF Thomas & Uber Cup Finals in Horsens, Denmark, came to an abrupt and sobering end following a comprehensive 3-0 defeat to China in the quarterfinals. The lopsided result — which saw Malaysia unable to win a single rubber against the perennial champions — has reignited debate about the depth and quality of Malaysian badminton's men's team, with former national team coach Pekan Ramli calling for a frank assessment of the structural gap between Malaysia and the world's best.

Pekan Ramli, speaking to The Sun Malaysia, said the Thomas Cup exit reflects deep-seated issues in Malaysia's squad depth that go beyond individual talent. While acknowledging that Malaysia boasts several world-class performers, he noted that the men's doubles and mixed doubles departments remain consistently weaker than those of China, Indonesia, and even Japan. Ramli called on the Badminton Association of Malaysia (BAM) to invest more aggressively in developing complete, versatile players rather than relying on a handful of star singles athletes.

World number one and Malaysian ace Lee Zii Jia had delivered a brilliant performance in the group stage — including a memorable victory over Japan's number one player — but was unable to single-handedly carry the team through the knockout rounds against a dominant Chinese contingent. China's team, featuring multiple world champions and Olympic gold medallists in both singles and doubles, presented a wall of quality that Malaysia could not breach.

The defeat extends Malaysia's painful wait to reclaim the Thomas Cup, which the country last lifted in 1992. In the intervening 34 years, Malaysia has reached the final on several occasions but has been unable to dethrone China, Indonesia, or more recently rising Asian powers in the knockout stages. The loss has prompted calls for BAM to accelerate its talent development pipeline and reconsider the training methodology at the national academy.

On the women's side, Malaysia's Uber Cup campaign was similarly curtailed, having reached the last eight before elimination. The women's team were hampered by the pre-tournament withdrawal of star doubles player Pearly Tan due to a recurring back injury, which significantly weakened their doubles pairings. The BAM has pledged to review its sports science and injury management protocols to prevent such setbacks in future major tournaments.
