Anwar's Cabinet Reshuffle: New Ministers Face Opportunities and Challenges Ahead of 2028 Polls

A detailed analysis by ISEAS-Yusof Ishak Institute's Dr Ong Kian Ming examines the political implications and practical challenges facing the new ministers appointed in Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's December 2025 cabinet reshuffle. The reshuffle — which proved more substantial than initially anticipated — saw six deputy ministers elevated to full ministerial positions, one entirely new face appointed, and 14 ministers reassigned to different portfolios, resulting in a reconfigured cabinet intended to energise governance ahead of the next general election, scheduled for no later than 2028.

Among the most notable changes was the appointment of Dr Mohammed Taufiq Johari as Minister of Youth and Sports, replacing Hannah Yeoh who was assigned to the Prime Minister's Department handling Federal Territories. Sim Tze Tzin joined as Deputy Minister at the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI), a strategically important portfolio given Malaysia's ongoing efforts to attract high-quality foreign investment. Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek, who had faced persistent speculation about her removal, retained her position — a signal that Anwar values continuity in the education sector as the new Blueprint 2026-2035 is being implemented.

Ong's analysis highlights that the reshuffle was driven by a combination of political imperatives and governance needs. On the political side, Anwar needed to reward coalition partners who had supported the government through a turbulent period marked by internal party tensions, opposition electoral gains in by-elections, and cost-of-living discontent among voters. By redistributing portfolios and elevating promising younger politicians, Anwar aimed to freshen the government's image while consolidating his control over key policy areas.

From a governance perspective, the new ministers face the challenge of delivering tangible results in their portfolios within a compressed timeframe. With the 2028 general election looming, ministers have at most two years to establish a track record and demonstrate progress on economic reform, public services improvement, and the implementation of flagship policies such as the New Industrial Master Plan (NIMP 2030) and the National Energy Transition Roadmap (NETR). Ministries seen as underperforming will come under intense scrutiny from both the media and the opposition.

Political analysts note that the reshuffle also reflects Anwar's awareness of the need to keep coalition partners — particularly Barisan Nasional (UMNO) and the Borneo-based parties — satisfied with their share of cabinet positions. Balancing the ethnic, regional, and partisan dimensions of cabinet composition in Malaysia's multi-party coalition government is a perennial challenge that shapes ministerial appointments as much as individual merit. Whether the reshuffled cabinet can translate political stability into policy results will be the defining test of Anwar's third year in office.
