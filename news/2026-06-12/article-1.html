FIFA World Cup 2026 Opens Today — How Malaysians Can Catch Every Match

The 2026 FIFA World Cup officially kicked off today, June 12, with an electric opening ceremony and the first match between co-host Mexico and South Africa at the legendary Estadio Azteca in Mexico City. In Malaysia, the match was broadcast live at 3:00 AM Malaysian Time (MYT), drawing dedicated football fans across the country who stayed up through the night to witness history as the world's biggest sporting event began its 32-team, 104-match tournament.

Malaysians have multiple options to follow all 104 matches throughout the tournament. RTM (Radio Television Malaysia) will broadcast every game free-to-air on TV1, TV2, TV Okey, and Berita RTM channels. The RTMKlik streaming app, available on smartphones and Android TV devices, offers free live streaming for all matches. For those preferring a dedicated sports platform, Unifi TV has secured full broadcast rights and offers a daily pass for RM20, giving subscribers access to all match-day fixtures.

This year's tournament is co-hosted by three nations — the United States, Canada, and Mexico — marking the first time the World Cup has been held across three countries simultaneously. The expanded format features 48 teams competing across 16 venues, culminating in the final on July 19 at the MetLife Stadium in New Jersey. Key contenders include Brazil, France, Argentina (defending champions), England, and hosts USA.

For Malaysian fans, the time difference means most matches in the group stage will be screened in the early hours of the morning, but that has not dampened enthusiasm. Viewing parties and sports cafes across Kuala Lumpur, Penang, and Johor Bahru have already announced extended opening hours and special World Cup promotions. The Harimau Malaya national team did not qualify for the tournament, but Malaysian supporters have rallied behind various Asian and underdog sides throughout the competition.

The 2026 World Cup is expected to generate significant economic activity in Malaysia, from broadcasting revenue and sports merchandise sales to tourism from football fans visiting the region. Analysts project that public viewing events and hospitality spending will provide a measurable boost to Malaysia's consumer economy in June and July.
