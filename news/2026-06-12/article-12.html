Malaysia Semiconductor Sector Gains from China+1 Supply Chain Shift

Malaysia's semiconductor industry is reaping significant benefits from the accelerating global trend of supply chain diversification away from China, according to a research note published by Hong Leong Investment Bank (HLIB). The report highlights that Malaysia — which already commands approximately 13% of global semiconductor back-end packaging and testing — is capturing a disproportionate share of new factory orders and capital expenditure as multinational chipmakers and electronics assemblers implement their China+1 strategies to reduce geopolitical concentration risk.

Two specific chip categories are driving particularly strong growth for Malaysian manufacturers: optical semiconductors and power semiconductors. Optical chips are in massive demand for data centre interconnects and telecommunications infrastructure, while power semiconductors are essential components for electric vehicles, renewable energy systems, and industrial automation. Both segments are experiencing multi-year capacity shortages as the world accelerates its transition to electrification and digital infrastructure, creating sustained demand for Malaysian production capacity.

The semiconductor ecosystem centred in Penang — often dubbed the "Silicon Island of the East" — is expanding its capabilities beyond traditional back-end assembly and test operations. Several global chip firms have announced plans to establish design centres and advanced packaging facilities in Malaysia, signalling a gradual migration toward higher-value activities in the supply chain. Government incentives under the National Investment Aspirations framework and the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry's (MITI) industrial policy have been instrumental in attracting these higher-value activities.

Sum Technology Berhad's recent IPO on Bursa Malaysia, described as "riding the semiconductor and data centre growth wave," exemplifies the investor confidence in Malaysia's semiconductor growth story. The company, which provides precision engineering components for semiconductor equipment, is expected to benefit directly from the capital expenditure cycles of major chipmakers expanding their Malaysian operations. The IPO was reportedly oversubscribed, reflecting strong retail and institutional appetite for Malaysia's tech sector exposure.

Analysts caution, however, that Malaysia must address ongoing challenges including a shortage of engineers with advanced semiconductor process knowledge, competition from emerging regional rivals including Vietnam and India, and the need for faster regulatory approvals for new industrial facilities. MITI and MIDA (Malaysian Investment Development Authority) have pledged to streamline investment processes and work with universities to accelerate the pipeline of semiconductor-ready engineering graduates.
