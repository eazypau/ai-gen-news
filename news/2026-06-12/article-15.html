CTOS SME Biz Day 2026: Helping Malaysian MSMEs Thrive Through Digitalisation

The CTOS SME Biz Day 2026, held under the theme "Digitalisation, Innovate, Growth," brought together hundreds of business leaders, entrepreneurs, and policymakers to explore strategies for helping Malaysia's micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) navigate rapid technological change. The annual event, organised by CTOS Data Systems — one of Malaysia's leading credit information and analytics companies — focused on equipping SME owners with practical knowledge and tools for digital transformation, AI adoption, regulatory compliance, and sustainable business growth.

Keynote speakers at the event emphasised that digitalisation is no longer an optional add-on for Malaysian SMEs but a fundamental prerequisite for survival and competitiveness in today's economy. MSMEs that have successfully integrated digital tools — from e-invoicing and cloud accounting to digital marketing and data analytics — reported significantly higher revenue growth and customer retention rates compared to those still relying on purely manual processes. CTOS presented data showing that digitally enabled SMEs also have measurably better access to credit, as digital records provide lenders with more transparent and reliable financial histories.

A major highlight of the event was a panel discussion on artificial intelligence (AI) adoption for small businesses, featuring entrepreneurs who have successfully implemented AI tools for customer service automation, inventory management, personalised marketing, and fraud detection. Panellists debunked the myth that AI is only accessible to large corporations, showcasing affordable and user-friendly AI solutions that SMEs can deploy without significant technical expertise or capital investment. Representatives from MDEC (Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation) outlined government incentive programmes available to MSMEs for digitalisation costs.

Leadership and succession planning also featured prominently in the day's programme, addressing the unique challenges facing family-owned SMEs in Malaysia — which make up the majority of the country's business landscape — as they manage transitions to the next generation. Business coaches discussed how digitalisation can be a catalyst for professionalising family business governance and reducing the over-reliance on founding entrepreneurs that often hampers growth and resilience.

SMEs contribute approximately 38% of Malaysia's GDP and account for over 7 million jobs, making their digital transformation a matter of national economic importance. The government's SME Digitalisation Grant and PENJANA initiatives have helped thousands of businesses adopt digital tools since 2020, but observers note that uptake remains uneven, with many micro-enterprises in rural areas and traditional sectors still lagging. CTOS and partners pledged to extend their SME support programmes to underserved communities throughout the second half of 2026.
