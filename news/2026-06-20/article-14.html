Malaysia's RM470 Billion 2026 Budget Reform Push Remains on Track, Says Treasury

Source: New Straits Times Date: 20 June 2026 Politics

Malaysia's Treasury has confirmed that the Madani Government's record RM470 billion (approximately US$111 billion) 2026 federal budget is progressing on schedule, with key fiscal reform milestones on track for completion by year-end. PM Anwar Ibrahim reaffirmed the government's commitment to responsible fiscal management while navigating an increasingly complex global trade environment.

The budget, tabled in October 2025, represents the largest in Malaysian history and incorporates a series of revenue-enhancing measures including an expanded sales and service tax framework and the continued rationalisation of fuel subsidies that began with RON95 petrol reforms in 2024. Subsidy rationalisation has been described as politically sensitive but fiscally necessary, freeing up resources for targeted cash transfers to lower-income households through the Sumbangan Tunai Rahmah programme.

Development expenditure is focused on five priority corridors: digital infrastructure, green energy transition, rural connectivity, public transportation and healthcare capacity expansion. Capital spending on rail projects — including the Klang Valley MRT3 and a new light rail extension in Penang — is proceeding within budgeted parameters, though some procurements face minor delays due to global construction material price volatility.

The fiscal deficit for 2026 is projected at 3.8% of GDP, a modest improvement from 4.3% in 2025, as revenue collections from corporate taxes, petroleum income tax and the expanded indirect tax base offset higher operating expenditures. Rating agencies Moody's and Fitch have maintained Malaysia's investment-grade sovereign credit ratings, pointing to credible fiscal consolidation intent.

Opposition lawmakers have criticised the pace of reform as insufficient, arguing that structural subsidies to government-linked companies remain bloated and that the government has been slow to implement promised public sector wage reforms. The Treasury defended its approach as measured and deliberate, prioritising social stability while maintaining macroeconomic credibility with international investors.
