Malaysia's Social Media Ban for Under-16s Enters Force; Non-Compliant Platforms Face RM10 Million Fines

Source: Malay Mail Date: 20 June 2026 Policy

Malaysia's landmark legislation prohibiting children under the age of 16 from accessing social media platforms formally entered force in June 2026, making Malaysia one of only a few countries in the world to impose a statutory minimum age on social media use. Technology companies that fail to implement adequate age verification mechanisms face penalties of up to RM10 million (approximately US$2.5 million) per violation under the Communications and Multimedia Act.

The law requires platforms with more than one million Malaysian users to deploy robust age-verification systems before granting account access. Approved verification methods include MyKad (national identity card) cross-referencing, parental consent workflows with guardian identity checks, and third-party age estimation technology. Platforms must also delete existing accounts found to belong to under-16 users and retain proof of compliance for regulatory inspection.

The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) has been designated as the enforcement body and has pledged to conduct active compliance monitoring from the enforcement date. Meta (Facebook, Instagram), TikTok, X (formerly Twitter) and YouTube are among the platforms required to comply, all of which have publicly committed to implementing the necessary technical safeguards.

Advocates for the legislation argue that social media exposure has measurable negative impacts on adolescent mental health, body image and academic performance, and that parents lack sufficient tools to monitor and control their children's online activity. Critics of the ban, including digital rights groups, have raised concerns about data privacy risks inherent in large-scale identity verification, the effectiveness of workaround techniques available to tech-savvy teenagers, and the precedent set for broader internet censorship.

Malaysia joins Australia, which passed a similar law in late 2024, in taking a legislative approach to child social media protection. The government has indicated it will review enforcement outcomes after 12 months and adjust penalties or technical requirements as needed based on compliance data and child wellbeing indicators.
