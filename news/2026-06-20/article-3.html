Malaysia Evaluates Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Naval Strike Missile Export Licence

Source: New Straits Times Date: 20 June 2026 Politics

Malaysia is actively evaluating alternative missile suppliers following Norway's decision to revoke the export licence for the Naval Strike Missile (NSM) system, a development that has raised serious questions about the reliability of European defence partnerships. The Royal Malaysian Navy (RMN) had been in advanced discussions to procure the NSM as part of a broader naval modernisation programme.

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim responded sharply to the Norwegian action, warning that developing countries may be compelled to seek alternative partners if European nations continue to apply what he described as politically motivated conditions on legitimate defence transactions. Anwar emphasised that Malaysia's procurement decisions are guided solely by national security interests and do not reflect alignment with any particular geopolitical bloc.

Among the alternative systems under consideration are the South Korean Haeseong missile, the French MBDA Exocet, and several surface-to-surface systems from the ASEAN region. Defence ministry officials declined to name specific suppliers, saying the evaluation process remains at an early stage and that multiple technical and budgetary criteria must be satisfied before a decision is made.

The Norwegian decision is believed to be linked to broader European concerns about end-user agreements in the context of ongoing global conflicts, with some Nordic governments tightening export controls across a range of defence hardware. Malaysia has rejected any suggestion that its armed forces' procurement poses third-party proliferation risks.

Defence analysts say the episode underscores a broader trend of middle-power nations in Asia reassessing their dependence on Western defence supply chains, and could accelerate Malaysia's interest in deepening procurement ties with South Korea, Turkey and India — all of which have been actively marketing missile systems in Southeast Asia.
