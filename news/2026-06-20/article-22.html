100-Foot Chinese Dragon Boat from Hunan Sets Record on Historic Melaka River Voyage

Source: Sin Chew Daily Date: 20 June 2026 Lifestyle

A spectacular 100-foot traditional Chinese dragon boat from Hunan province has set a remarkable double record in Malaysia, becoming both the longest dragon boat ever to visit the country and the longest vessel to navigate the historic Melaka River. The ceremonial maiden voyage drew enthusiastic crowds lining both banks of the river and was celebrated as a vivid symbol of the deep cultural ties between Malaysia's Chinese community and the ancestral homeland.

The dragon boat, elaborately decorated with hand-carved dragon motifs and vibrant paintwork in red and gold, made its way along the Melaka River flanked by traditional ceremonial escorts and accompanied by the sounds of drums and gongs from the crew on board. The spectacle delighted tourists at the Melaka Riverside, which has been significantly transformed in recent years into a heritage tourism attraction featuring riverside restaurants, museums and night cruise services.

The voyage was organised in connection with the Dragon Boat Festival (Duanwu) cultural celebrations and is part of an ongoing series of cultural exchange initiatives between Malaysia and various Chinese provincial governments. Hunan province has deep historical connections with several Malaysian Chinese communities, particularly the Hakka dialect group, and the dragon boat was presented as a gift of friendship and cultural solidarity.

Melaka's Chief Minister welcomed the event as a boost for the state's tourism industry, noting that the spectacular imagery would generate significant social media coverage and international media interest, further raising Melaka's profile as a UNESCO World Heritage City with a vibrant living cultural scene. Local hospitality businesses reported increased bookings in the days surrounding the event.

The dragon boat will remain on display in Melaka for several weeks before proceeding to Penang, where a similar cultural programme is planned in partnership with the Penang state government. Malaysia-China cultural exchanges have been on an upward trajectory throughout 2026, supported by enhanced bilateral diplomatic engagement and a growing Chinese tourist presence in the country.
