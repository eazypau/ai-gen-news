Malaysia Confirmed as Host of 2027 SEA Games Across Kuala Lumpur, Sarawak, Penang and Johor

Source: Malay Mail / New Straits Times Date: 20 June 2026 Sports

Malaysia has been officially confirmed as the host nation of the 34th Southeast Asian Games in 2027, with events to be spread across four major host cities: Kuala Lumpur, Sarawak, Penang and Johor. The Games are scheduled to open on 18 September 2027 and conclude on 29 September 2027, bringing together athletes from all 11 ASEAN member states for a 12-day multi-sport competition.

The multi-city hosting model reflects Malaysia's intent to leverage the SEA Games as a national development catalyst, with each host city responsible for constructing or upgrading venues and using the event to boost tourism, showcase regional identity and develop local sporting infrastructure. Sarawak will host aquatics and outdoor adventure sports, Penang will focus on racquet sports and cycling, Johor on football and athletics, while Kuala Lumpur will serve as the main hub for the opening and closing ceremonies and the highest-profile events.

Malaysia last hosted the SEA Games in 2017, an edition widely praised for its organisation and atmosphere. The 2027 Games are expected to feature approximately 35–40 sports and around 10,000 athletes, officials and delegates. Malaysia's Olympic Council has begun preliminary planning and has engaged a specialist events management consultancy to lead the coordination effort across the four cities.

The government has pledged RM2.1 billion in infrastructure and operational funding for the Games, with a significant portion directed at venue upgrades and athlete village construction. Legacy planning is a priority, with post-Games use of facilities mapped out for community sports development, competitive training centres and tourism attractions.

Sports Minister Datuk Hannah Yeoh expressed ambition for Malaysia to finish in the top three on the overall medal tally, surpassing the country's 2017 performance. She announced an accelerated athlete development programme with increased funding for national sports associations across the priority sports disciplines identified for the Games.
