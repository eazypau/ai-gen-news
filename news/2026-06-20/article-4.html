PM Anwar Proposes 10-Year Constitutional Limit on Prime Minister Tenure

Source: The Edge Malaysia / Bloomberg Date: 20 June 2026 Politics

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has reaffirmed his intention to table legislation in parliament that would constitutionally limit the tenure of a Malaysian prime minister to ten years, or two full five-year terms, whichever comes first. The proposed amendment is among the most significant institutional reforms the Madani Government has committed to in 2026.

The proposal, first floated in early January, is designed to prevent the kind of entrenched executive power that critics argue characterised earlier administrations. Anwar framed the reform as a reflection of his government's commitment to democratic accountability, telling reporters that no single leader should hold the nation's highest office indefinitely.

Legal experts have noted that implementing a term limit would require a two-thirds parliamentary majority to amend the Federal Constitution, a threshold the unity government currently commands with the support of Pakatan Harapan, Barisan Nasional and the Sarawak and Sabah component parties. However, some coalition partners have privately expressed reservations about the constitutional route, preferring a statutory cap that could be amended more flexibly in future.

The proposal has drawn mixed reactions. Civil society groups broadly welcomed it as a structural safeguard, while some political scientists cautioned that it does not address the underlying practices of patronage and party financing that enable authoritarian tendencies. Opposition leaders from Perikatan Nasional called the move a political distraction, arguing that more urgent reform — including anti-corruption enforcement — remains stalled.

The government is expected to present a formal Bill in the third quarter of 2026, with parliamentary debate anticipated to be vigorous. If passed, Malaysia would join a handful of Southeast Asian nations with constitutionally defined executive tenure limits.
