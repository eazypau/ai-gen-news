Jho Low Case: Malaysia Continues Legal Pursuit Despite US Pardon Talks

Politics Free Malaysia Today 4 July 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has reiterated that any pardon decision regarding fugitive financier Low Taek Jho, better known as Jho Low, remains a matter for US authorities. Speaking to reporters on the sidelines of a parliamentary session, the PM said Malaysia will continue to pursue the 1MDB case through proper legal channels regardless of any international developments.

Jho Low, the alleged mastermind behind the multibillion-dollar 1MDB scandal, remains a fugitive. The US Department of Justice has charged him with money laundering and bribery offences linked to the theft of billions from the Malaysian state investment fund. Reports earlier this year suggested that discussions in the US regarding the case's future were ongoing, though no official pardon had been granted.

Malaysian officials have repeatedly emphasised that the country's own legal proceedings against those implicated in the scandal are separate from US federal cases. The Attorney General's Chambers has confirmed that Malaysia continues to monitor the situation and will take all available legal steps to secure accountability for those responsible for the largest financial scandal in the nation's history.

Civil society groups and opposition figures have urged the government to be more forthcoming about any diplomatic communications with the US on the matter. Several Malaysian non-governmental organisations have written to the Prime Minister's office requesting clarity on whether Malaysia is actively lobbying for extradition or whether any backroom deals are being negotiated.

The Jho Low saga remains a politically sensitive topic in Malaysia, touching on questions of institutional integrity, accountability and the rule of law. With the Johor state election approaching, opposition parties have sought to keep the issue in public discourse, reminding voters of the economic harm caused by the 1MDB scandal and pressing the government on the pace of asset recovery efforts.
