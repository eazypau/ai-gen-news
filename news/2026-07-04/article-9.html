I-2SEA Undersea Cable to Link India, Malaysia and Singapore for AI Era

Technology GovInsider Asia 4 July 2026

Microsoft and Singapore-based Lightstorm are leading a consortium to build I-2SEA, a 3,600-kilometer undersea cable system connecting India with Malaysia and Singapore. The cable, expected to go live in late 2029, is designed to support the rapidly escalating demand for AI, cloud computing and data centre capacity across Asia, where infrastructure bottlenecks have become increasingly acute.

Malaysia's inclusion in the I-2SEA route reflects the country's growing importance as a regional data centre hub. Over the past three years, billions of ringgit in data centre investments have flowed into Malaysia from hyperscalers including Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Oracle, drawn by competitive energy costs, political stability and a relatively straightforward regulatory environment. The new undersea cable will provide critical high-bandwidth, low-latency connectivity to support those facilities.

The cable landing stations in Malaysia are expected to be located in the Klang Valley and Penang, consistent with where the bulk of the country's data centre infrastructure is concentrated. Local telecom operators are anticipated to play roles as consortium members and as distributors of the additional capacity to enterprise customers across the country.

For India, I-2SEA provides an important diversification of its submarine cable connectivity, reducing dependence on routes that pass through potentially congested or geopolitically sensitive chokepoints. The direct link to Malaysia and Singapore gives Indian cloud providers and content delivery networks faster access to Southeast Asian markets and the broader Asia-Pacific region.

Digital economy analysts say that undersea cable capacity is increasingly viewed as critical national infrastructure, on par with ports and airports. The I-2SEA project joins a pipeline of other planned submarine cable projects across the Asia-Pacific, reflecting the recognition that the physical internet backbone must scale rapidly to keep pace with AI workloads and the explosive growth in digital services consumption across emerging markets.
