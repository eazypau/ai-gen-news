80% of Malaysian CEOs Say AI Sovereignty Is Now a Core Business Priority

Technology GovInsider Asia / IBM Malaysia 4 July 2026

A new study by IBM Malaysia reveals that 80 per cent of CEOs surveyed in the country now consider AI sovereignty essential to their business strategy, a sharp increase from previous years. The finding reflects a broader shift among Malaysian corporate leaders who increasingly view control over AI infrastructure, data and decision-making processes as a matter of competitive and national security.

AI sovereignty — the concept of nations and organisations maintaining control over the AI systems they rely on rather than being dependent on foreign platforms and models — has become a hot topic in the Malaysian public and private sectors alike. The survey found that concern is highest in finance, healthcare and critical infrastructure sectors, where the stakes of algorithmic dependence on foreign systems are perceived as highest.

IBM Malaysia and GovInsider are co-hosting a roundtable event for Malaysian public sector officials titled "Powering Digital Sovereignty in Malaysia with Data and AI" on July 14, 2026, in Putrajaya. The event aims to catalyse policy conversations around building domestic AI capabilities, establishing regulatory frameworks for AI governance, and developing the human capital needed to support a sovereign AI ecosystem.

The Malaysian government has been active on this front. The National AI Roadmap, updated earlier this year, sets out ambitions for Malaysia to be AI-ready by 2030, with specific milestones for domestic model development, data governance infrastructure and AI talent pipeline expansion. MDEC (Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation) plays a central coordinating role in aligning industry and government efforts towards these goals.

Tech industry observers note that the confluence of geopolitical tensions, trade restrictions on AI chips and growing data privacy concerns has made AI sovereignty a genuinely urgent business and policy issue across Southeast Asia. Malaysia's relatively advanced digital infrastructure and proactive government stance position it well to lead the region in building a sovereign AI framework, though significant investment in talent and local computing capacity will be needed to turn ambition into reality.
