Malaysia Launches SemiconStart to Supercharge Semiconductor Startups

Technology New Straits Times 4 July 2026

Malaysia has officially launched SemiconStart Malaysia, a 2026 Budget initiative designed to accelerate the growth of homegrown semiconductor startups. The programme offers a comprehensive support ecosystem including mentorship from industry veterans, access to global funding and investment networks, prototyping tools and resources, and commercialisation pathways for technology companies looking to scale.

The initiative reflects the government's strategic ambition to move Malaysia's semiconductor industry up the value chain. For decades, Malaysia has been a key participant in global chip assembly, testing and packaging operations — but the government now wants the country recognised not merely as a trusted assembler, but as a source of original innovation, intellectual property and globally competitive technology companies.

Science, Technology and Innovation Minister Datuk Chang Lih Kang said SemiconStart represents a deliberate shift in Malaysia's industrial strategy. Speaking at the launch event, he highlighted that the global semiconductor industry is undergoing a period of rapid transformation driven by AI demands, geopolitical supply chain reconfiguration and the push for domestic chip sovereignty in key consumer markets. Malaysia, he argued, is uniquely positioned to benefit from these trends given its established ecosystem and geographical advantages.

Selected startups under SemiconStart will receive up to RM2 million in seed funding alongside access to shared laboratory facilities at designated technology parks. Partnerships with global semiconductor giants including those with Malaysian operations will provide mentorship access and potential commercial collaboration opportunities for the most promising cohort companies.

The launch comes weeks after Malaysia signed new memoranda of understanding with several Taiwanese and South Korean chipmakers to deepen technology transfer and localisation of advanced processes. Industry observers see SemiconStart as a critical complement to those agreements, ensuring that domestic companies can grow alongside foreign direct investment to create a more self-sustaining semiconductor ecosystem in the country.
