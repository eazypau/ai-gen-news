World Cup Round of 32 Fever Grips Malaysia as Nations Battle for Glory

Sports Malay Mail 4 July 2026

The FIFA World Cup 2026 Round of 32 is in full swing, and Malaysia is experiencing football fever as fans across the country stay up through the night and early morning to catch the action from North America. The 48-team expanded tournament, hosted across the United States, Canada and Mexico, has delivered a string of upsets and dramatic results that have kept fans glued to their screens.

Malaysians can watch all 104 World Cup matches through RTM's television channels and streaming platform RTMKlik, as well as via Unifi TV. In a significant shift from previous tournaments, Astro failed to secure the primary broadcasting rights for this edition of the global sporting showpiece. The move has been broadly welcomed by fans, with many praising RTM's free-to-air coverage for expanding access to the tournament beyond pay-TV subscribers.

Given Malaysia's time zone — many hours ahead of the North American host cities — most matches kick off between midnight and noon MYT. Cafes, mamak restaurants, sports bars and public spaces across Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Johor Bahru and other major cities have been packed with fans defying the late hours to experience the electric atmosphere of World Cup football.

The Round of 32 has produced memorable moments, with several lower-ranked teams causing upsets against traditional football giants. Malaysia's vibrant football fan community, which rallies behind both Asian teams and their adopted favourites from Europe and Latin America, has been particularly vocal on social media, generating trending topics and viral moments that reflect the country's deep love for the beautiful game.

With the Round of 16 approaching, attention is turning to which Asian nations — South Korea, Japan and Australia all qualified from the group stage — can go deepest into the knockout rounds. Malaysian football fans, whose national team did not qualify, are channelling their passion into supporting the region's representatives and hoping that Asia can improve on its historically modest World Cup knockout stage record.
