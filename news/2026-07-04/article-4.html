Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.4% in Q1 2026, Outpacing Expectations

Business Ministry of Finance Malaysia 4 July 2026

Malaysia's economy grew at 5.4% in the first quarter of 2026, exceeding analyst expectations for the second consecutive quarter despite heightened global uncertainty stemming from ongoing conflict in West Asia. The strong performance was driven by resilient household spending, expanding investment activity and sustained global demand, particularly for electrical and electronics exports.

The unemployment rate fell to 2.9%, the lowest level recorded in a decade, while inflation remained moderate at 1.6% during the quarter — a balance that economists described as reflecting the strength of domestic demand without overheating. Bank Negara Malaysia's measured monetary policy stance was credited with helping maintain this equilibrium during a period of global turbulence.

However, the headline number masked pockets of weakness. Mining and quarrying contracted by 2.1%, reversing a 1.4% expansion in the previous quarter, while agriculture growth slowed to 2.6% from 5.7%. Manufacturing expanded at 5.9%, a marginal slowdown from 6.0%, and construction growth moderated to 7.7% from 10.9%, though both sectors remained in healthy positive territory.

Malaysia's full-year growth projection remains at 4% to 5% for 2026, according to Bank Negara Malaysia. The OECD, in its latest Economic Outlook, projects GDP growth of 4.2%. Both forecasts reflect expectations that the economy has sufficient momentum to weather the initial shocks from global disruptions, though economists warn that more painful effects from higher energy costs and supply chain disruptions may only begin surfacing in the second half of the year.

Visit Malaysia 2026 continues to provide a tailwind for the services sector, with tourist arrivals holding up despite geopolitical concerns. Malaysia's position as a net energy exporter also provides a natural hedge against energy price volatility, offering a degree of insulation that peer economies dependent on energy imports do not enjoy.
