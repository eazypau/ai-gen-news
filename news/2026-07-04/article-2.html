PH Promises Shenzhen-Style Transformation for Johor in Election Manifesto

Politics The Edge Malaysia 4 July 2026

Pakatan Harapan has launched its Johor election manifesto, pledging to transform the southern state into a high-value economic hub modelled after China's technology powerhouse Shenzhen. The manifesto, unveiled at a packed launch event in Johor Bahru, outlines a vision of Johor as a globally competitive innovation corridor anchored in advanced manufacturing, digital industries and green technology.

Central to the manifesto is a promise to create 250,000 new jobs over the next five years, leveraging Johor's strategic location adjacent to Singapore and its position within the Special Economic Zone (SEZ) that the federal government has been developing in collaboration with Singapore. PH also pledged investments in education infrastructure, affordable housing and public transportation connectivity within the Johor-Singapore corridor.

DAP secretary-general Anthony Loke said PH's victory in the Johor state election is crucial to strengthening checks and balances in the state. He argued that having PH as the state government would ensure that Johor's economic development is more inclusive, transparent and aligned with federal initiatives. The manifesto also includes commitments on local governance reform, including accelerating the path towards elected local councils.

Critics from BN dismissed the manifesto as overly ambitious, arguing that the Shenzhen comparison was a rhetorical flourish rather than a realistic development blueprint. BN campaign leaders pointed to their track record as the party that has managed Johor's development for decades, arguing for continuity over experimentation.

The manifesto debate highlights the broader contest of narratives in this election. PH is banking on a reform and growth agenda to appeal to urban professionals and younger voters, while BN is relying on its administrative experience and deep roots in Malay-majority constituencies. With polling day on July 11, both sides are racing to win over undecided voters in the state's many competitive seats.
