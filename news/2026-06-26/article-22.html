Sabah Special Economic Zone Proposal on Table for Government Assessment

New Straits Times · June 2026 · Economy

The federal government has signalled its openness to evaluating appropriate forms of cooperation with the Sabah state government should the state choose to pursue its economic transformation through the establishment of a Special Economic Zone (SEZ). The statement comes amid growing discussion within Sabah about the potential benefits of an SEZ model similar to the highly successful Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ).

Proponents of a Sabah SEZ argue that the state possesses significant untapped economic potential, particularly in industries such as agriculture, fisheries, tourism, logistics and green energy. Sabah's location at the eastern tip of the Malaysian peninsula, bordering Indonesia and with maritime access to the Sulu Sea and the broader Indo-Pacific region, positions it as a potential regional trade and logistics hub.

The BIMP-EAGA (Brunei-Indonesia-Malaysia-Philippines East ASEAN Growth Area) Vision 2035 document has already incorporated Sabah into two economic development corridors. The West Economic Corridor focuses on logistics and transportation, manufacturing, industry, tourism and digital and financial services, while the Central Economic Corridor targets cross-border trade, the halal industry, and logistics and supply chains.

A formal SEZ designation would involve the creation of preferential regulatory conditions — including customs duty relief, streamlined business registration, tax incentives and enhanced infrastructure — within a designated geographic area, with the aim of attracting both foreign direct investment and domestic business activity. Similar models have been transformative for states and regions across Southeast Asia.

Sabah's economic development has historically lagged behind Peninsular Malaysia despite the state's rich natural resources, and political leaders across party lines have expressed frustration with the pace of development. A well-designed SEZ, backed by federal cooperation and strategic private sector partnerships, could represent a significant step toward narrowing the economic gap and unlocking Sabah's considerable growth potential.
