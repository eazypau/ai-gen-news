Kopitiam Culture Drives Malaysia's Food Tourism Boom as Oriental Kopi Expands

Travel And Tour World · June 2026 · Lifestyle

Malaysia's domestic tourism sector is experiencing a remarkable transformation as café culture and food-based experiences emerge as primary drivers of travel demand across urban centres. Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Johor Bahru and Selangor have all seen a surge in visitors specifically seeking out food and café experiences, creating a new segment of Malaysian lifestyle tourism that industry observers are calling the "kopitiam economy."

At the centre of this trend is Oriental Kopi, a rapidly expanding kopitiam-inspired café brand that has grown from a handful of outlets to a nationwide presence in under three years. Oriental Kopi's model blends traditional Malaysian coffee shop aesthetics — marble tabletops, wooden furniture, vintage signage — with modernised menus, quality-controlled beverages and Instagrammable interiors that attract both local millennials and international visitors.

The brand's expansion strategy has been deliberately tied to tourism hotspots, with new outlets opening in Penang's heritage George Town, Malacca's historic Jonker Street area, and near major Kuala Lumpur attractions. This positioning has made Oriental Kopi outlets not just cafés but de facto tourism waypoints, drawing visitors who might not have otherwise explored surrounding neighbourhoods and local attractions.

Food tourism as a sector aligns well with Malaysia's broader Visit Malaysia 2026 campaign ambitions. Tourism officials have long promoted Malaysia's street food culture — from Penang's UNESCO-recognised hawker traditions to Kuala Lumpur's vibrant mamak culture — as a distinctive competitive advantage. The rise of upscale kopitiam and artisan café concepts extends this appeal to a more premium travel segment.

Malaysian food entrepreneurs are responding to both domestic and international demand. Export-oriented food businesses are exploring bringing kopitiam culture to Malaysian diasporas in countries including Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States. Meanwhile, at home, the kopitiam revival is encouraging young Malaysians to rediscover and take pride in their culinary heritage, blending nostalgia with contemporary lifestyle aesthetics.
