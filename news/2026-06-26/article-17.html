Malaysia Strengthens Air Links with China and Indonesia via Four New Services

Free Malaysia Today · 25 June 2026 · Tourism

Malaysia has bolstered its international air connectivity with the launch of four new airline routes, expanding links with China and Indonesia. The new services connect Kuala Lumpur to Chongqing and Dalian via Nanjing, and add direct routes between Melaka and Pekanbaru, as well as between Kota Bharu and Jakarta. The routes span commercial aviation and regional budget carriers.

The expansion of air links with China comes at a strategic time, as Chinese outbound tourism continues to recover and grow following years of restricted travel during the pandemic period. China remains one of Malaysia's most important tourism source markets, and the addition of new city-pair connections beyond the traditional Guangzhou-Kuala Lumpur and Shanghai-Kuala Lumpur corridors is expected to attract visitors from less-tapped interior cities.

The Melaka-Pekanbaru and Kota Bharu-Jakarta routes are significant for facilitating bilateral travel with Indonesia, Malaysia's closest neighbour and fellow ASEAN partner. Both routes serve communities with strong cultural and family ties across the Straits of Malacca, and are expected to be popular with both leisure and visiting friends and relatives (VFR) passengers.

Malaysia's aviation regulator and the Ministry of Transport have been actively promoting new route development as part of the country's post-pandemic connectivity agenda. Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA) and Kuala Lumpur International Airport 2 (klia2) continue to serve as the primary regional hubs, while secondary cities are increasingly targeted for point-to-point services that reduce travel friction for both tourists and business travellers.

Tourism Malaysia expressed strong support for the new routes, noting that better air connectivity directly feeds into the Visit Malaysia 2026 campaign's target of attracting record international arrivals. With over two million Indian tourists expected this year and Chinese arrivals on an upward trajectory, the new routes contribute meaningfully to Malaysia's broader tourism growth strategy.
