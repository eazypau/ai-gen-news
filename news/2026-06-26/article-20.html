Typhoon Mekkhala Detected Northeast of Sabah, No Significant Threat to Malaysia

Bernama · 23 June 2026 · Weather

Malaysia's Meteorological Department (MetMalaysia) issued a public advisory on June 23 regarding Typhoon Mekkhala, which was detected approximately 1,616 kilometres northeast of Kudat, Sabah, in the western Pacific Ocean. Authorities confirmed that the typhoon does not pose a significant direct threat to Malaysian territory based on its current trajectory and projected movement.

Typhoon Mekkhala is tracking away from Malaysia's coastline and is expected to continue moving in a direction that keeps it well clear of Sabah and Sarawak. MetMalaysia noted that while the typhoon's outer bands could bring some increased cloud cover and occasional showers to parts of Sabah, no extreme weather conditions are anticipated on the Malaysian side of the South China Sea.

The advisory was issued as a precautionary measure to keep the public informed and to alert maritime operators and fishermen in Sabah waters to remain vigilant. Vessels operating in the open sea northeast of Sabah were advised to monitor weather updates closely and exercise caution, as conditions in the broader Western Pacific region can shift as typhoon systems intensify or change trajectory.

MetMalaysia will continue to monitor Typhoon Mekkhala and issue updated advisories should the situation change. The department uses a network of weather radars, satellite imagery and international meteorological data to track tropical cyclone systems in the region, providing timely warnings to protect coastal communities and maritime stakeholders.

Malaysia is geographically shielded from direct typhoon landfalls by the Philippine archipelago, which acts as a natural barrier for most storm systems originating in the western Pacific. However, the seasonal period from June through November sees elevated tropical cyclone activity in the region, and MetMalaysia maintains heightened monitoring during these months to ensure public safety.
