How Malaysians Can Watch FIFA World Cup 2026 — Complete Guide

Malay Mail · 7 June 2026 · Sports

The 2026 FIFA World Cup, which kicked off on June 12 across venues in Canada, Mexico and the United States, has transformed viewing habits for millions of Malaysian football fans. In a significant shift from previous tournaments, Astro — which had held exclusive World Cup broadcasting rights in Malaysia for over two decades — failed to secure the rights for 2026, opening the door for free-to-air options.

Malaysians can now watch all 104 matches of the expanded 48-team tournament through three main platforms. RTM (Radio Television Malaysia) is broadcasting a slate of matches for free over the air. Unifi TV, TM's internet television platform, is also carrying the World Cup, while the government's MyGOV Malaysia app provides additional live streaming access for users registered with a MyDigital ID.

Registering for MyDigital ID to access RTM's live broadcast through the MyGOV app is free and straightforward, requiring only a valid Malaysian identity card. The move has been welcomed as a democratisation of World Cup access, with fans no longer needing an expensive Astro subscription to follow the tournament in full.

Given the time zone difference between Malaysia (UTC+8) and the North American host venues, most World Cup matches kick off between midnight and noon Malaysian time. Late-night kickoffs have become a familiar feature of Malaysian football culture, with mamak restaurants and sports bars reporting surging business during the tournament period.

The 2026 World Cup features 80 group stage matches, 24 round of 16 ties, 8 quarter-finals, 4 semi-finals, one third-place play-off and the final on July 19. Malaysia, while not among the 48 competing nations, has embraced the tournament enthusiastically, with football fans rallying behind Asian representatives including Japan, South Korea, Saudi Arabia and Australia.
