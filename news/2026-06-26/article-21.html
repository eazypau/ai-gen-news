Malaysia Removes 755 Offensive 3R Online Content Items Since 2022

The Sun Malaysia · June 2026 · Social

Malaysian service providers have removed a total of 755 items of online content classified as offensive to the "3R" sensitivities — race, religion and the royal institution (Raja) — between January 1, 2022, and June 1, 2026. The figures were released by the authorities as part of ongoing transparency reporting on content regulation under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998.

The removals reflect Malaysia's sustained effort to police digital content that has the potential to inflame ethnic or religious tensions in the country's diverse multi-cultural society. Malaysia is home to Malay, Chinese, Indian and indigenous communities, along with a plurality of religious traditions, and successive governments have prioritised social harmony as a foundational national value.

The 3R framework has been operationalised through coordination between the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC), internet service providers and social media platform operators. Platforms including Facebook, TikTok, X (formerly Twitter) and YouTube have been required to comply with government takedown orders within specified timeframes, with larger platforms subject to tighter response deadlines.

Critics of the content removal regime have raised concerns about the potential for the framework to be used to suppress legitimate political dissent or satire under the broad "royal institution" category. Civil liberties groups have called for clearer public criteria and an independent review mechanism to distinguish genuine hate speech from protected political expression.

The government has maintained that the removals target only content that incites hatred or that could lead to real-world harm, not ordinary criticism or commentary. Digital rights advocates continue to monitor the situation, noting that the increasing sophistication of AI-powered content moderation tools makes it more important than ever to ensure robust safeguards against over-removal of lawful expression.
