TMJ: Malaysia Must Fix Domestic Football Woes Before Dreaming of World Cup Hosting

New Straits Times · June 2026 · Sports

Tunku Ismail Sultan Ibrahim (TMJ), the Crown Prince of Johor and one of Malaysia's most outspoken football figures, has delivered a blunt verdict on suggestions that Malaysia should aspire to host the FIFA World Cup: the country should "forget about it" until the sport's fundamental problems at home are resolved.

In remarks that quickly went viral, TMJ cited two particularly damning issues — dilapidated stadium facilities that fall short of international standards, and the ongoing scandal of footballers going unpaid by their clubs. He argued that a nation that cannot pay its own players and maintain proper training infrastructure has no business pitching itself on the world stage as a host candidate.

TMJ has long been a prominent and often controversial voice in Malaysian football, having served as president of the Football Association of Malaysia (FAM). His blunt communication style has earned him a large following among football fans who appreciate candid assessments over public relations optimism. His latest remarks were widely shared on social media and drew a mix of agreement and debate.

The backdrop to TMJ's comments is the spectacular success of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, which has generated renewed enthusiasm globally for football and prompted many nations to revive hosting ambitions. ASEAN as a bloc has occasionally discussed a joint hosting bid in the 2030s, though concrete plans remain at a preliminary stage.

Football Malaysia officials have not formally responded to TMJ's statement, though several football federation insiders acknowledged privately that structural governance reforms remain a work in progress. For now, TMJ's message is clear: clean up the domestic game first, and the world stage can come later.
