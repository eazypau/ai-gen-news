Malaysia Inflation Climbs to 2.0% in May, Highest Since July 2024

Malay Mail · June 2026 · Economy

Malaysia's headline Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose to 2.0% year-on-year in May 2026, up from 1.9% in April, marking the highest reading since July 2024. The increase came in slightly below the market consensus forecast of 2.1%, offering a measure of comfort to policymakers monitoring cost of living pressures.

The uptick in inflation was driven primarily by higher food and non-alcoholic beverage prices, alongside increases in the housing, water, electricity and gas categories. Transport costs also contributed modestly to the month-on-month rise, reflecting the gradual pass-through of global fuel price movements into domestic prices.

Economy Minister Rafizi Ramli reiterated that the government expects Malaysia's full-year inflation rate to remain between 1.5% and 2.5% in 2026, describing the current pace of price increases as manageable and below the global average of approximately 4.3%. He attributed the controlled inflation environment to targeted subsidy policies and prudent fiscal management.

Core inflation — which excludes volatile food and fuel prices — remained relatively steady, suggesting that the headline increase has not yet broadened into generalised price pressure across the economy. Bank Negara Malaysia is expected to maintain the Overnight Policy Rate at its current level at the next Monetary Policy Committee meeting, given that inflation remains within the comfort zone.

Consumer advocacy groups have, however, cautioned that average Malaysians continue to feel squeezed in areas such as fresh produce, dining out and utility bills. The government's ongoing review of targeted subsidies for RON95 petrol and utility tariffs will be closely watched for any potential inflationary effects in the second half of 2026.
