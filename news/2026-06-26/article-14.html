Malaysia's Carbon Tax Kicks In for Iron, Steel and Energy Sectors in 2026

NoviqTech / Trexon · June 2026 · Environment

Malaysia has moved to implement a carbon tax in 2026, with the initial phase targeting the iron, steel and energy industries — among the country's most emissions-intensive sectors. The tax forms part of Malaysia's broader commitment to reducing greenhouse gas intensity and transitioning to a lower-carbon economy, as outlined in the National Energy Transition Roadmap (NETR).

Revenue generated from the carbon tax will be strategically reinvested into green technology research and development programmes, supporting the development of renewable energy capacity and energy efficiency improvements across the industrial sector. Industry groups have broadly accepted the policy, though many have called for a longer transition timeline and more detailed guidance on compliance mechanisms.

An additional driver for action is Malaysia's exposure to the European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), which entered into full effect in 2026. Under CBAM, Malaysian exporters of cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen must purchase carbon certificates for every tonne of emissions embedded in their exports to the EU. Products without low-carbon credentials face significant additional costs, putting Malaysian exporters at a competitive disadvantage against producers from countries with more mature carbon markets.

The introduction of a domestic carbon price is seen as essential to help Malaysian companies prepare for CBAM compliance, build carbon accounting capabilities, and ultimately compete on a level playing field in global green trade. Malaysia's carbon market framework is expected to evolve, with discussions ongoing about the scope, price trajectory and sectoral coverage of the tax in subsequent phases.

Environmentalists and green economy advocates have broadly welcomed the carbon tax as a long-overdue policy step, though they stress that the initial price level must increase over time to provide a sufficiently strong signal for capital reallocation away from high-emission activities. Malaysia is among a growing number of ASEAN economies taking concrete steps on carbon pricing, following Singapore's pioneering carbon tax implementation in 2019.
