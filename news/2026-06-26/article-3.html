Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.4% in Q1 2026, Driven by Domestic Demand

Bank Negara Malaysia · June 2026 · Economy

Malaysia's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) expanded by 5.4% in the first quarter of 2026, according to data released by Bank Negara Malaysia. The growth moderates from the 6.2% recorded in the final quarter of 2025 but remains robust, underpinned by steady domestic consumption and continued strength in the country's key export sectors.

Domestic demand was the primary growth engine in Q1, supported by firm private consumption and ongoing public infrastructure investment. The government's budget allocations for development expenditure continued to flow through into economic activity, particularly in construction, utilities and digital infrastructure sectors.

Malaysia's electrical and electronics (E&E) exports remained a bright spot, benefiting from sustained global demand for semiconductors and related components. The E&E sector, which accounts for a significant share of Malaysia's total merchandise exports, continues to attract foreign direct investment as global technology companies seek to diversify their supply chains.

Headline inflation increased to 1.6% in the first quarter, even as core inflation moderated to 2.1%, indicating that while price pressures remain present, they are not broadening significantly. Bank Negara maintained its policy rate at the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) level, signalling confidence in the economy's ability to sustain growth without requiring additional monetary stimulus.

For the full year 2026, the government targets GDP growth within the range of 4% to 5%. The Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers projects 4.7% growth if global trade conflicts remain contained, though notes that escalating geopolitical tensions could trim the forecast to between 3.8% and 4.2%.
