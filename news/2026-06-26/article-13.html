Each Malaysian Produced 8.16 Tonnes of CO₂ in 2024 — Nearly Double 30 Years Ago

Malay Mail · June 2026 · Environment

Carbon dioxide emissions per capita in Malaysia reached 8.16 tonnes in 2024, according to data highlighted in recent environmental reporting. The figure is nearly double what it was 30 years ago, underscoring the rapid pace of industrial growth and energy consumption that has accompanied Malaysia's economic development since the 1990s.

The government has acknowledged the trend as a serious concern and reiterated Malaysia's commitment to achieving net zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. Interim milestones include reducing the emission intensity of GDP by 45% by 2030 relative to 2005 levels, in line with Malaysia's third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) submitted under the Paris Agreement.

Malaysia's emissions profile is heavily influenced by its energy mix, which remains dominated by coal and natural gas for electricity generation. The National Energy Transition Roadmap (NETR), launched in 2023, charts a course toward 70% renewable energy in the national grid by 2050, though analysts note that the current pace of transition falls short of what is required to hit net zero in the same year.

The transportation and manufacturing sectors are also significant contributors to Malaysia's carbon footprint. The government's push to expand public transport in the Klang Valley and encourage electric vehicle adoption is expected to yield emissions reductions in the medium term, though the scale of impact remains limited compared to power sector transformation.

Environmental groups have called for stronger climate policy instruments, including faster deployment of large-scale solar, stronger building efficiency standards and expanded forest protection measures. The upcoming implementation of a carbon tax on the iron, steel and energy sectors is seen as a positive step, but advocates argue the scope and price level need to be significantly expanded to drive the structural changes needed to meet Malaysia's net zero commitment.
