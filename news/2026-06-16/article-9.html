Malaysia and Turkmenistan to Sign Long-Term Hydrocarbons Partnership on June 19

Malaysia and Turkmenistan are set to sign a landmark long-term hydrocarbons cooperation agreement on June 19, 2026, as part of Malaysia's strategic push to diversify and strengthen its energy supply chain. The deal is expected to open new avenues for oil and gas collaboration between the two nations and reflects Malaysia's broader energy diplomacy agenda.

Turkmenistan possesses the world's fourth-largest proven natural gas reserves, centred on the massive South Yolotan field. Malaysia's national petroleum company, Petroliam Nasional Berhad (Petronas), has been actively pursuing international upstream partnerships to secure long-term gas supplies and enhance its global exploration and production footprint.

The cooperation agreement is expected to cover joint exploration and development activities, technical assistance, and potentially liquefied natural gas (LNG) supply arrangements. Malaysia's advanced LNG infrastructure, including the Bintulu LNG complex in Sarawak — one of the world's largest LNG export facilities — positions it well to process and redistribute Central Asian gas to global markets.

Energy security has been a growing priority for Malaysia, which is itself a net oil and gas exporter but faces declining domestic reserves. Partnerships with resource-rich nations such as Turkmenistan align with Malaysia's strategy to maintain its role as a significant player in global energy supply chains while ensuring domestic energy security over the long term.

The signing ceremony is expected to be attended by senior officials from both governments. This deal also builds on Malaysia's broader Central Asia engagement, which has seen increased diplomatic and commercial interactions with Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan, and Uzbekistan as part of Malaysia's South–South cooperation framework.
