Malaysia Attracts RM92.8 Billion in Q1 2026 Investments, Creating 50,000+ Jobs

Malaysia recorded an impressive RM92.8 billion in total approved investments for the first quarter of 2026, according to the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA). Japan emerged as the top foreign investor, underscoring Malaysia's continued appeal as a preferred destination for manufacturing and technology investments amid global geopolitical uncertainty.

Domestic investments rose 13% year-on-year, reflecting growing confidence among local businesses in Malaysia's economic fundamentals. The investment approvals are expected to create more than 50,000 new jobs, spread across manufacturing, services, and primary industries. High-value industries such as semiconductors, data centres, and green energy attracted a significant share of the investment commitments.

The manufacturing sector continued to dominate investment inflows, with major commitments in the electrical and electronics (E&E) subsector — Malaysia's largest export category. Several multinational corporations announced expansions of existing facilities in Penang, Selangor, and Johor, attracted by Malaysia's competitive cost base, skilled workforce, and strong logistics infrastructure.

The services sector also performed strongly, driven by a surge in investments related to data centre construction and digital services. Malaysia has positioned itself as a regional digital hub, with Kuala Lumpur and Johor Bahru among the top choices for hyperscale data centre operators from the United States, Singapore, China, and Europe.

The government credited the strong Q1 2026 numbers to its New Industrial Master Plan 2030 (NIMP 2030) and proactive investment promotion activities carried out by MIDA and the Malaysia External Trade Development Corporation (MATRADE). Deputy Economy Minister Datuk Mohd Shahar Abdullah noted that the upcoming KL20 Summit in June would further attract venture capital and startup investments into Malaysia's growing technology ecosystem.
