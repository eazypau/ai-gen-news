IMF Projects Malaysia GDP Growth at 4.3% in 2026, Inflation Stays Low at 1.9%

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) projects Malaysia's real GDP growth to moderate slightly from 4.6% in 2025 to 4.3% in 2026, primarily reflecting the impact of higher US tariffs on Malaysian exports. Despite the slight slowdown, the IMF assessment highlights Malaysia's notable resilience against global trade tensions and policy uncertainty.

Inflation is projected to remain low and stable at approximately 1.9% in 2026, providing the government and Bank Negara Malaysia with room to maintain an accommodative monetary policy stance. The IMF commended Malaysia's prudent fiscal management and ongoing subsidy rationalisation efforts, which have helped improve fiscal sustainability without triggering major inflationary pressure.

The IMF's 2026 Article IV Consultation report highlighted that Malaysia's economy continues to be supported by strong domestic consumption, solid employment growth, and a global technology-sector upcycle that benefits Malaysian semiconductor and electronics exporters. Foreign direct investment (FDI) inflows remained robust, particularly in high-value manufacturing and digital infrastructure.

The IMF noted that risks to the outlook are balanced. On the downside, a sharper-than-expected global slowdown, further US tariff escalations, and a weakening in the semiconductor cycle could weigh on growth. On the upside, stronger-than-expected investment from the data centre and green energy sectors could provide positive surprises to the growth trajectory.

The report also recommended that Malaysia continue to strengthen its social protection framework, expand the progressive wage policy, and accelerate structural reforms in the labour market to boost productivity and long-term competitiveness. Bank Negara Malaysia's Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) was judged to be broadly appropriate for current economic conditions.
