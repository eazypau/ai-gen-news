PAS-Bersatu Split Ends Decade-Long Opposition Alliance Ahead of GE16

The once-formidable partnership between PAS and Parti Pribumi Bersatu Malaysia (Bersatu) has officially collapsed, ending a political alliance that fundamentally shaped Malaysia's opposition landscape over the past decade. The public separation, confirmed by PAS leadership this week, marks one of the most consequential shifts in Malaysian opposition politics in recent memory.

The split had been simmering since the expulsion of Hamzah Zainudin from Bersatu in February 2026. PAS had long expressed discomfort with Bersatu's internal leadership struggles and was privately supportive of Hamzah's camp. The establishment of Parti Wawasan Negara (PWN) by Hamzah provided the catalyst for PAS to formally distance itself from the Muhyiddin-led Bersatu wing.

Political observers say the fallout gives rise to a new opposition configuration ahead of the 16th General Election (GE16). PAS, with its strong grassroots base particularly in the east coast states of Kelantan and Terengganu, is now expected to align more closely with PWN, while Bersatu faces the challenge of reconstituting its own alliances without PAS's traditional vote base in rural Malay-Muslim heartlands.

The break-up also has ramifications for Sabah and Sarawak-based parties within the PN coalition, who must now decide which faction to back. Some analysts suggest this could benefit Pakatan Harapan (PH), which leads the federal government under Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim, as the opposition remains divided going into GE16.

With the Dewan Rakyat resuming on June 22, the immediate political focus will be on how the opposition organises itself in Parliament, including leadership designations, committee placements, and the formal acknowledgement of PWN as a recognised political entity. The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether the opposition can present a unified front or remain fragmented.
