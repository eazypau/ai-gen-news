Malaysia Healthcare Draws Strong Indonesian Interest at Malaysia Fair Jakarta 2026

The Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC), in collaboration with Tourism Malaysia, recorded outstanding visitor engagement at Malaysia Fair Jakarta 2026, held from June 4 to 7 at Gandaria City mall in Indonesia's capital. The event underscores sustained and growing interest among Indonesian consumers in Malaysia's healthcare, wellness, and medical travel offerings.

The fair was held under the theme "Healing Meets Hospitality," in conjunction with Malaysia Year of Medical Tourism 2026 (MYMT 2026). The event brought together Malaysia's leading healthcare providers, wellness operators, tourism stakeholders, and higher education institutions under one roof, providing Indonesian visitors with a comprehensive showcase of what Malaysia has to offer as a healthcare and education destination.

Indonesian patients have historically been one of the largest groups of medical tourists visiting Malaysia, drawn by the combination of world-class medical facilities, English-speaking medical professionals, affordability relative to Singapore, cultural and linguistic familiarity, and convenient flight connections. Hospitals represented at the fair included Sunway Medical Centre, Pantai Hospital Kuala Lumpur, Gleneagles Kuala Lumpur, and several Penang-based medical centres.

Renowned higher education institutions including Taylor's University, UCSI University, INTI International University, Todak Academy, and MILA University also participated in the event, consulting with prospective students and their parents about course offerings, scholarships, and campus life. The dual healthcare-education positioning reflects Malaysia's strategy to attract Indonesian visitors across multiple verticals.

MHTC CEO Sherene Azli said the strong turnout at Malaysia Fair Jakarta 2026 reflects sustained confidence in Malaysia's healthcare ecosystem and confirms Indonesia as one of Malaysia's most important medical tourism source markets. Malaysia aims to attract over 1.5 million medical tourists and generate RM7.4 billion in healthcare travel receipts by year-end 2026.
