B15 Biodiesel Rolled Out Nationwide as Malaysia Boosts Green Energy Agenda

Malaysia officially launched its B15 biodiesel blend for nationwide use from June 1, 2026, as part of the government's ongoing green energy agenda and commitment to the palm oil sector. Under the new standard, all diesel sold in the country now contains 15% palm oil-based biodiesel, an increase from the previous B10 (10%) mandate.

The move was announced by Deputy Prime Minister Datuk Seri Ahmad Zahid Hamidi, who indicated that the transition to B15 would help reduce Malaysia's carbon footprint while simultaneously supporting the livelihoods of hundreds of thousands of smallholder palm oil farmers across the country. Malaysia is the world's second-largest palm oil producer.

Vehicle compatibility has been a key concern ahead of the rollout. The government confirmed that most modern diesel engines can handle B15 without any modifications, and that engine manufacturers have been briefed well in advance. Older vehicle owners and operators of heavy machinery have been advised to check with their vehicle manufacturers regarding compatibility.

The biodiesel mandate is part of Malaysia's broader National Bioenergy Policy and is tied to the country's Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) commitments under the Paris Agreement. The palm oil industry, represented by the Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) and the Malaysian Palm Oil Council (MPOC), has welcomed the higher blend mandate as it creates additional domestic demand for crude palm oil (CPO).

Looking ahead, Malaysia is on a roadmap to progressively increase the biodiesel blend to B20 and beyond in the coming years, with expansion to cover additional vehicle categories and sectors including marine and off-road applications. The government sees biodiesel as a key pillar of its energy transition strategy, alongside the expansion of solar power and electric vehicle adoption.
