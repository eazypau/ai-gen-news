Marine Police Seize 5,150 Litres of Diesel in Hulu Jabor Crackdown

Officers from Marine Police Region Three conducted a targeted enforcement operation at a premises in Hulu Jabor, successfully seizing 5,150 litres of diesel suspected to have been illegally stored and potentially earmarked for smuggling or unauthorised resale. Several individuals were detained at the scene for further investigation by the authorities.

The raid is part of ongoing nationwide enforcement operations against fuel smuggling, illegal bunkering, and subsidy abuse — a persistent challenge for Malaysian enforcement agencies given the country's subsidised fuel pricing structure. Diesel, which continues to receive government subsidies for commercial and agricultural use, remains a target for illicit traders seeking to exploit price differentials between Malaysia and neighbouring countries.

Marine Police Region Three, which covers the coastal and riverine areas of southern Peninsular Malaysia, has been conducting regular patrols and intelligence-led operations to intercept fuel smuggling networks. The Hulu Jabor area, with its network of rivers and proximity to the eastern coastline of Pahang, has been identified as a transit point for illicit goods.

The seized diesel has been handed over to the relevant authorities for further action, while those detained face investigation under the Control of Supplies Act 1961 and potentially other applicable legislation. Penalties for illegal fuel storage and smuggling include heavy fines and imprisonment.

The enforcement action underscores the government's commitment to curbing fuel subsidy leakage, which costs the national treasury billions of ringgit annually. Fuel subsidy reforms introduced over the past two years have narrowed the arbitrage opportunity somewhat, but enforcement agencies say illicit fuel networks continue to adapt and operate, necessitating sustained operational vigilance.
