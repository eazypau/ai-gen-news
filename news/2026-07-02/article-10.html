Malaysia Unemployment Falls to 2.9%, Lowest in a Decade

BusinessToday 2 July 2026 Economy

Malaysia's unemployment rate fell to 2.9% in Q1 2026 — the lowest level in a decade — as continued private sector investment and government job creation programmes drove robust employment growth. Department of Statistics Malaysia data showed that approximately 16.4 million Malaysians were employed during the quarter, with the largest gains recorded in manufacturing, services and the digital economy.

Meanwhile, the consumer price index rose by a modest 1.6% year-on-year in Q1 2026, well within the government's comfort zone and reflecting the moderating effect of fuel subsidy management alongside stable food prices. The combination of low unemployment and low inflation — sometimes called the "dual mandate sweet spot" — was welcomed by policymakers and business groups alike.

Chief Statistician Datuk Seri Mohd Uzir Mahidin attributed the employment gains partly to the expansion of the gig economy and digital freelance platforms, which have brought more workers — particularly youth and women — into the formal labour force. He noted that Malaysia's labour force participation rate rose to 70.3% in Q1, the highest since the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted employment patterns.

However, economists cautioned that the headline unemployment rate may mask structural underemployment, particularly among recent graduates working in jobs below their qualification level. Human Resources Minister Steven Sim said the government's MyNextSkills programme, which has upskilled over 280,000 workers since 2024, would continue to address skills mismatches in the labour market.

Looking ahead, the tight labour market is expected to support wage growth and private consumption, which is forecast to remain the primary engine of Malaysia's economic expansion in the second half of 2026. The central bank indicated it will monitor for signs of wage-driven inflationary pressure as the year progresses.
