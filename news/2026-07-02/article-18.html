Malaysian Unions Urge 1.13 Million Workers in Singapore to Return and Vote on July 11

Sin Chew Daily 2 July 2026 Sports

Malaysian labour unions are mounting an urgent campaign to encourage the estimated 1.13 million Malaysians employed in Singapore to return home for the Johor state election on 11 July 2026. Among this group, approximately 400,000 commute daily between Johor Bahru and Singapore, representing a substantial bloc of registered voters whose turnout could be decisive in closely contested seats.

The Malaysian Trades Union Congress (MTUC) and several sector-specific unions have written to employers on both sides of the Causeway requesting that workers be granted leave or flexible arrangements on election day. Some Singapore-based employers have reportedly responded positively, with at least one major logistics company announcing it will provide transport assistance for its Malaysian employees.

The cross-border voter dynamic has historically shaped Johor election results, as the working-age cohort in Singapore tends to have different policy priorities from those who remain in Johor — including stronger views on wages, housing affordability near the Causeway, and the pace of Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone development. Political parties have been active in outreach to this group on social media platforms popular among overseas workers.

The Election Commission confirmed that Malaysian citizens registered to vote in Johor constituencies are eligible to cast ballots at their designated polling centres on 11 July, and called on voters to check their registration status and polling centre assignment via the SPR online portal. Overseas Malaysians working in Singapore will need to cross the Causeway and present their identity cards at their registered polling stations.

Transport authorities in both Malaysia and Singapore have been advised to prepare for significantly elevated cross-border traffic on election day. The Johor Bahru Customs, Immigration and Quarantine (CIQ) complex and the Malaysia-Singapore Second Link (Tuas) are expected to handle larger-than-usual vehicle and pedestrian volumes, and both governments have confirmed they will deploy additional personnel to manage flows efficiently.
