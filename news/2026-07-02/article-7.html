Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.4% in Q1 2026, Beats Expectations

Ministry of Finance Malaysia 2 July 2026 Economy

Malaysia's gross domestic product expanded 5.4% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026, slightly above the preliminary estimate of 5.3% and marking a second consecutive quarter of strong above-forecast performance. While the figure represents an easing from the 6.2% growth recorded in Q4 2025, economists noted it reflects a healthy normalisation rather than a structural slowdown.

The strong performance was underpinned by robust private consumption, sustained foreign direct investment in the semiconductor and data centre sectors, and resilient exports driven by demand for electrical and electronics products from key markets including the United States and China. The services sector, particularly wholesale, retail and tourism, also contributed meaningfully to the quarterly output.

Bank Negara Malaysia Governor Datuk Shaik Abdul Rasheed said the central bank would maintain a supportive monetary policy stance while remaining vigilant about imported inflation risks stemming from elevated global energy prices. He highlighted that the economy's broad-based growth across multiple sectors reduces the risk of overheating in any single area.

Key risks on the horizon include the prolonged conflict in West Asia, which has kept energy and logistics costs elevated, and the potential for US Federal Reserve rate hikes to trigger capital outflows from emerging markets including Malaysia. Domestically, the government faces the challenge of managing fuel subsidy rationalisation without triggering a significant spike in consumer prices.

The Ministry of Finance reaffirmed its full-year 2026 GDP growth forecast of 5.0%–5.5%, citing a strong pipeline of infrastructure projects under the Madani Economy framework and continued growth in the digital economy. The revised Q1 figure positions Malaysia as one of the fastest-growing economies in ASEAN for the quarter.
