Ringgit Slides as US Fed Rate Hike Expectations Weigh on Market

Malay Mail 2 July 2026 Economy

The Malaysian ringgit closed lower against the US dollar on 1 July 2026, extending a modest decline driven by investor expectations that the US Federal Reserve may resume interest rate hikes to counter stubbornly elevated American inflation. Currency strategists noted that the prospect of a higher-for-longer US rate environment has renewed pressure on emerging market currencies across the board.

The ringgit slipped to around 4.46 against the greenback in late afternoon trading before a slight recovery pared losses. Regional peers including the Thai baht, Indonesian rupiah and Philippine peso also weakened on the day, reflecting broad risk-off sentiment among Asian investors ahead of key US economic data releases later in the week.

Bank Negara Malaysia is expected to keep its Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) steady at 3.00% at its next Monetary Policy Committee meeting, having judged that current domestic conditions do not warrant a rate adjustment. Analysts said the central bank would continue to intervene in the foreign exchange market as needed to prevent excessive ringgit volatility.

Malaysia's relatively strong Q1 2026 GDP growth and solid current account surplus provide a buffer against capital outflows, but fund managers have flagged that a sustained Fed tightening cycle could dampen appetite for Malaysian government bonds, putting further pressure on the currency. The government's fiscal consolidation programme has helped maintain investor confidence in Malaysian sovereign debt.

Currency watchers are closely monitoring the upcoming US non-farm payrolls report and the Federal Open Market Committee minutes, both due this week, for clearer signals on the trajectory of US monetary policy. For Malaysian exporters, a weaker ringgit provides some competitive advantage, though importers and businesses with US dollar-denominated liabilities face higher costs.
