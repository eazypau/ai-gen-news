Cybercrimes Bill 2026 Passed by Dewan Rakyat

The Sun 2 July 2026 Politics

Malaysia's lower house of Parliament, the Dewan Rakyat, passed the Cybercrimes Bill 2026 by a majority voice vote, replacing the ageing Computer Crimes Act 1997 with a comprehensive modern framework. The bill comprises 61 clauses addressing a broad spectrum of cyber offences, including online fraud, ransomware attacks, identity theft, unauthorised system access, and the creation and dissemination of digitally manipulated intimate images — commonly known as deepfakes.

Deputy Prime Minister Datuk Seri Dr Ahmad Zahid Hamidi, in his closing remarks, clarified that the bill does not grant absolute powers to enforcement authorities and does not override existing legislation. He stressed that the new law is subject to stringent checks and balances designed to protect the fundamental rights and privacy of Malaysians, and that enforcement actions will require court orders in most circumstances.

Under the new legislation, the Royal Malaysia Police will serve as the lead enforcement agency, with the Commercial Crime Investigation Department taking the lead on investigations. The National Cyber Security Agency (NACSA) will act as the strategic coordinator, working alongside the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC), Bank Negara Malaysia, and other technical agencies to ensure effective inter-agency response.

The bill was debated extensively by 48 Members of Parliament from both the government and opposition benches, with a number of MPs calling for even more stringent penalties — particularly around AI-generated pornographic content. Several lawmakers urged the government to introduce mandatory sentencing, including whipping, for perpetrators who weaponise deepfake technology against victims.

Cybersecurity experts and civil society groups have broadly welcomed the bill, though some digital rights advocates noted that certain provisions require further scrutiny to ensure they are not misused. The law is expected to be gazetted and come into force later this year, setting a new benchmark for digital governance in Southeast Asia.
