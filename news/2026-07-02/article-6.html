MPs Call for Mandatory Penalties Against Deepfake Pornography

The Edge Malaysia 2 July 2026 Politics

During the debate on the Cybercrimes Bill 2026 in the Dewan Rakyat, multiple MPs from both the government and opposition benches called for harsher mandatory penalties — including mandatory whipping — for individuals found guilty of creating and distributing AI-generated pornographic deepfake content. The calls reflected growing public outrage over the misuse of artificial intelligence to create non-consensual intimate imagery.

Several MPs highlighted high-profile cases in which women, including students and public figures, had been victimised by deepfake pornography, suffering severe reputational and psychological harm. They argued that the existing penalty framework in the bill, while an improvement over the 1997 Act, does not adequately deter offenders given the relative anonymity of online activity.

The government acknowledged the severity of the issue, with the Deputy Home Minister indicating that amendments to introduce minimum sentencing for the most egregious deepfake offences would be considered during the committee stage. Legal experts cautioned, however, that mandatory whipping provisions must be carefully crafted to withstand constitutional scrutiny.

Digital rights organisations welcomed the focus on victim protection but urged Parliament to simultaneously strengthen remediation pathways for victims, including the right to swift content takedowns, civil remedies, and psychological support services. They noted that the most effective deterrent would be a combination of criminal sanctions, fast-track judicial processes, and robust platform accountability obligations.

The Malaysian Bar Council also weighed in, supporting enhanced penalties while calling for clear definitions of "digitally manipulated intimate images" in the bill to prevent unintended overbreadth that could criminalise legitimate artistic expression. The Cybercrimes Bill 2026 is expected to be tabled in the Dewan Negara before the end of the current parliamentary session.
