PM Anwar: Anti-Corruption Cannot Rely on Single Institution

Sin Chew Daily 2 July 2026 Politics

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim declared that Malaysia's fight against corruption must involve every layer of society rather than depending on a single enforcement agency. Speaking at an integrity forum, he called for close collaboration among enforcement bodies, advisory councils, Parliament, the judiciary, and both the public and private sectors.

Anwar cited the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission (MACC) as the cornerstone of anti-graft efforts, but warned that no single institution — however effective — can shoulder the entire burden of ensuring integrity in governance. "Corruption thrives in siloes," he said, urging a whole-of-nation response that places accountability at every level of public life.

The Prime Minister highlighted several recent MADANI government initiatives aimed at systemic reform, including enhanced asset declaration requirements for public servants, stricter procurement oversight through the MyProcure digital platform, and new whistleblower protections expected to come into force before year-end.

He also signalled that the government intends to review the independence of oversight bodies, ensuring they have the structural autonomy to investigate without fear or favour. This included a reference to strengthening Parliament's Public Accounts Committee so it can follow through on MACC referrals with binding remedial powers.

Transparency International Malaysia commended the speech but urged the government to move beyond rhetoric and establish concrete timelines for each promised reform. Civil society observers noted that Malaysia's ranking on the Corruption Perceptions Index had improved modestly under the MADANI administration, but sustained institutional change rather than high-profile prosecutions would be the true test of progress.
