Police Launch AI Driver-Profiling System to Combat Drug and Drink Driving

The Sun 2 July 2026 Technology

The Royal Malaysia Police's Bukit Aman Traffic Investigation and Enforcement Department unveiled a new AI-driven driver profiling system designed to identify commercial vehicle operators at high risk of driving under the influence of drugs or alcohol. The system cross-references multiple government databases — including the Transport Ministry's commercial vehicle registry, the National Anti-Drug Agency's records and court conviction data — to flag drivers who warrant priority screening at roadblocks.

Director Datuk Zulkefly Yahya said the profiling system had already been piloted in Selangor and Johor over the past four months, with early results showing a 34% improvement in the detection rate of impaired commercial drivers compared to purely random stopping. The system generates risk scores based on a combination of historical violations, medical records (where available), and route frequency patterns.

The technology will be integrated with the existing MySejahtera health data infrastructure, subject to user consent provisions, and will also connect to the Automatic Enforcement System (AES) network to monitor driving behaviour patterns in real-time. Officers at roadblocks will receive alerts on their mobile devices indicating which vehicles and drivers merit closer scrutiny.

Privacy advocates raised concerns about the scope of data sharing across government agencies without explicit consent from drivers, urging the government to publish a clear data governance framework that specifies how profiling data will be stored, accessed and deleted. The police responded that all data usage falls within existing legal authorities and that the system is subject to internal audit controls.

The driver profiling system is part of a broader road safety push that includes stricter enforcement of rest period requirements for long-haul truck and bus drivers, mandatory dashcam installation for commercial vehicles above 3.5 tonnes, and increased penalties for repeat traffic offenders. Malaysia recorded 562 road fatalities involving commercial vehicles in 2025, a figure the government aims to reduce by 20% by end-2026.
