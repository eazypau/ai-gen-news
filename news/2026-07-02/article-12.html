Malaysia Positioned to Lead ASEAN's Next Semiconductor Growth Phase

Digital News Asia 2 July 2026 Technology

The CREST Open Day 2026 highlighted Malaysia's strong positioning to lead ASEAN's next phase of semiconductor growth, drawing on the country's decades of experience as a global back-end manufacturing hub and its increasingly capable ecosystem of design, testing and advanced packaging. Industry and government representatives underscored that Malaysia now has the talent pipeline, infrastructure and policy framework needed to climb the value chain.

Malaysia's semiconductor industry, which contributes over 60% of the country's total exports, is undergoing a strategic transformation guided by the National Semiconductor Strategy (NSS). The NSS calls for a deliberate shift from assembly, testing and packaging towards wafer fabrication, chip design and intellectual property creation — activities that carry significantly higher value-added per unit of output.

CREST (Collaborative Research in Engineering, Science and Technology) CEO Dr Mahes Visvalingam said the organisation's collaborative model — linking multinational chipmakers with Malaysian universities and research institutions — had already yielded over 400 joint research projects and helped attract RM28 billion in new semiconductor FDI since 2024. He said the talent development pipeline was the critical bottleneck and called for further investment in post-graduate engineering programmes.

Global chipmakers including Intel, Infineon, Texas Instruments and Renesas have continued to expand their Malaysian operations, drawn by competitive costs, a skilled workforce and improving ecosystem depth. Several companies announced new advanced packaging facilities at Batu Kawan and Kulim Hi-Tech Park during the event, signalling continued confidence in Malaysia's semiconductor future.

Analysts noted that Malaysia faces increasing competition from Vietnam, Indonesia and India for new semiconductor investments, and that moving decisively into innovation-led segments — rather than simply adding manufacturing capacity — is essential to maintaining its lead. The government is expected to announce additional incentives under NSS 2.0 before the end of 2026 to accelerate the transition.
