Federal Allies BN and PH Clash as Johor Election Campaign Begins

South China Morning Post 2 July 2026 Politics

The Johor state election campaign has officially commenced, and with it an unusual spectacle: Barisan Nasional and Pakatan Harapan — partners in Putrajaya's unity government — are contesting every single one of the 56 state seats against each other. Political analysts describe the dynamic as a stress test for the alliance that has governed federally since November 2022.

The unusual configuration means voters will repeatedly see the faces of parties that cooperate on national policy but aggressively campaign against each other on the ground. BN's incumbent state administration is defending its March 2022 landslide victory, while PH seeks to demonstrate that its share of the unity government's popularity can be converted into state-level support.

Perikatan Nasional, led by Bersatu and PAS, is contesting 33 seats and positioning itself as the sole true opposition, looking to capitalise on any voter disillusionment with the unity government's perceived contradictions. PN leaders have highlighted the BN-PH rivalry as evidence that the coalition is fundamentally unstable.

Campaign messaging from BN has largely centred on continuity and economic delivery for Johor, with its candidate for Menteri Besar emphasising infrastructure projects and the Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone. PH, meanwhile, is leaning on PM Anwar Ibrahim's federal track record and pushing a reform narrative.

Analysts from the East Asia Forum noted that the outcome of the Johor election — along with the Negeri Sembilan contest on 1 August — could recalibrate the balance of power within the unity government and carry significant implications for national politics ahead of the next general election.
