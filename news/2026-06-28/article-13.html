Selangor Islamic Authorities Raid PJ Premises, Inspect 226 Foreign Nationals Linked to Shiite Teachings

The Selangor Islamic Religious Department (JAIS) conducted an enforcement raid on a premises in Petaling Jaya, inspecting 226 foreign nationals who were found to be in attendance at a gathering linked to Shiite Islamic teachings. The operation, which took place without prior notice, forms part of ongoing efforts by Malaysian Islamic authorities to monitor and regulate religious teachings that deviate from the Sunni (Ahli Sunnah Wal Jamaah) doctrine officially endorsed by the state.

Malaysia's federal and state constitutions designate Islam as the religion of the federation but leave the administration of Islamic law to individual state governments. Selangor, like other states, has enacted specific religious laws that prohibit the propagation of Shiite teachings to Muslims, citing concerns about religious deviancy and potential societal harm. Enforcement actions targeting such gatherings have become more frequent in the Klang Valley over the past few years.

JAIS officers and police conducted document checks on all 226 individuals present, verifying their immigration status and documenting their particulars for investigation. Officials indicated that those found to have violated immigration regulations or religious laws would face appropriate action under the relevant statutes. No immediate arrests were announced at the time of the raid, but investigations are ongoing.

The raid has attracted commentary from civil liberties groups, some of whom have questioned the proportionality of enforcement actions targeting religious gatherings among non-citizen residents. Legal observers note that the intersection of immigration law and religious enforcement creates a complex jurisdictional landscape that is not always clearly delineated in existing legislation.

JAIS has maintained that its operations are conducted within the bounds of Malaysian law and are aimed at protecting the religious well-being of Muslims in Selangor. The department said it would continue to monitor and act against any activities deemed to constitute religious deviance, regardless of the nationality of those involved.
