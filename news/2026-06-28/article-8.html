Malaysian Firms Launch Advanced Packaging Consortium at SEMICON SEA 2026

A consortium of five Malaysian semiconductor companies has been officially launched at SEMICON SEA 2026, marking a significant step in the country's ambitions to climb higher in the global semiconductor value chain. The consortium has set an ambitious target of capturing 7% of the global advanced packaging market by 2035, a bold goal that would translate into billions of dollars in revenue for Malaysian industry.

Advanced semiconductor packaging — which involves integrating multiple chips into a single package to improve performance and efficiency — is one of the fastest-growing segments of the semiconductor supply chain. Major chip designers and cloud computing firms are increasingly relying on advanced packaging techniques to overcome physical limitations in traditional chip scaling, creating strong demand for capable packaging partners.

Malaysia already has a strong foothold in conventional chip assembly and test (AT) operations, with the country hosting facilities for many of the world's leading semiconductor companies in Penang, Kuala Lumpur and Johor. The consortium represents a deliberate effort to leverage this existing base and transition toward higher-value, more technologically sophisticated activities.

Government support for the initiative is substantial. The Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC) and the Malaysia Investment Development Authority (MIDA) have both indicated their backing for the consortium, offering incentives for capital expenditure, research and development activities, and talent development programmes. Industry observers note that the government's National Semiconductor Strategy provides the policy framework that makes such collaborative industrial initiatives viable.

If successful, the consortium could position Malaysia as a leading global hub not just for chip packaging but for the full spectrum of backend semiconductor manufacturing. With geopolitical tensions reshaping global supply chains and customers seeking to diversify away from single-country dependency, Malaysia's timing in this strategic push is widely regarded as astute.
