Economists Warn of Tougher Times as Iran War and Energy Crisis Filter Through

Malaysian economists are sounding the alarm that the secondary economic impacts of the Iran conflict and global energy crisis are increasingly being felt in the domestic economy, with higher production costs, supply chain disruptions and softening external demand beginning to weigh on growth prospects for the remainder of 2026.

The Edge Malaysia's State of the Nation analysis highlights that while first-quarter GDP growth of 5.4% provided a strong start to the year, momentum is expected to ease significantly in subsequent quarters. Economists now project full-year GDP growth of around 4.2%, a notable step down from 2025's performance, as the global economic headwinds prove more persistent than initially anticipated.

Key sectors feeling the pinch include manufacturing — particularly those reliant on energy-intensive inputs — as well as logistics and transportation, where fuel costs have escalated. Small and medium enterprises (SMEs), which form the backbone of Malaysia's domestic economy, are reported to be under particular strain as cost pressures eat into margins that were already compressed from the post-pandemic recovery period.

Analysts also note that the broader regional slowdown is dampening Malaysia's export revenues. Trading partners including China and parts of Europe are experiencing their own economic difficulties, reducing demand for Malaysian manufactured goods, palm oil and petrochemical products. The government's diversification efforts — pushing into semiconductors, AI and data centre sectors — are seen as a longer-term structural hedge rather than a near-term buffer.

The government has signalled that it may consider targeted support measures for affected industries, building on existing subsidy rationalisation and business facilitation programmes. However, economists caution that fiscal space is limited following years of stimulus spending, and that structural reforms remain the most sustainable path to building economic resilience against future external shocks.
