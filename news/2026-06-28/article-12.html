Flood Victim Numbers Declining at Perak and Selangor Relief Centres

The number of flood victims sheltering at temporary relief centres in Perak and Selangor has continued to fall as of this afternoon, with authorities reporting steady improvement compared to the figures recorded yesterday. The gradual improvement reflects receding floodwaters in the affected areas following several days of heavy monsoon rain.

Disaster management agencies from both states confirmed that the situation is stabilising and that some evacuees have already been allowed to return to their homes after assessing that water levels have dropped to safe levels. However, officials have urged residents not to return prematurely and to await official clearance from local disaster management committees.

The floods, which affected multiple districts in both states over recent days, prompted the activation of evacuation protocols at several community halls and school-based relief centres. Local authorities coordinated the distribution of food, clean water, medication and basic necessities to evacuees, supported by volunteers from Rela, the Red Crescent and various NGOs.

Meteorological department forecasts indicate that further rain is possible over the coming days across parts of the peninsula, which may complicate recovery efforts in some areas. Authorities have maintained their readiness posture at flood-prone districts and have pre-positioned additional resources at strategic staging points in anticipation of any fresh weather events.

The Department of Irrigation and Drainage (DID) has been monitoring river levels across key catchment areas in both states and has indicated that the risk of additional flooding remains moderate in certain low-lying zones. Residents in high-risk areas are advised to monitor official alerts and be prepared to evacuate at short notice if conditions worsen.
