KPKT Approves 941 Housing Loans Worth RM18.8 Million for Terengganu Low-Income Families

The Ministry of Housing and Local Government (KPKT) has approved a total of 941 housing loans worth RM18.8 million for low-income residents in Terengganu, covering applications processed from 2002 through to February this year. The announcement highlights the government's long-standing commitment to improving home ownership rates among Malaysia's B40 (bottom 40%) income group, for whom access to conventional bank financing has historically been limited.

Housing Minister Nga Kor Ming confirmed the figures during a ministerial appearance, adding that the loans were disbursed through government schemes specifically designed to serve applicants who do not qualify for standard commercial mortgages due to income constraints or employment status. The average loan value per applicant works out to approximately RM20,000, reflecting the focus on modest, affordable housing rather than premium developments.

Affordable housing access remains a critical policy challenge in Malaysia, particularly in states like Terengganu where income levels are below the national average and the gap between housing costs and household incomes is pronounced. The government's various housing assistance programmes — including People's Housing Programme (PPR) units and the PR1MA scheme — have sought to address this gap, though demand consistently outstrips supply in many areas.

KPKT has indicated that it is working to streamline the application process for housing loans and accelerate approvals, recognising that long processing times have been a source of frustration for eligible applicants. Digital improvements to the application pipeline and a reduction in documentary requirements for verified B40 applicants are among the reforms being implemented.

The ministry is also reviewing the quantum of individual loans available to ensure that they remain commensurate with current construction and material costs, which have risen significantly since the early years of the programme. An upward revision of loan ceilings would allow more applicants to access meaningful financing for home repairs and modest construction projects rather than partial funding that leaves significant gaps.
