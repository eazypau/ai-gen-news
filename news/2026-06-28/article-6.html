Ringgit Slides 4% in June; Bank Negara Steps Up Stabilisation Efforts

The Malaysian ringgit has depreciated approximately 4% this month, trading near 4.10 per US dollar — close to its weakest level since November last year. The currency weakness has been driven primarily by rising expectations of further interest rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve, which have strengthened the dollar broadly against emerging-market currencies, including the ringgit.

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) has signalled that it will step up measures to stabilise the currency and bolster confidence among investors. The central bank's toolkit includes increasing foreign exchange intervention, encouraging government-linked companies and large corporates to repatriate overseas earnings, and improving the depth and liquidity of domestic financial markets to reduce speculative pressure.

Despite the near-term weakness, BNM has sought to reassure markets that Malaysia's economic fundamentals remain sound. The country's current account has remained in surplus, foreign reserves are at adequate levels, and inflation is well-contained at 1.6%. Officials have drawn a distinction between short-term currency fluctuations driven by external factors and structural vulnerabilities, arguing that Malaysia's case falls squarely in the former category.

Currency analysts note that the ringgit could find support if global energy prices stabilise — as Malaysia remains a net oil and gas exporter — or if the US dollar rally stalls. The upcoming Johor state election has also attracted some attention from foreign investors monitoring Malaysia's political risk premium, though most analysts consider the domestic political situation manageable.

The Finance Ministry has reiterated that fiscal discipline and investor-friendly policies remain the cornerstone of the government's approach to economic management, and that efforts to attract foreign direct investment — particularly in high-tech sectors — will continue to underpin long-term ringgit fundamentals.
