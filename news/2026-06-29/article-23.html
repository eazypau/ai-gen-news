Malaysia and Vietnam Agree to Strengthen Medical and Halal Tourism Cooperation

Malaysia and Vietnam have agreed to deepen bilateral tourism cooperation, with both governments signing a new framework agreement focused on medical tourism, halal travel, and cross-border tourism promotion. The agreement was announced during a high-level bilateral meeting, reflecting the two countries' ambition to grow their tourism relationship well beyond traditional leisure travel.

Malaysia's world-class healthcare infrastructure — anchored by Penang's medical tourism hub, Gleneagles and Pantai hospitals in Kuala Lumpur, and KPJ Healthcare's network of facilities — is increasingly attracting Vietnamese patients seeking quality treatment at competitive prices. Under the new agreement, both countries will work to simplify medical visa processes and facilitate information sharing between healthcare providers and travel agents.

Halal tourism is the other pillar of the bilateral agreement, with Malaysia leveraging its status as a globally recognised halal-friendly destination to attract Muslim Vietnamese travellers. Vietnam has a growing Muslim minority population, and Malaysia's established halal dining scene, prayer facilities, and Islamic cultural heritage make it a natural fit for this segment.

Tourism Malaysia has identified Vietnam as a high-growth source market, with Vietnamese visitor arrivals to Malaysia increasing steadily over the past three years. In return, Malaysia is also looking to grow its outbound tourism to Vietnam, particularly to destinations such as Ho Chi Minh City, Da Nang, and Hoi An, which have become popular among Malaysian travellers.

Industry stakeholders from both sides welcomed the agreement, noting that enhanced bilateral cooperation can generate jobs, boost economic activity, and strengthen people-to-people connections between the two ASEAN neighbours. Full implementation of the agreement is expected to commence in the second half of 2026, ahead of Visit Malaysia Year peak season.
