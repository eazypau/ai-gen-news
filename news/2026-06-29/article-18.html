Three New Community Health and Rehabilitation Facilities Open in Serkam, Melaka

The Melaka chief minister officially opened three new community health, welfare, and rehabilitation facilities in Serkam, bringing improved healthcare access to residents of the area who previously had to travel considerable distances for similar services. The facilities are part of a broader state government initiative to decentralise healthcare and bring services closer to underserved communities across Melaka.

The three facilities include a community health clinic providing primary care services, a welfare centre catering to the needs of senior citizens and low-income families, and a rehabilitation unit equipped to support individuals recovering from injuries, strokes, and other physical conditions requiring ongoing physiotherapy and care.

Local residents expressed relief and gratitude at the opening, noting that the nearest equivalent facilities had previously been located in the town centre, requiring significant travel for elderly and mobility-impaired community members. The new facilities are expected to serve thousands of residents across the Serkam district and surrounding areas.

The Melaka government said the investment in decentralised healthcare reflects its commitment to ensuring equitable access to health services regardless of geography or income level. The facilities will be staffed by trained healthcare professionals, including doctors, nurses, social workers, and physiotherapists, supported by government funding.

Community leaders welcomed the announcement and called for similar investments in other underserved areas of Melaka. The state government indicated that additional facilities are planned for construction in 2026 and 2027 as part of its comprehensive rural health and welfare development programme.
