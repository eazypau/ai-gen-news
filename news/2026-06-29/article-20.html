Malaysia's Flood Losses Drop to RM636.9 Million in 2025 — a 32% Decline

Malaysia's total flood-related economic losses fell significantly in 2025, dropping to RM636.9 million from RM933.4 million in 2024 — a decline of approximately 32% — according to newly released government data. The improvement is equivalent to about 0.03% of nominal GDP and marks meaningful progress in the government's efforts to reduce the recurring human and economic toll of annual flooding events.

However, while overall losses declined, the cost of damage to public assets and infrastructure actually rose to RM380.2 million in 2025 from RM303.4 million in 2024. Experts say this reflects the impact of more intense but fewer large-scale flood events, which tend to damage roads, bridges, and drainage infrastructure disproportionately relative to private property.

The improvement is attributed to a combination of factors: expanded flood early-warning systems, investments in retention ponds and river widening projects, improved drainage maintenance in urban areas, and better flood response coordination between federal and state governments. Public awareness campaigns have also improved the speed and efficiency of evacuation efforts.

Despite the positive trend, environmental experts caution that climate change poses growing risks to Malaysia's flood vulnerability. Rapid urbanisation — particularly in the Klang Valley, Johor, and Penang — continues to reduce natural water absorption capacity and increase runoff into rivers and drains, creating conditions for flash floods even during moderate rainfall events.

The government has pledged to maintain its investment in flood mitigation infrastructure and called on local authorities to enforce stricter controls on hillside development and wetland reclamation. Urban planners are also being urged to incorporate nature-based solutions, such as urban green spaces and permeable paving, into new development projects to build long-term resilience.
