PERKESO Screening: Nearly 60% of Malaysian Workers Found Overweight or Obese

A comprehensive health screening programme conducted by Malaysia's Social Security Organisation (PERKESO/SOCSO) between 2023 and 2025 found that 59.2% of participating workers were either overweight or obese, revealing a significant and growing public health challenge in the Malaysian workforce. The data was released as part of PERKESO's ninth annual health programme review.

Beyond obesity, the screening uncovered alarmingly high rates of other chronic conditions: approximately 19% of workers had diabetes, 17.45% suffered from high blood pressure, and 59.24% had high cholesterol levels. Workers aged between 40 and 59 were disproportionately affected, with this age group accounting for nearly 30% of participants and showing the highest rates of metabolic risk factors.

Health authorities and workplace wellness advocates have called for urgent action to address the findings, including more workplace wellness programmes, subsidised gym memberships, and healthier canteen options in factories and offices. The Human Resources Ministry emphasised that poor worker health has direct implications for productivity, absenteeism, and long-term sustainability of Malaysia's labour force.

To combat the trend, PERKESO's SOCSO Run and Ride 2026 attracted its largest turnout ever, with 12,000 participants joining fitness activities as part of efforts to promote healthier lifestyles across the workforce. Organisers noted that growing awareness of the health risks has motivated more workers to take preventive steps.

Only 15% of screened workers were found to regularly engage in physical activity at recommended levels, highlighting the scale of the lifestyle change needed. Nutritionists and public health experts are calling for a whole-of-society approach — involving employers, government agencies, and civil society — to shift workplace culture toward prioritising employee wellbeing.
