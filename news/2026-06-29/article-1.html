LRT3 Shah Alam Line Free Rides Begin Today — 20 Stations Open to Public

The long-awaited LRT3 Shah Alam Line officially opened to the public at 6am on Monday, with Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim launching the service at Johan Setia depot in Klang the evening before. In a gesture to encourage ridership during the initial phase, all fares on the new line and its feeder bus services will be waived from 29 June to 31 July 2026.

The 37.8km line serves 20 stations, spanning from Klang and Shah Alam westward to Bandar Utama in Petaling Jaya, providing a critical new rail corridor for the densely populated Shah Alam–Klang corridor. At full capacity, the line can transport up to 18,630 passengers per hour in each direction, making it one of the highest-capacity rail lines in the Klang Valley.

To complement the rail service, Prasarana has deployed 40 feeder buses across 13 dedicated routes, covering 323 stops along the Shah Alam corridor. A Rapid On-Demand service operating 44 vans across 20 zones is also available at a flat rate of RM2 per trip, giving commuters flexible last-mile connectivity from LRT3 stations.

All 20 stations have been designed with full accessibility features, including wheelchair ramps, OKU-friendly toilets, and dedicated seating in every coach. The government said the inclusive design reflects its commitment to building a public transport network that serves all Malaysians, regardless of mobility needs.

Authorities are urging commuters to take advantage of the free-ride period to familiarise themselves with the new line and shift away from private vehicle travel. Transport analysts say the LRT3 has the potential to significantly reduce congestion on the Federal Highway and Lebuhraya Shah Alam, two of the most heavily trafficked roads in the greater Kuala Lumpur area.
