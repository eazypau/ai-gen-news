39 Flights Cancelled Across Malaysian Airports in June Travel Chaos

A disruptive wave of flight cancellations swept across Malaysia's major airports in June 2026, with 39 domestic services cancelled over two consecutive days, leaving thousands of passengers stranded or scrambling to rebook. The cancellations hit AirAsia and Batik Air hardest, affecting high-frequency routes connecting Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA) with Langkawi, Penang, Johor Bahru, and Kota Bharu.

Langkawi International Airport was particularly hard-hit, recording six cancelled departures that devastated the island's tourism-dependent passenger traffic. The timing was especially damaging, coinciding with the peak travel season for Southeast Asia — a period when Langkawi hotels and tour operators depend heavily on consistent air connectivity to fill bookings.

Affected passengers reported long queues at customer service desks, difficulties obtaining refunds, and limited options for rebooking on alternative carriers. Both airlines cited operational constraints and scheduling adjustments as the primary reasons for the cancellations, though no specific technical issues were publicly confirmed.

The Aviation Consumer Protection Agency called on the affected airlines to improve communication with passengers and expedite compensation claims. Consumer advocacy groups urged travellers to familiarise themselves with their rights under Malaysia's aviation consumer protection regulations, which entitle passengers to refunds or rebooking in cases of airline-initiated cancellations.

The episode has reignited debate about the resilience of Malaysia's domestic aviation network, with tourism stakeholders calling on the government and airlines to develop contingency protocols that can prevent similar disruptions from cascading across multiple routes. Airlines have since assured travellers that measures are in place to restore normal operations.
