Johor Election: Media Centres and 100 NADI Community Hubs Ready for Coverage

The Communications Ministry has completed preparations for comprehensive media and public information coverage of the 2026 Johor state election, confirming that dedicated media centres and 100 NADI (National Digital Access) community digital hubs have been set up and activated across the state ahead of polling day on July 11. The facilities are designed to ensure that both journalists and ordinary citizens have seamless access to election-related information and connectivity throughout the campaign and polling periods.

The media centres will provide accredited journalists with workspace, high-speed internet, power supply, and direct access to official election announcements and candidate information. The Election Commission is expected to use the centres as staging points for media briefings as results come in on polling night from the state's 56 constituencies.

The 100 NADI community digital hubs, located in community halls, mosques, and community centres across Johor, are intended to ensure that rural and elderly voters can access information about their polling stations, candidate profiles, and how to vote — bridging the digital gap between urban and rural communities ahead of the election.

Authorities also issued a firm reminder to all political parties and their supporters to conduct healthy and responsible campaigns, explicitly warning against raising sensitive issues involving race, religion, and royalty — commonly referred to as the 3R issues. The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) announced that it would continue to actively monitor social media platforms for extreme provocation and remove offending content.

Police have also been deployed across Johor to manage campaign activities and ensure public safety during the election period. Senior officials have expressed confidence that the election will be conducted peacefully and transparently, reflecting the maturity of Malaysia's democratic process.
