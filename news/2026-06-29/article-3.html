Johor Election: Campaign Begins as Federal Allies BN and PH Clash

The official campaign period for the 2026 Johor state election is underway, with 172 candidates cleared to contest 56 state seats in what has become a unique political spectacle — federal coalition partners Barisan Nasional (BN) and Pakatan Harapan (PH) fighting against each other at the state level. Polling day is scheduled for July 11, with early voting on July 7 for eligible voters.

The Johor state assembly was dissolved on June 1, 2026 by incumbent Menteri Besar Onn Hafiz Ghazi, with the dissolution signed by the Regent of Johor. The election was triggered ahead of schedule, with analysts suggesting the move was intended to take advantage of BN's incumbency advantage before any potential shift in public sentiment.

Nomination day produced a highly fragmented contest: 14 straight fights, 27 three-cornered contests, 12 four-cornered battles, and three five-way races across the 56 seats. The presence of Perikatan Nasional (PN) and smaller parties such as MUDA further complicates vote-splitting calculations across the state.

BN is defending 40 seats it held before dissolution, while PH held 12 and PN held 3. Political analysts say the key battlegrounds will be in urban and semi-urban constituencies where younger voters and multiracial populations are concentrated, with both BN and PH mounting aggressive campaigns to win over swing voters.

The unusual dynamic of federal allies competing at state level has drawn national and international attention as a test case for Malaysia's coalition politics. Observers are watching closely to see whether the result will shift power dynamics within the unity government and potentially influence the federal political landscape ahead of the next general election.
