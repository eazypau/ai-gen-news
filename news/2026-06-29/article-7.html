Ringgit Slides 4% This Month as Bank Negara Steps Up Stabilisation Efforts

The Malaysian ringgit weakened to 4.0880 per US dollar on June 26, registering a decline of approximately 4% over the month of June as persistent US rate-hike expectations continued to strengthen the greenback against emerging-market currencies. The ringgit's slide has placed it near its lowest level since November last year, prompting concern among investors and policymakers.

Bank Negara Malaysia responded by signalling that it would step up stabilisation measures, including efforts to attract fresh foreign capital inflows and encourage government-linked companies and major corporates to repatriate overseas earnings back into the domestic economy. The central bank sought to reassure markets that the recent weakness reflects portfolio rebalancing rather than deterioration in Malaysia's fundamentals.

Analysts broadly agree that Malaysia's underlying economic profile remains strong. Continued job creation has pushed unemployment to 2.9%, the lowest in a decade, while inflation stayed moderate at 1.6% during the first quarter of 2026. These indicators suggest the ringgit's weakness is externally driven rather than a reflection of domestic vulnerabilities.

The Edge Malaysia earlier flagged that tougher times may loom for Malaysia's economy as first-quarter growth showed early signs of slowing, driven partly by external demand softness from key trading partners. However, the Ministry of Finance maintained that Malaysia's growth trajectory remains broadly on track for the full year.

Currency traders and economists will be closely watching the Federal Reserve's next policy signals and whether Bank Negara deploys more direct market interventions in the coming weeks. A stabilisation of US interest rate expectations could provide the ringgit with the breathing room it needs to recover lost ground before the end of the third quarter.
