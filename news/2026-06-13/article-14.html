Malaysia's Flood Losses Fall 32% to RM636.9 Million in 2025, But Risks Remain

Malaysia's total economic losses from flooding fell significantly in 2025, dropping 32% to RM636.9 million from RM933.4 million recorded in 2024, according to data published by the Department of Irrigation and Drainage (DID) and the National Disaster Management Agency (NADMA). The improvement has been attributed partly to better flood forecasting, more effective early warning systems, and enhanced river engineering works in the most flood-prone catchments.

However, experts and environmental analysts caution that the apparent improvement masks a concerning rise in infrastructure damage costs, including roads, bridges, drainage systems and public buildings. While the overall economic loss figure declined, the cost of repairing flood-damaged public infrastructure was higher than in previous years, suggesting that the nature of flooding is shifting toward more localised but intense urban flash flood events that cause concentrated damage.

Floods remain Malaysia's most frequent and economically devastating natural hazard, accounting for approximately 85% of all recorded disasters since 2000. Climate projections from the Malaysian Meteorological Department (MetMalaysia) indicate that both the frequency and intensity of extreme rainfall events are expected to increase in coming decades as global temperatures continue to rise, presenting a growing adaptation challenge for city planners and disaster management authorities.

Bank Negara Malaysia has separately flagged that flood insurance penetration remains alarmingly low even in areas with a documented history of repetitive flooding. BNM estimated that fewer than 30% of residential properties in high-risk flood zones hold adequate flood coverage, leaving many homeowners and small businesses exposed to uncompensated losses following flood events. The central bank is working with the insurance industry to develop more affordable and accessible flood insurance products.

The government has committed RM4.2 billion over the 12th Malaysia Plan period (2021–2025) toward flood mitigation infrastructure, including the controversial Sungai Klang flood bypass tunnel, upgraded retention ponds, and coastal protection works in Penang and Johor. Independent assessments suggest that while these investments are necessary, they need to be complemented by stricter enforcement of development controls in flood-risk areas and accelerated urban green infrastructure programmes.
