SEMICON Southeast Asia 2026 Convenes Industry Leaders in Malaysia to Drive Chip Growth

SEMICON Southeast Asia 2026, the region's premier semiconductor industry conference and exhibition, convened in Kuala Lumpur this week, bringing together hundreds of technology companies, equipment makers, materials suppliers, government officials and research institutions to chart the next chapter of semiconductor growth in the region. The event drew participants from over 30 countries, reflecting Malaysia's central role in the global chip supply chain.

SEMI, the global semiconductor industry association, highlighted that Malaysia now hosts 33 active semiconductor capital projects with a combined estimated value of approximately US$20 billion — making it one of the most active investment destinations globally for semiconductor manufacturing expansion outside of Taiwan, South Korea and the United States. The projects span back-end assembly and testing, advanced packaging, and increasingly some front-end manufacturing activities.

Conference sessions focused heavily on four themes: supply chain resilience and diversification away from concentration in East Asia; the growing importance of advanced packaging technologies including chiplet architectures and 3D integration; the semiconductor talent challenge and how Malaysia can accelerate the development of its engineering pipeline; and sustainability and energy efficiency in semiconductor manufacturing operations.

International Semiconductor Equipment and Materials (SEMI) president and CEO Ajit Manocha said in his keynote that Malaysia has earned its seat at the table in global semiconductor conversations and challenged Malaysian industry and government to be bold in setting the country's ambitions for the next decade. He specifically called for Malaysia to develop more homegrown semiconductor design capabilities, which remain a weak link in the country's chip ecosystem compared to its strong manufacturing base.

Trade booths at SEMICON SEA 2026 featured notable announcements from several multinational companies confirming facility expansions and new investments in Penang, Selangor and Johor. The annual event has become an important bellwether for investment sentiment in Malaysian semiconductor manufacturing, and this year's strong attendance and positive business sentiment were widely interpreted as a vote of confidence in Malaysia's semiconductor growth trajectory.
