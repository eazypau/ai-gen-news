Malaysia Raises Employment Pass Salary Thresholds for Technical Specialists in June 2026

The Ministry of Home Affairs has implemented revised salary threshold requirements for Malaysia's Employment Pass (EP) effective 1 June 2026, with the changes specifically targeting Category III of the pass, which covers technical specialists and semiconductor manufacturing technicians. The new Category III floor salary has been raised from the previous range of RM3,000–RM4,999 to RM5,000–RM9,999, with manufacturing-sector applicants now required to meet a minimum monthly salary of RM7,000.

The revision is part of the government's broader strategy to attract higher-value, higher-skilled foreign workers to Malaysia's rapidly expanding technology manufacturing sector, while simultaneously encouraging companies to invest in upskilling their local Malaysian workforce rather than relying on cheaper foreign labour. The Ministry stated that the changes are aligned with the National Employment Policy and the 12th Malaysia Plan's human capital development objectives.

Industry groups including the Malaysia Semiconductor Industry Association (MSIA) and the American Malaysian Chamber of Commerce (AmCham) have given a broadly positive reception to the changes, viewing them as a signal of Malaysia's intent to position itself as a high-value manufacturing destination rather than competing purely on labour cost. However, some smaller manufacturers have raised concerns that the higher thresholds could create short-term recruitment difficulties in niche technical specialisations where local supply is limited.

Immigration Department officials clarified that existing Employment Pass holders will not be immediately affected and that the new thresholds apply to new applications and renewals processed from June 2026 onwards. Companies employing foreign technical specialists on existing contracts below the new thresholds will need to review and potentially renegotiate compensation arrangements when applications come up for renewal.

The Home Ministry indicated that it intends to continue reviewing EP thresholds annually to reflect changes in labour market conditions, cost of living and Malaysia's evolving human capital needs. A dedicated inter-agency committee has been established to monitor the impact of the revised thresholds on both the domestic labour market and Malaysia's attractiveness as an investment destination for technology-intensive industries.
