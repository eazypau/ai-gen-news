Malaysia's Headline Inflation Rose in Q1 2026 Even as Core Pressures Eased

Malaysia's headline inflation rate rose in the first quarter of 2026, driven primarily by higher food and beverage prices and the continued pass-through effects of fuel subsidy rationalisation measures introduced in late 2024 and extended in 2025. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) data, released by the Department of Statistics Malaysia, showed headline inflation averaging around 2.1% in Q1, above the 1.7% recorded in Q4 2025.

In contrast to the headline figure, core inflation — which excludes volatile food and energy components — eased modestly during the same period, suggesting that underlying domestic price pressures are not accelerating and that the higher headline reading is largely driven by specific supply-side factors rather than broad demand-driven inflation. Economists noted that this pattern is consistent with a well-managed inflation environment rather than an overheating economy.

Bank Negara Malaysia's full-year inflation forecast of 1.9% for 2026 implies some moderation in the headline rate through the second half of the year as base effects from the subsidy rationalisation fade. The central bank has identified global trade tensions and a potential depreciation of the ringgit as key upside risks to the inflation outlook, while weaker global commodity prices represent a downside risk.

Human resources and business groups have flagged the cost-of-living impact of rising food prices particularly on lower-income workers, many of whom spend a disproportionately high share of their income on food and transport. The government's Rahmah economic aid programme, which provides targeted cash assistance to lower-income households, has partially offset the impact of price increases, but advocates argue that the assistance amounts need to be reviewed and increased.

Bank Negara Malaysia's Monetary Policy Committee is expected to keep the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) unchanged at 3.25% at its next scheduled meeting, as policymakers assess incoming data on growth, inflation and global economic conditions. The IMF's 2026 Article IV consultation report for Malaysia, released in March, noted that current monetary policy settings appear appropriate given the balance of economic risks, and that significant monetary tightening is unlikely to be warranted in the near term.
