Malaysia Studies Proposal to Establish National Quantum Council

The Malaysian government is actively studying a formal proposal to establish a National Quantum Council, a dedicated inter-agency body that would coordinate the country's quantum technology strategy and development in a more systematic and strategically coherent manner. The proposal was tabled by the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation (MOSTI) following a series of expert consultations held in early 2026 with quantum researchers, technology companies and international partners.

Quantum computing, quantum communications and quantum sensing are increasingly being recognised by governments and technology companies worldwide as strategically critical technologies with implications for national security, financial systems, healthcare, materials science and logistics optimisation. Malaysia has been investing in foundational quantum research primarily through universities and institutions like MIMOS Berhad, but lacks a centralised coordination mechanism comparable to those established by Singapore, the United Kingdom, Germany and the United States.

A National Quantum Council, if established, would be charged with setting national quantum technology priorities, coordinating funding allocation across ministries, engaging with international quantum research consortia, and developing a talent pipeline of quantum-trained scientists and engineers. The council model has been adopted successfully by several peer economies in coordinating emerging technology policy.

Industry stakeholders, including Malaysian tech entrepreneurs who attended the Malaysia-Japan Technology Roundtable in Tokyo, have expressed strong support for the proposal, arguing that a coordinated national approach is essential to avoid duplication of effort and to attract serious international co-investment in quantum research infrastructure. Japan, which is a global leader in quantum hardware development, has indicated willingness to include quantum collaboration as part of its expanded technology partnership with Malaysia.

The Science and Technology Ministry said a decision on establishing the council is expected by the end of Q3 2026, pending a feasibility study and stakeholder consultation process that will include engagement with the private sector, civil society and academic institutions. If approved, Malaysia would join a growing list of Asian nations — including South Korea, India, Singapore and China — that have formalised national-level quantum strategy bodies in recent years.
