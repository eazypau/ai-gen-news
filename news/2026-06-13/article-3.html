Anwar Proposes Prime Minister Term Limit in Legislative Reform Bid

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has tabled a proposal to introduce a constitutional cap on the tenure of Malaysian prime ministers, limiting any individual to a maximum of two full five-year terms, or ten years in total. The announcement, described by analysts as a long-promised campaign commitment, is part of a wider package of institutional reforms the Madani Government intends to advance through Parliament before the next general election.

The bill, if passed, would mark a historic first for Malaysia, which has no existing formal limit on the tenure of its chief executive. Former Prime Minister Dr Mahathir Mohamad served a combined total of more than 26 years across two stints in office. Supporters of the proposed limit argue it is essential to preventing the concentration of executive power and ensuring regular renewal of political leadership.

Anwar's reform package also includes the Parliamentary Service Bill, designed to separate the parliamentary secretariat from the civil service and ensure greater legislative independence from the executive branch. Combined, the two bills represent the most ambitious structural reform proposals tabled by a Malaysian government in over two decades.

Opposition figures from Perikatan Nasional have given a cautious reception to the proposals, welcoming the intent while questioning the pace of implementation and whether the bills will receive sufficient parliamentary time before the election cycle begins. Some UMNO leaders within the government coalition have also raised concerns about the constitutional implications of the term-limit amendment.

Political analysts surveyed by Bloomberg described the proposals as a tactical move by Anwar to revive public support, with government satisfaction ratings having risen from 41% in mid-2025 to approximately 49% by the end of the year. Whether the bills can be legislated in the current parliamentary term remains a key question for observers tracking Malaysia's reform trajectory.
