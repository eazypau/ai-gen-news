Ringgit Dips 0.24% Against the Dollar on 12 June; Up 4.43% Year-on-Year

The Malaysian ringgit edged lower against the US dollar on 12 June 2026, with the USD/MYR rate slipping to 4.0576, a fall of 0.24% from the previous session. The modest decline was attributed to a brief uptick in global risk aversion following uncertainty over US Federal Reserve rate guidance, which temporarily boosted demand for the safe-haven dollar across emerging market currencies.

Despite the near-term softness, the ringgit's broader trajectory remains positive. Against a year-earlier level, the currency has strengthened by approximately 4.43% versus the US dollar, supported by robust foreign direct investment inflows into Malaysia's technology and manufacturing sectors, resilient export earnings from the E&E sector, and ongoing current account surpluses.

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) has maintained a broadly stable monetary policy stance, keeping the Overnight Policy Rate at 3.25%, a level considered appropriate given Malaysia's moderate inflation environment and steady economic growth. Governor Datuk Seri Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour reiterated at the most recent Monetary Policy Committee meeting that BNM stands ready to act if external headwinds materialise into significant domestic risks.

Headline inflation for Malaysia is forecast to average 1.9% for the full year 2026, up from 1.4% in 2025, reflecting the gradual pass-through from fuel subsidy rationalisation measures introduced in late 2024. Core inflation, which excludes food and energy prices, has been easing gradually, suggesting that underlying price pressures are manageable.

Currency strategists at regional investment banks generally hold a constructive outlook for the ringgit over the next 12 months, citing Malaysia's strong export fundamentals, high FDI commitments, and improving fiscal metrics. The consensus 12-month target for USD/MYR sits around 3.90–3.95, implying further modest appreciation if global conditions remain supportive.
