Kelantan Police Raid HIV Health Outreach Event, Arrest 20 Men; AIDS Council Says It Was Legitimate Programme

Kelantan state authorities raided what they described as an alleged "gay party" and arrested 20 men, subsequently charging three individuals with possession of homosexual pornographic material under Malaysia's Penal Code. The raid, which took place at a private venue in the state capital Kota Bharu, drew immediate controversy and condemnation from health advocates and civil society organisations.

Both the Malaysian AIDS Council (MAC) and Malaysia's Ministry of Health issued statements confirming that the gathering was in fact a legitimate HIV outreach and health information programme, not a social event. The programme was reportedly organised by a registered NGO working under a government grant to provide HIV testing, counselling, and health education services to marginalised and at-risk communities in Kelantan — one of Malaysia's states with persistently elevated HIV case rates.

MAC chief executive officer Murallitharan Munisamy said the authorities' actions have set a deeply damaging precedent that will make it significantly harder for health workers to conduct vital community outreach programmes. He warned that if community health workers fear criminalisation, at-risk populations will become even more reluctant to access testing and prevention services, directly increasing the risk of HIV transmission.

Kelantan police subsequently confirmed they found no evidence of sexual activity at the premises. The three men charged face proceedings under a provision of the Penal Code that has been used selectively in Malaysian states governed by PAS, the Islamist party. Human Rights Watch criticised the raid as an example of the ongoing criminalisation of LGBTQ+ individuals in Malaysia under broad and vaguely drafted moral offence statutes.

The incident has reignited debate about the compatibility of Malaysia's public health objectives — particularly in HIV prevention — with its criminal law framework targeting sexual minorities. Health advocates have called on the federal government to provide clearer legal protections for health programme participants and organisers, arguing that the current legal environment is a structural barrier to effective HIV management in the country.
