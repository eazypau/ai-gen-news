Indonesian Patients Drive Strong Growth in Malaysian Medical Tourism

The Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC) has reported a surge in Indonesian patient inquiries and bookings during the Malaysia Fair Jakarta 2026, held from 4 to 7 June, confirming sustained and growing demand from Malaysia's closest neighbour for specialised medical services. The fair featured over 30 Malaysian healthcare providers offering consultations, health screenings and treatment planning sessions directly to Indonesian consumers.

Indonesia continues to be the single largest source of foreign medical tourists to Malaysia, drawn by the country's comparatively lower treatment costs, English and Bahasa Malaysia proficiency, geographic proximity, and the high quality of care at private hospitals. Specialties in demand include cardiology, oncology, orthopaedics, fertility treatment and executive health screening packages.

MHTC officials confirmed that the Malaysia Year of Medical Tourism 2026 (MYMT 2026), themed "Healing Meets Hospitality," has generated record-level awareness in regional markets. The campaign brings together leading private hospitals such as Gleneagles, Pantai, KPJ, and Sunway Medical Centre with tourism authorities to offer bundled healthcare-holiday packages.

Beyond Indonesia, Malaysia is also seeing increased interest from patients in Cambodia, Vietnam and the Philippines, reflecting the country's growing reputation as the region's premium yet affordable healthcare destination. The government has set an ambitious target of attracting more than 1.2 million healthcare travellers in 2026, up from approximately 900,000 in 2024.

Health Minister Datuk Seri Dzulkefly Ahmad praised the performance of Malaysian hospitals in international quality benchmarking surveys and said the ministry will continue to facilitate public-private partnerships to expand specialist capacity, particularly in tier-two cities like Penang, Johor Bahru and Kota Kinabalu that are increasingly attractive to regional patients.
