Malaysia Evaluates Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Export Licence for Naval Strike Missile

The Royal Malaysian Navy (RMN) is evaluating alternative suppliers for its planned naval strike missile procurement after Norway's government revoked the export licence for the Kongsberg Naval Strike Missile (NSM), the weapon system Malaysia had been considering as part of its maritime capability modernisation programme. The licence revocation, linked to Norway's updated export control policies in the context of the broader European geopolitical environment, has created an unexpected procurement gap for the RMN.

The Ministry of Defence confirmed that it has directed the armed forces procurement directorate to conduct a comprehensive market survey of comparable anti-ship missile systems available from alternative suppliers. Systems under consideration are understood to include options from France, the United States, India and South Korea, all of which offer surface-to-surface and air-launched anti-ship missiles with performance specifications broadly comparable to the NSM.

Defence analysts noted that Malaysia's maritime security requirements have become more pressing given the evolving strategic environment in the South China Sea, where assertive maritime activities from larger regional powers have heightened the importance of credible naval deterrence. The RMN's existing fleet of frigates and offshore patrol vessels requires modern anti-ship missiles to maintain operational relevance against a range of potential threat scenarios.

The procurement setback is a reminder of the geopolitical complexities inherent in defence acquisitions, particularly for smaller nations that depend on established Western defence suppliers with increasingly stringent export licensing frameworks. Malaysia maintains a non-aligned foreign policy but has pursued a measured programme of defence modernisation to protect its territorial waters and exclusive economic zone.

Parliament's Defence Committee has been briefed on the situation and is expected to conduct hearings in July 2026 to assess the timeline for procuring a replacement system and the budgetary implications. Defence Minister Datuk Seri Mohamad Hassan said Malaysia will approach its alternative procurement options methodically and expects to conclude a new missile procurement decision within 18 months, subject to parliamentary approval of the necessary capital expenditure allocation.
