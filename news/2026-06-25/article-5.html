Malaysia's Trade Surges to Historic Record of RM1.127 Trillion in First 4 Months

New Straits Times | 25 June 2026 | Economy

Malaysia has recorded the highest-ever trade figures in its history, with total trade climbing 15.3% year-on-year to RM1.127 trillion in the first four months of 2026. April 2026 was particularly exceptional, marking the strongest single-month trade performance the country has ever seen, according to data released by the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI).

Exports surged 19% to RM609.31 billion over the January to April period, driven by robust global demand for Malaysia's electrical and electronics (E&E) products, palm oil, liquefied natural gas (LNG), and refined petroleum products. Imports grew 11.1% to RM517.4 billion, reflecting higher domestic consumption and strong investment activity in the manufacturing sector.

The stellar performance was achieved despite mounting geopolitical tensions and disruptions to global supply chains, particularly those linked to ongoing conflicts affecting transit routes through the Strait of Hormuz. Malaysia's strategic positioning as a key node in global E&E supply chains and its diversified export basket have helped insulate the economy from the worst of these external shocks.

Trade Minister Tengku Zafrul Aziz credited Malaysia's proactive trade diplomacy and the ongoing expansion of free trade agreements for the country's resilient performance. He noted that new market penetrations into Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries and deepened ties with ASEAN partners have broadened Malaysia's export base beyond traditional Western markets.

Analysts project that Malaysia's trade momentum will continue holding through the second half of 2026, though they caution that a potential global economic slowdown and weaker semiconductor demand from key markets such as the United States, China, and the European Union could moderate growth. The government remains confident of achieving full-year trade growth in the double-digit range.
