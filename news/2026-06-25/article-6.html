Malaysia's Headline Inflation Climbs to 2.0% YoY in May 2026

Free Malaysia Today | 25 June 2026 | Economy

Malaysia's headline inflation rate climbed to 2.0% year-on-year in May 2026, marking its highest reading in recent months, according to data released by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM). The increase was primarily driven by higher costs in the food and non-alcoholic beverages category, as well as persistent pressures in the housing and utilities segment.

Food inflation continued to be the dominant driver of the overall Consumer Price Index (CPI), with prices for fresh produce, processed foods, and dining out all recording modest increases. The cost of eating out at hawker stalls and food courts rose particularly in urban areas such as Kuala Lumpur, Penang, and Johor Bahru, where rental and labour costs for food operators have been creeping upward.

Core inflation — which strips out volatile food and energy prices — edged up slightly but remained contained, suggesting that underlying demand-side price pressures are manageable. Bank Negara Malaysia's monetary policy stance remains calibrated to support growth while keeping inflation anchored, with the overnight policy rate (OPR) held steady at its current level.

The government's targeted fuel subsidy rationalisation programme has had a contained pass-through effect on headline inflation so far, though economists warn that any further subsidy rationalisation in the second half of the year — particularly for electricity tariffs or additional fuel grades — could push inflation higher toward 2.5% to 3.0% by year-end.

Despite the uptick, Malaysia's inflation rate remains relatively low compared with many regional peers. The government has said it will continue to monitor price pressures closely and deploy targeted assistance measures to protect the lower-income B40 group from the impact of rising living costs, including through cash transfer programmes and price ceilings on essential food items.
