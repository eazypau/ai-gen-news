PM Anwar Praises Civil Service as Malaysia Climbs IMD World Competitiveness Index

Bernama | 25 June 2026 | Government

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim delivered a commendation to Malaysia's civil servants following the country's improved performance in the 2026 IMD World Competitiveness Yearbook, expressing pride in the professionalism and dedication demonstrated by public sector employees across all levels of government. The PM said the improved ranking reflects the hard work of civil servants who have driven key reforms and service delivery improvements over recent years.

The IMD World Competitiveness Yearbook, published annually by the International Institute for Management Development in Lausanne, Switzerland, assesses the economic competitiveness of 67 nations based on four factors: economic performance, government efficiency, business efficiency, and infrastructure. Malaysia's overall rank and notably its government efficiency sub-ranking improved compared to the previous year's assessment.

Anwar highlighted specific civil service initiatives that contributed to Malaysia's higher standing, including the digitalisation of government services through the MyDigital programme, streamlined business licensing processes, and improved responsiveness of frontline agencies such as the Inland Revenue Board, the Companies Commission, and LHDN. He said these improvements have directly translated into a better business environment and stronger investor confidence in Malaysia.

The Prime Minister said the government's ongoing public sector transformation programme — which includes performance-based incentive structures, competency development initiatives, and anti-corruption measures — will continue to be a key pillar of his administration's reform agenda. He called on civil servants to maintain their momentum and remain focused on delivering tangible outcomes for the Malaysian public.

Chief Secretary to the Government Tan Sri Shamsul Azri Abu Bakar said the improved IMD ranking is a testament to the collective effort of the entire civil service, and pledged to continue reforming public sector processes to make Malaysia an even more attractive destination for investment and talent. He also acknowledged that there remains significant room for improvement, particularly in areas such as digital government adoption and public procurement efficiency.
