Malaysia's Digital Finance Sector on Track to Surpass USD 25 Billion by 2034

Fintech News Malaysia | 25 June 2026 | Finance

Malaysia's digital finance sector is projected to exceed USD 25 billion in total market value by 2034, according to the latest industry analysis published by Fintech News Malaysia. The projection reflects the country's rapidly expanding base of digital banking users, e-wallet adoption, and growing participation in regulated cryptocurrency markets, all of which have seen accelerated growth in the first half of 2026.

An estimated four million Malaysians are projected to actively use cryptocurrencies in 2026, with investors aged under 35 constituting approximately 62% of all digital asset account holders on regulated platforms. Trading volumes on Securities Commission-registered digital asset exchanges jumped 23% in 2025 to over USD 4.3 billion, and the trend has continued into 2026 as global cryptocurrency markets enter a new bull cycle.

Beyond digital assets, Malaysia's five digital bank licensees — which commenced phased operations in 2024 and 2025 — are recording strong user acquisition figures, particularly among the unbanked and underbanked segments of the population. These digital-first financial institutions offer simplified account opening, micro-savings products, and AI-driven credit scoring that is better suited to gig economy workers and micro-entrepreneurs who lack traditional credit histories.

The broader e-wallet ecosystem, dominated by players such as Touch 'n Go eWallet, GrabPay, and Boost, continues to grow as more merchants and public services integrate digital payment options. The recent addition of Rapid KL's RM150 monthly travel pass to the Touch 'n Go eWallet platform is cited as a model for how digital payment integration can improve the convenience and accessibility of public infrastructure.

Malaysia's fintech industry body, the Fintech Association of Malaysia (FAOM), said the country is well-positioned to become a leading digital finance hub in ASEAN, provided that the regulatory environment continues to evolve in a manner that supports innovation while managing systemic risk. The association called for continued investment in digital financial literacy programmes to ensure that all Malaysians can safely and confidently participate in the digital economy.
