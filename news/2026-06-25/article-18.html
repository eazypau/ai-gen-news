Nutrition Month Malaysia 2026 Tackles the Double Burden of Malnutrition

Nutrition Month Malaysia | 25 June 2026 | Health

Nutrition Month Malaysia 2026 concluded its annual campaign this month with a range of public education activities aimed at tackling the country's growing double burden of malnutrition — a situation where both overnutrition (excessive calorie and nutrient intake) and undernutrition (deficiencies in essential vitamins and minerals) coexist within the same population, and sometimes within the same household.

This year's Nutrition Month activities, held from June 4 to 7 and extending through the remainder of the month with digital campaigns, included an interactive Food-Fit-Fun Fair that drew thousands of visitors across multiple venues. Participants had the opportunity to undergo free nutritional assessments, consult dietitians, and receive practical guidance on preparing balanced meals using affordable, locally available ingredients.

The theme for Nutrition Month Malaysia 2026 — "Delay No More: Eat Right, Be Active Today" — emphasises the urgency of addressing unhealthy dietary practices that are contributing to the rising burden of non-communicable diseases (NCDs) such as diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular conditions in Malaysia. Health authorities say that a large proportion of NCD cases could be prevented or delayed through simple lifestyle interventions.

A key focus of this year's campaign was on low-income communities, where dietary quality is often compromised by limited purchasing power and a lack of access to nutritional education. Community health workers and NGO volunteers partnered with the Malaysian Dietitians' Association to deliver targeted nutrition workshops in urban poor neighbourhoods, rural villages, and indigenous Orang Asli communities.

The Nutrition Society of Malaysia emphasised that changing dietary habits requires not only individual effort but systemic changes, including better school canteen standards, healthier food options in public institutions, and stronger regulations on the marketing of high-sugar and high-fat products to children. The organisation called on the government to use Nutrition Month as a springboard for sustained, year-round policy action on food and nutrition security in Malaysia.
