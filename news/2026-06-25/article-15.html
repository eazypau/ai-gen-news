Malaysians Lose RM830 Million to Online Scams in First Five Months of 2026

The Sun | 25 June 2026 | Crime

Malaysian scam victims collectively lost RM830 million to online fraud between January and May 2026, with fake investment schemes continuing to inflict by far the most severe financial damage, according to figures released by the Home Ministry. The staggering total underscores the persistent and escalating threat of digital fraud in Malaysia despite years of awareness campaigns and law enforcement crackdowns.

Fake investment scams alone accounted for RM361.63 million of the total losses — representing the single most damaging category of online fraud. These scams typically promise high returns through purported investment platforms in stocks, cryptocurrency, or foreign exchange trading, and target victims primarily through social media platforms, messaging applications, and online advertisements.

Other major scam categories contributing to the overall losses include Macau scams (where criminals impersonate government officials or law enforcement), e-commerce fraud, love scams, and parcel delivery scams. Scammers have increasingly adopted sophisticated tactics including the use of deepfake technology to impersonate celebrities and business leaders, and AI-generated voices to simulate telephone calls from family members or officials.

The Home Ministry and the Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) said that public education remains the most effective tool in the fight against scams, urging Malaysians to verify the legitimacy of investment platforms through official Securities Commission and Bank Negara Malaysia registries before transferring any funds. The national anti-scam centre (NASC) has been processing thousands of reports and has managed to freeze a number of suspect accounts to prevent further losses.

Malaysian banks were also recognised for their contributions to combating scam losses, with 68 banking staff honoured earlier this month for helping prevent RM139 million from falling into the hands of scammers. The banking sector has introduced additional verification steps, real-time fraud detection systems, and call kill switches as part of a coordinated national effort to slow the flow of funds to criminal organisations.
