Nine Foreigners Arrested in Pengkalan Kubor for Suspected Online Scam Activities

New Straits Times | 25 June 2026 | Crime

Nine foreign nationals were arrested in Pengkalan Kubor, Kelantan, on suspicion of entering Malaysia to conduct online scam operations, the Royal Malaysia Police announced. The detainees included six Chinese men, one Chinese woman, one Myanmar man, and one Myanmar woman, all of whom were found without valid immigration documents or valid reason for their presence at the border town.

Acting on intelligence reports, immigration officers and police personnel conducted the arrest operation following surveillance of a suspected safe house in the area. Checks on the premises uncovered 22 mobile phones, a Dell laptop, a laptop bag, a Chinese passport, and a Thai identity card — a cache of devices consistent with the scale of activity used in coordinated online scam operations targeting victims across multiple countries simultaneously.

Police investigators said the high ratio of mobile phones to individuals — more than two devices per person — strongly suggests the suspects were engaged in operating multiple fraudulent accounts or impersonation schemes concurrently. Online scam syndicates typically assign each operator several devices to manage different victims across various messaging platforms, investment apps, and social media sites.

The arrests form part of a broader law enforcement push in Kelantan, which shares a porous northern border with Thailand and has historically served as a transit and entry point for foreign nationals seeking to evade stricter border controls at major checkpoints. The Kelantan police chief said the operation reflects a heightened state of vigilance along the state's borders in response to intelligence reports about scam syndicate movements into Malaysia.

All nine suspects are being remanded for further investigation under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 and the Anti-Money Laundering, Anti-Terrorism Financing and Proceeds of Unlawful Activities Act 2001 (AMLA). Police are working to identify any Malaysian collaborators and to trace the financial flows connected to the suspected syndicate network.
