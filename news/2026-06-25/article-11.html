FIFA World Cup 2026: All 104 Matches Free to Watch in Malaysia via RTM and Unifi TV

Malay Mail | 25 June 2026 | Sports

Malaysian football fans have been given a golden opportunity to watch all 104 matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup through a combination of free-to-air and affordable paid platforms, marking a significant departure from the previous two decades of pay-TV exclusivity under Astro. Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil confirmed that RTM and Unifi TV together hold the full broadcasting rights for Malaysia.

RTM will carry World Cup matches free of charge on TV1, TV2, Sukan RTM, and TV Okey channels, with all broadcasts also available via the RTMKlik streaming platform and the MyGOV Malaysia app. However, viewers who wish to access RTM's live streams online are required to register with a MyDigital ID, in line with the government's push to increase digital identity adoption among Malaysians.

Unifi TV, as the primary broadcaster, will stream all 104 matches live in full HD with enhanced viewing features including multi-angle replays and real-time statistics overlays. Subscribers can purchase a full tournament season pass for RM60, while existing Unifi TV subscribers enjoy a discounted rate of RM50. A daily pass priced at RM20 is also available for more casual viewers who prefer not to commit to the full package.

The expanded 2026 FIFA World Cup features 48 teams — up from the previous 32-team format — and is co-hosted by the United States, Canada, and Mexico. With matches spanning a wider time range, many Malaysian fans are expected to flock to 24-hour mamak stalls, kopitiams, and community halls that traditionally become lively viewing hubs for major football tournaments, particularly for marquee knockout fixtures and the final.

Football associations and fan clubs across Malaysia have been organising communal viewing events to capture the excitement of the World Cup. The Federation of Malaysian Football Associations (FAM) said the expanded and accessible broadcast arrangement represents a major boost for grassroots football culture in Malaysia, and expressed hope that the World Cup's visibility would inspire the next generation of local footballers.
