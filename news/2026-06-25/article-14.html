Scam Syndicates Fleeing Southeast Asia May Be Relocating to Malaysia, NGO Warns

New Straits Times | 25 June 2026 | Crime

A leading Malaysian anti-human trafficking NGO has issued a stark warning that international scam syndicates being driven out of Cambodia, Laos, and Myanmar by intensified regional crackdowns may be shifting their operations to Malaysia. MHO secretary-general Datuk Hishamuddin Hashim told a press conference in Kuala Lumpur on June 2 that suspected networks allegedly connected to criminal groups originating from China have shown signs of establishing new bases within Malaysian territory.

The warning follows a series of raids conducted by Malaysian authorities in Perak, Selangor, Johor, and Kelantan that resulted in more than 100 arrests, the vast majority involving foreign nationals. Those detained are suspected of links to cryptocurrency investment scams, romance scams, and fraudulent job scheme operations that target victims primarily in Malaysia, Taiwan, China, and other Asian countries.

Datuk Hishamuddin said the syndicates' modus operandi typically involves luring vulnerable individuals — often young people from countries such as Myanmar, China, and Vietnam — with promises of legitimate employment abroad, only to force them to work in scam call centres once they arrive. Victims who refuse to cooperate reportedly face physical abuse and confinement, constituting elements of human trafficking and forced labour.

The Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) said it is working closely with international law enforcement agencies, including Interpol and the national police forces of China, Myanmar, and Singapore, to dismantle the cross-border syndicate networks. The Home Ministry has also announced plans to strengthen border controls and immigration screening processes at key entry points to intercept suspects before they can establish operations.

Civil society organisations have called on the Malaysian government to not only arrest and prosecute syndicate members but to ensure that victims of human trafficking receive proper rehabilitation and legal protection. The National Anti-Human Trafficking and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Council (MAPO) said it is expanding its victim identification and support programmes in response to the evolving threat landscape.
