Toh Ee Wei Refuses to Rule Out World Championship Title Defence Despite Injury

Free Malaysia Today | 25 June 2026 | Sports

Malaysia's national mixed doubles number one Toh Ee Wei has refused to completely close the door on defending her BWF World Championship title in New Delhi and competing at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, despite being sidelined with an injury. The 2025 World Champion, who partners with Chen Tang Jie, said she remains focused on her recovery and is determined to return to the court at the highest level.

Toh suffered the injury during a recent training session, with the Badminton Association of Malaysia (BAM) confirming she is currently undergoing a structured rehabilitation programme under the guidance of the national sports medicine team. The timeline for her return to competitive play has not been officially confirmed, but those close to the player say she is making positive progress.

The BWF World Championships in New Delhi represent a particularly significant tournament for Toh, as she and Chen Tang Jie will be defending the title they claimed in 2025 — a landmark achievement that ended a long wait for Malaysian mixed doubles glory on the world stage. A successful defence would cement their status as one of the greatest mixed doubles pairs in the country's badminton history.

The 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, is another priority event on the Malaysian badminton calendar. Toh's participation would give Malaysia a strong medal contender in the mixed doubles event, alongside the country's other competitive pairings. The Badminton Association of Malaysia said it is working closely with the sports ministry to ensure that Toh receives the best possible medical support to facilitate her timely recovery.

The Malaysian badminton fraternity has rallied behind Toh, with fans and former players expressing confidence that the resilient shuttler will return stronger. BAM deputy president Norza Zakaria said that Toh's mental strength and competitive spirit have always been among her greatest assets, and the association is confident she will make the right decision about her participation based on medical advice and her own readiness.
