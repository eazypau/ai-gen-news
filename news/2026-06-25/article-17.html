Survey: 54.4% of Malaysian Adults Are Overweight or Obese

Health Ministry / NHMS | 25 June 2026 | Health

More than half of Malaysia's adult population — approximately 54.4% — are classified as overweight or obese, according to findings from the National Health and Morbidity Survey (NHMS) 2023, which has been widely referenced in health policy discussions in 2026. The figure represents a sustained upward trend in the country's obesity burden and has prompted renewed calls for a comprehensive national strategy to address the public health crisis.

The NHMS data reveals that the prevalence of overweight and obesity is highest among adults aged 30 to 59, with women slightly more likely than men to be classified as obese. Urban populations in major cities such as Kuala Lumpur, Penang, and Johor Bahru show particularly high rates, attributed to sedentary lifestyles, high-calorie diets, and limited access to affordable healthy food options in dense urban environments.

Health experts point to Malaysia's unique food culture — characterised by a love of fried foods, sugary beverages, and high-portion meals — as a key contributor to the obesity epidemic. The proliferation of fast food outlets, convenience stores, and delivery apps has made it easier than ever for Malaysians to access calorie-dense food options while making little time for physical activity.

The financial burden of obesity-related non-communicable diseases (NCDs) such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease on Malaysia's public health system is estimated to run into billions of ringgit annually. The Health Ministry has called on employers, local governments, schools, and civil society organisations to join a coordinated national effort to promote healthier eating habits and regular physical activity from childhood.

Among the interventions being considered are an expansion of the sugar-sweetened beverage tax, tighter regulations on food labelling and advertising targeting children, subsidies for fresh fruits and vegetables in low-income areas, and the development of more public parks and recreational facilities. The government has also partnered with digital health platforms to offer personalised dietary coaching and fitness tracking tools to Malaysian adults at no cost.
