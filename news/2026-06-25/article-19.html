Transport Minister Defends Plan to Raise Traffic Violation Fine to RM500

Malay Mail | 25 June 2026 | Government

Transport Minister Anthony Loke has come out in strong defence of his ministry's proposal to raise the minimum penalty for traffic violations to RM500, pushing back against criticism from opposition lawmakers and consumer groups who argued the hike is excessive and would disproportionately penalise lower-income road users. Loke said the measure is necessary to improve road safety and reduce Malaysia's persistently high road fatality rate.

The proposal, which was announced as part of a broader road safety reform package, would increase fines for offences including running red lights, illegal lane changes, using a mobile phone while driving, and failure to wear a seatbelt. Under the current penalty structure, many of these offences carry fines as low as RM150 to RM300 — an amount that frequent violators dismiss as an insignificant deterrent relative to the convenience of breaking traffic rules.

Opposition lawmakers, including members from Perikatan Nasional, countered that the proposed RM500 minimum fine would place a heavy financial burden on low-wage earners, motorcycle couriers, and daily commuters who rely on motorcycles as their primary mode of transport. Several MPs called for a tiered fine system based on vehicle type and income level rather than a blanket increase across all categories of violations.

Loke acknowledged the concerns but maintained that road safety must be the overriding priority. He cited statistics showing that Malaysia consistently records among the highest road fatality rates per capita in Southeast Asia, with over 7,000 deaths on Malaysian roads annually. The minister argued that the current fine levels have demonstrably failed to change driver behaviour and that a more impactful financial deterrent is overdue.

The Transport Ministry has said it will continue to engage with stakeholders — including the Road Transport Department (JPJ), police, consumer associations, and industry groups — before finalising the revised penalty schedule. A public consultation process is expected to conclude by the end of July, with implementation targeted for the first quarter of 2027 pending parliamentary approval.
