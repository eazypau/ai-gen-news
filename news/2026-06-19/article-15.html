Malaysia Raises Employment Pass Salary Thresholds Effective June 1, 2026, Raising Industry Concerns

Business TechWire Asia / Ministry of Home Affairs 19 June 2026

Malaysia's Ministry of Home Affairs implemented significantly revised Employment Pass salary thresholds effective June 1, 2026, raising the minimum salary requirements that foreign professionals must meet to qualify for the pass category. The changes have been formally welcomed by some quarters as a move to attract higher-skilled foreign workers, but they have also raised concerns within the semiconductor and technology manufacturing sectors about their ability to attract and retain essential foreign talent at mid-level engineering roles.

The Employment Pass system is a key mechanism through which Malaysian employers sponsor foreign professionals across a range of technical, managerial, and specialist roles. The revised thresholds represent a meaningful upward revision from previous levels, particularly for the lower-tier pass categories that are commonly used to bring in mid-career engineers and technical specialists from countries including India, China, South Korea, and Europe.

Industry associations in the semiconductor sector have flagged that the higher thresholds could create friction in hiring pipelines at a particularly critical moment — as Malaysia is actively trying to scale up its chip manufacturing capacity and move up the semiconductor value chain. Smaller wafer fabrication support firms and assembly and test houses, which typically operate on tighter margins than larger multinational players, are the most likely to be affected.

Government officials have defended the revisions as a necessary step to ensure that Employment Pass holders are genuinely filling positions requiring high-level expertise, while simultaneously protecting the interests of Malaysian workers and encouraging companies to invest more in local talent development. The Ministry indicated that the new thresholds were calibrated based on current market salary surveys and international benchmarking.

Human resource professionals noted that the transition period may cause short-term disruptions for firms currently processing Employment Pass applications under the old thresholds. Many companies are now reviewing their foreign hiring strategies and in some cases accelerating plans to increase the pipeline of locally trained engineers through partnerships with Malaysian universities, technical colleges, and the Semiconductor Industry Association.
