Freedom of Information Bill Set to Be Tabled in Parliament, Anwar Reaffirms Commitment

Politics The Sun Daily / Prime Minister's Office 19 June 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has reaffirmed the government's commitment to tabling and passing a Freedom of Information (FOI) Bill in Parliament in 2026, as part of a broader legislative reform agenda announced in his New Year Address. The FOI Bill is considered one of the most significant transparency and accountability reforms the Malaysian government has proposed in recent years, and its introduction has been long demanded by civil society, journalists, and opposition politicians.

A Freedom of Information law would grant Malaysian citizens and organisations the legal right to request access to documents and information held by government ministries and agencies, subject to defined exemptions for national security, privacy, and commercially sensitive information. Proponents argue that such legislation would fundamentally improve government transparency, reduce opportunities for corruption, and strengthen citizens' ability to hold public officials accountable.

The government also confirmed it would table legislation to formally separate the powers of the Public Prosecutor from those of the Attorney General — another long-standing governance reform. Currently, the Attorney General serves dual roles as both the government's chief legal adviser and the sole authority over criminal prosecution decisions, a concentration of power that critics have long argued creates conflicts of interest and reduces prosecutorial independence.

Civil society groups, including the Centre to Combat Corruption and Cronyism (C4) and the Bar Council, have welcomed the announcements but urged the government to ensure that the FOI Bill's exemptions are narrowly drafted and not used as a loophole to shield information from legitimate public scrutiny. Comparative models from countries including New Zealand, India, and the United Kingdom have been cited as potential reference points for Malaysia's legislative drafting.

The Bills are expected to be introduced in the current or upcoming parliamentary session. Legal experts noted that the FOI Bill in particular will require careful drafting to balance transparency with the protection of genuinely sensitive information, and that the implementation framework — including the establishment of an independent information commissioner — will be as important as the law itself in determining whether the legislation delivers meaningful change in practice.
