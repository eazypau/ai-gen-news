Malaysia Semiconductor Sector Benefits from China+1 Shift and Surging AI Data Centre Demand

Technology TechWire Asia / Hong Leong Investment Bank 19 June 2026

Malaysia's semiconductor industry is experiencing a powerful tailwind from two major structural trends: accelerating supply chain diversification away from concentrated China-based manufacturing — the so-called China+1 strategy — and the surging global demand for optical and power semiconductors driven by artificial intelligence data centre build-outs. Hong Leong Investment Bank (HLIB) analysts highlighted these drivers in a recent sector report presented at SEMICON SEA 2026.

The electrical and electronics sector, Malaysia's single largest export category, attracted RM28.5 billion in approved investments in 2025, with momentum carrying strongly into 2026. Global semiconductor equipment makers are deepening localisation of their supply chains within Malaysia, further anchoring the country's position in the global chip ecosystem. Companies like Infineon, NXP, and several major US and European chipmakers have either expanded or announced significant new capacity investments at Malaysian facilities.

The SEMICON SEA 2026 conference, held in late May, brought together industry leaders to discuss Malaysia's role in the evolving semiconductor landscape. A recurring theme at the conference was the tension between Malaysia's impressive investment attraction record and a persistent talent gap — specifically, the country does not yet have enough semiconductor engineers to fully support the scale of chip investment flowing in, particularly for more advanced process nodes.

Malaysia's semiconductor value chain remains heavily concentrated in back-end processes: assembly, testing, and packaging. However, there is growing ambition — and some concrete movement — toward front-end wafer fabrication, which carries significantly higher economic value. Infineon's planned wafer fabrication expansion at Kulim Hi-Tech Park is the most high-profile example of this potential value-chain upgrade.

Analysts at HLIB maintained a positive outlook for Malaysia's semiconductor sector through 2026, noting that the structural demand tailwinds from AI infrastructure investment are likely to remain strong. However, they cautioned that the government needs to act decisively on talent development, educational pipeline investment, and Employment Pass policies to ensure that Malaysia can translate capital investment into sustained high-value manufacturing leadership.
