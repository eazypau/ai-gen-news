PAS Makes No Decision on Perikatan Nasional Future After Party Meeting

Politics MalaysiaNow 19 June 2026

PAS, the pan-Malaysian Islamic party and a key component of the Perikatan Nasional (PN) opposition coalition, has reported that it has not reached any decision regarding the latest developments within PN following a recent party meeting. PAS information chief Ahmad Fadhli Shaari confirmed that various internal coalition issues were discussed during the gathering but emphasised that no announcement would be made at this stage.

The meeting follows a period of heightened speculation about the cohesion of the Perikatan Nasional bloc, which groups PAS with Parti Pribumi Bersatu Malaysia (Bersatu) and other allied parties. Sources within political circles have hinted at growing tensions over coalition strategy, seat allocation considerations ahead of any future elections, and differences in approach to parliamentary business. However, party officials have been careful to publicly downplay any suggestion of a rift.

PAS's measured response reflects the party's historically cautious communication style on sensitive coalition matters. The party has previously navigated complex coalition dynamics following the collapse of the Perikatan Nasional government in 2021 and has since rebuilt its role as one of the dominant opposition forces in Parliament, particularly after strong performances by PAS candidates in recent state elections.

Meanwhile, Amanah vice-president Mahfuz Omar noted from the government side that Johor has significantly benefited from its close cooperation between the DAP and the federal government — a comment seen as an indirect contrast to the opposition coalition's current internal discussions about its long-term direction and cooperative framework.

Political observers are closely watching for any formal announcement from PN regarding its unified stance ahead of the next session of Parliament. The coalition's ability to present a coherent opposition front will be critical to its effectiveness in challenging the government's legislative agenda, particularly on key bills expected to be tabled in the coming months.
