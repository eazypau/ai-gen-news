Malaysia-Japan Partnership Key to Stronger Technology Ecosystem — Anwar

Technology New Straits Times 19 June 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has underscored the significance of Malaysia's partnership with Japan in strengthening the regional technology ecosystem. Speaking following high-level bilateral discussions, Anwar highlighted how the two nations' complementary strengths — Malaysia's growing expertise in semiconductor assembly, testing, and packaging, paired with Japan's leadership in materials, precision equipment, and advanced manufacturing — can forge a more resilient and competitive supply chain.

The Prime Minister emphasised that as global supply chains face mounting pressure due to geopolitical tensions and the ongoing push for diversification away from single-country dependency, collaboration with established technology partners like Japan is more critical than ever. Malaysia's strategic positioning in the ASEAN region, combined with its rapidly expanding digital infrastructure, makes it a natural partner for Japan's technology firms seeking to broaden their presence in Southeast Asia.

Officials from both sides discussed specific areas of cooperation including research and development in next-generation semiconductor processes, talent exchange programmes, and joint investment in Malaysia's existing technology corridors such as Penang's Silicon Corridor and the Kulim Hi-Tech Park. The government also signalled its intent to fast-track approvals for Japanese firms looking to establish or expand manufacturing facilities in Malaysia.

Industry observers noted that Japan has been one of Malaysia's longest-standing technology partners, with companies such as Renesas, Murata, and TDK all maintaining significant operations in the country. The renewed focus on deepening this partnership comes amid a broader Malaysian push to move up the semiconductor value chain from back-end packaging and assembly toward higher-value front-end wafer fabrication processes.

Anwar's administration has consistently positioned technology and digitalisation as central pillars of Malaysia's long-term economic strategy, with the country targeting an increase in its digital economy's contribution to GDP. Analysts said the Malaysia-Japan partnership, if sustained, could help Malaysia accelerate this ambition while providing Japanese manufacturers with a stable, high-skill manufacturing base in a geopolitically favourable location.
