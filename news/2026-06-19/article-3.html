Ringgit Strengthening Trend Continues, Could Test Pre-Conflict Highs Near 3.88

Finance IDNFinancials / Bloomberg 19 June 2026

The Malaysian ringgit has maintained its position as one of Asia's best-performing currencies in 2026, with financial analysts now eyeing the possibility that it could test resistance near the 3.88 per US dollar mark — levels last seen before the latest bout of geopolitical turbulence in West Asia. The currency is currently trading around 3.95 per US dollar, reflecting a meaningful appreciation from the weaker levels of late 2025.

Global macro strategist Hassan Malik at Loomis Sayles noted that Malaysia's strong cyclical fundamentals, its status as a clean energy exporter, and its linkage to the global technology investment cycle provide the ringgit with a competitive advantage relative to other regional currencies. A continued current account surplus, driven by strong export performance in the electrical and electronics sector, further underpins the currency's bullish outlook.

Economists across major financial institutions project the RM/USD exchange rate to average around 4.00 for 2026, with the possibility of ending the year closer to 3.90. Deutsche Bank, in particular, expects the currency to trade within the 3.85–3.90 range against the US dollar in 2026 under its base-case scenario. These projections are contingent on sustained global risk appetite and continued inflows of foreign direct investment into Malaysia's technology sector.

The ringgit's strength has also been supported by Bank Negara Malaysia's monetary policy stance, which has kept the Overnight Policy Rate steady, balancing domestic growth support with the need to maintain currency stability. The central bank's intervention capacity and its management of foreign exchange reserves have also provided a buffer against excessive volatility.

For Malaysian businesses and households, a stronger ringgit offers the dual benefit of lowering import costs — particularly for energy and raw materials — and potentially reducing inflationary pressures. However, exporters have been monitoring the currency's appreciation closely, as a stronger ringgit can compress margins for firms that invoice predominantly in US dollars. Overall, market sentiment remains constructive, with most analysts viewing the ringgit's trajectory as reflective of Malaysia's solid economic fundamentals.
