PHKL Launches AI-Powered Radiotherapy System for More Personalised Cancer Treatment

Health Digital News Asia / Pantai Hospital KL 19 June 2026

Pantai Hospital Kuala Lumpur (PHKL) has unveiled a new AI-powered radiotherapy system designed to deliver more personalised and precise cancer treatment for patients undergoing radiation therapy. The system represents a significant technological advance in oncology care in Malaysia, leveraging artificial intelligence to optimise treatment planning, target tumours with greater accuracy, and reduce the dose of radiation received by surrounding healthy tissue.

Traditional radiotherapy planning is a complex, time-intensive process that relies heavily on the expertise and judgment of oncology specialists working with imaging data. The new AI-assisted system at PHKL is designed to augment this process by automating elements of tumour contouring, treatment plan generation, and quality assurance verification — tasks that previously required hours of specialist time and were prone to inter-clinician variability.

Dr. Ahmad Fadzil, Head of Oncology at PHKL, described the new system as a "quantum leap" in the hospital's ability to provide world-class cancer care. He noted that the AI platform's ability to rapidly generate and compare multiple treatment plan scenarios allows oncologists to select the optimal approach for each individual patient, a key principle of personalised medicine. The system is also capable of adaptive planning, adjusting treatment delivery in response to changes in tumour size or patient anatomy over the course of a treatment course.

Cancer remains one of Malaysia's leading causes of mortality, with tens of thousands of new cases diagnosed annually across the country. Improving access to high-quality radiation therapy has been identified as a national health priority, and PHKL's investment in AI-assisted technology reflects a broader trend of Malaysian private hospitals upgrading their oncology capabilities to compete with regional medical tourism destinations such as Singapore and Bangkok.

The launch aligns with the Malaysian government's broader digital health agenda, which encourages public and private healthcare providers to adopt innovative technologies to improve patient outcomes and healthcare system efficiency. Industry observers expect more Malaysian hospitals to invest in similar AI-enhanced diagnostic and treatment technologies over the coming years, particularly as the cost of these systems continues to decline and the evidence base for their clinical efficacy grows.
