PM Term-Limit Bill Falls Short of Two-Thirds Majority by Just Two Votes

Politics Malaysiakini / Parliament Malaysia 19 June 2026

The Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026, which sought to impose a 10-year term limit on the office of Prime Minister, has failed to pass in the Dewan Rakyat after narrowly missing the two-thirds supermajority required for constitutional amendments. The March 2 vote saw 146 members supporting the bill with 44 abstentions and none voting against — but the tally fell short by just two votes of the 148 needed to clear the constitutional threshold.

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim had proposed the term limit as part of a broader reform agenda, initially announced in his January 2026 New Year Address. The proposal was widely seen as an effort to institutionalise safeguards against the concentration of political power and to rebuild public confidence in governance following years of political instability. Observers had initially expected the bill to enjoy bipartisan support given its reformist framing.

Political analysts attributed the shortfall to wavering support among certain government backbenchers and the position of several opposition-aligned MPs who abstained rather than voting in favour. Some abstaining members raised procedural concerns about the specific wording of the amendment, while others reportedly sought additional provisions that were not included in the final version of the bill.

The failure of the bill is seen as a political setback for Anwar's unity government, though legal experts noted that the Prime Minister could reintroduce an amended version in a future parliamentary session. The government retains options to negotiate with opposition MPs and address the concerns that led to the abstentions, potentially paving the way for a revised bill to pass.

Opposition leaders have offered mixed reactions — some expressing willingness to support a revised bill with stricter provisions, while others viewed the vote's outcome as a broader reflection of the government's difficulty in securing consensus on major constitutional changes. Constitutional law academics said the episode highlighted the challenges of governing with a supermajority requirement in an increasingly fragmented political landscape.
