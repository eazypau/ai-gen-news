Nutrition Month Malaysia 2026 Urges Public: Delay No More — Eat Right, Be Active Today

Health Nutrition Month Malaysia / MOH 19 June 2026

Nutrition Month Malaysia 2026 has officially launched its annual nationwide health awareness campaign under the theme "Delay No More — Eat Right, Be Active Today." The campaign, coordinated by the Malaysian Dietitians' Association in partnership with the Ministry of Health, aims to empower Malaysians of all ages to make healthier dietary choices and adopt more physically active lifestyles to reduce the risk of diet-related chronic diseases.

Malaysia faces a significant and growing burden of non-communicable diseases, with rates of obesity, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease among the highest in Southeast Asia. The Health Ministry's National Health and Morbidity Survey has consistently documented that poor dietary habits — characterised by high sugar consumption, excessive processed food intake, and low vegetable and fruit consumption — are major contributors to this public health challenge.

The 2026 campaign is deploying a range of outreach methods including community cooking demonstrations, school nutrition education programmes, workplace wellness initiatives, and a digital media campaign targeting young adults across social media platforms. Specially prepared educational materials including meal planning guides, healthy recipe booklets, and exercise tips are being distributed through health clinics, community centres, and schools nationwide.

Nutrition experts participating in the campaign emphasised that even modest improvements in diet quality can significantly reduce an individual's risk of developing chronic diseases. They highlighted practical, affordable steps such as replacing sugary beverages with water, increasing consumption of vegetables and whole grains, and incorporating at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week — guidelines that align with WHO recommendations.

The campaign also addresses the social and economic dimensions of healthy eating in Malaysia, acknowledging that cost and accessibility can be barriers to nutritious food choices for lower-income households. Partnerships with local markets and food vendors are being explored to make healthier options more accessible and affordable, while food labelling education is helping consumers make more informed choices when grocery shopping.
