Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.5% in Q1 2026, Fastest Expansion Since 2022

Malaysia's economy grew at its fastest pace in four years in the first quarter of 2026, with real GDP expanding 5.5% year-on-year — surpassing the Bloomberg consensus forecast of 4.8% and cementing the country's status as one of Southeast Asia's strongest-performing economies. The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) released the official Q1 GDP data, which showed broad-based strength across all major sectors of the economy.

The services sector, which accounts for approximately 55% of Malaysia's GDP, recorded 5.8% growth in Q1 2026, led by financial services, wholesale and retail trade, tourism-related activities, and digital and information technology services. The manufacturing sector grew 5.2%, driven by robust export orders in the electrical and electronics (E&E) segment — particularly semiconductors, integrated circuits, and electronic components destined for global AI hardware manufacturers.

Exports were the standout driver of Malaysia's Q1 performance, with total merchandise exports rising 12.3% year-on-year in ringgit terms. The E&E sector dominated export growth, reflecting Malaysia's critical role in the global AI supply chain. Non-resource-based manufactured exports — including medical devices, chemicals, and industrial equipment — also posted double-digit growth, pointing to a broadening of Malaysia's export base beyond its traditional strengths.

Private consumption remained healthy, growing 5.9% as household spending was supported by a robust labour market with unemployment at 3.2%, rising minimum wages, and strong consumer confidence. Public investment in infrastructure, including data centres, renewable energy projects, and transport connectivity, also contributed positively to growth through government capital expenditure of 8.1%.

Economists at major financial institutions revised up their full-year 2026 growth forecasts for Malaysia following the Q1 release, with median forecasts now clustering around 5.0–5.2% for the full year. The strong start to 2026 supports expectations of a full-year performance that could reach 5.5% if the current momentum is sustained through the second half — what would be Malaysia's best annual growth performance since the post-pandemic rebound of 2022.
