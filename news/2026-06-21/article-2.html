Anwar Warns Europe: Treat Developing Nations Fairly or We Will Deal Elsewhere

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim issued a pointed warning to European nations on Friday, stating that developing countries will not hesitate to seek alternative trading and strategic partners if they continue to experience unfair treatment in bilateral dealings. His remarks came during a high-level diplomatic engagement in Kuala Lumpur attended by several European envoys.

Anwar cited a long-standing defence procurement dispute with Norway as a prime example of what he described as inequitable treatment. Malaysia had paid 95 per cent of the contract value for a defence-related purchase but received no satisfactory explanation when the Norwegian side failed to fulfil its obligations, despite repeated diplomatic efforts to seek clarification. The Prime Minister said the episode was not an isolated incident, and that developing nations across Asia, Africa, and Latin America had raised similar concerns.

In a striking contrast, Anwar noted that Russia had honoured its commitments to Malaysia in their bilateral agreements despite significant geopolitical pressures, adding that Malaysia has not encountered any case of breach in its dealings with Moscow. He stressed that Malaysia's foreign policy is guided by principles of mutual respect, equal partnership, and non-interference — not by political alignment with any bloc.

Anwar's remarks drew attention to the broader Global South narrative of seeking a more equitable international order. He reiterated Malaysia's position that developing nations have a right to diversify partnerships and engage with all willing partners, including those in Asia, the Middle East, and beyond, when Western counterparts fail to deliver on commitments.

The Prime Minister's comments are expected to add to diplomatic pressure on European governments to review their engagement practices with Southeast Asian nations. Malaysia, as the current ASEAN chair, is seen as a key voice in shaping regional posture toward great power competition and economic partnership negotiations.
