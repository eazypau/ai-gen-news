Education Ministry Launches New Student Safety Guidelines and Protection Policy

Malaysia's Ministry of Education launched a comprehensive set of new student safety guidelines and a National Student Protection Policy this month, marking the most significant update to school safety standards in over a decade. The guidelines were developed in close collaboration with the National Institute of Occupational Safety and Health (Niosh), child welfare experts, school administrators, and parent-teacher associations across all 13 states and three federal territories.

The new framework covers four main areas of student safety: physical safety within school premises, psychological and emotional wellbeing, protection from bullying and harassment, and digital safety in an increasingly connected school environment. Each area includes specific protocols, mandatory training requirements for teachers and staff, and escalation procedures for reporting and addressing safety incidents.

A key highlight of the new policy is the introduction of mandatory safety audits for all government and government-aided schools, to be conducted annually by Niosh-certified assessors. Schools failing to meet minimum safety standards will be required to submit remediation plans and will be subject to follow-up inspections. The ministry also announced that a dedicated hotline and online reporting portal will be established for students, parents, and school staff to report safety concerns confidentially.

The policy also addresses the growing issue of school violence and bullying, introducing a tiered intervention system that distinguishes between minor misconduct, serious behavioural incidents, and criminal matters requiring police referral. All teachers will undergo mandatory training in de-escalation techniques and trauma-informed approaches to student discipline, replacing punitive measures with restorative practices where appropriate.

Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek said the new guidelines reflect the government's commitment to ensuring that every Malaysian student feels safe, supported, and respected in their learning environment. "A child who does not feel safe cannot learn effectively. Safety is not a luxury — it is a fundamental prerequisite for quality education," she said at the launch ceremony.
