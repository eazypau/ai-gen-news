Bank Negara: Ringgit Supported by Malaysia's Strong Economic Outlook and Reform Momentum

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) has affirmed that the ringgit's sustained appreciation against major currencies is underpinned by Malaysia's solid economic fundamentals and the government's ongoing structural reform programme. In a statement and subsequent remarks by senior officials, BNM pointed to the country's robust Q1 2026 GDP growth of 5.5%, contained inflation trending near the upper band of the central bank's comfort zone, and continued healthy current account surplus as the core factors that have made the ringgit increasingly attractive to international investors. The Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) has been held steady at 3.0% since mid-2024, providing monetary stability while keeping borrowing conditions supportive of investment and consumption.

BNM Governor Datuk Seri Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour noted that the ringgit's performance reflects a fundamental re-rating of Malaysia's economic prospects rather than a temporary speculative move driven by short-term capital flows. He cited the structural transformation of Malaysia's export base — now heavily weighted toward higher-value electronics, semiconductors, and digital services — as a durable source of foreign exchange earnings that supports the currency over the medium term. The Governor also highlighted the sharp increase in approved FDI commitments in manufacturing and the digital economy, which is translating into actual capital inflows that underpin demand for the ringgit.

Ongoing structural reforms under the Madani government were explicitly acknowledged by BNM as a contributing factor to investor confidence. The rationalisation of petrol subsidies completed in 2024, the gradual broadening of the goods and services tax base, improvements in public financial management, and the National Investment Facilitation Council's track record of speeding up project approvals were all cited as evidence that Malaysia is addressing the supply-side bottlenecks that historically held back productivity growth. BNM indicated that continued implementation of these reforms would be important to sustaining the currency's strength and the broader economic momentum.

BNM also addressed the question of whether the ringgit's appreciation poses risks to Malaysia's export competitiveness. The Governor indicated that the central bank monitors exchange rate developments carefully and does not target a specific exchange rate level, but does intervene to smooth excessive volatility that could disrupt business planning. He argued that a moderate and fundamentals-driven strengthening of the ringgit is not harmful to exporters — particularly in the semiconductor and electronics sectors, where Malaysia's products are not primarily competing on price but on quality, reliability, and supply chain positioning. Exporters with significant imported input costs may even benefit from a stronger ringgit on their input cost side.

Market analysts broadly concurred with BNM's assessment, noting that the ringgit's trajectory diverges favourably from several regional peers whose currencies have come under pressure due to fiscal deterioration, political uncertainty, or commodity price weakness. The USD/MYR exchange rate reaching 4.1459 in mid-June 2026 represents the strongest level since 2021 and marks a sharp reversal from the historic lows near 4.80 seen in early 2024. Analysts cautioned, however, that external risks including a potential US Federal Reserve policy pivot, renewed China-related trade tensions, or a sharp reversal in commodity prices could exert near-term pressure on the ringgit regardless of Malaysia's domestic fundamentals.
