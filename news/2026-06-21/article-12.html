Malaysia Launches New Education Blueprint 2026–2035 to Reshape the Nation's Learning System

Malaysia's Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek officially launched the Malaysia Education Blueprint 2026–2035 (MEB 2026-2035), a comprehensive 10-year roadmap that will guide the transformation of the country's pre-tertiary education system for the next decade. The blueprint succeeds the Malaysia Education Blueprint 2013–2025 and reflects lessons learned over more than a decade of education reform, while addressing the rapidly evolving demands of the digital economy and a globalised labour market.

The new blueprint is built on five strategic pillars: academic excellence and future-ready skills, holistic student development and wellbeing, teacher quality and professional development, inclusive and equitable access to education, and governance efficiency and accountability. Under each pillar, specific measurable targets have been set — including raising Malaysia's PISA performance to the top 30 globally by 2030, doubling TVET enrolment, and ensuring 100% digital connectivity for all schools by 2027.

A significant focus of the new blueprint is on strengthening Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET), which the government views as essential to meeting the skilled workforce needs of Malaysia's high-tech industrial ambitions. New dual-track TVET pathways will be introduced in secondary schools, allowing students to specialise in fields such as semiconductor technology, robotics, advanced manufacturing, and digital services alongside their academic subjects.

The blueprint also places strong emphasis on student mental health and holistic wellbeing, acknowledging the growing prevalence of mental health challenges among Malaysian students. Schools will be required to appoint qualified counsellors, and a new national student wellness monitoring system will be established to identify and support at-risk students early.

The Education Minister called the blueprint "a living document" that will be reviewed and updated every three years to ensure it remains responsive to changing national needs and global education best practices. She urged all stakeholders — parents, teachers, school administrators, and employers — to actively participate in its implementation and hold the government accountable for achieving its targets.
