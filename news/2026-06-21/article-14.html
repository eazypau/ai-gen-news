FIFA World Cup 2026: Complete Guide for Malaysians on How and Where to Watch

The 2026 FIFA World Cup kicked off on June 12 in the United States, Canada, and Mexico, marking the first edition of the tournament to feature an expanded 48-team format. For Malaysian football fans, this year's World Cup brought an unexpected twist: Astro, which had held broadcasting rights for all recent World Cup tournaments, failed to secure the 2026 rights, leading to a scramble by government-linked broadcasters to fill the gap.

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil confirmed that all 104 matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup will be made available to Malaysian viewers through RTM (Radio Television Malaysia) and Unifi TV. RTM's free-to-air channels and its streaming platform RTMKlik will carry all group stage and knockout matches, while Unifi TV offers a premium viewing experience with additional commentary options, multi-screen viewing, and match highlights.

Digital Minister Gobind Singh Deo also announced that Malaysians can stream World Cup matches via the MyGOV Malaysia app, making it accessible on smartphones and tablets for fans on the go. Unifi TV's full season pass is priced at RM60 for new customers and RM50 for existing subscribers, with RM20 daily passes available for casual viewers who only want to watch selected matches. All passes became available from June 12, 2026.

The expanded 48-team format means the World Cup group stage features 12 groups of four teams, with the two best-performing teams from each group plus eight best third-placed teams advancing to the round of 32. For Malaysian fans, match times vary significantly due to the time difference with North America — most games kick off between midnight and noon Malaysia time (MYT), with some early-morning fixtures scheduled as early as 3am or 4am MYT.

The knockout phase begins in late June and culminates in the World Cup Final on July 19, 2026, to be held at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey. Malaysian fans who wish to follow their favourite teams through to the end should prepare for several late-night and early-morning viewing sessions. Football cafés and sports bars across the country are reporting strong bookings, with fans gathering en masse to watch high-profile games together.
