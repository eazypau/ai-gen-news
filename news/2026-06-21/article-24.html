PETRONAS Confirms Fuel Supply at Its Stations Secured Through End of June 2026

PETRONAS has issued a formal assurance that fuel supply at all its petrol stations across Malaysia is fully secured through the end of June 2026, seeking to dispel concerns among motorists and businesses that had emerged in recent weeks. The statement, released through the company's official communications channels, confirmed that both RON95 and RON97 petrol, as well as diesel variants including Euro 5 diesel, are available in adequate quantities at PETRONAS stations nationwide. The company said its supply chain management team is monitoring inventory levels continuously and has contingency arrangements in place to address any localised distribution issues that may arise.

The reassurance came against the backdrop of heightened geopolitical tensions in the Middle East involving Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, the critical waterway through which approximately one-fifth of global oil trade transits. Earlier in June, concerns were raised by analysts and market commentators about whether disruptions to tanker traffic through the Strait — or the threat thereof — could affect Malaysia's crude oil and refined petroleum product supply chains. While Malaysia is itself a net oil producer through PETRONAS's upstream operations, the country still imports certain grades of crude and refined products to supplement domestic refining capacity, and any sustained closure or disruption of Hormuz shipping lanes would have ripple effects on global oil markets and supply availability.

PETRONAS confirmed that it has diversified its supply sourcing to ensure that its downstream operations — including its network of PETRONAS petrol stations which number in the thousands across Malaysia — are not dependent on any single supply route or source region. The company sources crude oil from a combination of its own Malaysian and international upstream fields, supplemented by term and spot purchases from producers in West Africa, Central Asia, and Southeast Asia in addition to the Gulf region. This diversification was cited as the primary reason why Hormuz-related concerns do not translate into an immediate supply vulnerability for Malaysian consumers.

The RON95 petrol subsidy, which is a critical element of Malaysia's social protection framework for lower-income motorists, was confirmed to remain in place through the end of June 2026. The government has been gradually reforming petrol subsidies since 2024, with the implementation of targeted subsidies that restrict the subsidised RON95 price to vehicles owned by Malaysian citizens earning below a specified income threshold. PETRONAS plays a central role in the administration of this subsidy mechanism at the point of sale, and the company affirmed that systems supporting the targeted subsidy implementation are operating normally. Eligible motorists should not experience any change in their access to subsidised fuel, the company said.

Beyond the immediate supply assurance, the episode highlighted the broader importance of energy security planning for Malaysia as the country navigates an increasingly volatile global geopolitical environment. PETRONAS has invested significantly in downstream storage capacity and logistics infrastructure over the past decade, enabling it to maintain buffer stocks that provide resilience against short-term supply disruptions. Energy security analysts noted that Malaysia's position as both a producer and refiner of petroleum gives it structural advantages relative to import-dependent neighbours, and that PETRONAS's integrated model — spanning exploration, production, refining, and retail distribution — allows for a degree of supply chain coordination that pure importers cannot achieve. The company said it would continue to provide updates as the situation in the Middle East evolves.
