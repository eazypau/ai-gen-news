Anwar: Malaysia Ready for Industrial Leap with Japan in AI, Digital and Clean Energy Sectors

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim extended a renewed and urgent invitation to Japanese companies to deepen their investments in Malaysia, describing the two countries' economic relationship as entering a new chapter defined by high technology, clean energy, and digital innovation. Speaking at a Malaysia-Japan Business Forum in Kuala Lumpur, Anwar said Malaysia possesses all the key ingredients to become Japan's preferred high-tech manufacturing and investment partner in Southeast Asia.

Anwar highlighted four priority sectors for expanded Malaysia-Japan cooperation: advanced semiconductor packaging and chip design, artificial intelligence and data infrastructure, quantum computing research and commercialisation, and clean energy — including hydrogen, offshore wind, and advanced solar technologies. He said Malaysia is prepared to offer competitive incentives, streamlined regulatory approvals, and a highly skilled workforce to Japanese companies willing to invest in these sectors.

The Prime Minister also acknowledged Japan's historically significant role as one of Malaysia's earliest and most consistent foreign investors, with Japanese companies having invested in Malaysia's manufacturing, automotive, electronics, and financial sectors for more than five decades. He described this long-standing relationship as a foundation of trust on which a more advanced technological partnership could now be built.

Japanese business leaders at the forum responded positively, with representatives from major corporations including Sony Semiconductor, Renesas Electronics, and Mitsubishi Electric expressing interest in expanded Malaysian operations. Several companies indicated they were actively evaluating new investment proposals in the semiconductor, robotics, and clean energy spaces, encouraged by Malaysia's improving business environment and policy predictability.

MITI Minister Tengku Zafrul Abdul Aziz, who accompanied the Prime Minister at the forum, announced that a joint Malaysia-Japan Task Force on Technology Investment will be established to fast-track the processing of high-tech FDI proposals from Japanese companies. The task force is expected to hold its inaugural meeting in Tokyo in August 2026, with concrete investment announcements anticipated before year-end.
