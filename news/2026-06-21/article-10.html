Malaysia's New Online Safety Rules Take Effect: What Changes for Social Media Users

Malaysia's Online Safety Act 2025 moved into a new enforcement phase on June 1, 2026, when the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) activated two key subsidiary codes: the Child Protection Code (CPC) and the Risk Mitigation Code (RMC). Together, these codes impose significant new obligations on licensed social media platforms and online service providers operating in Malaysia, fundamentally changing how they must manage safety, content, and user verification.

Under the Child Protection Code, all licensed social media platforms are now required to implement effective age-verification systems and ensure that only users aged 16 and above can register and maintain active accounts. This applies to major global platforms including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and X (formerly Twitter). Platforms are expected to use a combination of document verification, AI-based age estimation, and parental consent workflows to achieve compliance.

The Risk Mitigation Code goes further, mandating platforms to conduct regular risk assessments, strengthen content moderation systems against harmful and illegal material, introduce tools for users to identify AI-generated and deepfake content, and implement advertiser verification mechanisms to combat online scams — a serious and growing problem in Malaysia. Platforms must also review their recommendation algorithms to reduce the amplification of harmful content.

Non-compliance with either code may attract enforcement action under the Online Safety Act 2025, including prosecution under criminal provisions or administrative financial penalties of up to RM10 million per violation. The MCMC has set up a dedicated compliance unit to monitor platform adherence and investigate complaints from the public.

The new rules have been broadly welcomed by child safety advocates and consumer groups, though some digital rights organisations have raised concerns about potential overreach in content moderation and the privacy implications of age-verification systems. The government has acknowledged these concerns and said an independent review process will be established to ensure the law is applied proportionately and transparently.
