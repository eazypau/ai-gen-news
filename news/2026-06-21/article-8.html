Eni and Petronas Launch Searah: Southeast Asia's Largest Independent Integrated Energy Company

Italian energy major Eni and Malaysia's national oil company Petronas have officially launched Searah, a new 50/50 joint venture that combines their selected upstream and midstream assets across Indonesia and Malaysia. The formation of Searah creates Southeast Asia's largest independent integrated energy company, with operations spanning natural gas production, LNG processing, and downstream distribution.

The Searah portfolio consists of 19 gas-producing and development assets — 14 in Indonesia and five in Malaysia — spanning conventional and deepwater fields. At launch, the joint venture begins operations with production exceeding 300,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day (boe/d), making it an immediately significant player in regional energy markets. Within three years, Searah targets sustainable production of more than 500,000 boe/d through a disciplined development programme.

To fund its ambitious growth trajectory, Searah has already secured a US$6 billion revolving credit facility from an international banking syndicate. A projected investment programme exceeding US$20 billion over the next five years underpins plans to develop several new deepwater gas fields, expand LNG liquefaction capacity, and build out pipeline and distribution infrastructure across the region.

The formation of Searah is strategically timed to capitalise on strong and sustained demand for gas in the Asia-Pacific region, where countries are transitioning away from coal while building out renewable energy infrastructure. Natural gas is seen as an essential bridge fuel for the region's energy transition, and Searah is positioned to become a key supplier to power utilities, industrial users, and LNG buyers across Japan, South Korea, China, and Southeast Asia.

Petronas Group President and Group CEO Tan Sri Tengku Muhammad Taufik expressed confidence that Searah would deliver long-term value for both partners while contributing to energy security in the region. Eni CEO Claudio Descalzi called the venture a "landmark transaction" that exemplifies how national and international oil companies can collaborate to create world-class energy champions in growth markets.
