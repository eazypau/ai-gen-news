Malaysia Enforces Social Media Ban for Children Under 16: What Parents Need to Know

Starting June 1, 2026, Malaysia officially began enforcing rules that bar children under the age of 16 from registering or maintaining social media accounts, making it one of a growing number of countries to introduce strict age-based digital protections for minors. The measure, introduced under the Child Protection Code of the Online Safety Act 2025, requires social media platforms with more than 8 million Malaysian users to actively verify user ages and block those who do not meet the minimum age threshold.

The enforcement affects all major social media platforms including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Snapchat, and X. Each platform must implement a combination of document-based verification, AI-powered age inference, and parental consent mechanisms. Platforms that fail to adequately enforce the age restriction risk financial penalties and, in extreme cases, licence revocation under Malaysian communications law.

For parents, the change means that children who previously held accounts on these platforms may find their accounts suspended or required to complete age verification. The MCMC has advised parents to use this transition as an opportunity to have open conversations with their children about digital safety, responsible online behaviour, and the risks associated with unregulated social media use.

Child welfare organisations in Malaysia have broadly welcomed the measure, citing research linking excessive social media use in adolescents to mental health issues including anxiety, depression, body image disorders, and cyberbullying. Advocates noted that Malaysia's approach mirrors similar laws enacted in Australia and parts of Europe, reflecting a growing global consensus on the need to protect children in digital spaces.

Critics, however, have raised questions about enforcement feasibility and the potential for children to circumvent age restrictions using false identities or VPNs. Digital rights groups have also called for accompanying digital literacy education programmes so that when children do eventually gain access to social media at age 16, they are better equipped to navigate it safely and responsibly.
