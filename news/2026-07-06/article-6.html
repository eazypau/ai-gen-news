Cost-of-Living Pressures Persist Despite Stronger Ringgit

Despite the Malaysian ringgit strengthening to around RM4.00–4.10 against the US dollar — among its best levels in several years — many Malaysians report that everyday expenses continue to feel burdensome, with dining out, insurance premiums, and education costs rising noticeably in 2026.

The ringgit's appreciation has been widely celebrated as a sign of macroeconomic recovery, supported by a narrowing current account deficit, stronger FDI inflows, and relative political stability. However, economists caution that currency strength does not automatically translate into lower retail prices, particularly for services, which are priced domestically and driven by local wages and operational costs rather than exchange rates.

According to a recent consumer survey, the three areas where Malaysians feel the most pinch are dining and food service (up an estimated 8–12% year-on-year as restaurant operators pass on higher labour and ingredient costs), private insurance premiums (rising by 4.7% on average), and private school and tuition fees (climbing 6–9% amid surging demand). These categories are not well-captured in the official Consumer Price Index, which recorded headline inflation of just 2.0% in May 2026.

Housing remains a key pain point, particularly in urban centres like Kuala Lumpur, Penang, and the Klang Valley, where property prices have not softened meaningfully despite the government's affordable housing initiatives. Young professionals and families with median incomes find themselves priced out of ownership and paying increasingly steep rents, with some spending more than 40% of household income on accommodation.

Economists urge the government to go beyond macroeconomic indicators and address structural cost pressures, including improving competition in food retail, expanding public transport to reduce car dependency, and accelerating the rollout of government-subsidised housing programmes to ensure that economic growth translates into tangible improvements in living standards for average Malaysians.
