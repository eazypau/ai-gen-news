BateriHub Signs MOU to Strengthen Malaysia's Used Battery Recycling Chain

BateriHub, operated by Battery4U Sdn. Bhd., has formalised a three-way Memorandum of Understanding with Global Energy Battery Sdn. Bhd. and MNA Metal Resources to build a more comprehensive and transparent end-of-life automotive battery collection, documentation, and recycling network across Malaysia.

The MOU, signed on July 1 at a ceremony in Kuala Lumpur, marks a significant step toward formalising what has historically been an informal and fragmented scrap battery recycling sector in Malaysia. Used automotive batteries — both conventional lead-acid batteries and the rapidly growing volume of electric vehicle lithium-ion packs — contain hazardous materials that require regulated handling, yet a large portion of these batteries currently enters unregulated channels where environmental standards are not consistently observed.

Under the agreement, BateriHub will serve as the primary collection and aggregation hub, working with automotive workshops, dealerships, and fleet operators across the country to establish a standardised battery return process. Global Energy Battery will handle the processing and recovery of valuable materials, while MNA Metal Resources brings downstream smelting and metals reclamation expertise to ensure maximum recovery of lead, lithium, cobalt, and other valuable components.

The partnership also includes a digital tracking component, where each battery collected will be assigned a unique identifier that allows its journey through the recycling chain to be traced and verified. This addresses a long-standing concern from regulators and environmental groups about "battery laundering," where hazardous materials are officially reported as recycled but in practice diverted to unlicensed operators.

The initiative aligns with Malaysia's commitment to transitioning towards a circular economy and reducing hazardous waste in landfills, both of which are key pillars of the National Environmental Policy 2030. With EV adoption accelerating and an estimated 150,000 EV battery packs expected to reach end-of-life by 2030, the urgency of building proper battery recycling infrastructure cannot be overstated. Industry observers praised the MOU as a concrete step toward meeting that challenge.
