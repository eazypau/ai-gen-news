Malaysia Launches Digital Action Plan 2030 Targeting AI Leadership

The Malaysian government officially unveiled the Malaysia Digital Action Plan 2030 on July 1, a comprehensive whole-of-government roadmap that positions artificial intelligence as the centrepiece of the country's digital economic transformation over the next five years.

The plan, launched by the Ministry of Digital, outlines six strategic pillars: AI governance and infrastructure, digital talent development, data sovereignty, cybersecurity, digital inclusion, and cross-border digital trade facilitation. Central to the strategy is the operationalisation of a National Data Commission, which will set standards for how public sector and private sector data is collected, stored, shared, and monetised in compliance with Malaysia's existing data protection laws.

The government has also committed to developing a National Digital Trust and Data Security Strategy, which will establish clear guidelines for AI deployment in critical sectors including healthcare, financial services, education, and public administration. This is partly in response to increasing concerns from civil society and industry about data privacy, algorithmic bias, and the risks of unchecked AI adoption in government services.

A key ambition articulated in the Digital Action Plan is for Malaysia to shift from being a consumer of AI technology to a producer of it. This includes investing in homegrown AI research capability through public universities and research institutions, creating tax incentives for AI product development companies registered in Malaysia, and expanding compute infrastructure — data centres and high-performance computing facilities — to support local AI workloads.

Industry leaders have generally welcomed the plan, though some have cautioned that execution will be the real test. Previous digital blueprints in Malaysia have suffered from implementation gaps, inter-agency coordination challenges, and funding inconsistencies. The government has sought to address this by establishing a Digital Economy Delivery Unit modelled on the Pemandu approach, which will be responsible for tracking KPIs and reporting publicly on progress every six months.
