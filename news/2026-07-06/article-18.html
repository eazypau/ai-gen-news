Haze Risk Rises as South-West Monsoon Season Continues Through September

Malaysian authorities have raised the haze alert level as the South-West Monsoon season's drier conditions create favourable circumstances for open burning activities in Sumatra and Kalimantan, with transboundary smoke a growing concern for Peninsular Malaysia through July and August.

The Department of Environment (DOE) and Malaysian Meteorological Department have jointly issued advisories warning that reduced rainfall and lower humidity during the South-West Monsoon peak — typically July through September — historically coincides with elevated incidence of land and peatland fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan, Indonesia, from which smoke frequently drifts across the Strait of Malacca into Malaysia.

Air quality index (AQI) readings across several monitoring stations in Peninsular Malaysia have already shown intermittent elevated particulate matter readings in recent days, prompting the DOE to activate early warning protocols. Schools in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur have been advised to review their haze emergency action plans and ensure students are aware of health advisories that may be issued if AQI levels worsen.

Malaysia and Indonesia have both committed under the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution to coordinate fire prevention and suppression efforts, but enforcement of no-burn regulations at the local level in Indonesia remains inconsistent. Diplomatic channels have been activated to remind Indonesian counterparts of their obligations, with Malaysia's Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability Ministry expressing concern that this year's dry conditions may lead to a repeat of severe haze episodes seen in previous years.

Members of the public with respiratory conditions, the elderly, young children, and pregnant women are advised to minimise outdoor activity on days when AQI readings exceed the "Unhealthy" threshold. N95 masks remain available through public health clinics and pharmacies. The government has reiterated that open burning in Malaysia is prohibited year-round under the Environmental Quality Act, with inspections intensified during the current high-risk period.
