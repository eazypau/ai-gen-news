Malaysia Prepares Athletes for Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games

With the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games less than three weeks away — scheduled from July 23 to August 2 — the Olympic Council of Malaysia (OCM) has finalised its contingent and is ramping up preparations for what it hopes will be a medal-rich campaign in Scotland.

Malaysia's contingent for Glasgow 2026 is one of the largest the country has ever assembled for a Commonwealth Games, with athletes competing in badminton, squash, cycling, shooting, gymnastics, swimming, athletics, weightlifting, and lawn bowls, among other disciplines. The OCM has set a target of finishing in the top eight of the overall medal tally, which would be a historic achievement for Malaysian sport.

Badminton remains Malaysia's primary medal hope, with the national team fielding strong squads in both the men's and women's events. Squash, where Malaysia has traditionally punched above its weight internationally, is another priority sport, with several world-ranked players set to compete. The cycling team, which has seen significant investment in high-performance training and international competition experience since 2023, is also expected to contend for podium positions in track events.

The OCM has worked closely with the National Sports Institute (NSI) to ensure athletes arrive in Scotland at peak physical condition, with a planned acclimatisation camp in the UK before the Games begin. Sports science support including nutrition management, psychological preparation, and injury prevention protocols have been significantly enhanced following a comprehensive review of Malaysia's sports high-performance programme after the Paris 2024 Olympics.

Looking beyond Glasgow, Malaysian sport officials are simultaneously planning for the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games from September 19 to October 4, making the second half of 2026 one of the most demanding multi-event international competition periods in recent memory. Officials are managing scheduling carefully to ensure athletes competing at Glasgow can recover adequately before the Asian Games preparations intensify.
