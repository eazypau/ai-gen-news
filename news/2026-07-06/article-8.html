Malaysia Semiconductor Sector: Chip Rally Broadens Beyond AI

Malaysia's chip and semiconductor sector is experiencing a broadening rally, with the upcycle now extending beyond artificial intelligence applications to lift the wider supply chain, giving Malaysian players a foundation analysts describe as durable rather than speculative.

The Malaysia Advanced Packaging Consortium — comprising listed companies SkyeChip Bhd, Inari Amertron, and Pentamaster Corp Bhd — has outlined a localised technology roadmap targeting pilot validation in 2026 and commercial-scale 2.5D chip packaging by 2028. This positions Malaysian firms to capture higher-value parts of the semiconductor supply chain that have historically been dominated by Taiwan and South Korea.

Advanced packaging has become a critical area of competition globally as conventional chip-scaling approaches reach physical limits. The 2.5D and 3D packaging techniques being targeted by the Malaysian consortium involve stacking multiple chips or chiplets together in a single package, dramatically increasing computing density without requiring fundamentally new fabrication processes. Demand for these capabilities is growing rapidly, driven by AI accelerators, high-performance computing chips, and next-generation networking hardware.

Analysts at several investment banks have upgraded their outlook for Malaysian semiconductor stocks, citing improving order books, rising average selling prices, and new customer wins from US and European fabless chip design companies looking to diversify their supply chains away from geopolitically sensitive locations. Export data for Q1 2026 showed Malaysia's electrical and electronic exports rose 18% year-on-year, their fastest pace since 2021.

Despite the optimism, industry veterans caution that Malaysia faces talent shortages in advanced engineering disciplines, particularly process engineers, chip designers, and materials scientists. Closing this gap will require sustained investment in university research programmes, targeted immigration policies to attract overseas talent, and industry-academia partnerships — all of which the government has signalled it will prioritise under the Digital Action Plan 2030.
