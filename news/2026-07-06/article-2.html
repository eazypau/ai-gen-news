PM Anwar Warns Against Racial and Religious Rhetoric in Politics

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has delivered a stark warning against the growing use of racial and religious rhetoric in Malaysian politics, declaring that the nation's future hinges on rejecting extremism and reinforcing unity across all communities.

Speaking at a national unity event, Anwar emphasised that Malaysia's pluralistic makeup is its greatest strength, not a vulnerability to be exploited. He called on politicians from all parties to engage voters on substance — economic policy, governance, and social welfare — rather than stoking inter-ethnic and inter-religious tensions for short-term political gain.

The remarks come as Malaysia approaches a series of state elections, including the hotly contested Johor state election on July 11, where identity-based politics has increasingly surfaced on the campaign trail. Anwar has been particularly vocal in criticising parties that resort to polarising narratives as a way to mobilise base voters, warning that such tactics erode social cohesion and are ultimately counterproductive to national development.

The Prime Minister also addressed recent controversies over online statements by politicians and social media personalities that have inflamed racial sensitivities, calling on the Home Ministry and communication regulators to act more decisively. He reiterated that while freedom of expression is protected, it must be exercised responsibly, especially in the current politically charged climate.

Analysts say Anwar's speech reflects his broader challenge in holding together the diverse Pakatan Harapan coalition, which spans Malay, Chinese, Indian, and East Malaysian communities, while also trying to win over moderate voters who are disillusioned with both government and opposition camps ahead of the next general election.
