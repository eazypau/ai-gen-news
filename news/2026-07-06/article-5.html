Malaysia's Economy Grew 5.4% in Q1 2026, Beating Expectations

Malaysia's gross domestic product expanded by 5.4% in the first quarter of 2026, surpassing analyst forecasts and marking the second consecutive quarter of outperformance, even as global headwinds from the West Asia conflict and US trade policy uncertainty persist.

Bank Negara Malaysia confirmed the figures in its quarterly economic bulletin, noting that the growth was driven primarily by robust domestic demand, which expanded 5.2% year-on-year. Household spending remained resilient amid a tight labour market, with unemployment falling to a decade-low of 2.9% and inflation staying moderate at 1.6% during the quarter — well within the central bank's comfort zone.

The services sector remained the main growth engine, expanding 6.1% as tourism receipts surged with Visit Malaysia 2026 campaign driving record international arrivals. Manufacturing grew 5.9%, buoyed by continued strong export orders for electrical and electronic products, semiconductors, and rubber gloves, which benefited from supply-chain diversification trends as global firms relocated production from China.

However, not all sectors shared equally in the growth. Mining and quarrying contracted by 2.1%, reflecting lower crude oil and natural gas output from ageing fields, while agriculture grew a modest 2.6% amid weaker palm oil prices. Construction growth of 7.7% reflected continued public infrastructure spending under the Madani Economy framework, including highway projects, hospital expansions, and affordable housing construction.

Bank Negara maintained the overnight policy rate at 3.00%, signalling confidence in the recovery trajectory while acknowledging that risks from a potential escalation in the West Asia conflict and a sharper-than-expected global slowdown remain present. The central bank reiterated its full-year GDP growth forecast of between 4.5% and 5.5% for 2026, which appears comfortably achievable based on first-quarter performance.
