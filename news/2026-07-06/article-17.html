Johor and Selangor Face Rainfall Deficit Up to 60% Below Average

The Malaysian Meteorological Department has warned that several states — including Johor, Selangor, Negeri Sembilan, and Melaka — could experience cumulative rainfall deficits of up to 60% below their long-term average in July and August 2026, raising concerns about water supply, agriculture, and forest fire risks.

The drier-than-usual conditions are attributed to the South-West Monsoon, which commenced on May 14 and is expected to persist through September. During the South-West Monsoon, prevailing atmospheric conditions typically suppress cloud formation and reduce rainfall across much of Peninsular Malaysia. This year's monsoon season is tracking somewhat drier than average across multiple stations, based on readings from MetMalaysia's national weather observation network.

Water authorities in the affected states have been placed on alert. Syarikat Bekalan Air Selangor (SYABAS) confirmed that reservoir levels remain within safe operational ranges as of early July, but cautioned that if the rainfall deficit persists through August without significant precipitation, some form of water rationing may need to be considered for parts of the Klang Valley. Communities are encouraged to conserve water and report leaks in the supply network.

Agricultural sectors dependent on consistent rainfall — particularly oil palm plantations, paddy fields in northern states, and smallholder vegetable and fruit farms — are monitoring the situation closely. Prolonged dry spells during the critical crop development periods reduce yields and can trigger price increases in local food markets, adding to inflationary pressure on a population already sensitive to cost-of-living concerns.

Despite generally drier conditions, MetMalaysia noted that localised heavy downpours and thunderstorms remain possible, particularly in the early morning hours over the western parts of Peninsular Malaysia, due to squall line phenomena over the Strait of Malacca. Residents in affected areas are advised not to let their guard down and to remain prepared for sudden weather changes even as overall rainfall totals remain below normal.
