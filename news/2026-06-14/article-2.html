Anwar Proposes 10-Year Term Limit for Malaysian Prime Ministers

Bloomberg / ISIS Malaysia 14 June 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has formally proposed a constitutional amendment that would limit any prime minister to serving no more than two full five-year terms — a maximum of ten years in office. The proposal, tabled in parliament earlier this year, is widely interpreted as part of Anwar's effort to distinguish his administration from past governments and signal a commitment to democratic reform.

The suggestion has drawn mixed reactions across Malaysia's political spectrum. Supporters within Pakatan Harapan hail it as a long-overdue check on executive power, arguing it would prevent the entrenchment of power that characterized previous administrations. Critics, however, question whether the amendment goes far enough given the absence of other structural reforms such as electoral boundary reviews and campaign finance regulations.

Political scientist Dr Bridget Welsh commented that the term limit proposal, while symbolically significant, needs to be accompanied by institutional reforms to be truly meaningful. She noted that Malaysia has historically seen prime ministers wield considerable influence over the legislature, judiciary and civil service, and that term limits alone cannot address systemic governance weaknesses.

The proposed amendment would need the support of two-thirds of Parliament to be enshrined in the Federal Constitution. As of June 2026, the Unity Government coalition has the numbers to pass the amendment if all members vote in favour, though negotiations with opposition-aligned parties over the precise wording remain ongoing.

Beyond the term limit, Anwar has also signalled plans to pursue a wider set of governance reforms in 2026 and 2027, including greater parliamentary oversight of government-linked companies (GLCs), a strengthened independent anti-corruption commission and transparent judicial appointments. These measures, if implemented, could mark the most sweeping governance overhaul Malaysia has seen in decades.
