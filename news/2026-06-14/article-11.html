Pantai Hospital KL Launches AI-Powered Adaptive Radiotherapy System

Healthcare IT News 14 June 2026

Pantai Hospital Kuala Lumpur has unveiled a state-of-the-art AI-powered adaptive radiotherapy system, marking a significant milestone in personalised cancer care in Malaysia. The system, one of only a handful in Southeast Asia, uses real-time imaging and machine learning algorithms to dynamically adjust radiation delivery during each treatment session, ensuring that the tumour receives maximum dose while minimising exposure to surrounding healthy tissue.

Traditional radiotherapy plans are typically designed before treatment begins and remain fixed throughout a course of therapy, even as a tumour shrinks or shifts position. The new adaptive system at Pantai Hospital addresses this limitation by continuously re-evaluating the patient's anatomy and modifying the treatment plan on the fly, improving both precision and patient comfort. Early clinical results from centres in the United States and Europe have shown meaningful improvements in tumour control rates and reductions in treatment side effects.

The hospital's head of radiation oncology, Dr Lim Siow Meng, said the new system will be particularly beneficial for cancers of the abdomen and pelvis — such as prostate, cervical and rectal cancers — where organ movement poses the greatest challenge to accurate radiation delivery. He noted that Malaysian patients will now have access to a level of radiotherapy precision previously only available in Singapore, Australia or the United Kingdom without the need to travel abroad.

The investment in advanced oncology technology reflects the broader ambition of Malaysia's private hospital sector to attract both domestic and international cancer patients. With cancer remaining the third leading cause of death in Malaysia and incidence rates projected to rise as the population ages, demand for specialist oncology services is expected to grow substantially over the next decade.

Pantai Hospital's management indicated that the AI radiotherapy system will be integrated with the hospital's existing oncology data platform, allowing for longitudinal outcome tracking and contributing anonymised data to regional cancer research initiatives. Plans are also underway to extend the technology to Pantai Hospital's Penang and Ipoh branches by 2027, broadening patient access across the country.
