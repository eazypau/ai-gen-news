Malaysia's Tech Sector Rides High on AI and Data Centre Boom

Bernama 14 June 2026

Malaysia's technology sector is experiencing a landmark expansion phase, propelled by surging global demand for AI infrastructure and hyperscale data centre capacity. A comprehensive Bernama analysis published this week reveals that foreign direct investment (FDI) in AI and cloud-related facilities has reached record levels in 2026, cementing Malaysia's status as the preferred digital infrastructure destination in Southeast Asia.

Johor and Kuala Lumpur have emerged as the twin poles of this data centre gold rush, with major cloud providers including Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud and Alibaba Cloud all having established or expanded their Malaysian footprints in the past 18 months. The combined investment value of data centre commitments in Malaysia now exceeds RM150 billion, dwarfing the investments seen in comparable regional markets.

Malaysia's competitive advantages — stable electricity supply, English-language proficiency, political stability and a maturing regulatory environment — have made it attractive to hyperscalers seeking to serve the broader Asia-Pacific market. The government's proactive Digital Economy Blueprint and a streamlined approvals process for data centre construction permits have also been cited by investors as key deciding factors.

The AI boom is creating a parallel demand for skilled talent, however, that Malaysia is struggling to fully meet. The Malaysian Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC) has ramped up training programmes, targeting 50,000 AI-ready workers by the end of 2026, but industry leaders warn that the pipeline of specialised talent — particularly in machine learning engineering and AI safety research — remains far below what the expanding sector requires.

The Bernama analysis concludes that if Malaysia can address its talent bottleneck through targeted education reform and strategic immigration policies to attract international tech professionals, the country is well-placed to sustain its data centre and AI boom well into the 2030s. Officials from the Ministry of Digital are due to present a new National AI Talent Strategy to the Cabinet next month.
