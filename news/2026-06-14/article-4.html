Anwar's Cabinet Reshuffle: New Ministers Face Steep Challenges

ISEAS Perspective 14 June 2026

A detailed analysis published by the Institute of Southeast Asian Studies (ISEAS) has assessed the opportunities and challenges facing the new ministers appointed in Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's December 2025 cabinet reshuffle. The reshuffle, which replaced or reshuffled more than a dozen portfolios, was described as Anwar's most significant cabinet reorganisation since taking office in 2022.

The ISEAS paper notes that several incoming ministers inherit portfolios burdened by delayed reform agendas, bureaucratic inertia and public expectations shaped by years of promises. The health, education and economic planning portfolios in particular are under intense scrutiny, with civil society groups and the media closely monitoring whether new appointees can deliver tangible improvements within their first six months.

Among the new faces, the Minister of Economy faces the unenviable task of managing structural fiscal reforms — including subsidy rationalisation and a new investment incentives framework — in an environment of global economic uncertainty. Meanwhile, the incoming Minister of Health must press ahead with the ambitious national health system reform while managing staffing shortages in public hospitals and addressing the longstanding complaint of excessively long waiting times.

The ISEAS analysis also highlights the political dynamics at play, noting that coalition arithmetic means some cabinet positions reflect inter-party dealmaking rather than purely meritocratic selection. This constraint can complicate the ability of new ministers to implement bold policies, particularly when their party's interests diverge from the broader reform agenda.

Despite these challenges, the paper concludes on a cautiously optimistic note, arguing that the reshuffle has injected fresh energy into several critical portfolios and that some of the new appointees bring strong technical backgrounds that augur well for reform delivery. The critical test, ISEAS says, will come in the next 18 months when Anwar's government enters the second half of its term and political pressures around the next general election begin to intensify.
