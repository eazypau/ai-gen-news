International Scam Syndicates Shifting Base to Malaysia After Regional Crackdowns

MalaysiaNow / Free Malaysia Today 14 June 2026

Malaysian law enforcement agencies have raised a serious alarm after intelligence reports indicate that international cybercrime and scam syndicates are actively relocating parts of their operations to Malaysia. The shift follows intensified law enforcement crackdowns in Myanmar, Cambodia and the Philippines — countries that have been major hubs for scam call centres targeting victims across Asia, Australia, Europe and North America.

Sources within the Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) and the National Scam Response Centre (NSRC) say several new clusters of suspected scam operations have been identified in Johor, Kuala Lumpur and Sabah, with authorities having conducted a series of raids over the past three weeks. Those detained include both foreign nationals and Malaysians allegedly providing logistical support, telecommunications infrastructure and money mule services to the criminal networks.

The syndicates are known to operate a range of fraudulent schemes including romance scams, investment scams, job scams targeting job-seekers with false overseas employment offers, and so-called "pig butchering" crypto fraud schemes. Malaysian victims lost a reported RM1.6 billion to such scams in 2025, a figure authorities warn could be significantly higher in 2026 if the operational expansion of these groups is not checked.

Inspector-General of Police Tan Sri Razarudin Husain confirmed the intelligence about syndicates relocating to Malaysia and assured the public that PDRM, working with the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission and international partners including Interpol and ASEANAPOL, has mobilised a dedicated multi-agency task force to address the threat. He urged the public to remain vigilant and to report suspicious recruitment advertisements or unusual financial transactions via the NSRC hotline.

Cybersecurity experts say Malaysia's challenge in combating scam syndicate migration lies in the country's relatively sophisticated telecommunications infrastructure, which the criminals exploit to make high-volume calls and operate elaborate social engineering schemes. They recommend a combination of mandatory Know Your Customer (KYC) compliance by telcos, faster takedown of fraudulent online advertisements, and cross-border asset recovery cooperation to disrupt the financial flows that sustain these criminal networks.
