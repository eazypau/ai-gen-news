Injured Toh Ee Wei Still Eyes World Championship and Asian Games Glory

Free Malaysia Today 14 June 2026

National mixed doubles number one Toh Ee Wei has emphatically refused to shut the door on defending her World Championship title in New Delhi or competing at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, despite battling a persistent injury that has kept her off the court for several weeks. Speaking from the national training centre in Bukit Jalil, Toh said she is working closely with the BAM medical team and physiotherapists to rehabilitate as quickly as safely possible.

"I haven't given up," Toh told reporters on Saturday, adding that the injury — which she declined to describe in detail citing medical confidentiality — is responding to treatment and that she hopes to be back on court for light training within the next fortnight. She stressed that her primary focus is on recovering properly to avoid a recurrence, but that she remains motivated to represent Malaysia at both major events later in the year.

Toh Ee Wei, who won the World Championship title with her then-partner in 2025 in what was widely considered the highlight of her career to date, has been the cornerstone of Malaysia's mixed doubles programme for the past four years. Her absence from the Australian Open this week was keenly felt, with BAM coaches acknowledging that her experience and court craft are difficult to replicate among the newer pairs in the national squad.

BAM's deputy president Datuk Seri Norza Zakaria expressed optimism that Toh would recover in time, saying the association would give her "every support and resource" needed. He confirmed that BAM's selection policy for the World Championships and Asian Games would be flexible enough to accommodate players still completing rehabilitation, provided their fitness could be assured in time for competition.

The Malaysian badminton world is watching Toh's recovery closely, knowing that her presence in the squad for the World Championships and Asian Games would significantly boost the country's medal prospects. Her story of resilience is already being cited by coaches as an inspiration for younger players, reinforcing the culture of perseverance that has defined Malaysian badminton's most successful eras.
