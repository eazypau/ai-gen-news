Malaysia's RM15 Billion Flood Mitigation Plan Set to Run Until 2030

Eco-Business / Bernama 14 June 2026

Malaysia's government has reaffirmed its commitment to a comprehensive flood mitigation programme worth USD 3.3 billion (approximately RM15 billion), with implementation running through to 2030. The announcement follows a World Bank warning that Malaysia's annual flood-related economic losses — which climbed to RM933.4 million in 2024 — will continue to rise unless substantial preventive infrastructure is built ahead of increasingly severe monsoon seasons projected under climate change scenarios.

The flood mitigation programme encompasses a wide range of interventions including the construction of detention basins, the upgrading of drainage infrastructure in urban areas, the reinforcement of river embankments in flood-prone zones and the development of a national early warning system that can deliver real-time alerts to residents in affected areas. Particular focus is being placed on Klang Valley, Kelantan, Johor and Sabah — the regions historically most vulnerable to severe flooding.

Environmental scientists have pointed out that the absolute volume of rainfall in Peninsular Malaysia has increased by approximately 15% over the past 40 years, a trend consistent with global climate projections. This means that even the same drainage infrastructure that adequately handled rainfall in the past may now be insufficient, underlining the urgency of upgrading critical flood defence systems across the country.

The World Bank, which co-financed a major flood risk study with Bank Negara Malaysia in 2024, has urged the government to take a nature-based solutions approach alongside conventional infrastructure, including the preservation and restoration of wetlands, mangroves and forests that act as natural flood buffers. The government's National Adaptation Plan, targeting completion in 2026, is expected to incorporate these recommendations into a broader climate resilience strategy.

Civil society groups have broadly welcomed the investment commitment while cautioning that implementation track records for large public infrastructure projects in Malaysia have been mixed. They are calling for transparent procurement processes, rigorous project management oversight and meaningful community consultation — particularly for projects in indigenous communities' traditional territories — to ensure the flood mitigation funds deliver their intended impact on the ground.
