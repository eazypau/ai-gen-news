KL International Mobility Show 2026 Showcases Future of Transport

BizToday News 14 June 2026

The Kuala Lumpur International Mobility Show (KLIMS) 2026, running from June 12 to 21 at the Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre, is drawing thousands of visitors daily with its sweeping exhibition of next-generation mobility solutions. This year's edition marks a significant evolution from a traditional motor show into a full-spectrum mobility expo, featuring electric vehicles, autonomous driving technology, urban air mobility concepts, and connected transportation infrastructure.

Over 80 automotive and technology brands from Malaysia, Japan, South Korea, Germany and China have set up exhibitor booths, with several major global reveals taking place at KL for the first time. Among the highlights are the regional debut of Toyota's second-generation solid-state battery electric vehicle, Proton's long-awaited all-electric flagship SUV concept, and Geely's showcase of its autonomous driving stack adapted for Malaysian road conditions.

Malaysia's national car makers Proton and Perodua have used KLIMS 2026 to signal their ambition to compete in the emerging EV market. Proton's concept EV, developed in collaboration with parent company Geely, is expected to enter production in late 2027 and has already attracted significant pre-registration interest. Perodua, for its part, unveiled a hybrid powertrain system for its next-generation Axia, targeting the mass-market segment with an affordable electrification option.

Beyond the vehicles themselves, KLIMS 2026 features an Innovation Pavilion dedicated to smart city mobility solutions, where Malaysian start-ups and government agencies are showcasing intelligent traffic management systems, shared mobility platforms and last-mile delivery solutions powered by electric cargo bikes and autonomous delivery pods. The Pavilion has drawn interest from city planners and transport ministers from several ASEAN capitals who are visiting KL specifically for the show.

The Malaysian Automotive Association has projected that EV sales in Malaysia will surpass 30,000 units in 2026 — a record — helped by expanded government incentives, a growing charging infrastructure network and increased consumer confidence as EV model choices have broadened substantially. KLIMS 2026 is seen as a pivotal moment in normalising EV ownership for the Malaysian mainstream, and organisers expect total visitor numbers to exceed 200,000 over the 10-day event.
