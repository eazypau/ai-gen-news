Malaysia Seeks Alternative Missile Suppliers After Norway Revokes Export Licence

MalaysiaNow 14 June 2026

Malaysia's defence procurement plans have been thrown into disarray after the Norwegian government revoked the export licence for its Naval Strike Missile (NSM) system, a weapon that Kuala Lumpur had been counting on to substantially upgrade the Royal Malaysian Navy's (RMN) anti-ship strike capability. The revocation is understood to be linked to Norway's tightened arms export regulations amid shifting geopolitical alignments, though Norwegian officials have not publicly disclosed the specific grounds for the decision.

The Ministry of Defence has confirmed that it is now urgently evaluating alternative surface-launched anti-ship missile systems from several European and Asian manufacturers. Systems reportedly under consideration include the French MBDA Exocet MM40 Block 3, the Swedish Saab RBS-15 Mk4, and the MBDA Marte ER from Italy. South Korean and Turkish manufacturers have also reportedly approached Malaysian defence authorities to pitch their respective missile products.

Defence analysts say the development is a significant setback for the RMN's maritime domain awareness and strike capabilities at a time when tensions in the South China Sea remain elevated. Malaysia has ongoing maritime sovereignty disputes with several neighbours, and the planned acquisition of a modern anti-ship missile was considered a strategic priority for the navy's force modernisation programme under the current defence white paper.

The Minister of Defence, Datuk Seri Mohamad Hassan, said the government would move quickly to identify a suitable alternative and that the revocation would not cause a long-term capability gap. He acknowledged, however, that the procurement process for complex weapons systems takes time and that some delay in the upgrade timeline was inevitable. Opposition members of Parliament have used the episode to call for a review of Malaysia's defence procurement framework to reduce dependence on single-source suppliers.

The NSM episode has also renewed discussions within Malaysian defence circles about the country's approach to strategic arms procurement, with some analysts arguing that Malaysia should prioritise suppliers from nations that are less likely to impose politically motivated export restrictions. The incident is expected to feature prominently in upcoming parliamentary debates on the defence budget and the future direction of the RMN's modernisation agenda.
