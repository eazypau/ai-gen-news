Indonesian Patients Fuel Growth in Malaysia's Medical Tourism Industry

New Straits Times 14 June 2026

Malaysia's medical tourism industry is experiencing a significant uptick in revenue and patient numbers, driven primarily by a surge of Indonesian patients seeking specialised treatments unavailable or prohibitively expensive in their home country. The trend was prominently on display at the Malaysia Fair 2026 in South Jakarta on June 5, where Malaysian hospitals and clinics showcased their services to a capacity crowd of Indonesian consumers.

Hospitals from the Pantai, KPJ and Columbia Asia networks reported that Indonesian patients now account for the largest single nationality group among their international patient portfolios, surpassing traditional medical tourists from Brunei and the Middle East. The specialties most in demand include cardiology, oncology, orthopaedics and fertility treatments, where Malaysia's internationally accredited facilities offer outcomes comparable to those in Singapore or Thailand at significantly lower costs.

Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC) CEO Sherene Azli attributed the growth to improved air connectivity between Indonesian cities and Malaysia, competitive pricing, and the cultural and linguistic affinity between Malaysians and Indonesians. She noted that the average Indonesian medical tourist spends approximately RM8,000 per visit, contributing meaningfully to the hospitality and retail sectors in Penang, Kuala Lumpur and Johor Bahru.

The growing medical tourism inflow has prompted Malaysian hospitals to expand their Bahasa Indonesia translation and patient coordination services, and several Penang hospitals have set up satellite liaison offices in Medan and Pekanbaru to facilitate patient referrals. The Malaysian government has also streamlined the medical visit pass application process, reducing approval times to improve the patient experience.

Malaysia is targeting RM3.6 billion in medical tourism revenue by the end of 2026, a 25% increase over 2025 figures. Officials are particularly focused on growing the oncology and genomics segments, where Malaysia has made strategic investments in cutting-edge equipment and specialist talent over the past three years. The Malaysia Bioeconomy Development Corporation is also exploring medical tourism packages that combine treatment with wellness tourism to increase the average spend per visitor.
