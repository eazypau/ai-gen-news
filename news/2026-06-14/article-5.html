Malaysia and Japan Deepen Cooperation in AI, Quantum Tech and Semiconductors

New Straits Times 14 June 2026

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim and Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi have agreed to substantially deepen bilateral cooperation across three high-technology frontiers: artificial intelligence, quantum computing and semiconductor manufacturing. The agreement was cemented during a bilateral meeting on the sidelines of an international summit, with both leaders emphasising the strategic importance of technological self-sufficiency for their respective nations.

The cooperation framework envisions joint research programmes between Malaysian and Japanese universities, direct investment from Japanese semiconductor manufacturers into Malaysia's Penang and Kulim high-technology corridors, and knowledge-transfer programmes that will see Malaysian engineers trained at leading Japanese research institutions. Anwar specifically requested continued support from major Japanese corporations such as Sony, Renesas and Tokyo Electron to accelerate Malaysia's semiconductor ecosystem.

Malaysia's push to develop a homegrown quantum computing capability has gained momentum in 2026, with the government allocating RM800 million under the National Quantum Initiative. Japan, which has made quantum technology a national priority, sees Malaysia as a natural partner given its strong electronics manufacturing base and proximity to Southeast Asian markets. Officials say the two countries are exploring a joint quantum computing research centre to be established in Cyberjaya.

On the AI front, both governments have signed a memorandum of understanding to share regulatory frameworks and best practices for AI governance. Japanese PM Takaichi highlighted Japan's experience in responsible AI deployment across its manufacturing and healthcare sectors as something that could benefit Malaysia's own AI National Strategy, which aims to deploy AI solutions across 10 key government services by the end of 2026.

Industry groups have welcomed the bilateral agreement, with the Malaysia Semiconductor Industry Association (MSIA) calling it a "pivotal moment" for the local industry. They note that with US-China technology tensions reshuffling global supply chains, Malaysia is uniquely positioned to become a trusted manufacturing and R&D hub, and deep partnerships with Japan, a close ally of the United States, can only strengthen that positioning.
