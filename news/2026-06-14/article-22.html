Petronas and Japan's JERA Strengthen LNG Supply Partnership

New Straits Times / Bernama 14 June 2026

Malaysia's national oil company Petronas has announced an enhancement of its long-standing liquefied natural gas (LNG) supply partnership with Japan's JERA Co., Inc., one of the world's largest electricity-generating companies. The updated agreement extends the supply relationship into the 2030s and increases the annual contracted LNG volume, providing Japan with a dependable source of energy as it navigates its complex post-Fukushima energy transition.

JERA, which supplies approximately one-third of Japan's electricity generation, relies on LNG as a critical transitional fuel as Japan phases out coal and works toward its 2030 renewable energy targets. Malaysia's Bintulu LNG complex in Sarawak — one of the world's largest LNG production facilities — is a cornerstone of Petronas's export portfolio and has supplied Japan consistently for over four decades under the original Sarawak LNG long-term supply agreements.

Petronas President and Group CEO Tengku Muhammad Taufik described the strengthened partnership as reflecting the "maturity and mutual trust" built over decades of bilateral energy cooperation. He added that the deal demonstrates international confidence in Petronas's operational reliability and Malaysia's status as a long-term energy partner of choice for major economies in the Asia-Pacific region.

The enhanced partnership also includes provisions for collaboration on lower-carbon energy solutions, including the co-development of carbon capture, utilisation and storage (CCUS) technologies and the exploration of hydrogen and ammonia as potential future energy carriers under the broader energy transition roadmap. Both companies have committed to a joint working group to advance these initiatives with the aim of producing a concrete cooperation framework by 2027.

The Petronas-JERA deal arrives amid continued global competition for long-term LNG supply contracts, as European and Asian buyers seek to lock in supply following the disruptions to energy markets caused by geopolitical tensions in previous years. Analysts say the agreement reinforces Petronas's commercial position in the competitive LNG market while generating stable long-term export revenue that supports Malaysia's fiscal position and enables continued upstream investment in Sarawak and Sabah gas fields.
