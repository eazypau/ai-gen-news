Malaysia's Economy Grew 5.4% in Q1 2026, Exceeding Expectations

Ministry of Finance Malaysia 18 June 2026 Economy

Malaysia's economy expanded by 5.4% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026, slightly above the preliminary estimate of 5.3% and outpacing expectations amid a backdrop of global economic uncertainty. The robust performance represented an easing from the 6.2% growth recorded in the fourth quarter of 2025, but nonetheless signalled resilient domestic demand and improving investment activity.

Net foreign direct investment (FDI) inflows reached RM22.8 billion in Q1 2026, reflecting renewed confidence in Malaysia's economic fundamentals and its strategic position as a regional hub for advanced manufacturing and digital services. Domestic demand held firm, underpinned by strong private consumption and an uptick in capital formation.

The unemployment rate fell to 2.9%, marking its lowest level in a decade, while inflation remained moderate at 1.6% during the quarter. Exports reached a historic high of RM147 billion per month in January 2026, representing a 19.6% year-on-year increase driven by strong demand for Malaysia's electrical, electronics and semiconductor products.

The services sector, which accounts for around 54% of GDP, maintained its central role in driving growth, while the manufacturing sector — representing approximately 25% of GDP — continued to benefit from global supply chain diversification trends. Both sectors contributed positively to the headline growth figure.

The Ministry of Finance said Malaysia's full-year 2026 GDP growth is projected at around 4.8%, contingent on an easing of geopolitical tensions in West Asia. The government remains committed to implementing structural reforms and diversifying the economy to sustain long-term growth momentum.
