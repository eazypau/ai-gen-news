Malaysia Property Market Forecast: 3–5% Price Growth Expected in 2026

PropertyGuru Malaysia / EdgeProp 18 June 2026 Property

The Malaysian property market is entering a new cycle of recovery in 2026, with the national House Price Index forecast to grow between 3% and 5% for the full year, according to property analysts and industry reports. The recovery is being driven by improving economic fundamentals, stable interest rates, strong government support measures and a gradual normalisation of demand following years of sluggish growth.

Johor is projected to be the fastest-growing market in the country, buoyed by renewed optimism around the imminent completion of the Johor Bahru–Singapore RTS Link. Increased cross-border economic activity and heightened demand from Singapore-based buyers and investors are expected to drive price appreciation and transaction volumes in the southern state. Selangor continues to dominate overall market share, accounting for 21.6% of total residential property transactions nationally, followed by Johor at 16.3% and Perak at 11.4%.

As of early 2026, the average house price in Malaysia stands at approximately RM495,000, with an average price per square metre of around RM4,900 for residential property. However, there are significant regional and product-type variations, with luxury properties in prime Kuala Lumpur locations commanding substantially higher prices while affordable housing in secondary markets remains accessible to first-time buyers.

Government support measures continue to underpin the market, with stamp duty exemptions for first-time homebuyers on properties priced up to RM500,000 extended through 2027. The RM20 billion Housing Credit Guarantee Scheme (SJKP) has also expanded access to home financing for gig workers and self-employed Malaysians who would otherwise struggle to qualify for conventional mortgages.

One concern flagging among analysts is the rising unsold housing stock, with the residential supply overhang climbing 30.5% year-on-year to 28,672 units in Q3 2025. Industry stakeholders are calling for a more targeted approach to new development approvals to ensure supply is better aligned with actual market demand across different price segments and locations.
