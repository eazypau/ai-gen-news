SEMICON SEA 2026: Malaysian Firms Target 7% of Global Advanced Packaging Market

MIDA / TechWireAsia 18 June 2026 Technology

A consortium of five Malaysian semiconductor companies made a bold debut at SEMICON Southeast Asia 2026 in Kuala Lumpur, announcing a collective target of capturing 7% of the global advanced packaging market by 2035. The announcement was one of the headline moments at the annual industry event, held from May 5–7 at MITEC under the theme "Transform Tomorrow."

Malaysia's electrical and electronics sector generated RM465 billion in semiconductor exports last year, making it one of the country's most vital export industries. The country has long been a dominant player in back-end semiconductor operations such as assembly, testing and packaging, but SEMICON SEA 2026 signalled a clear ambition to move into more advanced, higher-value segments of the semiconductor supply chain.

Germany's Aixtron also signed an agreement with MIDA at the event to build a US$47 million semiconductor manufacturing facility in Malaysia, with production expected to commence in the second half of 2027. This adds to a growing list of global semiconductor firms expanding their Malaysian footprint, which includes major investments by companies from the United States, Europe, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan.

However, the industry's rapid expansion has exposed a significant talent challenge. Malaysia needs approximately 50,000 skilled engineers to meet current industry demand, while Malaysian universities produce only around 5,000 engineering graduates annually — a tenfold gap that industry leaders and government officials have described as one of the most pressing constraints on sector growth.

MIDA and the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry have announced a series of initiatives to address the talent shortage, including expanded university-industry partnerships, upskilling programmes for existing workers, and targeted efforts to attract Malaysian engineers working abroad to return home. The government is also exploring fast-track pathways for qualified foreign engineers to work in Malaysia's semiconductor sector.
