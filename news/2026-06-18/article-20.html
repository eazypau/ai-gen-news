Police Rescue Over 400 Cats from Illegal Slaughter Ring

Malay Mail 18 June 2026 Society

Malaysian police conducted a major cross-state operation that resulted in the arrest of nine individuals and the rescue of more than 400 cats that had been destined for illegal slaughter. The operation, which was coordinated by the Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) in collaboration with the Department of Veterinary Services, dismantled a criminal network that had been illegally capturing, transporting and killing cats for several months.

The rescued animals were found in distressing conditions, confined in overcrowded cages and showing signs of stress, malnutrition and injuries from capture. More than 40 of the rescued cats have since been reunited with their rightful owners, many of whom had reported their pets missing in the weeks leading up to the operation. Authorities are working to identify the owners of the remaining animals with the help of microchip records and social media appeals.

The nine suspects arrested face charges under Malaysia's Animal Welfare Act 2015, which prohibits cruelty to animals and the illegal killing of pets. If convicted, they could face significant fines and imprisonment. Investigators are also pursuing additional charges related to property theft for the stolen pets and possible links to the illegal wildlife and animal trade.

The case has sparked widespread public outrage on Malaysian social media, with tens of thousands of people sharing information about the rescue and expressing support for stronger enforcement of animal welfare laws. Animal welfare advocates have long called for increased resources for the Department of Veterinary Services and stiffer penalties for animal cruelty to serve as a more effective deterrent.

Members of the public who believe their cats may be among the rescued animals are encouraged to contact the nearest Department of Veterinary Services office with identification details and photographs. Animal welfare organisations are also volunteering to assist with the care and rehoming of the rescued cats that cannot be reunited with their original owners, ensuring all animals receive proper veterinary attention and appropriate placement.
