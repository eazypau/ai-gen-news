PAS Cuts Ties with Bersatu, Shaking Up Perikatan Nasional

Free Malaysia Today 18 June 2026 Politics

The Islamist party PAS has officially severed its coalition ties with Parti Pribumi Bersatu Malaysia (Bersatu), sending shockwaves through the Perikatan Nasional (PN) opposition bloc. The split follows months of internal tension between the two parties over policy direction, leadership decisions and electoral strategy.

Political observers say the rupture significantly weakens the Perikatan Nasional coalition's cohesion ahead of the next general election, which must be held by 2027 at the latest. PAS has long been the backbone of PN's electoral success in rural Malay-majority constituencies, and any fracture in the alliance could have major consequences for the opposition's ability to mount a credible challenge to the ruling Pakatan Harapan-led unity government.

Amanah vice-president Mahfuz Omar, commenting on the development, noted that Johor has benefitted significantly from its close cooperation with DAP and the federal government — a pointed remark suggesting the ruling coalition could capitalise on the PN fracture in swing states. Other Pakatan Harapan leaders have called the split a sign of the opposition's internal contradictions.

Bersatu, led by former Prime Minister Muhyiddin Yassin, had already been weakened by a series of legal challenges and defections. The loss of PAS as a coalition partner could further marginalise Bersatu politically, unless the party can form new alliances or consolidate support among Malay nationalist voters who previously backed PN.

PAS president Tan Sri Abdul Hadi Awang has not ruled out forming a new opposition alliance, and party leaders are said to be in preliminary discussions with other parties. The political realignment is expected to intensify as Malaysia approaches its next electoral cycle, with both government and opposition scrambling to reassess their strategies.
