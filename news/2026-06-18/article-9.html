JB–Singapore RTS Link on Track for End-2026 Passenger Launch

New Straits Times 18 June 2026 Infrastructure

The Johor Bahru–Singapore Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link remains on schedule to commence passenger service by the end of 2026, with project authorities confirming that construction and systems integration work are progressing as planned. The cross-border light rail project, a joint initiative between Malaysia and Singapore, will connect Bukit Chagar Station in Johor Bahru to Woodlands North Station in Singapore via a 25-metre-high bridge across the Straits of Johor.

The RTS Link will be capable of serving up to 10,000 commuters per hour in each direction during peak periods, with a total journey time of approximately five minutes between the two terminus stations. A unique feature of the project is the co-location of Customs, Immigration and Quarantine (CIQ) facilities at both ends, allowing passengers to clear both Malaysian and Singaporean authorities at their point of departure for a seamless cross-border experience.

The joint operating entity, RTS Operations Pte Ltd — a partnership between Malaysia's Prasarana Malaysia Bhd and Singapore's SMRT Corporation — is on track to begin trial operations ahead of the public launch. Extensive testing of signalling, rolling stock and station systems is underway to ensure safety and reliability ahead of commercial service.

The RTS Link is expected to dramatically ease congestion on the existing Johor–Singapore Causeway and the Second Link, which together handle millions of crossings annually. Authorities project a reduction of at least 35% in private vehicle traffic at the Causeway once the RTS Link is fully operational, providing significant relief to commuters who currently face lengthy queues during peak hours.

Johor's property market has already been responding to the anticipated completion of the RTS Link, with residential and commercial developments near Bukit Chagar recording increased interest from both local and Singapore-based buyers. Analysts expect the connectivity boost to further accelerate Johor's economic integration with Singapore, transforming the Iskandar Malaysia zone into a more vibrant economic corridor.
