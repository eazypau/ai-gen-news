Malaysia Lowers Primary School Entry Age to 6 Under New Education Plan

The Vibes / Bernama 18 June 2026 Education

Malaysia will lower the mandatory primary school entry age from 7 to 6 years old as part of the ambitious National Education Plan (NEP) 2026–2035, unveiled by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. The change is one of the most significant structural reforms to the national education system in decades and is designed to align Malaysia with international standards and provide children with more years of structured learning during their critical formative years.

The NEP 2026–2035 sets firm academic and holistic development targets for students across all levels of the education system. The plan emphasises preparing students for the future workforce by strengthening STEM education, digital literacy, critical thinking, and soft skills development alongside traditional academic subjects. It also places a renewed emphasis on Bahasa Malaysia and English proficiency, seen as essential for both national cohesion and global competitiveness.

The blueprint was developed following extensive consultation with educators, parents, employers and international education experts. It draws on lessons from high-performing education systems in East Asia, Scandinavia and Singapore, while accounting for Malaysia's unique multicultural and socioeconomic context.

The lowering of the school entry age has generated debate among parents and education professionals. Supporters argue that earlier structured learning provides developmental advantages, particularly in language acquisition and socialisation. Critics, however, caution that six-year-olds in Malaysia may not yet have access to quality kindergarten preparation, and that the reform must be accompanied by investments in early childhood education infrastructure and teacher training.

The Ministry of Education has committed to a phased implementation of the entry age change, beginning with pilot programmes in 2026 before a full national rollout. The government has also pledged additional funding for kindergarten expansion, preschool teacher professional development and learning support programmes for children from low-income households to ensure the reform delivers equitable outcomes.
