Anwar: Malaysia-Japan Partnership Key to Stronger Technology Ecosystem

New Straits Times 18 June 2026 Technology

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has emphasised that the Malaysia-Japan partnership is central to building a stronger and more resilient technology ecosystem in both countries. Speaking at a bilateral engagement event in Kuala Lumpur, Anwar noted that the complementary strengths of Malaysia and Japan — particularly in semiconductor manufacturing, advanced materials and digital transformation — provide a strong foundation for deeper collaboration.

Japan has emerged as one of Malaysia's most significant investment partners in the technology sector, with 93.6% of Japanese approved investments in Q1 2026 directed toward digital transformation activities. This trend reflects Japan's confidence in Malaysia's growing capabilities in semiconductor assembly, testing and advanced packaging, which complement Japan's own leadership in materials science, precision equipment and advanced manufacturing processes.

Anwar highlighted the need for Malaysia to move up the technology value chain, transitioning from back-end semiconductor operations toward higher-value activities such as chip design, advanced packaging and innovation-driven manufacturing. He noted that collaboration with Japan — including joint R&D initiatives and technology transfer programmes — would be critical to achieving this ambition.

The prime minister also spoke about the need to address Malaysia's talent gap in the technology sector. The semiconductor industry requires around 50,000 skilled engineers, while Malaysian universities currently produce approximately 5,000 engineering graduates annually. Anwar indicated that Japan's expertise in technical education could help Malaysia bridge this gap through collaborative training programmes and student exchange schemes.

The Malaysia-Japan bilateral relationship has deepened significantly in recent years, with Japan contributing substantially to Malaysia's electrical and electronics, automotive and chemical industries. The two countries are also collaborating on green energy and carbon reduction initiatives, as both nations work toward net-zero emissions goals.
