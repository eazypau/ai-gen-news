KL Kampung Sungai Baru Residents March to Suhakam Over July Eviction

Malay Mail 18 June 2026 Society

Residents of Kampung Sungai Baru, a long-established village community located in the heart of Kuala Lumpur, marched to the offices of the Malaysian Human Rights Commission (Suhakam) to submit a formal memorandum protesting their scheduled eviction on July 16, 2026. The community, which has lived on the land for generations, is facing displacement to make way for an urban development project.

The memorandum calls on authorities to halt the eviction order, enter into genuine dialogue with the affected residents and provide adequate, fair and timely resettlement options for all displaced families. Residents argue that they have not been given sufficient notice, consultation or compensation commensurate with the significance of losing their homes and livelihoods in a community that has existed for decades.

Community leaders leading the march said they turned to Suhakam as a last resort after what they described as an inadequate response from city authorities and the developer involved in the project. They expressed hope that Suhakam's intervention would compel a review of the process and ensure that the rights of affected residents are fully respected in accordance with Malaysia's commitments under international human rights standards.

The Kampung Sungai Baru case has attracted the attention of housing rights advocates and civil society organisations, who have highlighted it as part of a broader pattern of urban displacement in Kuala Lumpur as the city undergoes rapid redevelopment. Rights groups are calling for stronger legal protections for urban village communities and clearer, more equitable procedures for handling forced evictions in Malaysia.

Kuala Lumpur City Hall (DBKL) has not yet issued a formal public response to the residents' memorandum. Suhakam has confirmed receipt of the submission and indicated that it will review the case. The situation is expected to develop further in the coming weeks as the July 16 eviction date approaches, with legal challenges and further public advocacy likely.
