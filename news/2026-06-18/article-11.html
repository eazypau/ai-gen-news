Education Ministry Launches Student Safety and Protection Policy

New Straits Times 18 June 2026 Education

Malaysia's Ministry of Education has unveiled new Safe School Management Guidelines and a comprehensive Student Protection Policy, marking a significant step in strengthening student welfare and safety across the country's educational institutions. The initiatives were announced by Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek, who described them as essential pillars for creating safe, inclusive and nurturing learning environments for all Malaysian students.

The Safe School Management Guidelines were developed in collaboration with experts including the National Institute of Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) and will serve as the primary reference for all educational institutions in managing physical safety, emergency preparedness and hazard mitigation. Schools are expected to implement the guidelines throughout the 2026 academic year.

The Student Protection Policy addresses a broader range of welfare concerns, including protections against bullying, abuse, discrimination and neglect. It outlines clear reporting procedures, roles and responsibilities for teachers, administrators and support staff, and establishes minimum standards for student counselling and mental health support services at both primary and secondary school levels.

Minister Fadhlina also highlighted other concurrent initiatives, including the launch of the Malaysia Education Plan 2026–2035 portal, the empowerment of technical secondary schools to align with national workforce development needs, and the ministry's iPayment system to streamline administrative processes for parents and guardians. The ministry also received a National Protection Security Special Award in recognition of its robust information and communication security management.

Education advocates have welcomed the new policies but have called for sustained funding and training to ensure effective implementation at the school level. They noted that policy success will depend heavily on teacher capacity-building and the availability of dedicated welfare and counselling staff, particularly in under-resourced rural schools.
