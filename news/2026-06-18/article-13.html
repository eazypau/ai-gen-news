Malaysia Year of Medical Tourism 2026 Records Strong Indonesian Demand

PRNewswire / MHTC 18 June 2026 Health

The Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC), in partnership with Tourism Malaysia, reported strong visitor engagement and significant commercial interest at Malaysia Fair Jakarta 2026, held from June 4–7 at Gandaria City mall in the Indonesian capital. The event, which featured health screenings, specialist consultations and hospital representatives, underscored the sustained and growing demand among Indonesian consumers for Malaysia's healthcare, wellness and tourism offerings.

The Malaysia Fair Jakarta was organised in conjunction with Malaysia Year of Medical Tourism 2026 (MYMT 2026), themed "Healing Meets Hospitality." The campaign positions Malaysia as a premier healthcare travel destination in Southeast Asia, leveraging the country's combination of internationally accredited medical facilities, experienced specialists, competitive pricing and warm cultural affinity with neighbouring countries.

Indonesia remains Malaysia's single largest source of medical tourists, with hundreds of thousands of Indonesian patients travelling to Malaysia annually for specialist treatments including oncology, cardiology, orthopaedics and fertility services. Patients are attracted by Malaysia's shorter waiting times, English-language proficiency among medical staff, and treatment costs that are significantly lower than in Singapore while matching or exceeding comparable quality standards.

MHTC chief executive Mohd Daud Mohd Arif highlighted that Malaysia is on track to achieve its 2026 medical tourism targets, with visitor numbers recovering strongly following the pandemic years. Key medical tourism hubs including Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Johor Bahru and Kota Kinabalu are all reporting increased international patient volumes in the first half of 2026.

The government has earmarked MYMT 2026 as a catalyst for positioning Malaysia among the top five medical travel destinations globally by 2030. Investments in hospital infrastructure, specialist medical education and digital health platforms are being accelerated to support this ambition and ensure Malaysia can meet growing regional and international demand for quality healthcare.
